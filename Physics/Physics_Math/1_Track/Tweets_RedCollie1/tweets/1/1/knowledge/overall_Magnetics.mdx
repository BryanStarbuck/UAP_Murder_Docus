### 1.1 Static Magnetic Constraint — The Ring of North Magnets

Source: page_001.json, tweet 2046520282811441300 (note_tweet), tweet 2045383245861392487 (note_tweet)

The baseline magnetic configuration consists of:
- A central South (S) disc magnet (designated "blue" in Drew's diagrams), free to translate along a vertical pole
- An outer ring of four North (N) permanent magnets (designated "red"), arranged in a coplanar ring geometry
- The S disc magnet is attracted to the central plane of the N-ring from both above and below, creating a planar potential well (a stable equilibrium at the plane of the ring)

The static force on the central S magnet is restoring in z: if the magnet rises above the ring plane, the attractive force from the four N magnets pulls it back down; if it falls below, the same attraction pulls it back up. This is a magnetic equivalent of a potential well confining motion to the plane of the N-ring.

**Potential well approximation:** For a dipole of moment **m_S** at displacement z from the ring plane, the restoring force is approximately:
F_z ≈ -k_mag · z
where k_mag depends on the geometry of the N-ring (spacing, radius, individual dipole moments of the N magnets). This is the no-spin baseline.
