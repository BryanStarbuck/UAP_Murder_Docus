## 1. 3-Phase Rotating Magnetic Field Mathematics

### 1.1 Phase Sequence and Field Rotation

Source: page_001.json, tweet 2044942914154082552 (note_tweet)

A ring of 6 wire coils, powered U-V-W-U-V-W in standard 3-phase (120° phase separation between U, V, W), produces a rotating magnetic field (RMF). The spatial field at angle φ around the ring produced by a 3-phase system with coils at positions φ_k = k·(2π/6) for k = 0,...,5, driven as I_k(t) = I_0·cos(ωt - phase_k):

**B_x(t)** = B_0 · cos(ωt)
**B_y(t)** = B_0 · cos(ωt - 2π/3) [for 3-coil equivalence; 6-coil arrangement produces same result with higher harmonic suppression]

Resultant field magnitude: |**B**(t)| = B_0 = constant (rotating at angular velocity ω)
Field direction angle: θ_B(t) = ωt

The field rotates uniformly at the supply frequency ω. Inside the ring, it rotates clockwise; outside the ring, it rotates anticlockwise (this property of a 6-coil 3-phase arrangement is the physical basis for the inner/outer counter-rotation).

### 1.2 Torque on Spinning Disc Magnet in RMF

The torque on a magnetic dipole **m** in the rotating field **B**(t):
**τ** = **m** × **B**(t)

When the magnet is spinning at angular velocity Ω (about the z-axis) in a field rotating at ω, the slip angular velocity is Δω = ω - Ω. For Δω > 0 (field rotating faster than magnet), the torque accelerates the magnet. For Δω = 0 (synchronous rotation), the torque is zero in the rotating frame. The equation of motion for the spin angular velocity Ω:

I · (dΩ/dt) = m · B_0 · sin(Δω · t + φ_0) - γ · Ω

where I is the moment of inertia, γ is a drag coefficient, and φ_0 is the initial phase angle between **m** and **B**.

### 1.3 Helical Ascent Force

Source: page_001.json, tweet 2044925361457398171 (note_tweet): "A disc magnet spins in a 3-phase helical magnetic field, and rises against the vacuum."

The 3-phase field has a spatial helical structure along the z-axis if the coil arrangement has phase variation in z. For a helical pitch of 2π/k_z per turn (k_z = axial wave vector), the field in cylindrical coordinates (r, φ, z):

B_φ(r, φ, z, t) = B_0(r) · cos(ωt - k_z·z - n·φ)

The axial force on a magnetic dipole m_z in this helical field:
F_z = m_z · (∂B_z/∂z) + ...

For a spinning dipole synchronized with the rotating field, the time-averaged axial force is non-zero if the field has a non-zero axial gradient — the helical field naturally provides this. The magnet "screws" up the helical field, like a nut moving along a bolt.

## 2. Angular Momentum and its Conservation

### 2.1 Conservation in the No-Current Case

Source: 1992724483212497230.yaml (note_tweet), video 13_1992723358082101248_video.mdx

Standard conservation: for an isolated system (no external torques),
d**L**/dt = 0
**L** = **L**_magnet + **L**_device = I_m·ω_m·ẑ + I_d·ω_d·ẑ = constant

For initial condition **L** = 0 (stationary):
I_m·ω_m + I_d·ω_d = 0
∴ ω_d = -(I_m/I_d)·ω_m

When the internal blade reverses (ω_m → -ω_m), ω_d must reverse sign too, to maintain L_total = 0. This is what the boat/motor video demonstrates.

### 2.2 Angular Momentum Balance Under Lorentz Force

Source: 1992724483212497230.yaml (note_tweet)

When 3-phase current is used to drive the magnet, the equation changes. Let L_field denote angular momentum stored in the electromagnetic field:

d/dt(L_mech + L_field) = 0 [total conservation]
d/dt(L_magnet + L_device) = -d/dt(L_field)

The Lorentz force exchanges angular momentum between the mechanical system and the electromagnetic field. Drew's hypothesis is that the 3-phase driving field can absorb the "downward" angular momentum reaction entirely, leaving:

L_magnet_upward ≠ 0 (net upward spin)
L_device_downward = 0 (no downward counter-spin of the vehicle)
L_field ≠ 0 (field carries the reaction)

The accomplishment mathematically: if this is achievable, then the mechanical device gains net angular momentum in the upward sense, and by the spin-orbit coupling in the gyroscopic model, this produces net upward translational force.

### 2.3 Upward Force from Gyroscopic Precession

For a spinning body of angular momentum L = I·ω, precessing in a potential V(z):

Precession rate: Ω_p = (dV/dz) / (L·sin θ) = F_z / (I·ω·sin θ)

Rearranging for the upward force equivalent: if the precession rate Ω_p is forced by the magnetic field (rather than arising from gravity), and the precession axis is directed upward, then:

F_z_effective = I·ω·Ω_p·sin θ

For the 45-degree wobble (θ = 45°):
F_z_effective = I·ω·Ω_p·(1/√2) = (L·Ω_p)/√2

This is the upward force on the device. To maximize F_z: maximize L (high spin ω, large I) and maximize Ω_p (fast precession, driven by the 3-phase field).

## 3. Magnetic Dipole Potential Well in the N-Ring

Source: page_001.json, tweet 2045383245861392487 (note_tweet), tweet 2046520282811441300 (note_tweet)

For a South disc magnet (dipole moment **m** = -m_0·ẑ) on the axis of a ring of 4 North magnets at radius R_ring, the interaction potential along the z-axis:

U(z) = -**m** · **B**_ring(z)

where B_ring(z) is the axial field produced by the 4 North dipoles at axial position z. For small z (near the ring plane):
B_ring(z) ≈ B_ring(0) - (1/2)·B_ring''(0)·z² + ...

The force: F_z(z) = -dU/dz = m_0 · B_ring'(z)

Near z = 0 (the ring plane): F_z(z) ≈ -m_0 · B_ring''(0) · z

For a positive B_ring''(0) (field diverges away from the ring plane on axis), the force is restoring: F_z ∝ -z. This is the harmonic magnetic trap.

The equilibrium condition (static case, no spin) is z = 0. The spin-modified equilibrium is what Drew observes experimentally: the spinning magnet descends to a position slightly **below** the ring plane (z slightly negative), as reported in multiple tweets: "That (blue) magnet moves slightly downward, to just below the flat plane of that counter-spinning flywheel (red)." (tweet 2044734616641417251, note_tweet)

Mathematical consequence: at z < 0 (slightly below the ring plane), the restoring force F_z > 0 (upward) is non-zero, meaning the spinning magnet is always exerting an upward push on the ring through the attractive magnetic force. This provides the persistent upward push on the flywheel from below.
