## 1. Gyroscopic Mechanics and Precession as the Lift Mechanism

### 1.1 Spinning Top Analogy (Barbury Castle 1991 Interpretation)

Source: 1929005668222042437.yaml, referenced_tweet text from tweet 1928967971021705481

Drew interprets the Barbury Castle crop picture (July 1991) as describing: "the horizontal-spin, then rise-precession, of a 'spinning top'."

A spinning top under gravity undergoes gyroscopic precession: the torque **τ** = **r** × m**g** applied to the spinning axis causes the angular momentum vector **L** = I**ω** to precess. For a top spinning at angular velocity ω_spin, with moment of inertia I and spin axis tilted at angle θ from vertical, under gravitational torque τ = mgd·sin(θ):

Precession angular velocity: Ω_p = τ / L = (mgd·sin θ) / (I·ω_spin)

As ω_spin increases, Ω_p decreases and the top "rises" — the precession axis approaches vertical (reduced nutation). In Drew's interpretation, the "rise-precession" of the spinning top corresponds to the upward Z-axis translation of the spinning magnet as it accelerates to high rotational speed in the 3-phase field.

The silicone bead (mentioned in 1929005668222042437.yaml) serves the mechanical role of constraining the spinning magnet in its "rising" motion — like the pointed tip of a spinning top that provides a pivot point for precession — capturing the "rising" motion rather than allowing uncontrolled jumping.

### 1.2 Gyroscope with Two Axes of Precession

Source: 1946698758093443281.yaml (note_tweet)

Drew's proposed experimental apparatus involves spinning gyroscope-like magnets with **two separate axes of precession and spin** — analogous to a toy gyroscope with both spin axis and precession axis free to move. This is a key mechanical distinction: a body with two free rotational axes can exhibit nutation in addition to precession.

For a symmetric top with moment of inertia I_3 (about symmetry axis) and I_1 = I_2 (about transverse axes), Euler's equations for the torque-free case:

I_1·(dω_1/dt) + (I_3 - I_1)·ω_2·ω_3 = 0
I_1·(dω_2/dt) + (I_1 - I_3)·ω_1·ω_3 = 0
I_3·(dω_3/dt) = 0

The body frame precesses about the space-fixed angular momentum vector **L** at the Euler frequency. When a torque is applied (e.g., from gravity or from the 3-phase magnetic field), the full Lagrangian treatment in Euler angles (θ, φ, ψ) is required.

Drew proposes that the fast spin in the 3-phase magnetic field provides the large ω_3, while the magnetic restoring force of the outer N-ring provides the equivalent of gravity, producing the two-axis gyroscopic behavior needed for "rise-precession."

He explicitly rejects simpler approaches: "Just spinning mercury by DC power and a radial electric field will not do it, because that produces only one axis of spin. The spinning mercury has to somehow rise like a top." This distinguishes single-axis Lorentz-force rotation from the two-axis gyroscopic behavior required for the lift mechanism.

## 2. Lenz Levitation and Non-Newtonian Spin Forces

### 2.1 Standard AC Lenz Levitation

Source: page_001.json, tweet 2044930235347701787 (note_tweet), tweet 2045812737897558122 (note_tweet)

Standard AC Lenz levitation: an alternating magnetic field from a coil induces eddy currents in a nearby conductor. The induced currents (by Lenz's law) oppose the change in flux — this produces a repulsive force that levitates the conductor above the coil. The force on the conductor is:

F_Lenz ∝ (dB/dt)² · σ · V / R²

where σ is electrical conductivity, V is conductor volume, and R is the distance. This is a well-established classical electromagnetic effect.
