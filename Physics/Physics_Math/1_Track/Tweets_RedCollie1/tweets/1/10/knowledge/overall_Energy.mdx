The energy stored in the Kaluza-Klein tower of massive modes (Kaluza-Klein excitations) represents a reservoir of energy above the 4D mass spectrum:
  M_KK^(n) = n / R_5  for n = 1, 2, 3, ...

If R_5 ~ 10^-34 m (as required by α ≈ 1/137):

The first Kaluza-Klein excitation has a mass of order ~300 MeV (comparable to π mesons). If UAP technology can access these massive KK modes as energy sources, the energy densities involved would be nuclear-scale — far beyond chemical reactions.

## Section: 1964861677742264334.yaml — Induced Electromagnet as Energy Storage

Drew's claim that the Faraday disc/homopolar motor creates an "induced electromagnet" — where the aligned spin magnetic moments of current-carrying electrons reinforce the applied field — represents a form of stored energy:

  Energy density of induced magnetic field: u_B = B²/(2μ_0)

If the induced field B_induced adds to the external field B_external:
  B_total = B_external + B_induced

  u_total = (B_external + B_induced)²/(2μ_0)
           = (B_external² + 2B_external·B_induced + B_induced²)/(2μ_0)

The cross-term 2B_external·B_induced/(2μ_0) = B_external·B_induced/μ_0 represents additional energy stored in the combined field beyond what either field stores individually. This is the energy of interaction between the external field and the induced magnetization.

In the context of the spin/counter-spin levitation device, the induced magnetization from the current-carrying coils (acting as the "induced electromagnet" analog) contributes to the total field interacting with the permanent magnets, enhancing the net force. This is analogous to the motor action in a classical electromagnet-driven system.
