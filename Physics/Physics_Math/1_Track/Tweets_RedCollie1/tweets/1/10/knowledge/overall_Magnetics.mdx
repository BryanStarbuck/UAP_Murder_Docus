### Physical Analysis

The Faraday disc (homopolar motor/generator) consists of a conducting disc rotating about its axis within a uniform axial magnetic field B directed along the rotation axis (Z-axis). Radial current flows from center to rim (or rim to center).

The Lorentz force on a charge carrier q moving with velocity v in field B is:
  F = q(v × B)

For a radial current element dr at radius r, with the disc rotating at angular velocity ω, the instantaneous velocity of a charge carrier has both a radial component (from the applied electric field) and an azimuthal component (from disc rotation v_φ = ωr). The cross product F = q(v × B) produces:

- Azimuthal force from radial current in axial field: F_φ = q(v_r × B_z) — this is the torque-producing term
- Axial force from azimuthal velocity in axial field: F_z = q(v_φ × B_z) = 0 (parallel vectors, cross product vanishes)

Drew's claim about electron spin alignment: when the radial electric field accelerates conduction electrons through the axial magnetic field, the electrons' intrinsic spin angular momentum precesses about B (Larmor precession). At sufficient current density, the net alignment of spin magnetic moments along B creates a macroscopic induced magnetization — analogous to the mechanism in a solenoid. Drew frames this as the conduction electrons collectively forming an "induced electromagnet" oriented parallel to the external N-S field.

The "turns sideways by a spiral motion" description refers to the cyclotron-like trajectory: an electron subject to crossed E and B fields executes a drift perpendicular to both (E × B drift). The spiral component arises from the superposition of the cyclotron orbit (circular motion in the plane perpendicular to B) with the drift velocity. This is mathematically:

  v_drift = (E × B) / |B|²

The induced field from aligned electron spin magnetic moments adds to the external field. This is the mechanism Drew identifies as relevant to the homopolar motor's anomalous torque properties and claims it connects to UFO propulsion physics.

### 3-Phase Field Dynamics in XY Plane

In a standard 3-phase motor with U-V-W coil arrangement, the three currents are:
  I_U(t) = I_0 cos(ωt)
  I_V(t) = I_0 cos(ωt - 2π/3)
  I_W(t) = I_0 cos(ωt - 4π/3)

These produce a rotating magnetic field vector in the XY plane:
  B_xy(t) = B_0 [cos(ωt) x̂ + sin(ωt) ŷ]

The field magnitude |B_xy| = B_0 is constant; the direction rotates at angular frequency ω.
