### Homopolar Motor / Faraday Disc Physics

The Faraday disc (homopolar generator/motor) operates via the Lorentz force law:
  F = q(E + v × B)

In the homopolar motor configuration:
- Applied magnetic field B = B_z ẑ (axial, along Z)
- Radial electric field E = E_r r̂ (drives radial current)
- Disc rotating at angular velocity ω = ω ẑ

Velocity of a charge carrier at radius r has components:
  v = v_r r̂ + v_φ φ̂  where v_φ = ωr

The magnetic force on the charge carrier:
  F_mag = q(v × B)
  = q[(v_r r̂ + ωr φ̂) × B_z ẑ]
  = q[v_r (r̂ × ẑ) B_z + ωr (φ̂ × ẑ) B_z]
  = q[v_r (-φ̂) B_z + ωr (-r̂) B_z]      [using right-hand rule: r̂ × ẑ = -φ̂, φ̂ × ẑ = -r̂]
  = q[-v_r B_z φ̂ - ωr B_z r̂]

The azimuthal component F_φ = -q v_r B_z produces the motor torque (tangential force on the disc).
The radial component F_r = -q ωr B_z modifies the effective driving EMF.

### Cyclotron / Spiral Motion

"When we apply a sideways force to a fast-spinning electron using a plus/minus electrical field, that electron will turn sideways by a spiral motion."

In crossed E and B fields, an electron executes drift motion with:
  v_drift = (E × B) / B²  [the E×B drift, independent of charge and mass]

Superimposed on this drift is circular cyclotron motion at the cyclotron frequency:
  ω_c = eB/m_e = (1.602 × 10^-19 C × B) / (9.109 × 10^-31 kg)
  ω_c = 1.759 × 10^11 × B  rad/s  (for B in Tesla)

The resulting helical/spiral trajectory is the "turns sideways by a spiral motion" Drew describes. For B = 0.1 T:
  ω_c = 1.759 × 10^10 rad/s ≈ 2.8 GHz

This frequency is in the microwave range; the cyclotron radius for thermal electrons (v_th ~ 10^5 m/s):

### Angular Momentum and Z-Axis Force

The central magnet spinning at ω_c (forward) has angular momentum:
  L_c = I_c ω_c ẑ

The outer ring spinning at ω_o (counter, opposite direction) has angular momentum:
  L_o = -I_o ω_o ẑ

Net angular momentum: L_net = (I_c ω_c - I_o ω_o) ẑ

In steady state, if I_c ω_c > I_o ω_o, L_net points upward (+ẑ). Drew's device aims to have the central magnet "rise up through its empty center" — i.e., the central magnet translates upward along its own spin axis.

The force along Z requires a gradient of the effective potential energy U(z):

For a magnetic dipole m in an inhomogeneous field B(z):
  F_z = m · ∂B_z/∂z

Drew's claim is that the counter-spinning outer ring creates an axial field gradient at the center that results in a net upward force on the spinning central magnet's dipole moment. This is consistent with the principle of diamagnetic levitation or magnetic gradient levitation, but here the gradient is dynamically generated by the counter-spinning ring rather than a static external magnet.
