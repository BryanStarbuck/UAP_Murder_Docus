Entirely off-topic from UAP physics. Drew discusses lithium orotate as a proposed Alzheimer's preventative:

"The orotate group can deliver lithium ions through hydrophobic cell membranes into the brain, where free lithium ions (say with carbonate) cannot penetrate. Hence taking lithium carbonate will increase lithium in blood plasma, but not in the brain where it is needed. This is called 'bio-delivery'."

**Technical biochemistry content:**
- Lithium orotate: Li⁺ complexed with orotic acid (uracil-6-carboxylic acid). Orotic acid is a lipophilic ligand at physiological pH, allowing the Li-orotate complex to traverse hydrophobic lipid bilayers of the blood-brain barrier (BBB)
- Lithium carbonate: Li⁺ released as a hydrophilic ion in plasma, does not cross BBB lipid bilayer efficiently; reaches steady-state plasma concentration but poor CNS penetration
- The orotate ligand acts as an ionophore: a molecular carrier that shuttles the ion across the hydrophobic barrier
- Clinical relevance: if lithium has neuroprotective effects in Alzheimer's (inhibition of GSK-3β, a kinase involved in tau phosphorylation), then CNS-penetrating Li delivery is required

**Drew's comment on clinical trial access:** "No one will EVER pay for a human clinical trial, for two reasons: firstly, the compound is very cheap and has no patent (sold on eBay as an [supplement])." This is a common critique of unpatentable compounds in clinical medicine — no financial incentive for pharma companies to fund trials.
