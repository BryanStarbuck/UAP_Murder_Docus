**Rotating Magnetomotive Force from 3-Phase Coils**

For a single 3-phase winding with N turns per coil, coils at angular positions φ_k = (k-1)·2π/3 for k = 1, 2, 3, driven by balanced 3-phase currents:

I_k(t) = I_0 cos(ωt - (k-1)·2π/3)

The instantaneous MMF contribution of coil k at azimuthal angle θ is:
f_k(θ, t) = N · I_k(t) · cos(θ - φ_k)

Total rotating MMF from all 3 coils:
F(θ, t) = Σ_{k=1}^{3} N I_0 cos(ωt - (k-1)·2π/3) · cos(θ - (k-1)·2π/3)
         = (3/2) N I_0 cos(θ - ωt)

This is a pure traveling wave with:
- Amplitude: (3/2) N I_0
- Angular velocity: ω (electrical radians/second)
- Spatial wavelength: 2π (one pole pair per revolution)

**Two 3-phase sets offset by 60°:**

Second set coils at φ_k = (k-1)·2π/3 + π/3, driven with phase offset δ:
F₂(θ, t) = (3/2) N I_0 cos(θ - ωt - α)

where α is chosen to make F₁ + F₂ constructively interfere. For α = 0 (same phase excitation, positions offset 60°):
F_total(θ, t) = (3/2) N I_0 cos(θ - ωt) + (3/2) N I_0 cos(θ - ωt) = 3 N I_0 cos(θ - ωt)

The amplitude doubles to 3NI_0, and the spatial harmonic content is improved — specifically, the 5th and 7th harmonics (which cause torque ripple) are reduced.

**Physical meaning:** The doubled amplitude means twice the force on permanent magnets at a given current level; the harmonic suppression means smoother rotation with less torque ripple. Both effects enhance the efficiency of driving the inner magnet assembly to spin.

**Force on Magnetic Dipole in a Non-Uniform Field**

The gradient force on a magnetic dipole **m** in an external field **B**:
**F** = ∇(**m** · **B**)

In component form:
F_x = m_x ∂B_x/∂x + m_y ∂B_y/∂x + m_z ∂B_z/∂x
F_z = m_x ∂B_x/∂z + m_y ∂B_y/∂z + m_z ∂B_z/∂z

For the central magnet with **m** = m_z ẑ:
F_z = m_z ∂B_z/∂z

**Condition for non-zero axial force:** ∂B_z/∂z ≠ 0 at the magnet center. In the plane of the coils (z = 0), by symmetry B_z = 0 for a rotating purely in-plane field. But near coil ends (fringe region), field lines from the coil ends curve axially, creating ∂B_z/∂z ≠ 0.

**Optimization statement:** "Place a central magnet close to the center of six 3-phase wire coils to get the most force." The "center" here likely means the z-position of maximum ∂B_z/∂z (the inflection point of the fringe field), not the geometric center where B_z → 0. The optimization is over z-position to maximize the gradient force.

**Time-averaged force in a rotating field:** For a rotating in-plane field B(t) = B_0[cos(ωt) x̂ + sin(ωt) ŷ], the time-averaged ∂B_z/∂z depends on the 3D coil geometry. The rotating field creates a rotating fringe field; the time-averaged gradient may be non-zero if the axial fringe field has a net average component along z. This is the mechanism by which a rotating field could produce a steady upward force — analogous to how a rotating electric field (circularly polarized light) can exert steady radiation pressure.

**Lorentz Force on Current Elements in the Coil-Magnet Geometry**

Force per unit length on a current-carrying wire in a magnetic field:
d**F**/dl = I (**ê**_l × **B**)

where **ê**_l is the unit tangent to the wire and **B** is the local field at the wire position.

For a coil carrying phase current I_k(t) = I_0 cos(ωt - φ_k) in the field of the inner magnets **B**_magnets:

The net torque on the coil (which is fixed in the stator) equals and opposite to the torque on the magnets (Newton's 3rd law):
τ_{on magnets} = -τ_{on coils} = -∮ **r** × (I_k d**l** × **B**_magnets) 

This torque drives the magnets to rotate. The right-hand rule for the cross product d**l** × **B** gives the direction:
- If current in coil segment flows in +x̂ direction at position **r** in the +ŷ field: force is in +ẑ
- If field is in +ẑ and current in +x̂: force is in -ŷ

The right-hand rule is thus the operational guide for determining which direction the magnets rotate given the instantaneous phase current directions and magnet field orientations.
