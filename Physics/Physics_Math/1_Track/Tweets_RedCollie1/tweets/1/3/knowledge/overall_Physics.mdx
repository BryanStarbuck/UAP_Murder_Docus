**3-Phase Coil Geometry and Rotating Magnetic Field Physics**

Two sets of 3-phase coils offset by 60° create a rotating magnetic field. The physics:

A single 3-phase set (coils at 0°, 120°, 240°) driven by:
- I_A(t) = I_0 cos(ωt)
- I_B(t) = I_0 cos(ωt - 2π/3)
- I_C(t) = I_0 cos(ωt - 4π/3)

produces a magnetomotive force (MMF) that rotates at angular velocity ω:

F(θ, t) = (3/2) N I_0 cos(θ - ωt)

where N is the number of turns per coil and θ is the azimuthal position. This is the rotating field standard result from symmetric polyphase winding theory.

Adding a second set offset by 60° and driven with appropriate phase shift doubles the amplitude uniformity and fills in spatial harmonics. The combined MMF from a 6-coil (two 3-phase, offset 60°) arrangement suppresses the 5th and 7th spatial harmonics that are present in a single 3-phase winding — a known engineering advantage in motor design.

The mechanical output — making "any magnetic flywheel spin" — follows from the interaction torque:
τ = -∂W/∂θ_mech

where W is the magnetic co-energy as a function of rotor mechanical angle θ_mech.

**Ambiguity between propeller and anti-gravity modes:** Drew notes the same geometry could produce either aerodynamic propulsion or anti-gravity lift. This is a key physical distinction: aerodynamic propulsion requires fluid coupling (pressure differential), while anti-gravity lift would require a direct electromagnetic-gravitational coupling with no fluid intermediary.

**Kruskal Coordinates — General Relativity and Wormhole Geometry**

Kruskal–Szekeres coordinates extend the Schwarzschild solution to encompass the complete causal structure of a black hole spacetime. The key physics:

The Schwarzschild metric in standard coordinates:
ds² = -(1 - 2GM/c²r)c²dt² + (1 - 2GM/c²r)⁻¹dr² + r²dΩ²

has a coordinate singularity at the Schwarzschild radius r_s = 2GM/c². Kruskal–Szekeres coordinates (T, X) remove this coordinate singularity:

T² - X² = (1 - r/r_s) e^(r/r_s)

In the (X, T) plane, the structure reveals:
- Region I: our exterior universe (X > |T|)
- Region II: black hole interior (T > |X|)
- Region III: white hole interior (T < -|X|)
- Region IV: the other exterior universe (X < -|T|)

The Einstein-Rosen bridge connecting regions I and IV is the wormhole. However, this wormhole is non-traversable in the classical Schwarzschild solution — it closes before any signal can pass through. Traversable wormhole solutions (Morris-Thorne type) require exotic matter with negative energy density to hold the throat open:

T^μν_exotic: ρ < 0 (violation of the null energy condition)

Drew's claim that ET craft enter a spacetime wormhole using magnetic propulsion implies the magnetic field configuration generates the required negative energy density condition locally — an extraordinary claim with no current experimental or theoretical support in mainstream physics.

**Anomalous Axial Force — "Jumping Magnet" Experiment**

The claim: a central permanent magnet placed at the center of six 3-phase wire coils undergoes vertical (Z-axis) displacement when the coils are energized.

Classical electromagnetic force analysis on a magnetic dipole in an external field:
F = ∇(m · B)

For a magnetic dipole **m** = m_z ẑ at position (0, 0, z₀) in a rotating in-plane field B(θ, t) = B_0[cos(ωt) x̂ + sin(ωt) ŷ]:

m · B = m_z ẑ · (B_0 cos(ωt) x̂ + B_0 sin(ωt) ŷ) = 0

Therefore F_z = ∂(m·B)/∂z = 0 for a purely in-plane rotating field. A net axial force on a Z-oriented dipole requires a Z-component of the field, i.e., ∂B_z/∂z ≠ 0 at the magnet location.

If the coil geometry creates field lines that curve toward the axis (fringe field effects), there will be a ∂B_z/∂z that produces a net axial force. Whether this is sufficient to produce a visible "jump" depends on the specific coil geometry, field strength, and magnet mass.

Drew's experimental claim that the magnet "jumps up like a rabbit" is either:
1. A classical fringe-field effect (gradient force on Z-dipole in fringing field)
2. An anomalous effect beyond classical EM (requires new physics)

This ambiguity is the central unresolved experimental question.

**3I/ATLAS Jet Rotation — Physics of Rotation Rate**

> "Its brightest 'jet' rotates by about 30 degrees in 60 seconds. Meaning one full rotation every 12 minutes!"
> "There is NO POSSIBLE MECHANISM for a natural comet to show such fast, spinning, jet-like behavior, especially as it goes further from the Sun and stops 'outgassing'."

The physics argument:

For a natural comet, jets form by sublimation of volatiles from the nucleus surface heated by solar radiation. As a comet recedes from the Sun, the sublimation rate Q ∝ r_h^(-n) (where r_h is heliocentric distance and n ≈ 2 for water ice, larger for more volatile species) decreases rapidly. Jet activity should therefore decrease and eventually cease as the comet moves to large heliocentric distances.

The observed behavior of 3I/ATLAS: brightest jet rotates 30° per 60 s = 0.5°/s = 3°/min, completing one full rotation in ~12 minutes. This corresponds to a nucleus rotation period of 12 minutes, IF the jet is fixed to the nucleus surface. For comparison, typical comet nucleus rotation periods are hours to days (e.g., comet 67P/Churyumov-Gerasimenko has a rotation period of ~12.4 hours).

Drew's claim: 12-minute rotation is orders of magnitude faster than any known cometary nucleus, AND the jet persists beyond the heliocentric distance at which outgassing should cease. This combination is presented as evidence for artificial origin.

His proposed explanation:
> "My view is that 3I/ATLAS is a large artificial object which spins rapidly, to produce either (a) gyroscopic stability or (b) artificial gravity?"

Gyroscopic stability: an axially symmetric rigid body with moment of inertia I spinning at angular velocity Ω has angular momentum L = IΩ; precession rate for an external torque τ is Ω_prec = τ/L. Rapid spin (short period = 12 min) would maximize L for a given I, providing high gyroscopic stability against perturbations from solar radiation pressure, Yarkovsky forces, etc.

Artificial gravity: for a rotating torus or cylinder of radius R, the centripetal acceleration providing "gravity" at the rim is a = Rω² = R(2π/T)². For T = 12 min = 720 s and R = 10 km: a = 10,000 × (2π/720)² ≈ 10,000 × 7.6×10⁻⁵ ≈ 0.76 m/s² ≈ 0.077 g. Habitable, but modest.

**Rotary Magnetic System — Physics of Spin and Counter-Spin**

Full system physics from lab demonstration:

**Configuration:**
- 6 outer wire coils, 3-phase power, sequence U-V-W-U-V-W (clockwise in space)
- Outer magnet ring: 8 N-S permanent magnets, alternating polarity
- Inner ring/disc magnet
- 3 wire coils below, wired U-V-W (second 3-phase field)
- Stainless steel ball bearings (low friction)

**Observed behavior (video transcript):**
"When we turn on three-phase power to these six wire coils, UVW, UVW, the eight magnets will begin to spin anti-clockwise opposite the field."

Physics of anti-clockwise counter-rotation of 8-magnet ring against clockwise rotating field:

A ring of 8 alternating N-S magnets has a 4-pole-pair spatial period (p = 4). The rotating MMF from the outer 6-coil system has a fundamental spatial period of 1 pole pair (p = 1) per electrical cycle. The synchronous speed for p pole pairs in a field rotating at electrical frequency ω is:
n_s = ω/p (mechanical radians per second)

For the fundamental (p=1): n_s = ω (fast, in the field direction)
For the 8-magnet ring coupling to the p=4 harmonic: the synchronous speed is n_s = ω/4, but the relative motion between the ring and the fundamental field harmonic determines the torque direction.

A backward-rotating harmonic of the MMF (produced by winding asymmetries or spatial harmonic content) could interact with the p=4 magnet ring to drive counter-rotation. Alternatively, if the ring spins faster than the fundamental field (super-synchronous), it appears to counter-rotate relative to the field in the rotor reference frame.

The absence of this explanation in standard motor textbooks — combined with the crop formation analog — is what Drew presents as beyond-classical behavior.

**Lorentz Force in the Device Context**

The Lorentz force law:
**F** = q(**E** + **v** × **B**)

For current-carrying conductors (the coil wire carrying phase currents):
d**F** = I (d**l** × **B**)

The right-hand rule (or equivalently, the cross product direction) determines the force direction on each coil segment. In the rotary induction motor context, this force is what drives the rotating magnetic field and, through field coupling, drives the magnet rotation.

Drew's assertion that 6 or 15 outer coils act as a "rotary induction motor" using this Lorentz force is the classical mechanism. The non-classical claim is the additional axial force (anti-gravity) not predicted by integrating the Lorentz force over the standard coil geometry.
