### Homopolar Motor — Apparent Absence of Back-EMF and Back-Reaction

**The central anomalous energy claim:**
In a standard electromagnetic motor, the spinning rotor generates a back-EMF (counter-electromotive force) that opposes the supply voltage and limits current and torque. This is the energy-balance mechanism: input electrical energy = mechanical output energy + resistive losses. The back-EMF is expressed as:

    V_supply = I·R + EMF_back = I·R + k·ω

where k is the motor constant, ω is the rotor speed, R is the resistance. At maximum RPM (no load), V = EMF_back and I → 0; no torque, all power returned to source as back-EMF.

**Drew's claim for the homopolar motor and liquid-metal version:**
"No back-reaction" for the Lorentz force driving the homopolar disc. Drew does not claim the homopolar motor violates conservation of energy; rather, he claims that the conventional notion of "action-reaction" as a mechanical couple (third-law pair of forces on interacting bodies) does not apply straightforwardly to the homopolar geometry. The reaction is distributed into the magnetic field rather than appearing as a mechanical back-torque on the stator.

**Potential anomalous energy implication:**
If the spinning magnetic disc does not generate a back-EMF proportional to its spin speed (unlike conventional motors), then the input power does not decrease as the disc speeds up. In principle, one could continuously pour energy into accelerating the disc without the usual back-EMF limit. This would not violate conservation of energy per se — the energy goes into kinetic energy of the spinning disc — but it removes the usual self-limiting mechanism of motor behavior.

For a homopolar motor specifically: the back-EMF is:

    EMF_back = ½ ω B_z R²

This is smaller than for conventional AC motors because R (disc radius) is small and B_z is limited by permanent magnet field strength. Thus the homopolar motor can accept higher currents at lower speeds, producing higher torque at the cost of higher resistive dissipation — it is not that back-EMF is absent, but that it is small relative to resistive losses.

**Drew's interpretation:** The small back-EMF of the homopolar motor means that most of the electrical input goes into accelerating the disc (building kinetic energy and angular momentum) rather than into back-EMF energy feedback. Once the disc is spinning fast and the 3-phase field is applied, the hypothesis is that additional energy is extracted from the rotating field to maintain the Z-axis lift — the 3-phase AC source provides the energy for levitation.

**No claim of over-unity or free energy in these tweets.** Drew's energy source for propulsion is the electrical input (DC battery for Lorentz spin + AC power for 3-phase field). The anomalous aspect is the geometric efficiency of converting XY-plane electrical energy input into Z-axis mechanical lift output, mediated by the helical field topology.
