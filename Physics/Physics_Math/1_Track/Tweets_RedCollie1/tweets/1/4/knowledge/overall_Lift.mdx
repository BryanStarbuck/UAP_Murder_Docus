The lift arises from the asymmetric circulation around the cylinder. In the magnetic analog, a spinning permanent magnet in a rotating magnetic field may experience an analogous force — a "magnetic Magnus force" — perpendicular to both the spin axis (ẑ) and the direction of the field's angular velocity vector (also ẑ for a coaxial rotating field). Since both vectors are along ẑ, the cross product is zero for pure axial rotation.

If the precession direction has a Z-component (i.e., if the top begins to rise), this could be the observed levitation mechanism.

**Quantitative relationship:** For a gyroscope with L = Iω, subjected to torque τ from gravity (or from an applied force), the precession angular velocity is:

For larger ω (faster spin), Ω_prec is smaller — the gyroscope precesses more slowly and more stably. Applied to the magnetic system: a faster-spinning disc magnet has larger L, and thus responds more stably and with a longer precession timescale to any perturbation — this stability is needed for controlled levitation.
