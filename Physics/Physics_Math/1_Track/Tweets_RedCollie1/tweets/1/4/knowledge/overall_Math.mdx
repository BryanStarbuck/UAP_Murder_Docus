### Lorentz Force Equation — Fundamental to All Device Mathematics

The Lorentz force on a charge carrier:

    F = q(E + v × B)

For the homopolar/Faraday disc in the magnetostatic limit (E ≈ 0 from static fields, and the v × B term dominates):

    F = q(v × B)

**Term definitions:**
- q: charge of carrier [Coulombs]; for electrons q = −1.602 × 10⁻¹⁹ C
- v: velocity of the charge carrier [m/s]; in the spinning disc, this is the tangential velocity v = ωr φ̂
- B: external magnetic field [Tesla]; in the homopolar configuration, B = B_z ẑ (axial)
- F: force on the carrier [Newtons]; this force drives radial current in the generator, or arises from the radial current in the motor

**For a disc element at radius r spinning at angular velocity ω:**

    v = ωr φ̂

    F/q = v × B = (ωr φ̂) × (B_z ẑ) = ωr B_z (φ̂ × ẑ)

Using cylindrical coordinate cross products: φ̂ × ẑ = −r̂ (pointing radially inward)

    F/q = −ωr B_z r̂

This drives electrons (negative charge) radially outward (force on electron = −q·(−ωrB_z r̂) = +qωrB_z r̂), i.e., outward. Conventional current flows inward (radially toward center). This is the homopolar generator.

In motor operation (externally driven current I flowing radially):

**Torque element on current-carrying strip at radius r, width dr, carrying current dI:**

    dτ = r × (dI L̂ × B) = r × (dI dr r̂ × B_z ẑ)

    r̂ × ẑ = −φ̂

    dτ = r × (−dI dr B_z φ̂) = −r B_z dI dr (r̂ × φ̂) = −r B_z dI dr ẑ

Wait — let us be careful. The torque about the z-axis from a force F_φ at radius r:

    τ_z = r · F_φ = r · (dI dr · B_z)   [where F_φ = dI·dr·B_z from the cross product]

Integrating from r = 0 to r = R with uniform current density J_r = I/(πR²):

    dI = J_r · 2πr dr = (2I/R²) r dr

    τ = ∫₀ᴿ r · B_z · dI = ∫₀ᴿ r · B_z · (2I/R²) r dr = (2IB_z/R²) ∫₀ᴿ r² dr = (2IB_z/R²)(R³/3) = (2/3) I B_z R

Note: this result (τ = (2/3)IB_zR) applies for uniform current density. For current flowing only at the rim and center (brush contacts at r = R and r = 0), the effective torque is:

    τ = ∫ r × (I dl × B) along the current path

For a simplified model with all current at radius R:
    τ = R · F_φ = R · (I · R · B_z) = I B_z R²

More precisely, the standard result for a homopolar motor with brushes at r = 0 and r = R is:

    τ = ½ I B_z R²

**The EMF of the homopolar generator (integrating motional EMF from center to rim):**

    EMF = ∫₀ᴿ (v × B) · dr̂ dr = ∫₀ᴿ ωrB_z dr = ½ ω B_z R²

**Relationships between quantities:**
- EMF ∝ ω (proportional to spin rate) → faster spin = higher generator voltage
- τ ∝ I (proportional to current) → more current = more torque in motor mode
- τ ∝ B_z (proportional to field strength) → stronger magnet = more torque
- τ ∝ R² (proportional to disc area) → larger disc = more torque

### Gyroscopic Precession — Mathematics of Spinning Top Deflection

**Angular momentum of spinning top:**

    L = I ω ẑ

where I is the moment of inertia about the spin axis, ω is the spin angular velocity, and ẑ is the spin axis direction (vertical, for an upright top).

**Applied lateral torque from air jet:**

    τ = F × d

where F is the horizontal force from the jet and d is the moment arm (distance from pivot to point of force application).

**Precession equation:**

    dL/dt = τ

    d(Iω ẑ)/dt = τ

If |L| = Iω is constant (constant spin speed), the spin axis ẑ precesses without changing |L|:

    Ω_prec × L = τ

    |Ω_prec| = |τ| / |L| = τ / (Iω)

**Direction of precession:** The precession angular velocity Ω_prec is perpendicular to both L and τ. For a horizontal τ (east-west), the precession is in the north-south direction. The upward component arises because the top's pivot is not free; the constraint force from the pivot has an upward component that redirects horizontal torque input into angular momentum change of the spin axis.

**Why this appears to violate Newton's Third Law (Drew's pedagogical point):**
- A non-spinning top subjected to lateral force F goes left: Δp = F Δt → p in the -x direction.
- A spinning top subjected to the same lateral force does not go left; instead it precesses upward.
- No equal-and-opposite leftward force appears in the air jet.
- The "reaction" is stored as a change in the direction of L — the angular momentum of the spin absorbs the torque and converts it to precession.

**Mathematical statement:** For a gyroscope, the linear response (F → ma) is suppressed relative to the rotational response (τ → dL/dt). When I·ω >> m·v_characteristic, the system responds to applied forces by precessing rather than translating. This is mathematically expressed by the dominance of the rotational kinetic energy term T_rot = ½Iω² over the translational term T_trans = ½mv².

### Garden-Sprinkler Jet Propulsion — Angular Momentum Conservation

For a sprinkler-type propulsion system with three jets at 120° separation, each jet at angle α from radial:

**Thrust from each jet:**
    F_jet = ṁ v_exhaust   (Newton's Third Law, rocket equation)

**Torque from each jet (about rotation axis):**
    τ_jet = F_jet · R_nozzle · sin(α) = ṁ v_exhaust R sin(α)

**Total torque (three jets, 120° apart, all contributing same sign torque):**
    τ_total = 3 · ṁ v_exhaust R sin(α)

**Angular acceleration of the source body:**
    τ_total = I α̈   →   α̈ = 3 ṁ v_exhaust R sin(α) / I

The net linear thrust (averaged over rotation) vanishes if the three jets are symmetric (equal and opposite contributions in x-y plane). However, if the jets have a component along the Z-axis (oblique ejection), net Z-axis thrust can be produced. The "anti-tail and tail" observation (material ejected both toward the Sun and away from the Sun) is consistent with Z-axis thrust components pointing in opposite directions from two oppositely-directed jets, with the third jet providing the rotation drive.
