A conducting disc of radius R spins at angular velocity ω about the ẑ-axis in a static axial field B = B_z ẑ.

Velocity of a volume element at radius r: v = ωr φ̂

Lorentz force per unit charge (the motional EMF force):
f = v × B = ωr φ̂ × B_z ẑ = ωrB_z (φ̂ × ẑ) = -ωrB_z r̂

This radially inward force drives electrons outward (since electron charge is negative, the force on electrons is radially outward), creating a potential difference between rim and center. The EMF of a homopolar generator is:

EMF = ∫₀ᴿ ωrB_z dr = ½ωB_zR²

In motor operation (current supplied externally), the torque on the disc is:

τ = ∫ r × (J × B) dV = I · B_z · ∫₀ᴿ r dr = ½ I B_z R²

where I is the total radial current. This torque spins the disc.

**Significance for UFO device:** The homopolar motor provides efficient, continuous, frictionless (if liquid-metal brushes are used) torque to the spinning magnetic disc — the first stage of the proposed two-stage levitation device. The torque is proportional to I (supply current), B_z (static axial field strength), and R² (disc area). Larger disc, stronger magnet, higher current → faster spin → stronger rotating field topology → more lift (in Drew's model).

### Three Physical Demonstrations of Non-Newtonian-Appearing Force Redirections

**Demonstration 1: Spinning Top + Lateral Air Jet → Vertical Displacement**

A spinning top with angular momentum L = Iω ẑ (spin about ẑ-axis) subjected to a horizontal torque τ = F × r (where F is the lateral air jet force) does not accelerate in the direction of F as Newton's Second Law for linear motion would predict for a non-spinning object. Instead, it precesses:

dL/dt = τ

The precessional angular velocity is:
Ω_prec = τ / L = τ / (Iω)

The direction of precession is perpendicular to both L and τ. For a top spinning clockwise viewed from above (L pointing down), a lateral horizontal torque causes upward precession (the tip rises). This is standard gyroscopic precession — but it appears as an upward force arising from a horizontal input, violating naive Action-Reaction intuition.

**The key phenomenological point Drew makes:** No equal-and-opposite downward force appears in the air jet (the jet does not deflect downward as a reaction). The "reaction" is in the gyroscopic restoring torque, which is internal to the spinning body. This is the first example of what Drew terms physics that "does not violate laws of physics but uses physics our Great Professors do not teach."

**Demonstration 2: Permanent-Magnet Brushed Homopolar Motor**

The Lorentz reaction torque in an ideal homopolar motor with a symmetric magnetic circuit is zero for the external mechanical system (reaction appears in the electromagnetic field energy, not as a mechanical force on the stator).

**Demonstration 3: Liquid-Metal Homopolar Motor**

The liquid metal (typically mercury Hg or gallium-indium eutectic GaIn) has very low electrical resistivity (ρ_Hg ≈ 9.6 × 10⁻⁷ Ω·m at 25°C) and flows to conform to the contact surface, maintaining good ohmic contact throughout rotation.

**Synthesis:** All three demonstrations involve angular momentum storage/transfer through rotating systems in which the "reaction" is stored in a field (gravitational, for the top; electromagnetic, for the motors) rather than appearing as a directly observable mechanical back-force on the source. ### 3-Phase Coil Ring — Magnetic Field Configuration for Counter-Spin

**Winding specification:**
- 12 coils arranged in a ring.
- Phase sequence around ring: U-V-W-U-V-W-U-V-W-U-V-W (3-phase, 4 poles per phase).
- Connection: U coils in series, V coils in series, W coils in series.

**Rotating magnetic field physics:**
In a 3-phase winding with n poles per phase and supply frequency f, the rotating magnetic field in the air gap has synchronous angular velocity:

ω_s = 4πf / p   (rad/s)

where p is the total number of poles (for 4 poles per phase with 3 phases: p = 4 × 2 = 8 for a 4-pole winding, giving ω_s = 4πf/8 = πf/2 rad/s). At 50 Hz supply: ω_s = 25π ≈ 78.5 rad/s ≈ 750 RPM. At 60 Hz: ω_s ≈ 900 RPM.

For counter-spin operation, the winding must produce a field that rotates in the direction opposite to the freely spinning inner magnet. By reversing any two phases (e.g., swapping U and V connections), the direction of the rotating field reverses, producing the counter-spin condition.

**Physical advantage of counter-spin:** When the inner magnet spins in the direction opposite to the rotating outer field, the total rotational frequency of the helical field pattern (as seen in the reference frame of the medium below the device) is the sum of the two angular velocities: ω_total = ω_inner + ω_outer. This increases the frequency of the torsional forcing on the medium below, potentially increasing the lift/torsional force.
