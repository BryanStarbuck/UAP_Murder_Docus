**Downward Torsional Levitation — Beyond Standard Electromagnetic Lift**

Drew's claim: "Alien UFOs levitate by a strong, downward-torsional force."

Why this is beyond Newtonian mechanics:

In Newtonian mechanics, lift requires either:
a) Pressure gradient force (aerodynamic lift): F_z = ∫(P_bottom - P_top)dA
b) Reactive thrust: F_z = -dp_exhaust/dt (rockets, jets)
c) Magnetostatic force: F_z = ∇(m · B)_z (requires external field gradient)

None of these requires or predicts a "torsional" lift mechanism. Torsional forces are azimuthal (θ-direction) in cylindrical coordinates, not axial (z-direction). In classical mechanics, a torsional force on a system creates rotation, not translation.

The "downward torsional force producing upward lift" requires a mechanism that converts azimuthal force (torsion) into axial force (lift). This conversion is not present in:
- Classical Newtonian mechanics
- Standard Maxwell electrodynamics with macroscopic permanent magnets
- Linear elastic mechanics

It would require either:
a) Coupling between spin angular momentum and linear momentum (spin-orbit coupling extended to macroscopic scale)
b) Torsion tensor effects from general relativistic or Cartan geometry (torsion is a concept in differential geometry with no Newtonian analog)
c) Non-linear electromagnetic effects at high field/spin combinations
d) Vacuum/zero-point field interaction mediated by the rotating electromagnetic structure

Drew's claim is that the crop circle evidence and UAP encounter evidence both consistently point to this torsional mechanism, which therefore must represent physics beyond what is currently taught publicly.

**Galinstan Non-Response to 3-Phase Alone**

Drew's observation: "it [galinstan] does not spin using 3-phase power alone. It will spin by adding below a rotor with permanent NSNSNS magnets."

This is a non-trivial beyond-classical observation: a liquid conductor (galinstan) in a 3-phase rotating field does not rotate spontaneously. In standard induction motor theory, a conducting body in a rotating magnetic field should develop induced eddy currents that experience Lorentz force and produce rotation (this is the principle of the induction motor).

The absence of rotation for a liquid conductor suggests that either:
a) The surface tension and viscosity of the liquid metal prevent the shear forces needed to accelerate it in bulk, even though eddy currents are induced — a classical fluid dynamics limitation, not beyond-Newtonian
b) The geometry of the uncontained liquid (spreading rather than a fixed disc shape) means the eddy current pattern is non-optimal
c) The induced currents are too small in the absence of a permanent magnet reference field to produce measurable torque

The practical conclusion: the full system (permanent magnet + liquid metal brushes + 3-phase field) is necessary; neither the liquid metal nor the 3-phase field alone is sufficient. This is consistent with the system being a *coupled* beyond-classical effect requiring all components simultaneously.
