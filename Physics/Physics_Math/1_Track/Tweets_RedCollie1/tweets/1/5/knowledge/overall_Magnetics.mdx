**3-Phase Rotating Magnetic Fields — Core Mechanism**

Drew identifies "3-phase, rotating magnetic fields" as the operative electromagnetic mechanism in the spinning magnetic levitation devices he has been studying in drone videos. A 3-phase system consists of three sinusoidal AC currents of equal amplitude and frequency, mutually phase-shifted by 120°:

    I_U(t) = I₀ cos(ωt)
    I_V(t) = I₀ cos(ωt - 2π/3)
    I_W(t) = I₀ cos(ωt - 4π/3)

When these three currents are driven through coils arranged at 120° spatial intervals around a common axis, the superposition of the three individual fields produces a net magnetic field vector of constant magnitude that rotates uniformly in the XY plane at angular frequency ω. This is the standard rotating magnetic field of AC induction motors.

Drew's claim is that this well-known field, when applied in specific geometries (including the homopolar disc configuration), produces anomalous effects not predicted by standard induction motor theory.

    B(r,θ,t) = Σₙ [aₙ Rₙ(r)] cos(nθ - ωt)

where the n=6 harmonic is dominant in the hexagonal case, rather than the n=1 dipolar term dominant in a standard 2-pole motor field.

**Faraday Disk Induced Magnetic Field**

The Faraday disk (homopolar generator geometry) when spinning rapidly develops an induced magnetic field. For a conducting disc of radius R spinning with angular velocity Ω in an external axial magnetic field B_z, the motional EMF is:

    ε = ∫₀ᴿ (v × B) · dr = ∫₀ᴿ (Ωr)(B_z) dr = ½ Ω B_z R²

This drives a radial current through the disc (when a circuit is closed through brushes at the rim and axis). This radial current J_r in the presence of the axial field B_z produces an azimuthal Lorentz force:

    F_φ = J_r × B_z (per unit volume)

Additionally, the radial current in the spinning disc creates its own toroidal (azimuthal, closed-loop) magnetic field component B_φ via Ampere's law:

    ∮ B · dl = μ₀ I_enclosed

Drew is claiming that the interaction between the externally applied 3-phase rotating field and this self-induced field of the spinning disc creates a net vertical (Z-axis) force component. The mechanism for this Z-force is the non-trivial coupling of the XY-plane rotating field and the toroidal self-field of the disc current.

**12-Coil Ring with Series-Wired Phases — Magnetic Field Topology**

Configuration: 12 coils arranged in a ring, assigned phases U-V-W-U-V-W-U-V-W-U-V-W (12 coils / 3 phases = 4 coils per phase). The non-standard wiring: all U-phase coils wired in series (not parallel), similarly for V and W.

In a standard 3-phase motor, coils of the same phase are typically wired in parallel to maintain equal voltage distribution. Wiring them in series means the same phase current I_U flows through all 4 U-phase coils sequentially, but the coils are spatially distributed at 120° intervals (since 4 coils of one phase in a 12-coil ring are at positions 0°, 90°, 180°, 270°). This creates a 4-pole field pattern from each phase rather than the 2-pole pattern of a standard winding.

The superposition of 4-pole patterns from 3 phases at 120° phase offset produces a net field with 4-fold spatial symmetry rotating at ω — a "counter-spin" field in Drew's terminology, meaning the field rotation direction creates a repelling rather than attracting torque on a co-spinning permanent magnet element.

The "counter-spin" designation: if the permanent magnet disc is spinning at Ω_mech in the +θ direction, and the magnetic field is rotating at ω_elec also in the +θ direction but faster (ω_elec > Ω_mech), the induced torque on the disc is in the -θ direction (braking). But Drew's counter-spin implies the field may rotate in the -θ direction relative to the disc, creating a specific force distribution with a net Z component.
