**Homopolar Motor — Core Equations**

The homopolar (Faraday disc) motor is the central device in Drew's experimental program.

**Motional EMF of spinning disc** (generator mode):

    ε = ∫₀ᴿ (v × B) · dr̂ dr = ∫₀ᴿ (Ωr)(B_z) dr = ½ Ω B_z R²

where:
- R = disc radius [m]
- Ω = angular velocity of disc [rad/s]
- B_z = axial magnetic field (external, from permanent magnet) [T]

**Radial current density** in disc (motor mode, current I injected via brushes):

    J_r(r) = I / (2π r h σ) × σ = I / (2π r h)

where h = disc thickness. (For a thin disc approximation, J_r = I/(2πr h) per Ohm's law with uniform conductivity.)

**Azimuthal Lorentz force per unit volume** on disc:

    f_φ = J_r × B_z = [I/(2πr h)] × B_z

**Torque on disc** due to Lorentz force:

    T = ∫₀ᴿ ∫₀ʰ ∫₀²π r · f_φ · r dφ dz dr
    T = ∫₀ᴿ (2πr h) · r · [I/(2πrh)] · B_z · r dr
    T = I B_z ∫₀ᴿ r dr = ½ I B_z R²

This result T = ½ I B_z R² is the standard homopolar motor torque formula. Note that the torque is:
- Linear in current I
- Linear in field B_z
- Quadratic in radius R

This makes large-R, high-B_z devices proportionally stronger.

**Power** input (electrical) = Power output (mechanical) + Ohmic losses:

    P_elec = ε × I = (½ Ω B_z R²) × I = T × Ω = P_mech

(Ideal efficiency, ignoring brush contact resistance and disc resistivity.)

**3-Phase Rotating Magnetic Field — Mathematical Structure**

Three-phase currents at frequency ω (electrical angular frequency):

    I_U(t) = I₀ cos(ωt)
    I_V(t) = I₀ cos(ωt - 2π/3)
    I_W(t) = I₀ cos(ωt - 4π/3)

Field contribution from each phase (for coils at angular positions 0, 2π/3, 4π/3 in the XY plane):

    B_U(θ, t) = (B₀/2)[cos(θ - ωt) + cos(θ + ωt)]
    B_V(θ, t) = (B₀/2)[cos(θ - 2π/3 - ωt) + cos(θ - 2π/3 + ωt)]
    B_W(θ, t) = (B₀/2)[cos(θ - 4π/3 - ωt) + cos(θ - 4π/3 + ωt)]

Superposition (using cos(A) + cos(B+2π/3) + cos(B+4π/3) = 0 for the backward-rotating component):

    B_total(θ, t) = (3B₀/2) cos(θ - ωt)

The backward-rotating terms cancel identically; only the forward-rotating component survives. The result is a field of constant amplitude 3B₀/2 rotating uniformly at ω. This is the mathematical basis for AC induction and synchronous motor operation.

**For Drew's 12-coil, series-wired configuration** (4 coils per phase, coils at 0°, 90°, 180°, 270° for each phase):

The U-phase coils at positions 0°, 90°, 180°, 270° produce spatial harmonics at n = 4, 8, 12, ... rather than n = 2 (the 2-pole fundamental). The dominant term is a 4-pole field (n=4):

    B_U^(4)(θ, t) ∝ B₀ cos(4θ - ωt)

For three phases with 120° electrical phase offset and 4-pole spatial distribution:

    B_total^(4)(θ, t) ∝ (3B₀/2) cos(4θ - ωt)

This is a 4-pole rotating field at electrical frequency ω, rotating in the XY plane. A permanent magnet rotor with p_r pole pairs interacting with this field sees a relative electrical frequency of:

    ω_rel = ω - p_r × Ω_mech

Synchronous condition: ω_rel = 0, giving Ω_mech = ω / p_r.

For Drew's counter-spin: p_r × Ω_mech = -ω (rotor spinning opposite to field), giving ω_rel = 2ω — a doubled interaction frequency.

**Counter-Spin Wiring — Mathematical Consequence**

Standard parallel wiring of p coils per phase: voltage V_phase appears across each coil; coil impedances Z sum in parallel giving Z_total = Z_coil/p. Current per coil = V/Z_coil (same as single coil). Field from each coil independent and additive.

Series wiring of p coils per phase: voltage V_phase divided across p coils; each coil sees V/p. Total impedance Z_total = p × Z_coil. Current through each coil I = V/(p × Z_coil) = I_parallel/p.

But: the spatial positions of the series-connected coils are at angular separations of 360°/12 × (spacing factor). If the 4 U-phase coils are at 0°, 90°, 180°, 270° (spatial quadrature), their sequential current flow creates a rotating phasor in 90° increments per coil:

    Resultant field direction at time t: sum of 4 equal-amplitude vectors at 0°, 90°, 180°, 270° with current I

The sum is zero in vector terms (the 4 directions cancel pairwise). This appears to imply no net field — but the *time-varying* current I_U(t) = I₀ cos(ωt) modulates this sum at frequency ω, creating an oscillating rather than rotating 4-pole field.

Drew's counter-spin claim implies the specific arrangement creates a *counter-rotating* component by the arrangement of winding sense (whether each coil is wound clockwise or counter-clockwise). The specific cross product of winding sense × spatial position × current phase creates the net counter-rotating field.

This is a nontrivial electromagnetic design choice not captured by standard motor winding tables, and is consistent with Drew's claim that this specific wiring is the "key" to the UFO-style counter-spin behavior.

**4D Periodic Electron — Mathematical Framework**

Drew's model: particles are "4-D periodicities through time."

In standard quantum field theory, a free electron is described by the Dirac equation:

    (iγ^μ ∂_μ - m)ψ = 0

where γ^μ are the 4×4 Dirac matrices, ψ is a 4-component Dirac spinor, and m is the electron mass. The plane-wave solutions are of the form:

    ψ(x) = u(p) e^{-ip_μ x^μ}

These are indeed "4D periodicities" in the sense that e^{-ip_μ x^μ} = e^{-i(Et - **p**·**x**)}, a periodic function in all four spacetime dimensions with temporal period T = 2π/E = h/(mc²) (at rest) and spatial period λ = h/|**p**| (the de Broglie wavelength).

Drew's additional claim: "we only 'see' those time-flowing waves, when a particle is in relative motion to ourselves, and intersects our 3-D space." This is formally similar to the relativistic Doppler/Aharonov interpretation of wave-particle duality — a stationary particle (p=0) has only temporal periodicity (e^{-iEt/ħ}), while a moving particle has spatial periodicity visible as an interference/diffraction pattern.

The "spin-1/2 or spin-1 symmetries" refer to the SU(2) group structure of fermionic (spin-1/2) particles and the SO(3)/SU(2) structure of bosonic (spin-1) particles in 4D spacetime. These are constraints on the 4D periodicity structure — not all 4D periodic functions are valid particle descriptions, only those transforming as irreducible representations of the Lorentz group SO(3,1).
