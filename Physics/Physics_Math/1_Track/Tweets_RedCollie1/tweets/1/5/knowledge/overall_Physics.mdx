**Homopolar Generator/Motor Physics**

The Faraday disk (homopolar device) operating principles:

**As a generator**: A conducting disk of radius R and thickness h, spinning with angular velocity Ω in an external uniform axial field B_z. The motional EMF between axle and rim:

    ε = ∫₀ᴿ (Ω r B_z) dr = ½ Ω B_z R²

For R = 0.1 m, B_z = 0.1 T, Ω = 100 rad/s: ε = 0.05 V. Practical high-field versions require large B_z or fast Ω.

**As a motor**: When current I is forced radially through the spinning disc via brushes, in the presence of axial field B_z, the Lorentz force per unit volume on the disc:

    **f** = **J** × **B** = (J_r r̂) × (B_z ẑ) = J_r B_z φ̂

This azimuthal body force creates a torque T = ∫ r f_φ dV, driving the disc to spin. The homopolar motor is notable for producing a *continuous* (non-pulsating) torque — it does not have the commutation ripple of conventional DC motors. This smooth torque profile may be relevant to why Drew identifies this as the motor type used in the UAP propulsion system.

**Reaction force**: By Newton's third law, the force on the disc creates an equal and opposite reaction force on the magnetic field source (the permanent magnet or electromagnet producing B_z). In a closed mechanical system, this reaction is borne by the magnet support structure. Drew's proposal is that with the disc spinning rapidly while generating its own induced field, the reaction force distribution is modified, potentially creating a net Z-axis imbalance.

**Downward Torsional Force as UAP Levitation Physics**

Drew's claim: "Alien UFOs levitate by a strong, downward-torsional force."

The physical picture of torsional force as a levitation mechanism departs from standard aerodynamic (pressure differential) or electromagnetic (Lorentz force) lift. The key concept:

A "torsional force" in the context of spinning electromagnetic systems refers to the transfer of angular momentum from the spinning craft system to the medium below. If a craft generates a rapidly rotating electromagnetic or mechanical field directed downward, the interaction of this field with the local medium (air, Earth's magnetic field, or vacuum zero-point field) transfers angular momentum downward. Conservation of angular momentum requires an equal and opposite angular momentum to be stored in the craft — but crucially, conservation of *linear* momentum in a torsional (rotationally symmetric) system requires a reaction in the axial (Z) direction.

Formally: if the torsional field carries angular momentum **L** downward (in the -ẑ direction when using the right-hand rule for a clockwise-viewed rotation), and if the medium cannot absorb this angular momentum (e.g., vacuum), then the interaction must produce a force along the ẑ axis to conserve total momentum in the coupled system.

Evidence cited: Edwin Fuhr case (Canada, 1974) — rotating UAP left circular impression in field, with plant stems exhibiting rotational bending/flattening pattern consistent with a downward torsional force.
