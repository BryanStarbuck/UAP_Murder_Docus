A polyphase AC system with N phases, equal amplitudes, and phase separations of 2π/N produces a rotating magnetic field vector of constant magnitude. For 3-phase (N=3, 120° separation):

B_total(t) = B_m * [cos(ωt) x̂ + cos(ωt − 2π/3) ŷ direction rotated] → constant magnitude B_m rotating at angular frequency ω

The synchronous angular velocity of a 2-pole rotating field: ω_s = 2πf (rad/s), where f is supply frequency.

### Faraday Disk Physics

The Faraday homopolar generator: a conducting disk of radius R rotating at angular velocity ω in a uniform axial magnetic field B_z generates an EMF between center and rim:

EMF = ½ · ω · B_z · R²

For a motor operating in reverse (applied voltage drives current radially, field provides azimuthal Lorentz force):
F_θ(r) = J_r(r) × B_z per unit volume, driving azimuthal acceleration.

- The superposition of three phases (120° apart) on a disk creates a spatially rotating pattern of Lorentz force
- This is functionally analogous to the rotating field in a conventional motor, but applied to a conducting disk geometry rather than a wound rotor
