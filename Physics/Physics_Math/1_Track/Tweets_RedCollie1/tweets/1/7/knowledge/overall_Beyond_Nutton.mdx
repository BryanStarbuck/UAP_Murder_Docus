**What Newtonian mechanics predicts:**

The Euler-Lagrange equations for a spinning symmetric top under gravity in the Newtonian framework give:

  Phi_dot (precession rate) = (M * g * r) / (I_3 * psi_dot) * (1 / sin^2(theta)) * (...)

For the simple precession case (theta = const, no nutation):
  Omega_precession = M*g*r / (I_3 * omega_spin)

The sign of Omega_precession is ALWAYS in the same sense as omega_spin (viewed along the upward z-axis, both clockwise or both counter-clockwise). This is a topological result from the cross-product structure of dL/dt = tau. Classical Newtonian mechanics does not permit precession in the opposite sense for a symmetric top under gravity.

**Why this is beyond Newton:**

The additional ingredient is the electromagnetic torque from the rotating magnetic field. The Newtonian treatment of a gyroscope does not include electromagnetic body torques on a magnetized rotor. When the external electromagnetic torque vector has a z-component of the appropriate sign and magnitude, the effective torque experienced by the spinning magnet reverses the precession sense. The full equation of motion is:

  dL/dt = tau_gravity + tau_electromagnetic

where tau_electromagnetic includes torques from the interaction of the magnet's dipole moment with the rotating field (both the gradient torque and the orientation torque). In the standard Newtonian gyroscope, tau_electromagnetic = 0. Setting tau_electromagnetic ≠ 0 and with a z-component exceeding 2*tau_gravity in magnitude (and opposite sign) would reverse the precession. This is an extension beyond classical Newtonian mechanics — it requires electromagnetic theory (Maxwell's equations coupled to mechanics), which is not part of Newton's original framework.

Classical coupled magnetic circuit theory (Neumann mutual inductance formula):
  M_12 = (mu_0 / 4*pi) * integral_C1 integral_C2 (dl_1 · dl_2) / |r_1 - r_2|

For concentric coil pairs (6 outer + 3 inner), there IS non-zero mutual inductance M_12 ≠ 0 in general. A non-zero M_12 means the time-varying current in the outer coils induces a voltage in the inner coils and vice versa — this is standard classical electromagnetic induction (Faraday's law).

Drew's claim that "no loss of power" occurs between the two subsystems implies that the mutual inductance between the two coil sets is negligible or that the geometric arrangement produces M_12 ≈ 0. This is achievable in principle (it is the basis of Helmholtz coil design and balanced transformer design), but requires careful geometric optimization.

If M_12 ≈ 0 is engineered successfully, then the two magnetic subsystems are electromagnetically independent and can produce opposite-direction rotating fields without mutual energy transfer — consistent with Drew's claim. This is not "beyond" classical electromagnetism per se, but represents a non-obvious engineering achievement.
