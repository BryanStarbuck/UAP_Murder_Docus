### Central Claim: Magnetic Spinning Top as UAP Propulsion Analog

Dr. Drew explicitly frames his laboratory device as "new physics related to UFO levitation" — not a theoretical claim, but an experimental result. The beneficial output targeted is vertical lift (Z-axis displacement) achieved without any chemical propellant, aerodynamic surface, or reaction against a fluid medium. The mechanism is purely electromagnetic: a spinning magnetic flywheel whose precession dynamics differ qualitatively from all classical gyroscope behavior.

**The Critical Departure from Classical Gyroscope Dynamics:**

A conventional top or gyroscope, when losing energy under gravity, precesses in the SAME rotational sense as its spin direction (this is the standard Euler-Lagrange result for a symmetric top: the precession rate Omega_p = M_g * r / (I_spin * omega_spin), always co-directional with the spin when the top is in a gravity-decelerating regime).

Drew reports that his magnetic spinning top precesses in the OPPOSITE direction to its spin sense when the top gains energy and rises. This is stated as experimentally observed behavior with no prior theoretical prediction. He describes this as "new physics, not predicted by any theory."

**Mechanistic Chain Linking Physics to Lift:**

1. Three-phase AC power (phases U, V, W at mutual phase offsets of 0°, 120°, 240°) is applied to three wire coils arranged around a central axis.
2. The three-phase current in the coils creates a rotating magnetic field (r.m.f.) in the horizontal (XY) plane.
3. A permanent magnet (tested at 40mm and 60mm diameters) seated on a central shaft (aluminum rod or stainless steel, 6mm or 8mm diameter) is driven to spin by the rotating magnetic field.
4. The spinning magnet, constrained to a ball-joint mount, exhibits precession dynamics.
5. When the system parameters are tuned (frequency, magnet geometry, mechanical coupling), the precessing magnetic top exerts a net upward (Z-axis) force.
6. Measured output: upward force of ~1000 grams (1 kg-force, or approximately 9.8 N) sustained at drive frequencies of 20–30 Hz, increasing with increasing drive frequency.

**The Role of the Silicone Bead Coupling:**

Without a mechanical damper/coupler between the spinning magnet and its support, the magnet "jumps up and down" — meaning the upward impulse is intermittent and uncontrolled. The silicone bead functions as a viscoelastic mechanical coupler that smooths the impulse profile into a nearly constant upward force. This transforms an oscillatory kick into a steady-state thrust. The bead's viscoelastic modulus (frequency-dependent complex shear modulus G*(omega)) damps the resonant bouncing while transmitting the mean precession-derived upward force component.

**Scale-Up Target:**

Drew identifies the condition for achieving net levitation of the entire device: the sustained upward force from the spinning magnetic top must exceed the total weight of the drive coil assembly (described as 6 or 3 wire coils). If F_up(omega) > M_coils * g, the device lifts off. At 1 kg-force upward and spin frequencies of 20–30 Hz (increasing with rpm), this threshold appears accessible with optimized coil-magnet geometry.

### Three Distinct Methodological Paths to Spin/Counter-Spin for UFO Levitation

Drew articulates three engineering approaches to produce the spin and counter-spin motions he hypothesizes are necessary for UAP-style levitation:

**Method 1: Counter-rotating magnetic field driving liquid metal (Lenz induction)**
A counter-rotating r.m.f. applied to a conducting liquid (gallium, mercury) induces circulation in the opposite sense via Lenz's law (the induced current opposes the change in flux, so the liquid is dragged opposite to the field rotation direction when the field reverses). This method requires large electrical power input and is mechanically complex (liquid gallium flowing through tubes past successive coil assemblies). Assessed as viable in principle but practically difficult.

**Method 2: Small ball magnet in a rotating field**
A single permanent magnet (ball geometry) placed in a rotating magnetic field can be made to spin — the direction depends on the magnet's initial orientation and the field geometry. Technical problems noted with this approach include stability and control of the magnet's orientation.

**Method 3: Multiple wire coil rings (6, 9, 12, 15, or 18 coils) with three-phase excitation in axial/vertical flux configuration**
This is the method Drew has advanced furthest experimentally. Rings of N coils (N = 6, 9, 12, 15, 18) arranged in a torus around the central axis, wired as U-V-W-U-V-W... in series (not parallel) and driven with three-phase power, produce a rotating magnetic field whose interaction with a permanent magnet rotor produces either co-rotation or counter-rotation depending on the polarity arrangement of the rotor magnets:
  - Rotor with alternating N-S-N-S-N-S-N-S poles: spins OPPOSITE to r.m.f. direction
  - Rotor with all-N (N-N-N-N-N-N-N-N) poles: spins WITH r.m.f. direction

The key practical constraint: for 6 coils, 8 rotor magnets; for 9 coils, 12 rotor magnets; for 12 coils, the same scaling. These ratios determine the pole-pair count and thus the slip/synchronism relationship analogous to induction motor theory, but with the counterintuitive reversal property for all-same-polarity rotors.

**Inner spinning magnet:** A 70mm permanent magnet with a 14mm bore, seated on an 8mm stainless steel rod with a ball-bearing top. Three wire coils below (also 3-phase, U-V-W) drive this inner magnet to spin clockwise — opposite to the outer rotor.

**Critical bearing specification:** ZrO2 (zirconia, cubic/tetragonal phase, non-magnetic, non-paramagnetic) bearing or Si3N4 (silicon nitride, also non-magnetic) bearing. Conventional steel bearings are ferromagnetic and would be attracted to the spinning magnets, distorting the field geometry and preventing independent spin. Cost: ZrO2 ~$150, Si3N4 ~$300 (aerospace grade). The non-magnetic isolation of the bearing is the enabling technical requirement that makes the concentric counter-spin possible.

**Coil wiring for counter-spin:**
For 12 coils: wire all "U" coils in series; all "V" coils in series; all "W" coils in series. Apply 3-phase AC as U-V-W-U-V-W-U-V-W-U-V-W. This produces the rotating magnetic field. The outer flywheel with 8 alternating N-S magnets spins counter to the r.m.f. The inner flywheel with a large central magnet responds to the separate inner coil set.

**Claimed lift mechanism:** Two concentric magnetic flywheels spinning in opposite directions in the same horizontal plane, driven by separate 3-phase coil sets (outer: 6 coils; inner: 3 coils), are claimed to produce the conditions for upward levitation of the assembly. The demonstration video shows the inner magnet rising upward on its rod when power is applied to the inner coil set while the outer rotor is already spinning.
