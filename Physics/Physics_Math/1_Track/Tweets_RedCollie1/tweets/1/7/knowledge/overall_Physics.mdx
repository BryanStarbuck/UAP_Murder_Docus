For a symmetric top with moment of inertia I_spin about its symmetry axis, spinning at angular velocity omega_spin, with center of mass at distance r from the pivot, under gravity g:

Gravitational torque: tau = r × (M * g * z_hat) = M * g * r * sin(theta) * phi_hat

(where theta is the tilt angle from vertical, phi_hat is the azimuthal unit vector)

Precession rate (Euler's result): Omega_p = tau / L_spin = (M * g * r) / (I_spin * omega_spin)

### Spin/Counter-Spin Device — Component Physics

**Outer rotor — induction-like counter-rotation:**

8 alternating N-S magnets on a ring. When the 6 outer coils produce a clockwise rotating magnetic field (r.m.f.), the alternating-pole rotor spins ANTI-CLOCKWISE. This is the experimentally confirmed key result: the alternating-pole rotor counter-rotates relative to the driving r.m.f.

In standard three-phase induction motor theory, a conducting (squirrel-cage) rotor would be dragged IN THE SAME direction as the r.m.f. (slip drives torque in the direction of the field). The alternating-pole permanent magnet rotor is a synchronous machine variant. For a synchronous machine with alternating poles, the rotor locks to the r.m.f. and spins at synchronous speed — in the SAME direction. The counter-rotation observed by Drew for alternating-pole permanent magnets is therefore anomalous relative to standard synchronous machine theory.

Possible explanation not given by Drew: the magnetic geometry of the specific coil-to-magnet spacing and the 6-coil, 8-magnet configuration creates a pole-pair number mismatch (6 coil pairs / 2 = 3 pole pairs from the stator; 8/2 = 4 pole pairs from the rotor). A pole-pair mismatch in a synchronous machine produces a net torque in the counter-rotation direction in some geometric configurations. This warrants detailed finite-element analysis.

**Inner rotor — upward lifting magnet:**

70mm permanent magnet on 8mm stainless steel rod with ZrO2 ball-bearing top. Driven by 3 lower coils (also clockwise r.m.f.) → inner magnet spins clockwise. The inner magnet is the "spinning top" of the earlier experiments. When inner coil power is applied while the outer rotor is already spinning anti-clockwise, "the magnet lifts up" — it rises along the central rod.

**ZrO2 (zirconia) bearing physics:**

Chemical formula: ZrO2. Crystal structure: cubic or tetragonal phase (stabilized with Y2O3 for the cubic form, aka YSZ). Relative magnetic permeability: mu_r ≈ 1.0000 (essentially non-magnetic, diamagnetic correction of order 10^-5). This property ensures zero magnetic coupling between the bearing and the rotating magnets, allowing the magnets to respond only to the coil-generated fields and to each other.

**Si3N4 (silicon nitride) bearing:**
Covalent ceramic, mu_r ≈ 1.0. Non-magnetic, non-conducting (insulator). Widely used in aerospace bearings for high-speed, high-temperature applications. Drew describes this as "an aerospace bearing, very powerful and non-magnetic."
