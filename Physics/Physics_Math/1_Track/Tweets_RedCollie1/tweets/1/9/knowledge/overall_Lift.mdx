### Jumping Ring — AC-Induced Upward Force as Baseline

The jumping ring experiment is the clearest classical physics analog to the claimed
lift mechanism. It demonstrates that a time-varying magnetic flux induces eddy
currents in a nearby conductor; the interaction between the induced current and the
original flux produces a repulsive (upward) force — which is, at its core, an
electromagnetic levitation force.

Lenz's Law basis: the induced EMF opposes the flux change, and the force between
the inducing coil and the induced-current ring always acts to oppose the relative
motion — here the flux is increasing as the coil is energized, so the ring is
pushed away (upward).

Force magnitude in a simplified model:
    F_z ≈ μ₀ (I_coil)² (dM/dz) / 2

where M(z) is the mutual inductance between coil and ring as a function of axial
separation z.

The limitations of classical jumping ring: it requires the ring to be in the region
of spatially changing mutual inductance (the ring cannot hover freely far above
the coil where dM/dz → 0). Drew's experimental finding: three wire coils with
3-phase power and a spinning magnet do not give sufficient upward force by
themselves. The spin-augmentation hypothesis is that spin adds a gyroscopic coupling
that extends the effective force range.

### Magnetic Top — Precession-Driven Lift

"A magnetic top being spun up rather than falling down, precesses in the opposite
direction."

The conventional top, when its spin axis tilts from vertical, undergoes precession
and eventually spirals downward (nutation-precession coupling → increasing tilt
angle → "sleeping top" gives way to falling). The magnetic top claim: the magnetic
torque component reverses the precession direction, which in turn means the nutation
dynamics change. If the magnetic torque acts to reduce the tilt angle (restore the
spin axis toward vertical), the top can sustain levitation rather than falling.

The relevance to UFO lift: a craft using counter-rotating magnetic components would
experience a similar magnetically-driven precession that, rather than allowing the
craft to tilt and fall, maintains vertical orientation and produces a sustained
upward gyroscopic force. This is the "spun up rather than falling down" behavior
described — the magnetic modification of precession dynamics changes falling into
levitation.

"UFOs may fly using two magnetic flywheels, inner and outer, which spin in opposite
directions."

### Six-Coil + 16-Magnet — Lab Lift Attempt

The 6-coil 3-phase assembly driving a 16-magnet NNSS ring is the most detailed
experimental configuration reported. The goal is to achieve a net upward (Z-axis)
force on the magnet ring by the interaction of the rotating 3-phase stator field
with the permanent-magnet rotor ring.

In standard AC motor theory, the force between stator and rotor is purely
azimuthal (torque) when both are in the same horizontal plane and the geometry
is symmetric about the midplane. For a net Z-axis (axial) force, the system must
break the midplane symmetry: either the stator and rotor are axially offset, or
the field geometry produces an asymmetric axial flux distribution.

Drew's spin-and-counter-spin approach is to break this axial symmetry through the
combination of: (a) the rotating field from the 6 coils (XY-plane rotation) and
(b) the translational interaction between the NNSS pattern on the ring and the
rotating field. When the ring is also spinning (driven by another mechanism), the
Lorentz force in the Z direction (derived in overall_Math.mdx) becomes a DC force
at the synchronous condition, providing sustained axial lift.
