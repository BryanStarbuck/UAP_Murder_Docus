### Gyroscopic Precession — Classical and Magnetic-Modified

Classical precession angular velocity (vector equation):

    dL/dt = τ
    Ω_p × L = τ
    Ω_p = τ / |L| (magnitude, direction from cross products)

Full expansion:
    τ_gravity = r_cm × (m g (-ẑ))
    L = I ω ω̂  (ω̂ = unit vector along spin axis)
    Ω_p = |τ_gravity| / (I ω) = (r_cm m g sin θ) / (I ω)

where θ is the angle of the spin axis from vertical.

For a magnetic top in external field B:
    τ_total = τ_gravity + τ_mag
            = r_cm × mg(-ẑ) + μ × B

where μ = g_s μ_B S / ℏ (classical analogue: μ = I_loop × A × n̂, magnetic
moment of the spinning magnetized body).

Reversal condition:
    τ_total · (Ω_p direction) changes sign
    ⟺ τ_mag dominates AND has opposite handedness relative to L

Precession reversal (claimed experimental result):
    sign(Ω_p) changes when τ_mag > τ_gravity and their cross-product orientations
    with L are antiparallel.

### Homopolar Generator EMF

Faraday (homopolar) EMF for a conducting disc of outer radius r rotating at angular
velocity ω in uniform axial magnetic field B_z:

    V_homopolar = ½ ω B_z r²

Derivation: motional EMF for a radial element at radius ρ:
    dV = (v × B) · dr = (ωρ φ̂ × B_z ẑ) · dρ r̂
       = ωρ B_z (φ̂ × ẑ) · r̂ = ωρ B_z (-r̂) · r̂ · ... 

Using φ̂ × ẑ = -r̂ in cylindrical coordinates:
    dV = ωρ B_z dρ
    V = ∫₀ʳ ωρ B_z dρ = ½ ω B_z r²

Motor torque (when DC current I is fed from center to rim via liquid-metal brushes):
    T_disc = B_z I r² / 2

Dimensional check: [T/A · A · m²] = [N·m] ✓

### Newton's Third Law Applied to Homopolar Motor

Force on external current-carrying wire (length dl at position r from axis) in
field B_z:
    dF_wire = I (dl × B_z ẑ)

If wire is azimuthal (φ direction): dl = dl φ̂
    dF_wire = I B_z dl (φ̂ × ẑ) = -I B_z dl r̂

This is a radially inward force on the wire — not a torque in φ. For torque on
wire, need wire with radial component:
    dl = dl r̂
    dF_wire = I B_z dl (r̂ × ẑ) = I B_z dl φ̂ ... produces azimuthal force.

Torque on wire at radius ρ: dτ_wire = ρ × dF_wire = ρ I B_z dl

Newton's Third Law requires: τ_wire = -τ_disc ⟹ B_z I r²/2 (disc) = -τ_wire

If the wires in the homopolar motor are external (outside the magnetic field region
where the disc resides), then τ_wire ≈ 0 since B ≈ 0 at the wire location. The
equal-and-opposite torque then resides entirely in the electromagnetic field angular
momentum stored between disc and magnets — and this field momentum does not
mechanically push the wires.

This is the mathematical basis for the observed apparent Newton's Third Law
violation: the reaction torque goes into the electromagnetic field (the Maxwell
stress tensor stores angular momentum), not into the wire mass.

### Three-Phase Rotating Magnetic Field — Mathematical Structure

Three symmetric phases (balanced system), N pole pairs:

    B(θ, t) = B₀ [cos(ωt - Nθ) r̂ + sin(ωt - Nθ) φ̂]

This is a helical wave in (θ, t) space. The field rotates at:
    ω_sync = ω / N  (synchronous angular velocity)

For counter-rotation with reversed phase sequence:
    B_counter(θ, t) = B₀ [cos(-ωt - Nθ) r̂ + sin(-ωt - Nθ) φ̂]
                    = B₀ [cos(ωt + Nθ) r̂ - sin(ωt + Nθ) φ̂]

Superposition of clockwise and counter-clockwise fields:
    B_total = B_cw + B_ccw
    B_x = 2 B₀ cos(ωt) cos(Nθ)
    B_y = 2 B₀ sin(ωt) cos(Nθ) ... (wait, this is for equal amplitudes, same N)

Actually for equal amplitude N-pole-pair fields rotating in opposite directions:
    B_total = 2 B₀ cos(Nθ) [cos(ωt) x̂ + sin(ωt) ŷ]
           ... this does NOT cancel — it produces a linearly polarized field whose
    amplitude has spatial dependence 2B₀ cos(Nθ) and whose direction rotates at ω.

The spatial nodes (where cos(Nθ) = 0) are at θ = π/(2N), 3π/(2N), ... — these
are N equally spaced angular locations where B_total = 0. Between nodes the field
is non-zero and rotating. The force on a conducting rotor placed in this field
has a spatial dependence that concentrates torque at the antinodes (cos(Nθ) = ±1)
and creates no torque at nodes. This asymmetric torque map in the azimuthal direction
is qualitatively different from a single rotating field.

### Lorentz Force — Z-Axis Component Calculation

Carrier velocity in rotating disc:
    v_carrier = v_r r̂ + ω_disc ρ φ̂

Applied field with rotating transverse component:
    B = B_z ẑ + B_⊥ cos(Ωt) x̂ + B_⊥ sin(Ωt) ŷ

In cylindrical coordinates:
    B = B_z ẑ + B_⊥ [cos(Ωt - φ) r̂ - sin(Ωt - φ) φ̂]

Lorentz force on charge q with velocity v_carrier:
    F = q (v_carrier × B)
      = q [(v_r r̂ + ωρ φ̂) × (B_z ẑ + B_⊥r r̂ + B_⊥φ φ̂)]

Expanding using cylindrical cross-product table:
    r̂ × ẑ = -φ̂
    φ̂ × ẑ = r̂
    r̂ × r̂ = 0
    r̂ × φ̂ = ẑ
    φ̂ × r̂ = -ẑ
    φ̂ × φ̂ = 0
    ẑ × r̂ = φ̂
    ẑ × φ̂ = -r̂

Therefore:
    F = q [v_r B_z (r̂ × ẑ) + v_r B_⊥φ (r̂ × φ̂) + ωρ B_z (φ̂ × ẑ) + ωρ B_⊥r (φ̂ × r̂)]
      = q [-v_r B_z φ̂ + v_r B_⊥φ ẑ + ωρ B_z r̂ + ωρ B_⊥r (-ẑ)]
      = q [ωρ B_z r̂ - v_r B_z φ̂ + (v_r B_⊥φ - ωρ B_⊥r) ẑ]

Z-component of Lorentz force:
    F_z = q (v_r B_⊥φ - ωρ B_⊥r)

Where:
    B_⊥r = B_⊥ cos(Ωt - φ)
    B_⊥φ = -B_⊥ sin(Ωt - φ)

Therefore:
    F_z = q [-v_r B_⊥ sin(Ωt - φ) - ωρ B_⊥ cos(Ωt - φ)]
        = -q B_⊥ [v_r sin(Ωt - φ) + ωρ cos(Ωt - φ)]

This oscillates at the 3-phase drive frequency Ω. The time-averaged F_z = 0 for
arbitrary phase (Ωt - φ). For non-zero time-averaged F_z, a phase-locking condition
is required: the disc must spin at ω_disc such that the carrier's azimuthal position
φ = ω_disc t + φ₀ tracks the rotating B field. When ω_disc = Ω (synchronous
condition), Ωt - φ = Ωt - Ω t - φ₀ = -φ₀ = constant, and:
    F_z(synchronous) = -q B_⊥ [v_r sin(-φ₀) + ωρ cos(-φ₀)] = constant ≠ 0

This is the mathematical condition for a sustained Z-axis force: the disc must spin
synchronously with the 3-phase rotating field (ω_disc = Ω). At synchronous speed,
the Z-force becomes a DC (constant) value rather than AC (oscillating). The sign
and magnitude of F_z depend on the phase offset φ₀ and can be tuned by adjusting
the relative phase of the DC spin drive and the 3-phase field.

### 16-Magnet NNSS Ring — Pole-Pair Mathematics

16 magnets arranged NNSS-NNSS-... around a ring.
Each NNSS group = 4 magnets.
16 / 4 = 4 groups = 4-fold azimuthal symmetry.

Within each NNSS quadrant:
- NN pair: two north poles adjacent → strong repulsion zone, net B radially outward
- SS pair: two south poles adjacent → strong repulsion zone, net B radially inward

The 16-magnet NNSS ring has an effective pole-pair count of N_ring = 8 (each NN
acts as one effective N-pole, each SS as one S-pole, giving 8 effective pole
transitions around 360°).

The 6-coil 3-phase winding has a spatial harmonic content. For 6 coils arranged
symmetrically at 60° intervals driven as two groups of 3-phase (U, V, W, U', V',
W' in series), the fundamental pole pair number is N_stator = 3 (for standard 3-
phase 6-coil winding) or N_stator = 1 (for alternate winding).

The synchronous interaction between the rotating field (pole pair number N_s) and
the magnet ring (effective pole pair number N_r = 8) requires:
    ω_ring = (N_s / N_r) × Ω_supply

For N_s = 3, N_r = 8: ω_ring = (3/8) × Ω_supply

For N_s = 1, N_r = 8: ω_ring = (1/8) × Ω_supply

The beat frequency between field and ring rotation (at non-synchronous speeds) is:
    f_beat = |N_s × f_supply - N_r × f_ring| / (2π)

At f_beat = 0 (synchronous lock), the torque is purely DC and maximum. At non-zero
f_beat, torque oscillates and averages to a lower value, potentially zero for
induction machines.

### Oberth Maneuver — Energy Mathematics

Vis-viva equation for orbit:
    v² = GM(2/r - 1/a)

where v = orbital speed at radius r, GM = gravitational parameter, a = semi-major
axis.

At perihelion r = r_p (minimum), v is maximum. A burn of Δv at perihelion:
    ΔKE = ½m(v + Δv)² - ½mv² = mv Δv + ½m(Δv)²

At perihelion v is large, so mv Δv dominates. This is the Oberth effect: the same
Δv produces more kinetic energy gain at higher background speed.

For a hyperbolic trajectory (interstellar object 3I/ATLAS):
    v² = (v_∞)² + 2GM/r

where v_∞ is the hyperbolic excess velocity at infinity (measured velocity of
3I/ATLAS far from the Sun). The perihelion occurs at r_p where all excess energy is
kinetic.

A propulsive burn at perihelion would change v_∞ by:
    Δ(v_∞²) = 2v_p Δv + (Δv)²

where v_p = perihelion speed. This is directly observable as a change in the
hyperbolic orbit's asymptotic direction after perihelion passage — the strongest
test of artificial propulsion for an interstellar object.
