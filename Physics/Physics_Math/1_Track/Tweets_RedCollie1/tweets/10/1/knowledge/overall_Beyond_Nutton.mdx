  interacting via electromagnetic fields — or it implies that the electromagnetic
  field itself carries the reaction momentum (field momentum = E x B / c^2 in
  classical EM). The latter is formally allowed in standard EM but typically
  negligible at macroscopic scales.

### Field momentum as the resolution:

g_EM = epsilon_0 * (E x B) = S / c^2

where S = E x B / mu_0 is the Poynting vector and S/c^2 is the momentum flux.

If the rotating magnetic field configuration creates a nonzero net Poynting
vector with a downward component, the field carries downward momentum, allowing
the device to receive upward momentum — without expelling material mass.
This is within standard EM but is a mechanism rarely considered in propulsion
engineering. Drew does not invoke this explicitly but it is the closest
