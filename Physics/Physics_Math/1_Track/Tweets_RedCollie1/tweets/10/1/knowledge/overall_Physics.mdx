For a permanent magnet (magnetic dipole moment m = m * z_hat, assumed aligned
with Z-axis initially) placed in the rotating field B_net:

Torque tau = m x B_net
           = m * z_hat x B_0 * [cos(omega_e * t) x_hat + sin(omega_e * t) y_hat]

spin about the Z-axis (following the rotating field). There is NO Z-component

## III. Helical Magnetic Field: Distinction from Rotating Planar Field

A helical magnetic field is not the same as a rotating planar field. A helix
has:
* Azimuthal component: B_phi (rotates around the Z-axis)
* Axial component: B_z (directed along Z)
* Together: B = B_phi(r, phi, z) phi_hat + B_z(r, phi, z) z_hat

In cylindrical coordinates (r, phi, z), a helical field can be parameterized as:
B_helix = B_0 * [sin(k*z - omega*t) phi_hat + cos(k*z - omega*t) z_hat]

where k = 2*pi/lambda is the helix wavenumber (lambda = pitch of the helix) and
omega is the rotation frequency.

The force on a magnetic dipole m in an inhomogeneous field B is:
F = nabla(m . B)

For a dipole aligned with the helix:
m . B = m * B_0 * [sin(k*z - omega*t) * m_phi/m + cos(k*z - omega*t) * m_z/m]

If the dipole's orientation is phase-locked to the helix (synchronous with
rotation), there will be a nonzero gradient of (m . B) along Z, producing a
net axial force:
F_z = m * B_0 * k * d/dz[cos(k*z - omega*t)] evaluated at synchronous condition
    = -m * B_0 * k * sin(k*z - omega*t) at z = (omega*t)/k (synchronous point)

magnetic bottle / magnetic mirror — the axial gradient of the field creates an
axial (Z) force component. This could produce RISE if the field is configured
such that the gradient pushes the dipole upward.
