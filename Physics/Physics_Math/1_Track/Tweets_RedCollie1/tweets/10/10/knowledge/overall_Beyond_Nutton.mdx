Newton's Third Law states: for every force exerted by body A on body B, body B exerts an equal
and opposite force on body A. In classical mechanics this is universal.

Total momentum is conserved: p_particle + p_field = constant.
But p_particle alone is NOT conserved when the field stores momentum — the particle's momentum
changes without a simultaneous equal-and-opposite mechanical momentum change elsewhere in the
apparatus.

Drew's experimental claim: in his specific device geometry, the Lorentz force produces an upward
force on the rotor without producing an equal downward force on the stator frame. The electromagnetic
field absorbs the reaction momentum. If this is correct, it is not a violation of fundamental
physics (since total momentum, including field momentum, is conserved) but is a violation of the
naive mechanical Newton's Third Law applied only to material bodies.

The anomaly: the torque on the disc is not accounted for by the mechanical torque of the wires.
