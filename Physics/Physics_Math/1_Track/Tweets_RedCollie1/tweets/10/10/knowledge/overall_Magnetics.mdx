## 3. Lorentz Force in Liquid Metal (MHD Stirring)

The Lorentz body force in a conducting fluid is:
  f = J x B  [N/m^3]
where J is the current density [A/m^2] and B is the applied magnetic flux density [T].

Applied to liquid metal carrying a DC current density J in the presence of the external DC field B
from the six outer coils, this force is perpendicular to both J and B. If J is vertical (z) and
B is radial (r), then f is azimuthal (phi), driving circular rotation — i.e., spin — of the liquid
metal in the XY plane. This is standard electromagnetic stirring used in metallurgical applications.

## 7. Bearing Materials for High-Speed Magnetic Rotors

From tweet 2033853862105518134:
"Too soft! they only make bearing with hard metals such as chrome steel, stainless steel,
ZrO2 or Si3N4."

This specifies the required bearing materials for the spinning magnet assembly:
* Chrome steel (bearing steel, AISI 52100): hardness ~60-65 HRC, low magnetic permeability.
* Stainless steel (316L or 440C grades): corrosion resistance, moderate hardness.
* ZrO2 (zirconia): ceramic bearing, electrical insulator, very low magnetic interaction,
  hardness ~1100 HV, density ~6.0 g/cm^3 (lighter than steel).
* Si3N4 (silicon nitride): ceramic bearing, electrical insulator, very high hardness (~1500 HV),
  low thermal expansion, density ~3.2 g/cm^3, ideal for high-speed applications.

Ceramic bearings (ZrO2, Si3N4) are preferred because they are non-magnetic and non-conductive,
preventing eddy current heating and magnetic force interference with the rotor's magnetic field.
At high rotational speeds in a magnetic drive, this is essential for stable operation.

## 8. Einstein-de Haas Effect: Quantum Spin Angular Momentum as a Real Magnetic-Mechanical Coupling

Video 13_1935610801450070016_video.mp4 (transcription):
"If electrons truly have angular momentum, then flipping their spin should cause the entire bolt
to start rotating. Now in an atom, an electron's mass is tiny, only about 0.03% of the total
atom's mass. So while each electron has a lot of angular momentum, it has to move a huge amount
of mass. That means that the rotation of the bolt will be very small. To detect it, I'll hang
the bolt from a string and an electric current through these coils, flip their spin simultaneously,

The Einstein-de Haas effect establishes the equivalence between spin angular momentum S and
mechanical angular momentum L. When the magnetization M of a ferromagnetic sample is reversed
(flipping electron spins from spin-up to spin-down or vice versa), the total angular momentum
of the system is conserved:
  ΔS_electrons + ΔL_mechanical = 0
  => ΔL_mechanical = -ΔS_electrons

The gyromagnetic ratio g connects magnetic moment mu to angular momentum J:
  mu = g * (e / 2m_e) * S = g * mu_B / hbar * S

For free electrons, g ≈ 2.002. For iron (Fe), the measured g-factor is g ≈ 2.09, consistent
with predominantly spin (rather than orbital) angular momentum.

The practical significance for the UFO drive hypothesis: if quantum spin angular momentum is
physically real and can be transferred to macroscopic mechanical rotation (as demonstrated by
the Einstein-de Haas experiment), then manipulating the collective spin state of a ferromagnetic
or paramagnetic spinning medium through applied electromagnetic fields could constitute a mechanism
for transferring angular momentum to or from the surrounding spacetime in ways not fully captured
by classical mechanics.
