# Mathematical Structures: Equations, Components, and Relationships

## 1. Lorentz Force Body Force Density in Conducting Fluids

Fundamental equation:
  f = J x B   [N/m^3]

Components:
* f: Lorentz body force density vector [N/m^3]
* J: electric current density vector [A/m^2]
* B: magnetic flux density vector [T = kg/(A*s^2)]
* x: cross product (vector product), producing a vector perpendicular to both J and B

Geometry for the liquid-metal vortex:
If J is directed along the z-axis (vertical current injection) and B is directed radially (r-hat,
from outer DC electrodes inward), then:
  f = J * B_r * (z-hat x r-hat) = J * B_r * (-phi-hat)
producing azimuthal (tangential) acceleration of the liquid metal — i.e., spin in the phi
direction (azimuthal = XY plane rotation).

Alternatively, if J is azimuthal (driven by tangential electrodes) and B is vertical (axial):
  f = J_phi * B_z * (phi-hat x z-hat) = J_phi * B_z * (-r-hat)
producing radial force (inward or outward depending on sign) — this geometry can create a
centripetal force that holds a rotating liquid column together.

Drew's claimed Z-axis force: if J is azimuthal (tangential) and B is also azimuthal (from the
spinning magnet), then:
  f = J_phi * B_phi * (phi-hat x phi-hat) = 0
This would produce no force — suggesting the mechanism requires a more complex geometry or a
time-dependent field structure. The specific tensor structure enabling Z-axis force generation
is not explicitly derived in the source material; the claim rests on experimental observation.

## 2. Angular Momentum: Conservation Equations

Angular momentum of a rigid body:
  L = I * omega  [kg*m^2/s]

where I is the moment of inertia tensor and omega is the angular velocity vector.

For contra-rotating discs:
  L_1 = I_1 * omega_1  (disc 1, CCW: omega_1 = +omega * z-hat)
  L_2 = I_2 * omega_2  (disc 2, CW: omega_2 = -omega * z-hat)

For equal moments of inertia (I_1 = I_2 = I) and equal speed (|omega_1| = |omega_2| = omega):
  L_total = L_1 + L_2 = I * omega * z-hat + I * (-omega) * z-hat = 0

Conservation of total angular momentum (no external torque):
  d/dt (L_total) = tau_external = 0

Therefore: if L_total = 0 and there is a net upward force F_z on the system, no net torque
is required from the environment to maintain the spin states. This is the mathematical basis
for Drew's contra-rotation proposal.

## 3. Einstein-de Haas Effect: Gyromagnetic Ratio

The relationship between spin angular momentum S and magnetic moment mu_s for an electron:
  mu_s = -g_s * (e / 2 m_e) * S
where:
* g_s ≈ 2.002319 (electron spin g-factor, from QED)
* e = 1.602176634 * 10^-19 C (elementary charge)
* m_e = 9.1093837015 * 10^-31 kg (electron mass)
* S: spin angular momentum (magnitude = hbar * sqrt(s(s+1)) = hbar * sqrt(3)/2 for s=1/2)
* mu_B = e*hbar/(2*m_e) = 9.2740100783 * 10^-24 J/T (Bohr magneton)

For a ferromagnetic material, the bulk magnetization M is related to the average spin per atom.
When M is reversed by external field, total angular momentum conservation requires:
  Delta L_mechanical = -Delta S_electrons = -(2 M * V) / (g_s * e / m_e)

where V is the sample volume and M is the magnetization per unit volume [A/m].

Quantitative estimate from the experiment:
Electron mass fraction: m_e / m_atom ≈ 0.03% = 3 * 10^-4.
For a bolt of mass M_bolt, the electron mass is approximately 3 * 10^-4 * M_bolt.
The electron spin angular momentum per electron: S = hbar/2 = 5.27 * 10^-35 J*s.
The macroscopic rotation angle theta detected by string torsion satisfies:
  I_bolt * omega_bolt = 2 * n_electrons * hbar/2  (factor 2 for spin flip from +1/2 to -1/2)
  omega_bolt = n_electrons * hbar / I_bolt

For a steel bolt of mass ~10 g, radius ~5 mm, length ~5 cm:
  I_bolt = (1/2) * M_bolt * r^2 ≈ (1/2) * 0.01 * (0.005)^2 = 1.25 * 10^-7 kg*m^2
  n_electrons (conduction electrons): ~6 * 10^23 * 0.01 / 0.056 * 26 ≈ 2.8 * 10^24 electrons
  omega_bolt ≈ 2.8 * 10^24 * 1.054 * 10^-34 / 1.25 * 10^-7 ≈ 2.4 * 10^-3 rad/s

This is tiny but detectable with a string torsion pendulum at sufficiently long period.

## 5. Quantum Spin State Topology: Linking Numbers

Drew's model maps spin states to topological linking numbers, analogous to DNA plasmid topology.

For a closed curve C in 3-D space, the self-linking number Lk is:
  Lk = Tw + Wr
where Tw (twist) measures the local winding of the curve about its axis and Wr (writhe) measures
the global configuration in 3-D space.

For DNA: Lk is a topological invariant — it cannot be changed without breaking the strand.
Relaxed DNA has Lk = Lk_0 = N/10 (one right-handed turn per 10 base pairs).
Supercoiled DNA has Lk ≠ Lk_0; the superhelical density is sigma = (Lk - Lk_0)/Lk_0.

Drew's proposed analogy for electron orbitals:
* s orbital (l=0): Lk = 0 (no angular winding — spherically symmetric)
* p orbital (l=1): |Lk| = 1 (one unit of angular winding — dumbbell symmetry)
* d orbital (l=2): |Lk| = 2 (two units — cloverleaf symmetry)
* f orbital (l=3): |Lk| = 3

This provides a topological classification of orbitals where the quantum number l corresponds
directly to the topological winding number of the closed electron path in 4-D spacetime.

The probabilistic 3-D geometries (wavefunctions psi(r,theta,phi)) arise from the projection of
a deterministic 4-D closed path onto 3-D space — analogous to how a DNA ring has a well-defined
Lk but many possible 3-D geometries (conformations) with the same Lk.

## 6. Spin-1/2 as 4-D Rotation Topology

Two spin states (++, --) and (+-, -+) in 4-D xyzt space represent distinct topologies of
a linked pair of 2-D rotations.

In the (x,t) and (y,z) planes simultaneously:
* State (++, --): both 2-D rotations wind in the same topological sense (both counterclockwise
  when viewed from a fixed reference). Spin-up.
* State (+-, -+): one rotation winds CW, the other CCW. Spin-down.

Under a 2*pi rotation of the embedding 3-D space, a spin-1/2 particle returns to its original
state only after a 4*pi rotation — the SU(2) double cover of SO(3). In Drew's topology model,
this corresponds to the fact that exchanging (++, --) and (+-, -+) requires traversing a
topological boundary in the 4-D configuration space, not accessible by continuous deformation
in 3-D.

Mathematically, this is consistent with the fiber bundle structure of spinors: the spin-1/2
representation of SU(2) is a section of a non-trivial principal bundle over S^2, and the
topological obstruction to trivializing this bundle is the first Chern class c_1 = 1.

## 7. Rayleigh/Mie Scattering: Wavelength Dependence

Rayleigh scattering cross section (particle radius r << lambda):
  sigma_R = (128 * pi^5 * r^6) / (3 * lambda^4) * ((n^2 - 1)/(n^2 + 2))^2

where n is the refractive index of the particle and lambda is the wavelength.

Key scaling: sigma_R ~ lambda^{-4}
  sigma_R(blue, 450nm) / sigma_R(red, 700nm) = (700/450)^4 ≈ (1.556)^4 ≈ 5.86

Blue light scatters approximately 5.86 times more than red light from the same dust grain.
This quantitatively explains why:
* The 3I/ATLAS dust tail is invisible at blue wavelengths (blue photons are scattered away).
* The tail becomes visible at green (lambda ~ 550 nm) and red (lambda ~ 700 nm) wavelengths.
* The coma (central bright region) appears round and blue-dominated because the dust
  density near the nucleus is high enough to produce forward-scattering regardless of wavelength,
  but the extended tail has lower dust column density and is wavelength-selective.

## 8. Coil-Magnet Pole Count Design Rules

Stator pole count P_s = number of coil poles = 2 * N_coils (each coil spans one N and one S pole).
Rotor magnet count P_r = number of magnet poles.

For smooth torque (minimizing torque ripple), the condition is:
  GCD(P_s, P_r) should be small and P_s - P_r should be ±1 or ±2.

Drew's stated configurations:
  6 outer coils -> P_s = 6 poles per phase * 3 phases / (poles per coil) ...

Simplified interpretation from the stated formula:
  6 coils outside -> 8 magnets (N-S-N-S-N-S-N-S) = 4 pole pairs
  or 16 magnets (NN-SS-NN-SS...) = 8 effective pole pairs (Halbach-like)

  9 coils outside -> 12 magnets = 6 pole pairs
  or 24 magnets = 12 effective pole pairs (Halbach-like)

Pole ratio P_r/P_s:
  8/6 = 4/3 (standard switched reluctance / BLDC ratio)
  12/9 = 4/3 (same ratio, scaled up)

This 4/3 ratio is common in brushless DC motor design for smooth torque without cogging.

## 9. Information-Theoretic Argument: Binary vs. Quaternary

Shannon information content per symbol:
  H = log2(N) bits/symbol

For N=2 (binary): H = 1 bit/symbol
For N=4 (quaternary): H = 2 bits/symbol

Landauer's principle: minimum energy to erase 1 bit:
  E_Landauer = k_B * T * ln(2) ≈ 2.87 * 10^-21 J at T = 300 K

For a fixed physical volume storing n symbols:
  Total information (binary):    I_bin = n bits
  Total information (quaternary): I_quat = 2n bits

For the same total information I_total:
  Number of elements needed (binary):    n_bin = I_total
  Number of elements needed (quaternary): n_quat = I_total / 2

Energy cost (binary vs quaternary) for same I_total, same T:
  E_bin = n_bin * k_B T ln2 = I_total * k_B T ln2
  E_quat = n_quat * k_B T ln2 * log2(4)/log2(2) = (I_total/2) * k_B T * ln4 = I_total * k_B T ln2

Equal energy — but half the number of physical elements required, reducing device size and
manufacturing complexity. This is the mathematical basis for Drew's claim that quaternary
computing "requires less electrical power" — the dynamic switching energy (C*V^2 per cycle)
is halved because half as many switching elements are needed.

DNA analogy: DNA stores 2 bits per base pair using 4 bases. A binary DNA (2 bases) would
store 1 bit per base pair and require twice as many base pairs for the same genome — consistent
with all known biology exclusively using 4-base alphabets.
