1. LORENTZ FORCE IN CORBINO DISC (FORMALIZING DREW'S DESCRIBED MECHANISM)
====================================================================

Source descriptions: tweet 1992514814242099289 (note_tweet),
tweet 2008156787409822198 (note_tweet), video 13_1971360840071933952

Setup: conductive liquid (galinstan) in stainless steel container, axial
permanent magnet (m1) below, DC current I flowing radially (Corbino geometry).

Governing Maxwell-Lorentz relations in cylindrical coordinates (r, phi, z):

Current density (radial, assuming uniform azimuthal symmetry):
    J_r(r) = I / (2 pi r d)
where I = total radial current [A], d = disc thickness [m], r = radial position [m].

Magnetic flux density (axial, from magnet m1, approximated as uniform near axis):
    B = B_z * z_hat [T]

Lorentz body force per unit volume on liquid conductor:
    f = sigma * (E + v × B) × B   (full MHD form)

In the DC steady-state, current is maintained by external source:
    f_body = J × B
    f = J_r * r_hat × B_z * z_hat = J_r * B_z * (r_hat × z_hat)
    r_hat × z_hat = -phi_hat   (in standard cylindrical orientation)
    f_phi = -J_r * B_z   [N/m^3]

Total azimuthal torque on liquid column between r_inner and r_outer, height d:
    tau = integral(r_inner to r_outer) f_phi * r * (2 pi r d) dr
         = -B_z * d * integral(r_inner to r_outer) J_r(r) * r * 2 pi r dr
         = -B_z * d * integral I/(2 pi r d) * r * 2 pi r dr
         = -B_z * I * integral(r_inner to r_outer) r dr
         = -B_z * I * (r_outer^2 - r_inner^2) / 2

Direction reversal: I -> -I gives tau -> -tau (azimuthal rotation reverses).
This is the mathematical description of the DC reversal experiment (video 13_1971360840071933952).

2. ANGULAR MOMENTUM CONSERVATION IN COUNTER-ROTATION DESIGN
====================================================================

Source: tweet 1992514814242099289 (note_tweet)

Disc magnet m2, moment of inertia I_m2, angular velocity omega_m2 (clockwise, positive):
    L_m2 = I_m2 * omega_m2    [kg m^2 / s]

Galinstan liquid in Corbino disc, effective moment of inertia I_gas,
angular velocity omega_gas (anti-clockwise, negative under Drew's sign convention):
    L_gas = -I_gas * omega_gas_magnitude   [kg m^2 / s]

Design target (Drew's stated goal):
    L_total = L_m2 + L_gas = I_m2 * omega_m2 - I_gas * |omega_gas| = 0

This requires:
    I_m2 * omega_m2 = I_gas * |omega_gas|

For fixed geometry (fixed I_m2 and I_gas determined by device dimensions and
material densities), this is a constraint on the ratio of angular velocities:
    omega_m2 / |omega_gas| = I_gas / I_m2

In practice, omega_m2 is controlled by the 3-phase frequency (drive frequency),
and |omega_gas| is controlled by DC current magnitude I and B_z of m1. The
system can be tuned to satisfy L_total = 0 by adjusting either the 3-phase
frequency or the DC current.

3. GYROSCOPIC PRECESSION (TOP ANALOGY)
====================================================================

Source: tweet 1964253437702656144 (note_tweet), video 13_1964248354038435843

For a symmetric spinning top with:
    M = mass of disc magnet [kg]
    g = 9.81 m/s^2
    r_cm = distance from pivot to center of mass [m] (effective for magnet on flexible rod)
    I_s = moment of inertia about spin axis [kg m^2]
    omega_s = spin angular velocity [rad/s]

Gyroscopic precession angular velocity:
    Omega_p = (M g r_cm) / (I_s * omega_s)

At low omega_s (low 3-phase frequency), Omega_p is large => erratic precession observed.
At high omega_s (high 3-phase frequency, ~20-25 Hz), Omega_p is small => stable spin,
magnet "rises like a top."

The 3-phase frequency f [Hz] maps to disc magnet spin rate omega_s via the
synchronous speed relationship (assuming synchronous tracking with the rotating field):
    omega_s = 2 pi f   [rad/s]

At f = 20 Hz: omega_s = 125.7 rad/s ~ 1200 RPM.
At f = 30 Hz: omega_s = 188.5 rad/s ~ 1800 RPM.

These are the regime in which the magnet rises ("like a spinning top") in
Drew's experiments.

7. MAGNETIC LEVITATION OF m2 ABOVE GALINSTAN SURFACE
====================================================================

Source: tweet 1992514814242099289 (note_tweet)

Magnet m1 (stationary, below the galinstan pool) must provide sufficient
repulsive force on m2 (above the galinstan surface) to levitate m2 at the
desired height h above the liquid metal. The levitation force must balance
gravity on m2:

    F_mag_levitation(h) = M_m2 * g

where M_m2 is the mass of disc magnet m2, and F_mag_levitation(h) is the
repulsive force between the like poles of m1 and m2 at separation distance h.

For two aligned disc magnets of radius R and thickness t, the axial force
between them at separation h (for like poles facing each other):

    F = (mu_0 / 4 pi) * ... [full expression involves elliptic integrals for
    finite-size magnets]

In the dipole approximation (valid when h >> R):
    F ≈ (3 mu_0 / 2 pi) * (m1 * m2) / h^4

where m1, m2 are the magnetic dipole moments of the two magnets [A m^2],
mu_0 = 4 pi × 10^-7 T m/A.

The equilibrium height h_eq is found by setting F(h_eq) = M_m2 * g.
This equilibrium must be stable (dF/dh < 0 at h_eq on the repulsive branch,
which is satisfied for like-pole axial repulsion).

Without m1, m2 placed in the galinstan would be dragged into the liquid
and the viscous/resistive drag would prevent spinning. The levitation gap
created by m1 allows m2 to spin freely. This is the dual-function role of m1.
