## 1. Vortex Magnetism: Polarity-Independent Z-Axis Lift

### Claim

"More 'vortex magnetism', right in front of your eyes! None of this is in physics textbooks.
It does not depend on whether the central magnet is North-up or South-up. That 'spinning vortex'
increases the gravitational potential energy of a central spinning magnet."

### Physical Significance

In classical magnetostatics, the force on a magnetic dipole m in an external field B is:
  F = ∇(m · B)

For a dipole with moment m = m_z ẑ:
  F_z = m_z · (∂B_z/∂z)

This force is polarity-dependent: reversing the dipole orientation (m_z → −m_z, i.e., North-up
to South-up) REVERSES the sign of F_z (attractive becomes repulsive and vice versa).

Therefore, polarity-independent lift would be inconsistent with the classical dipole-field
gradient force. If the experimental observation of polarity-independent z-axis force is confirmed,
it implies either:
(a) A higher-order multipole interaction (quadrupole or higher) that is symmetric under dipole
    reversal, OR
(b) A non-classical magnetomechanical coupling mechanism.

### Experimental Status

Claimed observed in laboratory; not yet independently verified. The researcher states this
effect is not in physics textbooks.

## 2. Simultaneous Spin and Z-Axis Rise Beyond Standard Induction Motor Behavior

### Claim

In a standard three-phase induction motor, the rotating field produces azimuthal torque only
— the rotor spins but does not translate along the rotation axis. In the described experiments,
the central magnet simultaneously:
(a) Spins about the z-axis (expected in classical induction motor behavior).
(b) Translates upward along the z-axis (NOT expected from a uniform rotating field with no
    gradient along z).

### Physical Significance

For pure translational z-axis motion to arise from a rotating field in the xy-plane, a coupling
mechanism between rotational and translational degrees of freedom is required. In classical EM,
this requires a specific spatial gradient of the field along z (∂B/∂z ≠ 0). The experimental
coil configuration (symmetric coils in the xy-plane) would nominally produce minimal ∂B_z/∂z
along the axis of symmetry.

The observed lift without an engineered axial field gradient — if confirmed — would be a
non-trivial departure from standard linear induction motor theory. The researcher attributes
this to the "vortex magnetism" mechanism.

## 3. Counter-Spin Reaction Force Topology (Partial Non-Classical Interpretation)

### Claim

"None of this is in physics textbooks."

The spin/counter-spin pattern (inner magnet spins clockwise + rises while outer body spins
counter-clockwise + is pressed down) is described as something not documented in standard
physics textbooks, despite invoking Newton's Third Law explicitly.

### Assessment

Newton's Third Law itself is classical. The non-trivial aspect is the specific topology:
* A freely spinning magnetic rotor inside a three-phase coil assembly simultaneously
  translating vertically while the outer assembly experiences downward force.
* This coupled rotational-translational reaction is not a standard textbook example, even
  though it follows from classical mechanics.

The BEYOND-Newtonian aspect (if genuine) would be the vortex mechanism producing the z-axis
force — NOT the angular momentum conservation, which is purely Newtonian.

* Multiple smaller objects accompanying the main body (counted as 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 companion

Anomaly 8-9: Not specified in text beyond Loeb's reference to an 8-anomaly compilation.

Drew's attribution: all anomalies are cited as consistent with 3I/ATLAS being an artificial
