## Three-Phase Rotating Magnetic Field — Mechanism

The three-phase current system (phases U, V, W separated by 120°) produces a spatially rotating
magnetic field vector B_rot(t) in the plane perpendicular to the axis:

  B_rot(t) = B_0 [cos(ωt) x̂ + sin(ωt) ŷ]

where ω = 2πf (f = applied frequency in Hz), x̂ and ŷ are orthogonal radial unit vectors in
the coil plane.

This rotating field induces a torque on the central permanent magnet (magnetic dipole moment m):

  τ = m × B_rot

The torque causes the magnet to follow the rotating field (synchronous or near-synchronous
rotation), and simultaneously a vertical (z-axis) force component is generated — the source
of the observed lift. The exact mechanism coupling rotational motion to vertical force in this
frictionless open-air geometry is the subject of the ongoing experimental program.

## Winding Topology and Field Cancellation: The UVW vs. UVW + U'V'W' Problem

### Single-set UUVVWW wiring (fails):

When six outer coils are wired as a single three-phase set repeating U, U, V, V, W, W:
* Each phase appears twice with opposite geometric placement.
* The opposing coil pairs (U opposite U, V opposite V, W opposite W) produce fields that
  exactly cancel at the geometric center.
* The central magnet sits in a field null: it experiences no net rotating field.
* Result: magnet does not spin; lift is destroyed.

### Dual-set UVW + U'V'W' wiring (succeeds):

When six outer coils are wired as two independent three-phase sets (UVW and U'V'W'):
* The three coils of each set are interspersed geometrically around the ring.
* In the geometric center: the fields from set UVW and set U'V'W' cancel (anti-clockwise field
  is zero at center).
* At radii beyond the center: the fields do not fully cancel; a residual anti-clockwise rotating
  field persists.
* The central magnet (located at center) is exposed only to the clockwise field from the lower
  three coils.
* Result: clockwise spin and z-axis rise are driven by the lower coils; the outer six coils do
  not impede this motion.
* Confirmation: compass needle placed at intermediate radius from center shows anti-clockwise
  rotation, confirming residual anti-clockwise field exists away from center.

## Spin and Counter-Spin Dynamics: Magnetic and Mechanical Coupling

### Newton's Third Law in Rotating Systems

Total angular momentum of system (magnet + coil structure + housing) is conserved in the
absence of external torques:

  L_total = L_magnet + L_body = constant

If the central magnet acquires clockwise angular momentum L_magnet (↑+z direction by convention),
the outer body must acquire equal and opposite angular momentum L_body (counter-clockwise, ↓−z).

This counter-clockwise body spin is observed experimentally (Edwin Fuhr sighting, 1974: UFO body
spins counter-clockwise while pressing downward on the ground, magnet equivalent spins clockwise
and rises).

This counter-clockwise body reaction was successfully reproduced in lab experiments, confirming
the Newtonian angular momentum conservation interpretation.

### The Problem: Counter-Spin Prevents Net Lift

If the counter-clockwise body spin is allowed to occur freely:
* The outer structure rotates in the direction opposite to the desired upward thrust direction.
* This rotation is associated with a downward force on the body (reaction to upward magnet
  motion).
* Net lift of the overall craft system is reduced or eliminated.

### Proposed Resolution: Lorentz-Force Liquid Metal Counter-Spin Replacement

To decouple the downward-pushing counter-rotating force from the outer body, Drew proposes:
* Counter-spinning liquid metal (galinstan: Ga-In-Sn eutectic alloy, liquid at room temperature)
  driven by 6-volt DC battery.
* The liquid metal is placed over another magnet below the assembly.
* The Lorentz force F = J × B (where J is current density in the liquid metal, B is the
  magnetic field of the lower magnet) drives an azimuthal (counter-clockwise) flow in the
  liquid metal.
* This azimuthal liquid metal flow carries counter-clockwise angular momentum without being
  mechanically connected to the outer solid body.
* Net effect: angular momentum conservation is satisfied by the liquid metal rather than by
  counter-rotation of the craft body, freeing the body from downward reactive force.
