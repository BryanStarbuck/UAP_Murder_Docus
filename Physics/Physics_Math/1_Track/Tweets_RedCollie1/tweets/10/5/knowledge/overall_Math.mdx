## Corbino Disc Electrodynamics

The Corbino disc is a conducting disc of inner radius r₁ and outer radius r₂. With an axial magnetic field B_z and a radial current density J_r (driven by voltage applied between center and rim):

    F_azimuthal = J_r × B_z    (Lorentz force density, vector form)

In cylindrical coordinates (r, φ, z):
  J = J_r ê_r      (radial current)
  B = B_z ê_z      (axial field)
  F = J × B = J_r B_z (ê_r × ê_z) = J_r B_z (-ê_φ)    [or +ê_φ depending on sign convention]

The azimuthal force produces rotation of the conducting fluid/disc. For liquid metal galinstan:
  Torque: τ = ∫∫ r (J_r B_z) dA = J_r B_z ∫∫ r dA

Total power P = τ × ω, where ω is the angular velocity of the spinning liquid metal.

Direction of spin: Depends on signs of J_r and B_z; Drew establishes counterclockwise (anticlockwise) for the liquid metal when the central permanent magnet drives the three-phase coils clockwise.

## Angular Momentum Conservation in Spin/Counter-Spin System

Let:
  L₁ = angular momentum of the central permanent magnet (clockwise, +Z direction by convention)
  L₂ = angular momentum of the liquid metal in Corbino disc (counterclockwise, -Z direction)

Design intent (from note_tweet 1992566706888495443):
  L₁ + L₂ = 0  ⟹  |L₁| = |L₂|

For liquid metal of mass m_2, effective radius R_2, spinning at angular frequency ω₂:

Balance condition:
  ½m₁R₁²ω₁ = ½m₂R₂²ω₂
  m₁R₁²ω₁ = m₂R₂²ω₂

## Gyroscopic Precession (Contextual Framework)

The gyroscopic behavior of the spinning permanent magnet under the rotating three-phase field relates to:

    dL/dt = τ

where τ is the applied torque from the external rotating field and L = Iω is the angular momentum of the spinning magnet.

Precession rate (standard gyroscope):
    Ω_precession = τ/L = τ/(Iω)

For a disc magnet of moment of inertia I = ½mR² spinning at ω:
    Ω_precession = τ/(½mR²ω)

As ω increases (faster spin), precession rate decreases and gyroscopic stability increases. Drew notes (1964324568568647915): "The rise of a spinning magnet through a 'helical' magnetic field may also be related to gyroscopic stability." This connects the Z-axis levitation force to the gyroscopic stiffness of the spinning magnet.
