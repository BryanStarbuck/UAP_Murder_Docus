## 1. Magnetohydrodynamics (MHD) of Liquid Metal Under Combined DC and Rotating Fields

### 1.1 Governing Equations

The incompressible, resistive MHD equations for a liquid metal (density rho,
kinematic viscosity nu, electrical conductivity sigma) in an external field:

Continuity:    div(v) = 0

Navier-Stokes with Lorentz body force:
  rho * (Dv/Dt) = -grad(p) + eta*grad^2(v) + J x B

Ohm's law in moving conductor:
  J = sigma * (E + v x B)

Faraday's law:
  dB/dt = curl(v x B) - (1/(mu_0*sigma)) * curl(curl(B))

where:
  v = velocity field of liquid metal (m/s)
  p = pressure (Pa)
  eta = dynamic viscosity (Pa.s)
  J = current density (A/m^2)
  B = total magnetic flux density (T) = B_applied + B_induced
  E = electric field (V/m)
  mu_0 = 4*pi*10^-7 H/m

Magnetic Reynolds number (characterizes field advection vs. diffusion):
  Rm = mu_0 * sigma * L * V
For liquid gallium: sigma ~ 3.7e6 S/m; for lab-scale L ~ 0.1 m, V ~ 0.1 m/s:
  Rm ~ 4*pi*10^-7 * 3.7e6 * 0.1 * 0.1 ~ 0.05 << 1
-> Low Rm regime: field advection by the fluid is negligible; B_applied dominates.
-> This simplifies to the inductionless MHD approximation:
   rho * (Dv/Dt) = -grad(p) + eta*grad^2(v) + sigma*(E + v x B) x B

### 1.2 Homopolar Motor: Lorentz Body Force Calculation

For azimuthal (phi-direction) spin in cylindrical coordinates (r, phi, z):
- B = B_z * z_hat (uniform axial field from disc magnet)
- J = J_r * r_hat (radial current from DC source)
- F = J x B = J_r * B_z * (r_hat x z_hat) = -J_r * B_z * phi_hat

The azimuthal body force per unit volume = -J_r * B_z (phi direction).
Sign determines CW vs. CCW: reversing J_r (current polarity) or B_z (magnet flip)
reverses rotation.

### 1.3 Three-Phase RMF Induction: Torque on Liquid Metal

The time-averaged azimuthal electromagnetic torque per unit volume in an induction
motor with a conducting cylinder (analogous to squirrel-cage rotor):
  T_em = (pi * sigma * B_0^2 * R^2 * s * omega) / 2
where:
  B_0 = peak rotating field amplitude (T)
  R = radius of conducting cylinder (m)
  s = slip = (omega - omega_r) / omega  (omega_r = angular velocity of rotor/fluid)
  omega = field angular frequency (rad/s)

At low slip (s << 1):
  T_em ~ (pi * sigma * B_0^2 * R^2 * omega * s) / 2  [linear in s]

When the DC-driven spin is OPPOSITE in direction to the RMF, the effective slip is:
  s_eff = (omega + omega_DC) / omega  > 1
This produces maximum electromagnetic braking/reversal torque, which is what is
observed experimentally when the DC-spinning gallium is reversed by the three-phase power.

## 2. Relativistic Origin of Magnetism: 4D to 3D Rotation

Source: YAML 1917826496196141183

Formal statement: In the rest frame of stationary positive charges in a wire, a moving
charge q with velocity v in the x-direction sees a Lorentz-contracted charge distribution.
The transformation of the electric field:
  E'_perp = gamma * (E_perp + v x B)
  B'_perp = gamma * (B_perp - v/c^2 x E)

For a wire carrying current I:
- In the lab frame: net charge = 0; B = mu_0*I / (2*pi*r) (azimuthal).
- In the frame of a moving test charge q at velocity v parallel to wire:
  the wire acquires a net charge density due to Lorentz contraction of moving electrons,
  creating an electric field E' that corresponds to the magnetic force observed in the
  lab frame.

The author's statement: "Certain right-handed features of the electron, not seen for
v = 0, rotate from time 4-D into space 3-D to create right-handed magnetism."

This is a qualitative description of the Lorentz boost mixing of the electromagnetic
4-tensor F_mu_nu:
  F_mu_nu = [[ 0,    -E_x/c, -E_y/c, -E_z/c ],
             [ E_x/c,  0,    -B_z,    B_y   ],
             [ E_y/c,  B_z,   0,     -B_x   ],
             [ E_z/c, -B_y,   B_x,    0     ]]
Under a Lorentz boost in the x-direction with velocity v:
  E'_x = E_x
  E'_y = gamma*(E_y - v*B_z)
  E'_z = gamma*(E_z + v*B_y)
  B'_x = B_x
  B'_y = gamma*(B_y + v/c^2 * E_z)
  B'_z = gamma*(B_z - v/c^2 * E_y)

The "rotation from 4-D to 3-D" is precisely this mixing of the time-like and
space-like components of the antisymmetric field tensor under the boost.

## 3. Gravitoelectromagnetism (GEM): Gravity Analog of Magnetic Force

Source: YAML 1917826496196141183

The author asks whether there is a "right-handed" feature to gravity for masses in
fast relative motion, analogous to the magnetic force for charges.

The GEM formalism (linearized GR in the weak-field, slow-motion limit):
  g = -grad(Phi_g) - dA_g/dt       (gravitoelectric field)
  B_g = curl(A_g)                   (gravitomagnetic field)
  Phi_g = -G*M/r  (gravitoelectric potential, Newtonian limit)
  A_g = -2*G*J / (c^2 * r^3) x r   (gravitomagnetic potential of rotating mass J)

Field equations (GEM Maxwell analogs):
  div(g) = -4*pi*G*rho
  div(B_g) = 0
  curl(g) = -dB_g/dt  [+ small corrections]
  curl(B_g) = (16*pi*G/c^2)*rho*v + (1/c^2)*dg/dt

Equation of motion for a test particle in GEM:
  a = g + v x (2*B_g)   [factor of 2 distinguishes gravity from EM, not 1]

Lense-Thirring precession of a gyroscope with spin S near a rotating mass:
  dS/dt = Omega_LT x S
  Omega_LT = G*(3(J.r_hat)*r_hat - J) / (2*c^2*r^3)   [Lense-Thirring angular velocity]

Geodetic precession (de Sitter) for an orbiting body:
  Omega_dS = (v x g) / (2*c^2) * [correction factors involving gamma]
  ~ (3*G*M / (2*r*c^2)) * (v x r_hat) for circular orbit

Known observational confirmations:
- Gravity Probe B (2011): measured Lense-Thirring = 37.2 +/- 7.2 mas/yr (theoretical: 39.2 mas/yr).
- Geodetic precession measured at 6601 mas/yr (vs. 6606 mas/yr predicted).

## 6. Hall Effect Accelerator Physics

Source: MDX 13_2018894777127936000_video, YAML 2018894910129352739

The device demonstrated: six wire coils in sequence, 24V DC, ball magnet projectile.
Mechanism (as a coilgun / Gauss gun):
- When the leading coil is energized, it creates a solenoid field pulling the ferromagnetic/
  permanent magnet projectile toward the coil center.
- Sequential switching (in time with the projectile position) maintains an accelerating
  force on the projectile throughout the barrel.
- Each stage imparts kinetic energy: delta_KE = (1/2) * mu_0 * n^2 * A * L * (I_1^2 - I_2^2)
  [energy released from inductance change as projectile enters/exits coil]
- At 24V: limited impulse demonstration; proof of concept.

Note: The author calls this a "Hall Effect accelerator," but the classic Hall effect
(Hall, 1879) describes a transverse voltage V_H = I*B / (n*q*t) in a current-carrying
conductor in a perpendicular B-field. The confusion may arise from the use of magnetic
fields in the accelerator, but the operative mechanism is inductive electromagnetic
acceleration, not the Hall effect per se.

## 8. Counterclockwise vs. Clockwise Rotation: Observational Physics

Source: MDX 13_1976970916849762306_video, 13_1985115126673133568_video

Field observation (witness account in video transcript):
"It's going the opposite way. It's going counterclockwise. It's not going clockwise.
It's going counterclockwise. Grass was moving one way and the machine was going the other way."

Physical interpretation by author: This is the signature of the counter-rotation
mechanism — two co-existing rotational motions in opposite senses, consistent with the
spin/counter-spin hypothesis. The grass motion (induced air flow from the outer spinning
element) is opposite to the craft's inner spin, consistent with angular momentum
conservation in a closed mechanical system: if no external torques are applied, the
total angular momentum is conserved, requiring inner and outer elements to counter-rotate.

Angular momentum conservation in a two-component system:
  L_total = I_inner * omega_inner + I_outer * omega_outer = const
  If L_total = 0 (equal and opposite angular momenta), then:
  I_inner * omega_inner = -I_outer * omega_outer
  omega_outer = -(I_inner / I_outer) * omega_inner

This is the simplest mechanical model consistent with the counter-rotation observation.
