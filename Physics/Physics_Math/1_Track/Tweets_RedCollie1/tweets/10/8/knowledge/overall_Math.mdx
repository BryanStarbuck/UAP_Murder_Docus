## 1. Proposed Spin-Levitation Equation

### Equation
w = h / a

### Variables
* w = angular velocity of the spinning magnetic disc or flywheel (units: rad/s)
* h = height of the disc above the ground (or equivalently the height-coordinate of the disc center; units: meters)
* a = horizontal cross-sectional area of the spinning magnetic structure (footprint area in the XY plane; units: m^2)

### Source
Tweet 1946538389651505543 (referenced tweet text): "I was shown the physics of UFO levitation in a dream several years ago. They showed me a page from a science textbook of the future. Several equations, the simplest being w = h/a where w is angular velocity, h is height and a is area. That equation describes the rise of a spinning magnet."

### Status
Speculative / claimed. Not derived from existing classical or quantum mechanics. Not yet experimentally verified in the source material. Presented as a constraint relating the three variables: for a disc of fixed physical dimensions (fixed a), increasing w requires increasing h (disc rises); alternatively, for a disc at fixed height, increasing w must decrease a (disc cross-section contracts). The equation implies a proportionality:

  w * a = h

i.e., the product of angular velocity and area equals the height, or equivalently:

  h = w * a = (angular velocity) * (cross-sectional area)

Dimensional consistency check:
  [h] = m
  [w] * [a] = (rad/s) * (m^2) = m^2/s

This does not dimensionally balance (m ≠ m^2/s), implying a proportionality constant with dimensions (s/m) is missing, or the equation is symbolic/qualitative rather than a strict dimensional equation. The source does not address dimensional analysis.

## 2. Lorentz Body Force — Standard Formulation

### Equation

### Variables
* f = body force density on conducting fluid (units: N/m^3)
* J = current density vector (units: A/m^2), directed radially outward from central electrode to peripheral electrodes in the XY plane
* B = applied static magnetic flux density vector (units: T), directed axially (along Z-axis) by the permanent magnet

### Application
In the liquid-metal galinstan disc, J and B produce an azimuthal force density that drives the vortex. The total torque on the fluid about the Z-axis is:
  tau = integral over volume [ r × (J × B) ] dV
where r is the position vector from the axis.

For uniform J_r (radial) and uniform B_z (axial) in a disc of inner radius r_in, outer radius r_out, thickness d:
  J × B = J_r * B_z * (r_hat × z_hat) = J_r * B_z * (-phi_hat)   [or +phi_hat depending on sign convention]

This gives a uniform azimuthal body force density of magnitude J_r * B_z, driving the fluid to rotate.

Total torque magnitude:
  tau = integral_r_in^r_out integral_0^2pi integral_0^d  r * (J_r * B_z) * r dr d_phi dz
      = 2*pi*d * J_r * B_z * integral_r_in^r_out r^2 dr
      = 2*pi*d * J_r * B_z * (r_out^3 - r_in^3) / 3

## 4. 3-Phase AC Motor — Phase Sequence and Coil Topology

### Phase Assignment
* 12 coils arranged in a ring.
* Phase assignment: U-V-W repeating 4 times around the ring: positions 1,4,7,10 = phase U; positions 2,5,8,11 = phase V; positions 3,6,9,12 = phase W.
* Within each phase group: coils wired in series (not parallel).

### Rotating Magnetic Field
For 3-phase sinusoidal currents:
  i_U(t) = I_max * cos(omega_t * t)
  i_V(t) = I_max * cos(omega_t * t - 2*pi/3)
  i_W(t) = I_max * cos(omega_t * t - 4*pi/3)

The superposition of the magnetic fields from the three spatially distributed phase windings produces a rotating magnetic field vector that rotates at the electrical angular frequency omega_t of the AC supply. For a 4-pole-pair arrangement (12 coils, 3 phases, 4 repetitions), the mechanical rotation speed of the field is:
  omega_mech = omega_t / p
where p = 4 is the number of pole pairs.

### Rotor Synchronous Speed
For co-rotating (N-S-N-S alternating) magnets: rotor locks to the r.m.f. at omega_mech.
For counter-rotating (N-N-N-N same pole) magnets: rotor runs at -omega_mech (against the field).
