## 1. Lorentz Force in Conducting Liquid Metal

### Setup
A cylindrical or disc-shaped container of liquid-metal galinstan (or mercury) is placed with a static permanent magnet providing an axial magnetic field B (along the Z-axis). DC current I is driven radially through the conducting liquid from a central electrode (cathode) outward to peripheral electrodes (anodes), or equivalently current density J flows radially outward in the XY plane.

### Force Law
The Lorentz body force density on the conducting liquid is:
  f = J × B

Where:
  J = radial current density vector (pointing outward in XY plane, from center to periphery)
  B = axial magnetic field vector (pointing along +Z or -Z, set by the permanent magnet polarity)

Result: f = J × B is azimuthal (tangential to circles centered on the Z-axis), creating a body torque that drives the liquid to rotate. The rotation direction (clockwise or counterclockwise as viewed from above) is determined by the cross product J × B:
  * J radially outward, B upward (+Z): f is counterclockwise (by right-hand rule)
  * J radially outward, B downward (-Z): f is clockwise
  * Reversing magnet polarity reverses vortex chirality. (Confirmed experimentally: 13_1967150217704484864_video)

### Homopolar Motor Analogy
The disc configuration with a central DC electrode and peripheral electrodes in a conducting liquid in an axial magnetic field is the liquid-metal equivalent of a homopolar motor. The solid-state homopolar motor (Faraday disk) drives current radially through a conducting disc in an axial field to produce rotational torque by the same F = IL × B (for current element IL in field B) mechanism.

## 2. Newton's Third Law — Back-Torque Test

### Classical Prediction
If a torque τ acts on the spinning liquid (or rotor), Newton's third law requires an equal and opposite torque -τ on the device frame (housing, electrodes, magnet support). Placing the device on a freely rotating "Lazy Susan" bearing should reveal this counter-torque as observable counter-rotation of the platform.

No measurable counter-rotation of the platform was observed when DC current drove the galinstan vortex. Drew notes this was expected from prior work on Lorentz force "brush" experiments.

### Experimental Result (3-Phase Magnetism Case)

The anomalous claim is that the counter-torques are not equal and opposite as Newton's third law requires, or that they interact in a way that produces net unidirectional force rather than zero net force on the system.

## 3. Angular Momentum Conservation — Spinning Disc / Boat Experiment

### Classical Demonstration
Video (13_2028588573575069696): A motor spinning a blade is placed on a floating boat with no friction. Initially, total angular momentum L_total = 0. When the blade direction is reversed (angular momentum of blade changes from +L_blade to -L_blade), the boat must acquire angular momentum +2*L_blade_change to conserve total angular momentum:
  L_total = L_blade + L_boat = constant = 0
  When blade flips: L_boat_new = -L_blade_new = -(-L_blade_old) = +L_blade_old

This is a standard demonstration of angular momentum conservation. Drew presents it to establish baseline understanding before discussing claimed violations.

## 4. Gyroscopic Instability as Proposed Lift Mechanism

### Classical Gyroscope
A spinning rigid body with angular momentum L = I*omega (moment of inertia tensor I times angular velocity vector omega) resists change in orientation. Applied torques cause precession rather than tipping, with precession angular velocity:
  Omega_precession = tau / (I * omega)
where tau is the applied torque magnitude.

### Drew's Extension (Speculative / Claimed)
When a magnetic disc (spinning with low height H and large area A in the horizontal plane) spins more rapidly, a "gyroscopic instability" develops that "pushes against the vacuum."

Proposed relation: w = h/a
Where:
  w = angular velocity (of the spinning magnetic disc or flywheel)
  h = height of the disc above the ground (or of the disc itself)
  a = area of the disc (footprint or cross-sectional area in the XY plane)

This is presented as a relation from a "future physics textbook" seen in a dream (tweet 1946538389651505543), not derived from classical mechanics. It predicts that as a disc of fixed height spins faster (w increases), the ratio h/a must increase — either h increases (disc rises) or a decreases (disc contracts in cross-section), or both.

Physical interpretation proposed: The increase in spin rate causes the gyroscopic effect to push against the vacuum (treated as having mechanical impedance), which is relieved by the disc moving upward (increasing h) and/or reducing its cross-section (decreasing a).

Tweet 1946538389651505543 (note_tweet): "It pushes against the vacuum, like a propeller pushes against air or water."

## 6. 3-Phase Rotating Magnetic Field Motor Physics

### Classical Operation
A rotating magnetic field (r.m.f.) produced by 3-phase AC current in spatially distributed coils induces currents in a conducting rotor (induction motor) or synchronously drives a permanent-magnet rotor (synchronous motor). The torque on the rotor is:
  T = (3/2) * P * (psi_d * i_q - psi_q * i_d)
in d-q (direct-quadrature) frame analysis, where psi_d, psi_q are d-axis and q-axis flux linkages and i_d, i_q are d-axis and q-axis currents.

### Drew's Implementation
* 12 coils arranged in a ring, each set of 4 coils (U, V, W) wired in series.
* Power sequence: U-V-W-U-V-W-U-V-W-U-V-W (4 pole pairs).
* Rotor: a magnetic "flywheel" placed slightly above the coil ring.
* Polarity effect:
  * N-S-N-S alternating polarity (8 magnets): rotor tracks the r.m.f. synchronously (co-rotation).
  * N-N-N-N all same polarity (8 magnets): rotor opposes the r.m.f. (counter-rotation).
* The low coil temperature ("coils don't get hot") is consistent with efficient electromagnetic coupling and low resistive heating at the operating point.
