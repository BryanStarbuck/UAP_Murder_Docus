Physical content:

- The superposition of three sinusoidally varying magnetic fields of equal amplitude and phase offset 2π/3 produces a net field of constant magnitude that rotates in the plane of the coil array at the drive frequency ω.
- Mathematically: B_net(t) = B_0 [cos(ωt) x̂ + cos(ωt - 2π/3) ŷ + cos(ωt - 4π/3) ẑ] projected onto the coil plane; the net rotational component produces a traveling wave in the azimuthal direction.
- This is the same principle as a three-phase induction motor's stator field, but applied here to drive rotation of a macroscopic flywheel or outer disk element.
- Drew notes that the magnets must "spin up against the vacuum, and not by friction with a rod," implying non-contact magnetic drive — i.e., the 3-phase field exerts azimuthal torque on magnets attached to or embedded in the rotor, without mechanical contact.
- The condition that magnets spin up "equally whether aligned N/S or S/N" implies the driving torque is independent of polarity orientation, which is consistent with a rotating field that induces eddy currents or aligns alternating poles without requiring a fixed polarity reference.

XY-plane dynamics:

- The outer flywheel rotates anticlockwise in the XY plane.

### 2. Central Inner Magnet and Corbino Disc Interaction

Tweet 2008461880466264140 (referenced_tweets[].text):
"Inside we have an electrically-charged 'Corbino disc'. When we add a N/S magnet below (or in the center), any liquid metal in that Corbino disc may spin..."

Physical content:
- The Corbino disc is a flat conducting disk with current flowing radially (from center to rim or rim to center).
- When an axial magnetic field B_z is applied perpendicular to the disk plane, the Lorentz force F = qv × B acts on the radial current carriers, generating an azimuthal (circumferential) force component.
- This is the Corbino effect: the Hall-force-driven azimuthal drift of charge carriers in a radial current geometry under an axial B field.
- In a solid conductor, this appears as Hall voltage. In a liquid metal (galinstan), this azimuthal force drives actual rotational flow of the liquid itself.
- The liquid metal galinstan (Ga-In-Sn eutectic, liquid at room temperature, electrical resistivity ~29 nΩ·m) is the conducting medium.
- The axial magnet providing B_z is described as "a N/S magnet below (or in the center)."
- The sense of rotation of the inner element (liquid metal + inner magnet) is opposite to the outer flywheel direction.

- The interaction between the central spinning magnet and the outer rotating field creates a net axial (Z-axis) electromagnetic effect — the formation of a helical or toroidal vortex field structure.

### 7. Magnetic Properties of Ball Bearing Materials

Tweet 2033809993309557020 (note_tweet.text):
"The problem I found was that we have to use a large-bore ball bearing that is NOT para-magnetic! Thus stainless steel works poorly, but I will test other bearings made from zirconia or silicon nitride soon."

Physical content:
- Paramagnetic materials (including many grades of stainless steel, particularly austenitic grades with residual ferromagnetism) are attracted to strong magnetic fields.
- A paramagnetic ball bearing race in the vicinity of the strong permanent magnets used in the device experiences magnetic attraction forces that interfere with the counter-spin mechanism — coupling the bearing to the magnet rotationally through magnetic drag rather than allowing clean mechanical counter-spin.
- Required property: the ball bearing must be diamagnetic or non-magnetic.
- Candidate materials tested/proposed:
  * Zirconia (ZrO2): ceramic, non-magnetic (diamagnetic with χ_m ~ -1.3 × 10^-5), extremely hard (Vickers hardness ~1200 HV), low density (~5.68 g/cm^3), stable in the presence of strong magnetic fields.
  * Silicon nitride (Si3N4): ceramic, non-magnetic, hardness ~1700 HV, density ~3.2 g/cm^3, used in precision hybrid ceramic bearings for high-speed/high-load applications.
- The selection criterion: χ_m (magnetic susceptibility) must be negligibly small (essentially zero relative permeability μ_r ≈ 1) to prevent magnetic coupling between the bearing and the rotor magnets.
