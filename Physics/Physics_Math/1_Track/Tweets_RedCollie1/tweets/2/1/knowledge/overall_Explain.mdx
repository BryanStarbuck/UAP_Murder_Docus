## Section: 1964864724551745953.yaml — Stage 1-2 Bridge: Homopolar Motor → Spin Without Counter-Reaction

### Beneficial Output
Angular momentum generated in spinning disc with no (or reduced) equal-opposite angular momentum generated in the source magnet. The output of this stage is a "free" angular momentum — one not paired with a counter-angular momentum that would cancel any downstream force.

### Physical Bridge
The homopolar motor converts electrical energy (from battery or 3-phase supply) into angular momentum of the disc via the curvy Lorentz force pathway. Normally, Newton's 3rd Law would require an equal reaction torque on the source magnet. Drew's experimental observation is that this reaction is absent or reduced ("Where are the torques interacting? This is not equal and opposite torques").

The beneficial consequence: if angular momentum can be stored in a spinning component (disc, magnet, conducting ring) without paying the Newton's 3rd Law "tax" of an equal counter-angular-momentum, then that angular momentum can subsequently be used for work output (in this case, conversion to linear upward force) without a pre-existing counter-force canceling the result.

### Lorentz Force Geometry as the Mechanism
Standard linear Lorentz force (wire between two poles): reaction is clearly localized and equal-opposite.
Curvy Lorentz force (homopolar — Lorentz force acts along a closed azimuthal path): the reaction pathway is distributed through the electromagnetic field (L_em = ε₀ ∫ r × (E × B) dV), not localized on the source magnet. Drew's claim is that in this distributed-field case, the reaction is stored in the field, not returned to the magnet, creating a mechanical angular momentum imbalance.

## Section: 1954539399120855193.yaml — Stage 3 Bridge: Angular Momentum → Z-Axis Force via Counter-Rotation and Precession

### Beneficial Output
Net upward force F_z on the central spinning magnet, as it rises through the counter-spinning outer ring. The output here is the full beneficial lift.

### Physical Bridge
This is the most critical and least-specified stage in Drew's framework. Two candidate mechanisms:

Mechanism A — Magnetic Pressure Gradient:
As the central magnet (spin L_c = I_c ω_c ẑ) rises through the ring, it moves through a position-dependent magnetic field from the ring. The gradient of the interaction energy:
    U(z) = −m_c · B_ring(z)
    F_z = −∂U/∂z = ∂(m_c · B_ring)/∂z

provides an axial force. If the ring is counter-spinning, the effective B_ring seen by the central magnet is time-varying and non-static, and the time-averaged F_z depends on ω_c, ω_ring, and the geometric coupling. For the central magnet to "rise" spontaneously, F_z must be positive (upward) throughout a range of z.

Mechanism B — Gyroscopic Precession Coupling:
The central spinning magnet has angular momentum L_c = I_c ω_c ẑ. Under an applied torque τ (from the ring's field), it precesses:
    τ = dL_c/dt = Ω_p × L_c

If the torque has an xy-component (from the ring's horizontal-plane rotating field), the precession Ω_p has a z-component, and the coupling between precession and the asymmetric magnetic environment produces a net force F_z on the precessing body. This is the gyromagnetic/gyroscopic lift mechanism.

- Lower rotating B field (CW) → XY plane dynamics → swirling wheat (physical trace)
- Upper structure precessing (CCW) → angular momentum differential
- Net result: sustained hover above ground → net upward F_z

Drew's interpretation: the hover is maintained by Stage 3 of his proposed bridge — the precessing upper structure converting XY angular momentum into the Z-axis force that maintains altitude against gravity.
