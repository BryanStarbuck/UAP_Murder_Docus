### Standard AC Lenz Magnetic Phenomenology
The standard Lenz effect in an AC magnetic field: the source coil generates a time-varying magnetic field B(t) = B₀ sin(ωt) ẑ (for axial orientation), which induces eddy currents in the conducting disc. The eddy current distribution J_eddy(r,φ,t) is azimuthally symmetric in the non-spinning case. The resulting secondary magnetic moment m_eddy = (1/2)∫r × J_eddy dV opposes the applied B(t) (Lenz's law). The axial force is:

    F_z = ∇(m_eddy · B) = m_eddy · (∂B_z/∂z) ẑ  (in axial geometry)

This force is downward (braking/repulsive, opposing approach of the source magnet).

### Observed Modification: Spinning Disc
When the disc is given angular velocity ω_disc about the z-axis:
- The motional EMF term σ(v × B) with v = ω_disc × r now contributes to J_eddy.
- The additional current component is:

    J_motional = σ(ω_disc r_hat × B_z ẑ) = σ ω_disc r B_z φ_hat

(azimuthally directed for axial B, consistent with the normal Lenz current, but the total eddy pattern is modified by the combination.)

- More critically, for off-axis or radially non-uniform B components (B_r ≠ 0 at disc edges), the motional EMF produces a radial eddy component that couples differently to the axial force balance.

Drew's observation: "There is much less downward Lenz force when the Alu disc is spinning." He treats this as a non-Newtonian outcome requiring verification on a weighing scale.
