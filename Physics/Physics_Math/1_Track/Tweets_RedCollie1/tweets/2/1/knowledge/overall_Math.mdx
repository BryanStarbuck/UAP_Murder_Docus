## Section: 1964864724551745953.yaml — Lorentz Force and Torque in Homopolar Motor

### Lorentz Force on Current Element
    dF = I (dl × B)

Terms:
- I: current (amperes) through conductor element
- dl: infinitesimal vector element of conductor path (meters), direction = current flow direction
- B: external magnetic field vector (Tesla) at the location of dl
- dF: infinitesimal Lorentz force (Newtons) on the current element

For a radial current element in the homopolar motor:
    dl = dr r̂    (radially outward)
    B = B_z ẑ    (axial magnetic field)
    dF = I dr (r̂ × B_z ẑ) = I dr B_z (r̂ × ẑ) = −I dr B_z φ̂

The force is in the azimuthal (φ) direction — purely tangential, producing torque.

### Torque on the Disc
    τ_z = ∫_a^b r · dF_φ = ∫_a^b r · (−I B_z dr) = −I B_z ∫_a^b r dr = −I B_z [r²/2]_a^b

    τ_z = −(I B_z / 2)(b² − a²)

where a = inner radius, b = outer radius of the disc.

Sign conventions:
- I > 0, B_z > 0: τ_z < 0 (clockwise torque when viewed from +z)
- Reverse I (current out-to-in): τ_z > 0 (counter-clockwise)
- Reverse B_z (flip magnet S/N): same reversal

This fully accounts for the observed spin-direction reversal with polarity or current reversal.

### Angular Momentum

where I_disc = (1/2) m r² is the moment of inertia of a thin disc of mass m and radius r, and ω is the achieved angular velocity.

The reaction torque should equal and oppose the disc torque. This would be carried via the electromagnetic field:
    g_em = ε₀ (E × B)      [electromagnetic momentum density, kg/(m²·s)]
    P_em = ε₀ ∫ (E × B) dV [total electromagnetic momentum]

The angular momentum of the field:
    L_em = ε₀ ∫ r × (E × B) dV

Drew's experimental claim: the mechanical reaction torque observed on the wire assembly is insufficient to account for the disc's angular momentum, suggesting that L_em is not being properly transferred back to the source, or that the Lorentz force geometry in the homopolar motor stores angular momentum in the field in a way that decouples it from mechanical reaction on the magnet.
