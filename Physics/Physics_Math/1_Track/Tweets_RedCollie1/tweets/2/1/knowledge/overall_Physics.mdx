### Physics Content
The standard AC Lenz effect: a time-varying magnetic flux Φ(t) threading a conducting disc of conductivity σ induces eddy currents J_eddy (A/m²) whose associated magnetic moment opposes the source flux by Faraday's law:

    ∮ E · dl = −dΦ_B/dt

The resulting force on the disc is repulsive/braking (Lenz's law, a direct consequence of energy conservation and the Lorentz force law). In the classical static-disc case, the induced eddy-current distribution is azimuthally symmetric about the axis of the applied field, and the net axial force F_z = −∂U_em/∂z is purely repulsive.

Drew's observation: when the aluminum disc is given rapid axial angular velocity ω (via a DC motor in the trial; proposed to use 3-phase power and perimeter magnets in a UAP-equivalent configuration), the downward component of the Lenz braking force is measurably reduced compared to the non-spinning disc under identical AC excitation conditions.

### Proposed Physical Mechanism — Spin-Modified Eddy Current Distribution
In the rotating frame of the spinning disc, the eddy current trajectories acquire a Coriolis-like deflection. The eddy current density J is governed by:

    J = σ(E + v × B)

where v = ω × r is the velocity field of the rotating conductor element at radial position r. The v × B term represents the motional EMF due to spinning. In the lab frame, the eddy current loops are no longer purely azimuthal — they acquire a radial component that modifies the spatial distribution of the induced magnetic dipole moment. This shifts the effective force balance between vertical (z-axis) and in-plane (xy) components.

Drew does not yet provide a quantitative formula for the force reduction, framing this as an experimental observation requiring further verification on a weighing scale (mass measurement).

### Configuration Description
- Conductor material: aluminum disc
- Excitation: alternating current (AC) applied to induce Lenz force
- Spin mechanism (trial): DC motor driving disc rotation
- Proposed full implementation: 3-phase AC power driving the disc + perimeter permanent magnets (as described for a UAP configuration)

### Relationship to Newton's Laws
Drew categorizes the reduced Lenz force as "non-Newtonian" in the sense that the combined electromagnetic-rotational system does not behave as predicted by straightforward application of classical electromagnetic induction to a stationary disc. He frames this as a potential "window in modern physics" enabling upward levitation without counter-force (see also overall_Beyond_Nutton.mdx).
