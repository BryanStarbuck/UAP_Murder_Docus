### Liquid-Metal Vortex Also Called "Corbino Disc" and "Homopolar Motor" — Terminology Clarification

Three names for the same physical device in Drew's literature:
1. **Liquid-metal vortex**: descriptive of the physical appearance — a circulating flow of liquid metal
2. **Corbino disc**: named after Orso Mario Corbino (Italian physicist, 1911), who analyzed the galvanomagnetic effect in a circular disc with radial current and axial magnetic field. The "Corbino effect" is the Hall-effect-induced azimuthal current in such a geometry.
3. **Homopolar motor**: general term for a motor driven by the direct (non-alternating) Lorentz force on current-carrying conductors in a static magnetic field. "Homopolar" refers to the fact that the magnetic polarity does not alternate — only DC is required.

All three terms describe the same fundamental device: a conducting medium (solid or liquid) carrying radial current in an axial magnetic field, driven azimuthally by the Lorentz force.
