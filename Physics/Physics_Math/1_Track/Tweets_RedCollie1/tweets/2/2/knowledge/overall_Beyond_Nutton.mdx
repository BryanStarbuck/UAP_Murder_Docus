- Φ(r) = redshift function (must be finite throughout for traversability)
- b(r) = shape function (b(r₀) = r₀ at the throat radius r₀)
- dΩ² = solid angle element

2. Flaring-out condition: b'(r₀) < 1 at the throat

   ∮ T_μν k^μ k^ν dτ < 0

The induced velocity field:

This creates a lift force (Kutta-Joukowski theorem) on the cylinder in a cross-flow:
L = ρ U Γ per unit length

where U is the cross-flow velocity and ρ is the fluid density. This is the Magnus effect — a classical physics result within Newtonian fluid mechanics.

**Beyond Newtonian extension:** The claim is that the same vortex mechanism operates in the quantum vacuum rather than a classical fluid. In the quantum vacuum analog:

This requires the quantum vacuum to behave as a compressible, vortex-supporting fluid — the "superfluid vacuum" hypothesis. In superfluid helium, quantum vortices (quantized circulation: Γ = n h/m for n = 1, 2, ...) are well-established. The superfluid vacuum theory (Volovik, Barceló, Liberati, Visser) extends this to the relativistic quantum vacuum, treating it as a medium with superfluid properties.

If the vacuum is a superfluid, quantum vortices in the vacuum would carry quantized momentum, and a spinning body could nucleate vacuum vortices, exchanging momentum with the vacuum medium. This is beyond Newton's Third Law in the sense that Newton assumed vacuum = empty (no reaction medium), whereas the superfluid vacuum provides a reaction medium for momentum exchange without mass ejection.
