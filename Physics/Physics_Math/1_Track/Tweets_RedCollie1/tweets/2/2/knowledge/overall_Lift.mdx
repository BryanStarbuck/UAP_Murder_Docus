### Primary Lift Mechanism: 3-Phase Spin of Wobbling Magnet Against Vacuum

The central lift claim in this batch: the 3-phase spin of a wobbling magnet against the surrounding vacuum causes it to be "pushed up" along a central pole, analogous to how a spinning top rises.

**Spinning Top Lift Analogy (Classical Physics Baseline):**

A spinning top rises (increases its tilt angle toward vertical, increasing its height above the table) due to gyroscopic precession in a gravitational field. The mechanism:
1. Gravity exerts a torque **τ** = **r** × m**g** on the tilted spin axis, perpendicular to both the spin axis and the vertical
2. This torque causes the spin axis to precess horizontally (rotate around vertical)
3. Dissipation (friction at the tip) causes the precession radius to decrease, i.e., the top's axis moves toward vertical
4. As the axis becomes more vertical, the top's center of mass rises

This rising is not anti-gravity; it is torque-induced reorientation. The top does not levitate — it rises while maintaining ground contact.

**Claim Extension to Vacuum-Coupled Magnet:**

Drew's claim extends this to a free magnet in vacuum: the "3-phase spin of a wobbling magnet against the vacuum" causes the magnet to be pushed up along a central pole without ground contact, without an external gravitational torque, and without a normal force from a substrate. If true, this would require the vacuum to provide both the torque (analogous to gravity × lever arm) and the restoring force (analogous to the normal force at the top's tip).

The vacuum torque on a spinning magnetic dipole — if it exists — would arise from the interaction of the rotating magnetic field with the quantum vacuum's electromagnetic fluctuations (zero-point field). In standard QED, the vacuum exerts radiation pressure symmetrically on a stationary dipole, but a rotating dipole radiates (magnetic dipole radiation), and the reaction to this radiation could in principle exert a recoil force. The direction of this recoil force is generally opposite to the direction of radiation emission, which for a precessing dipole is predominantly equatorial — not axial. So standard QED does not predict an axial (Z-direction) force from precessing magnetic dipole radiation.

The claim requires either:
(a) The vacuum has anisotropic electromagnetic properties not captured by standard QED
(b) The wobbling/precessing motion creates a preferential coupling in the Z-direction through a mechanism not in the standard model
(c) The spinning top analogy is exact and the vacuum plays the role of "gravity + ground" in a way that produces net upward force
