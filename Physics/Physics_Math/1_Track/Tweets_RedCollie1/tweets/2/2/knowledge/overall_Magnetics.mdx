### Homopolar Magnetic Configuration

The inner spin configuration is described as "homopolar." A homopolar magnet is a permanent magnet (typically a disc or cylinder) that, when rotated about its symmetry axis, presents a constant (non-rotating) magnetic field to any external observer — because the field has the same symmetry as the rotation axis. A disc magnet spinning about its cylindrical axis has:

**B**(r, z) = B_r(r,z) r̂ + B_z(r,z) ẑ  (cylindrical coordinates)

This field is azimuthally symmetric: ∂**B**/∂φ = 0. Therefore mechanical rotation of the magnet about its z-axis does not change the external field distribution. This is the distinguishing feature of homopolar vs. dipole configurations.

However, the claim is not that the homopolar field is static during spinning — rather, the homopolar magnet wobbles as it spins (it has both spin angular momentum and precession), so the field is not purely static from an external perspective. The precessing wobble introduces a time-varying component to **B**(t) even for a nominally homopolar magnet, creating the conditions for coupling to the vacuum.

**Homopolar generator connection:** A homopolar (Faraday disc) generator produces DC EMF when a conducting disc spins in a static magnetic field. The connection here is that a homopolar magnet spinning produces an EMF in the surrounding conductor or vacuum medium — a DC, axially-directed electromagnetic effect, distinct from the AC oscillating field of a rotating dipole. This DC component is proposed as the primary driver of the Z-axis propulsive force.

### 5-Phase Rotating Magnetic Field

A 5-phase rotating magnetic field uses five coils separated by 72° carrying currents phase-shifted by 72°:

I_k(t) = I₀ cos(ωt − 2πk/5),  k = 0, 1, 2, 3, 4

The resulting field is a more symmetric rotation than 3-phase, with the harmonic content:

**B**(t) = Σ_k I_k(t) · **B**_k(φ_k)

For n phases (n odd prime), the 5th harmonic content is eliminated (vs. 3-phase where the 3rd harmonic content is eliminated). A 5-phase system thus produces a purer, more sinusoidally-shaped rotating field with reduced torque ripple. The claim is this is used in the ET propulsion system, perhaps because the purer rotating field produces a more stable vortex structure in the vacuum.

- Secondary function: serve as gyroscopic stabilizer (large L_spin provides stability)

**Outer (3-phase, slow counter-spin):**
- Three or more electromagnets or permanent magnets arranged in a ring
- Energized in 3-phase sequence OR mechanically counter-spinning
- Function: absorb the Newton's Third Law reaction torque from the inner spin

Mathematical condition for torque cancellation without Z-force cancellation:

τ_outer = −τ_inner  (torque balance condition in XY-plane)
F_z,outer ≈ 0  (outer counter-spin produces negligible Z-force due to geometry)
F_z,inner ≠ 0  (inner spin produces net Z-force via proposed mechanism)

The geometric asymmetry (inner spin is a compact disc magnet, outer is a ring) is claimed to explain why the Z-forces don't cancel: the inner disc geometry concentrates the axial force, while the outer ring geometry disperses it into torque.
