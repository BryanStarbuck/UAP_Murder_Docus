**The claim (experimental, not theoretical):**
"The sideways-spinning motion of a homopolar motor does not seem to follow Newton's 3rd Law. There is a forward reaction, without any backward counter-reaction."

**Newton's 3rd Law statement:**
For every action force F₁₂ exerted by body 1 on body 2, there exists a reaction force F₂₁ = -F₁₂ exerted by body 2 on body 1, equal in magnitude and opposite in direction.

**Classical EM analysis of the homopolar motor:**
In standard electromagnetic theory, the force on a current-carrying disc in a magnetic field is fully accounted for by the Lorentz force on charge carriers. The equal and opposite reaction is transferred to the magnet (or to the external circuit) via the electromagnetic field itself — momentum is stored in the electromagnetic field (Poynting momentum: `g = ε₀(E × B)`). Classical EM does conserve total momentum when field momentum is included.

**Drew's anomaly:** He observes that the observable mechanical reaction to the forward tangential force is absent or unmeasurable. If true, either:
(a) The reaction is absorbed into the electromagnetic field and not mechanically transmitted to the magnet or frame (this is actually what standard EM predicts — "hidden momentum" in the magnet's current loops), or
(b) The reaction is genuinely absent — a violation of momentum conservation in the mechanical subsystem

**Beyond-Newtonian significance:** If interpretation (b) is correct, the homopolar motor could produce a net linear impulse from rotational electromagnetic action — a reactionless drive. Drew's proposal to redirect this into a helical (Z-axis) trajectory is the proposed UAP propulsion mechanism.

**Key reference for comparison:** The Feynman disk paradox and related EM momentum analyses show that electromagnetic field momentum is real and must be included. However, Drew's claim appears to be that in the specific homopolar geometry, even the total-system momentum (mechanical + field) may not be conserved — which would be a genuine violation of standard electrodynamics.
