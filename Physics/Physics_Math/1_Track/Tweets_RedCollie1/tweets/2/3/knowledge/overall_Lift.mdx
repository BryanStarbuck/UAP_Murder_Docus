### The Core Lift Equation

**Equation:**
```
w = h / a
```

**Z-axis interpretation:** `h` is explicitly height — the Z-axis displacement of the craft above its starting position. The equation states that height is the product of angular velocity and precessional area (`h = w × a`). For lift to occur and increase, one of two conditions must hold:
1. Angular velocity `w` increases while precessional area `a` is held constant → `h` increases proportionally
2. Angular velocity `w` increases faster than `a` decreases as the precession cone collapses → net `h` increase despite shrinking `a`

**The mechanism for lift generation:**
The precessing/spinning system transitions from flat horizontal spin (large `a`, `h = 0`) to axial spin (small `a → 0`, `h` large). This transition is the lift event. The physical driver of this transition is not identified in the equations, but the experimental program aims to achieve it with a magnetically-driven spinning top using 3-phase power.

**Slide 4 geometry (attached to tweet 1947293588943262133):**
- Upper center: flat horizontal spin, `h = 0`, large `a`
- Lower right: large `h`, `a → 0`
This is the qualitative trajectory of the lift mechanism.

### AC Lenz Levitation + Spin — Enhanced Lift

**Standard Lenz lift (known physics):**
A spinning conducting disc above an AC coil is repelled upward by induced eddy currents (Faraday/Lenz principle). This is well-documented electromagnetic induction lift — achievable, but limited to low mass-to-power ratios.

**Enhanced lift (non-classical claim):**
When the disc also spins rapidly about its own axis (rotational spin added to the translational levitation), an additional lift force appears. This force is "non-Newtonian" in Drew's terminology.

**Experimental evidence cited (YouTube video: youtu.be/QER9D2iXJ40):**
"AC Lenz levitation shows non-Newtonian forces, when its disc conductor is given rapid spin."

**Physical basis (partial derivation — see overall_Math.mdx for full derivation):**
The cross term F_cross = q(Ω_disc × r) × B introduces a `B_r ẑ` component proportional to the radial magnetic field gradient and the disc spin rate. This term lifts the disc with a force:
```
ΔF_z ∝ σ A t Ω_disc ∂B/∂r
```
where σ = disc conductivity, A = disc area, t = disc thickness, Ω_disc = disc spin angular velocity, ∂B/∂r = radial magnetic field gradient at the disc.

**Design implication:** To maximize this enhanced lift force, one wants:
- High disc spin rate Ω_disc
- Large radial magnetic field gradient ∂B/∂r
- High conductivity disc material (aluminum, copper)
- Large disc area A

### Lift Coupled to Forward Spin Momentum Addition

**Key claim for lift generation:**
```
F_z ∝ dL/dt
```

The upward force is not static — it requires continuous injection of angular momentum. A device that continuously adds forward spin momentum (via electromagnetic drive) will continuously generate a proportional upward force. This means:

1. The device is not a static levitator but a dynamic one — it requires ongoing power input to maintain altitude
2. The altitude is proportional to the rate of angular momentum addition, not to total angular momentum stored
3. Stopping the drive (cutting power) immediately removes the lift force

**Relationship to the `w = h/a` equation:**
If `dL/dt = I × dw/dt` (moment of inertia × angular acceleration), then the lift condition can be written:
```
F_z ∝ I × dw/dt
```
Combined with `h = w × a`, increasing `w` (positive `dw/dt`) both increases `h` directly and contributes to `F_z` through the coupling. This creates a positive feedback loop: increasing spin rate increases both altitude and the force driving further altitude increase — until the precession collapses to `a = 0`.

**Solution:** A counter-spinning Lorentz force (inner flywheel, Corbino or homopolar geometry) provides an upward force that cancels the downward counter-spin reaction.

**Net lift condition:**
```
F_lift_net = F_z(outer, upward) - F_z_counter(downward reaction) + F_z(Lorentz inner)
```
For `F_z(Lorentz inner) ≥ F_z_counter`: net lift is achieved.

Drew reports this as the current experimental target: demonstrating that the Lorentz inner flywheel cancels the downward counter-reaction while the outer flywheel generates the upward force.
