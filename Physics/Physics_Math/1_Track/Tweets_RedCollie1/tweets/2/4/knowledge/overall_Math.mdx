### Distance Scaling Laws for Magnetic Dipole Force

**Dipole field magnitude on axis at distance r:**

B_axis(r) = (μ_0 / 2π) · (m / r³)

Where:
- μ_0 = 4π × 10⁻⁷ T·m/A (permeability of free space)
- m = magnetic moment [A·m²]
- r = axial distance from dipole center

**Force between two axial dipoles (m₁, m₂) at distance r:**

F(r) = (3μ_0 / 2π) · (m₁ m₂ / r⁴)

Scaling: F ~ r^{-4}

Drew states this is "consistent with an r-squared dependence for dipole magnetism" — this refers to the field B scaling as r^{-3} (field squared ~ r^{-6}... but more likely he means force law observed in experiment which goes as r^{-4} for the dipole-dipole interaction, with r^{-2} perhaps being a loose empirical description of the short-range regime).

**Long-range mechanism (helical field):**

If the long-range levitating force follows F ~ r^{-n} with n < 4, the crossover distance r_c between dipole dominance and helical dominance:

At r = r_c: F_dipole(r_c) = F_helical(r_c)
(μ_0/2π)(m₁m₂/r_c⁴) = A_h / r_c^n
→ r_c = [(μ_0/2π)(m₁m₂) / A_h]^{1/(4-n)}

where A_h is the amplitude of the helical interaction. Since r_c is observable (the distance at which levitation transitions from attractive dipole to weightless), and m is measurable (30 g magnet), A_h can be estimated from experiment.

### Gyroscopic Anti-Gravity Model: r(ω) → 0

**Proposed functional form:** r decreases from large to zero as angular velocity ω increases.

Candidate functional form consistent with dimensional analysis:

r(ω) = r₀ · exp(-(ω/ω_c)²)

or power-law:

r(ω) = r₀ · (ω_c / ω)^α  for ω >> ω_c

**Anti-gravity condition:** r(ω) = 0 achieved in the limit ω → ∞. This can be interpreted as the gyroscope's effective gravitational coupling constant g_eff → 0:

g_eff(ω) = g · r(ω)/r₀ → 0

**Precession frequency (standard gyroscope):**

Ω_p = τ / L = (Mgd) / (Iω)

where:
- τ = Mgd = gravitational torque (M = gyroscope mass, g = 9.81 m/s², d = center-of-mass offset)
- L = Iω = angular momentum
- I = moment of inertia about spin axis

As ω → ∞: Ω_p → 0 (no precession). The gyroscope's symmetry axis remains fixed. This is the classical gyroscope behavior at high spin, not anti-gravity. Drew's claim goes beyond this to assert that at high ω, coupling to gravity itself decreases (g_eff → 0), which is a non-classical claim.

**Z-axis vs. XY-plane dynamics:**

Spin axis vertical (along z): angular momentum L = Iω ẑ, parallel to gravitational acceleration g = -g ẑ. Gravitational torque τ = r_cm × Mg → 0 when r_cm is along ẑ (no offset). System decouples from precession. This corresponds to the "low energy form" in the video.

Spin axis horizontal (along x or y): L = Iω x̂, gravitational torque τ = Mgd ŷ (for a pivot at origin, center of mass at z = d). Precession rate Ω_p = Mgd/(Iω) (large for small ω). This is the "high energy form" — the configuration that "pushes against the vacuum."

**Energy transition:**

E_high = (1/2)I_x ω_x² + (1/2)I_y ω_y² + Mgd (potential energy offset due to tilt)
E_low = (1/2)I_z ω_z²

Transition from high to low energy releases:

ΔE = E_high - E_low = Mgd + (1/2)(I_x - I_z)ω²

This released energy could potentially drive Z-axis motion if the transition is coupled to the z-coordinate.

### Lorentz Force — Azimuthal Torque from Radial Current in Axial Field

**Geometry:**

Current density J = J_r(ρ) r̂ (radial, in cylindrical coordinates)
Magnetic field B = B_z ẑ (axial, from permanent magnet below)

**Force per unit volume:**

f = J × B = J_r r̂ × B_z ẑ = J_r B_z (r̂ × ẑ) = -J_r B_z φ̂

The minus sign means the force is in the -φ̂ direction (clockwise) for J_r > 0 (outward current) and B_z > 0 (N pole up). Reversing B_z (magnet flip) → force in +φ̂ (counterclockwise).

**Total torque on conducting disk of radius R, thickness h:**

τ = ∫₀^R ∫₀^{2π} ∫₀^h ρ · |f_φ(ρ)| · ρ dρ dφ dz = 2πh ∫₀^R J_r(ρ) B_z ρ² dρ

For uniform J_r = I/(πR²h):

τ = 2πh · (I/(πR²h)) · B_z ∫₀^R ρ² dρ = 2I B_z R / 3

**Homopolar motor efficiency:**

For applied voltage V and current I:

Mechanical power P_mech = τ · ω = (2I B_z R/3) · ω
Electrical power input P_elec = V · I

Efficiency η = P_mech / P_elec = (2 B_z R ω) / (3V)

**Reversal of direction with magnet polarity:** When B_z → -B_z:

τ → -τ (torque reverses sign)

This is consistent with the experimental observation: CCW → CW (or CW → CCW) when magnet is flipped.

### Special Relativity as Trigonometric Rotation

**Drew's formulation:** sin(θ) = v/c defines the rotation angle between space and time.

**Derived quantities:**

cos(θ) = √(1 - sin²θ) = √(1 - v²/c²) = 1/γ

tan(θ) = sin(θ)/cos(θ) = (v/c) / (1/γ) = γv/c = p/(mc)

where p = γmv is the relativistic momentum and mc is the rest momentum.

**Lorentz factor:**

γ = 1/cos(θ)

Time dilation: Δt' = Δt · γ = Δt / cos(θ)

Length contraction: L' = L / γ = L · cos(θ)

**Lorentz transformation in angular form:**

t' = t/cos(θ) - x·sin(θ)/(c·cos(θ)) = γ(t - vx/c²)  ✓
x' = x/cos(θ) - ct·sin(θ)/cos(θ) = γ(x - vt)  ✓

(This is the standard Lorentz transformation, just rewritten with the angular parameterization.)

**de Broglie connection:**

de Broglie wavelength: λ = h/p = h/(mγv)

Using sin(θ) = v/c and tan(θ) = γv/c = p/mc:

λ = h/(mc·tan(θ)) = (h/mc) · cot(θ) = λ_C · cot(θ)

where λ_C = h/(mc) is the Compton wavelength.

**Right-handed spinning motion:** In 4D (x, y, z, t), a rotation in the (x, ct) plane by angle θ = arcsin(v/c) is a right-handed rotation (by convention of the right-hand rule in the Euclidean sense, after Wick rotation it → τ). The claim is that this rotation is inherently right-handed — only right-handed motion (chirally specific) manifests as relative velocity in 3D space.

**Möbius/closed loop topology:** The spinor representation of SO(3,1) uses SU(2) × SU(2) (or SL(2,C)). A spinor transforms as ψ → -ψ under a 2π rotation, requiring a 4π rotation to return to original state. The double cover structure creates the topology of a Möbius strip in the spinor bundle. Drew is connecting the sin(θ) = v/c angular formulation to this topological double-covering.

**Winding configuration:** U-V-W-U-V-W in series → 6 coils, each pair sharing a phase, at 60° spatial intervals.

Coil 0 (φ=0°): Phase U, current I_U(t) = I₀ cos(ωt)
Coil 1 (φ=60°): Phase V, current I_V(t) = I₀ cos(ωt - 2π/3)
Coil 2 (φ=120°): Phase W, current I_W(t) = I₀ cos(ωt - 4π/3)

**Net B field at center (from 6 coils with unit vectors along coil axes n̂_k):**

B(t) = Σ_{k=0}^{5} μ_k I_k(t) n̂_k

For balanced 3-phase, the superposition gives:

B(t) = B₀[cos(ωt) x̂ + sin(ωt) ŷ]

A uniformly rotating field at angular frequency ω. The amplitude B₀ depends on coil geometry and number of turns N.
