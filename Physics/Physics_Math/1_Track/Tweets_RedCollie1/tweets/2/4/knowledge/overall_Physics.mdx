### Two-Regime Magnetic Levitation — Physics Framework

**Regime 1: Classical dipole (r small)**

The dipole-dipole interaction energy between two axially aligned magnetic dipoles m₁ and m₂ separated by distance r:

U_dipole(r) = -(μ_0 / 4π) · (2m₁m₂ / r³)

Force: F = -dU/dr = -(μ_0 / 2π) · (3m₁m₂ / r⁴)

This r^{-4} force is what Drew observes at short range — the spinning magnet is held attractively at short distances with r^{-2} dependence for the field itself (r^{-4} for force), consistent with magnetic dipole physics.

**Regime 2: Helical field (r large)**

In the far field, the spinning magnetic dipole radiates (or produces near-field helical fields). For a rotating magnetic dipole, the power radiated (Larmor formula extension to magnetic dipole):

P = (μ_0 / 6πc³) · |m̈|²

However, the near-field helical component (not yet radiative) has longer range. The vector potential of a rotating magnetic dipole m(t) = m₀[cos(ωt) x̂ + sin(ωt) ŷ] in the near field (r << c/ω):

A(r, t) = (μ_0 / 4π) · (m × r̂) / r² [static part]
          + corrections due to rotation

The rotating part generates a field with spatial structure B ∝ r^{-2} in the equatorial plane, which is softer than the r^{-3} axial dipole field. This slower decay explains the longer-range levitation.

**Physical interpretation:** The helical winding of the magnetic field lines around the spin axis creates a magnetic "screw" structure. A test magnet approaching along the z-axis interacts not with the static dipole field (which averages to zero azimuthally) but with the helical field structure, experiencing a net force that can balance gravity at longer range than the dipole force would permit.

### Homopolar Motor Physics — Newton's 3rd Law Anomaly Claim

**Standard homopolar (Faraday) motor:**

A conducting disc of radius R, thickness d, electrical conductivity σ rotates in axial magnetic field B_z. DC current I flows radially (in or out through center vs. rim contacts).

Lorentz force on current element dl carrying current I in field B:

dF = I dl × B

For radial current element at radius ρ, length dρ, in axial field B_z:

dF = I dρ r̂ × B_z ẑ = I B_z dρ φ̂

Total azimuthal force: F_φ = I B_z R (integrated from 0 to R)

Torque on disc: τ = ∫₀^R ρ · I B_z dρ = (I B_z R²)/2

This is the forward (azimuthal) reaction. The backward counter-reaction on the magnet:

By Newton's 3rd Law, the disc exerts a torque on the magnet equal and opposite. However, in the homopolar motor, the source of B_z (the permanent magnet) is not mechanically connected to the rotating element in the same circuit. The current path (in-out through disc) and the magnetic source (axial permanent magnet) are mechanically separate. 

**The claimed anomaly:** "There is a forward reaction, without any backward counter-reaction." This is a disputed claim in homopolar motor physics. Standard electromagnetic theory predicts that the magnet does experience a reaction torque (by Maxwell stress tensor analysis). The claimed anomaly would require that the torque transfer through the magnetic field is incomplete or absent under certain configurations.

Drew frames this as a demonstration that the Lorentz force in the homopolar geometry does not produce equal reaction, and asks: "If we could just change the forward direction" — redirecting azimuthal Lorentz force to axial, the same asymmetry would produce net axial thrust without reaction, i.e., propulsion without exhaust.
