## Materials

### Source: Various — Galinstan Properties and Usage

**Galinstan (Ga-In-Sn eutectic alloy):**
- Composition by weight: Ga₆₈.₅In₂₁.₅Sn₁₀ (approximate eutectic)
- Melting point: -19°C (liquid at all room temperatures)
- Density: ~6.44 g/cm³ (6.44 times water)
- Electrical conductivity: ~3.46 × 10⁶ S/m (metallically conducting liquid)
- Vapor pressure: extremely low (non-volatile, unlike mercury)
- Toxicity: non-toxic (unlike mercury)

**Used in Drew's experiments as:** The conducting liquid medium in which Lorentz forces drive azimuthal motion (homopolar motor effect at liquid metal scale). Galinstan is chosen because it is conducting, liquid, dense (good inertia), non-toxic, and safe for laboratory use.

**Mercury (Hg):** Also mentioned as an alternative conducting liquid for the outer perimeter disc spin. Higher conductivity than galinstan but toxic. The 3I/ATLAS Lorentz-force spin laboratory analog uses both argon gas (ionized, as plasma) and mercury as conducting analogs for the gaseous coma.

- Substrate: steel (Fe + C, ferromagnetic)
