**Helical Magnetic Fields**

Helical magnetic field topology: field lines wind in a helix about a central axis (the spin
axis Z). In the astrophysical context, this geometry arises from the combination of a poloidal
B-field (in the RZ plane, i.e., the meridional plane) and a toroidal B-field (azimuthal, φ
direction). The resulting helical winding is parameterized by the pitch angle α:

tan α = Bφ / Bp

where Bφ is the toroidal component and Bp is the poloidal component. In MHD equilibrium
(force-free conditions), ∇ × B = αB where α is a scalar field (not to be confused with the
pitch angle above — convention dependent). Force-free helical fields are a standard result
in solar/stellar coronal physics.

Drew's lab demonstration: a Dremel-type rotary tool spins a rotor magnet in a "horizontal
spinning magnetic field." The rotor magnet is an axial dipole (north on one face, south/blue
on the other). The horizontal spinning field is in the XY plane. As the rotor spins, it
traces a helical path through the rotating external field — creating effective helical field
exposure. The observed result: "We see the magnet goes way up." (Transcribed from video
13_1919490490132791299_video.mdx and confirmed in video 13_1990168681251471361_video.mdx
which is an identical repeat of the same experiment.)

The astrophysical analogy: in proto-stellar accretion discs and active galactic nuclei, helical
B-fields drive bipolar jets (Blandford-Znajek mechanism for AGN; Blandford-Payne for discs).
The jet material is magnetically centrifugally accelerated along the helical field lines. Drew
posits the same mechanism produces Z-axis force on a spinning lab magnet.

**Homopolar Motor Magnetic Geometry**

The homopolar motor (Faraday disk): a conducting disc rotates in an axial magnetic field B_z.
The motional EMF drives a radial current J_r (from center to rim or rim to center depending
on polarity). The Lorentz force on this current is:

F = J × B

For radial current J_r r̂ and axial field B_z ẑ:
F = J_r B_z (r̂ × ẑ) = J_r B_z (−φ̂)

This is a tangential (azimuthal) force, driving the disc to rotate. This is the standard
homopolar motor force — entirely in the XY plane (azimuthal direction). There is no Z-axis
force component in the standard analysis.

**3-Phase Stator Implementation Details**

Six wire coils connected in 3-phase as U-V-W-U-V-W in series. This is an unusual series
connection: normally 3-phase coils are connected as independent phases or in delta/wye
configurations. The U-V-W-U-V-W series sequence means each phase pair (U₁ and U₂, V₁ and
V₂, W₁ and W₂) are in series within their phase, while the three phases are connected to
the 3-phase supply. This doubles the number of poles per phase relative to a standard
3-phase induction motor stator.

With 6 coils in this configuration, and a rotor with 8 magnets in N-S-N-S-N-S-N-S
alternating polarity around the periphery: this creates an 8-pole rotor interacting with
a rotating field having 6 coil positions — the slip-frequency dynamics are complex and
would produce harmonics and non-synchronous torque components.

**Rotating Field Direction**

"As UVW, UVW" — the phase sequence determines field rotation direction. Standard UVW forward
sequence produces counterclockwise rotation (as viewed from the driven end). The rotor
spins anti-clockwise. The inner disc magnet is driven clockwise (opposite). The magnetic
field from the outer 6-coil assembly has its rotating vector direction determined by the
phase sequence; reversing to UWV reverses rotation.

**N-Pole Rotor Magnets**

Four magnets, all north-pole facing upward, arranged around the periphery of the outer
rotor — this is a consequent-pole or unipolar configuration. Interaction with the rotating
stator field: each N-pole magnet is attracted to the S-pole regions of the rotating field
and repelled from N-pole regions. The net torque creates rotation. The axial force on each
N-pole magnet in the stator field depends on the local ∂B_z/∂z — the gradient of the axial
field component. If the stator field has a nonzero ∂B_z/∂z at the rotor magnet position,
there is a net vertical (Z-axis) force.
