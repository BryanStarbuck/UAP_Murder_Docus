**Magnetic Dipole Force in a Non-Uniform Field**

For magnetic dipole moment μ (units: A·m²) in external field B (units: T):

Potential energy: U = −μ · B = −|μ||B|cos θ

where θ is the angle between μ and B.

Force (gradient of potential energy):
F = ∇(μ · B)

Component form:
F_x = ∂(μ · B)/∂x = μ_x ∂B_x/∂x + μ_y ∂B_y/∂x + μ_z ∂B_z/∂x
F_y = μ_x ∂B_x/∂y + μ_y ∂B_y/∂y + μ_z ∂B_z/∂y
F_z = μ_x ∂B_x/∂z + μ_y ∂B_y/∂z + μ_z ∂B_z/∂z

For an axial dipole μ = μẑ (aligned with Z-axis, as in Drew's rotor magnet):
F_z = μ ∂B_z/∂z

For lift (F_z > 0, upward force), require ∂B_z/∂z > 0 at the rotor position.

Time-averaged force for a spinning rotor in a rotating field:
The rotor has angular velocity ω_rotor, field rotates at ω_field.
At synchronism (ω_rotor = ω_field), the dipole is locked to the field and the time-averaged
force is determined by the static field gradient at the equilibrium angle.
At non-synchronism, the time-averaged torque includes contributions at beat frequency
(ω_rotor − ω_field).

**Helical Field Geometry**

Helical magnetic field: combining axial (poloidal B_z) and azimuthal (toroidal B_φ) components
in cylindrical coordinates (r, φ, z):

B_helix = B_z(r,z) ẑ + B_φ(r,z) φ̂

Pitch angle: α(r,z) = arctan[B_φ(r,z) / B_z(r,z)]

Force-free helical field condition (Chandrasekhar-Kendall field): ∇ × B = λB (λ = scalar)
This gives: ∇²B + λ²B = 0 (Beltrami equation)

Solutions in cylindrical geometry use Bessel functions:
B_z = B₀ J₀(λr)
B_φ = B₀ J₁(λr)

where J₀ and J₁ are Bessel functions of the first kind. The pitch reversal (from positive
to negative α) occurs at the first zero of J₁, i.e., at λr ≈ 3.832.

**Three-Phase Rotating Field Mathematics**

Three coils at angles 0°, 120°, 240° in XY plane, carrying currents:
I_U(t) = I₀ cos(ωt)
I_V(t) = I₀ cos(ωt − 2π/3)
I_W(t) = I₀ cos(ωt − 4π/3)

Each coil produces a field proportional to its current along its axis. Let unit vectors
along each coil axis be:
n̂_U = x̂
n̂_V = cos(2π/3)x̂ + sin(2π/3)ŷ = −(1/2)x̂ + (√3/2)ŷ
n̂_W = cos(4π/3)x̂ + sin(4π/3)ŷ = −(1/2)x̂ − (√3/2)ŷ

Total field:
B_total(t) = B_max [I_U(t)n̂_U + I_V(t)n̂_V + I_W(t)n̂_W] / I₀

Expanding:
B_x(t) = B_max[cos(ωt) + cos(ωt−2π/3)(−1/2) + cos(ωt−4π/3)(−1/2)]
B_y(t) = B_max[cos(ωt−2π/3)(√3/2) + cos(ωt−4π/3)(−√3/2)]

After trigonometric reduction:
B_x(t) = (3/2)B_max cos(ωt)
B_y(t) = −(3/2)B_max sin(ωt)

Result: constant-magnitude rotating field:
B_total(t) = (3/2)B_max [cos(ωt) x̂ − sin(ωt) ŷ]

Magnitude: |B_total| = (3/2)B_max = constant
Rotation: clockwise (for UVW sequence as written, counterclockwise for UWV sequence)
Angular velocity: ω (same as current frequency)

**Angular Momentum Conservation in Counter-Rotating System**

For two coil sets A and B, each with moment of inertia I_A and I_B:
L_A = I_A ω_A
L_B = I_B ω_B

Newton's 3rd Law constraint: if flywheel exerts +τ on set A:
dL_A/dt = +τ → ω_A increases (clockwise)
dL_B/dt = −τ → ω_B decreases, reverses direction (counter-clockwise)

For steady state (constant angular velocities), net torques must be zero.
Total angular momentum: L_total = L_flywheel + L_A + L_B

If the craft is to have zero net angular momentum externally (not to spin the whole vehicle):
L_flywheel + L_A + L_B = 0
→ I_fw · ω_fw = −(I_A ω_A + I_B ω_B)

The slight misalignment in the drone implies L_A ≠ −L_B, and the craft does have some
residual angular momentum which may be what causes the observed slight wobble.

**MHD Equation of Motion**

ρ(∂v/∂t + (v·∇)v) = −∇p + J × B + η∇²v + ρg

where:
- ρ [kg/m³] = fluid mass density
- v [m/s] = fluid velocity field
- p [Pa] = pressure
- J [A/m²] = electric current density
- B [T] = magnetic flux density
- η [Pa·s] = dynamic viscosity
- g [m/s²] = gravitational acceleration

Generalized Ohm's law (for MHD with Hall effect neglected):
J = σ(E + v × B)

Induction equation:
∂B/∂t = ∇ × (v × B) − ∇ × [(1/μ₀σ)∇ × B]
       = ∇ × (v × B) + (1/μ₀σ)∇²B   [using ∇·B = 0]

Magnetic Reynolds number:
Rm = μ₀σvL

For galinstan (σ = 3.46×10⁶ S/m), lab scale v = 0.1 m/s, L = 0.05 m:
Rm = (4π×10⁻⁷)(3.46×10⁶)(0.1)(0.05) = 0.0217

Low Rm → resistive diffusion dominates over advection. Field does not convect with fluid.

**Homopolar Motor: EMF and Torque**

Motional EMF in a conducting disc of radius R rotating at angular velocity ω in axial field B_z:
EMF = ∫₀ᴿ (v × B)·dr = ∫₀ᴿ (ωr B_z) dr = (1/2)ωB_z R²

For current I flowing through the disc (DC circuit with resistance R_tot):
I = EMF / R_tot = ωB_z R² / (2R_tot)

Torque on the disc:
τ = ∫₀ᴿ r · (dF) = ∫₀ᴿ r · (I · B_z · dr) · (1/R)
  = I · B_z · R / 2

(for uniform current distribution)

Power dissipated: P = τ · ω = (I · B_z · R / 2) · ω = EMF · I [checks out]

Force on the current element in radial direction:
dF_φ = I · dr · B_z (azimuthal, tangential)

where v_r is radial velocity and B_r is radial field component.

**Gyroscope Equations and r → 0 Proposal**

Moment of inertia (axial symmetry, disc):
I = (1/2)mR² (solid disc)

Angular momentum: L = Iω = (1/2)mR²ω

Gravitational torque (for tilted gyroscope with center of mass at distance r from support):
τ = mger  (where e_r is the horizontal distance from support to CM)

Precession angular velocity:
Ω_prec = τ/L = (mgr) / ((1/2)mR²ω) = 2gr / (R²ω)

Drew's model: r decreases as ω increases (specific functional form not given, but the
hypothesis is r(ω) such that r → 0 as ω → ∞, e.g., r = r₀/ω or r = r₀e^(−αω)).

If r = r₀/ω: Ω_prec = 2g(r₀/ω) / (R²ω) = 2gr₀/(R²ω²) → 0 as ω → ∞
This means the gyroscope's effective gravitational coupling goes as 1/ω².

If r = 0 exactly: τ = 0, Ω_prec = 0. The gyroscope has no precession and no gravitational
coupling — it behaves identically whether in a gravitational field or not (for small
perturbations). This is the "UFO-like anti-gravity effect."

Physical realization: a sphere (r = 0 by symmetry for uniform density) — hence the
relevance to the "Buga orb-sphere" which is a spherical UAP. For a perfect sphere spinning
about any axis, there is no preferred gravitational torque arm, and the gyroscopic motion
would be Ω_prec = 0 regardless of spin rate.
