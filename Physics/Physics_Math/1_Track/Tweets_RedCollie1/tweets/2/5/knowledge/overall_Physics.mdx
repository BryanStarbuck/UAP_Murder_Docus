**Force on a Magnetic Dipole in a Non-Uniform Field (Standard Physics)**

For a magnetic dipole μ in an external field B, the potential energy is:
U = −μ · B

The force is:
F = ∇(μ · B)

For a dipole aligned with field direction (μ = μẑ, B = B(r)):
F_z = μ ∂B_z/∂z

For a spinning rotor magnet in a helical field, the effective force is the time-averaged
gradient force, including both axial and transverse components:
⟨F_z⟩ = μ_eff · ⟨∂B_z/∂z⟩

The key: if the helical field has nonzero ⟨∂B_z/∂z⟩ at the rotor position (which it does,
because the helical winding creates a pitch-dependent axial gradient), then ⟨F_z⟩ ≠ 0
even when the drive field is nominally horizontal.

**Lorentz Force in a Rotating Conductor — MHD Physics**

The Lorentz force on a charged particle: F = q(E + v × B)
For a conducting fluid element with current density J = σ(E + v × B):
Force density (body force per unit volume): f = J × B

In the MHD framework, the full equation of motion for the conducting fluid:
ρ(Dv/Dt) = −∇p + J × B + η∇²v + ρg

where:
- ρ = fluid density
- p = pressure
- J = current density
- B = magnetic field
- η = dynamic viscosity
- g = gravitational acceleration

The induction equation (governing B-field evolution in the fluid):
∂B/∂t = ∇ × (v × B) + (1/μ₀σ)∇²B

The magnetic Reynolds number: Rm = μ₀σvL, where v is characteristic velocity and L is
characteristic length scale. For high Rm, the field is "frozen in" to the fluid. For low
Rm, diffusion dominates. Liquid metals have intermediate Rm for lab-scale experiments.

For galinstan: σ ≈ 3.5×10⁶ S/m, typical v ~ 0.1 m/s, L ~ 0.05 m:
Rm = (4π×10⁻⁷)(3.5×10⁶)(0.1)(0.05) ≈ 0.022

Low Rm: field not frozen in, diffusion important. The 3-phase rotating field can penetrate
and drive the fluid despite the low Rm.

The gyroscope in classical mechanics: angular momentum L = Iω (I = moment of inertia,
ω = angular velocity vector). For a gyroscope, the torque equation:
τ = dL/dt = Ω × L

where Ω is the precession angular velocity. For a gyroscope under gravity with L along the
spin axis and torque τ = Mg × r (gravitational torque at center of mass offset r from
support):
Precession rate: Ω_prec = Mgr / (Iω)

Note that as ω → ∞, Ω_prec → 0 and the gyroscope resists precession (gyroscopic stiffness).
