The magnetic analog of gyroscopic precession may have additional force components that are absent in the purely mechanical case.

Specifically, a spinning magnetic dipole in a non-uniform magnetic field experiences:
  F = ∇(m · B)  [standard]

If the spinning motion causes the effective m to acquire a vertical component through precession, and if ∇B has a vertical component (from the 3-phase coil geometry), then a net upward force F_z = m_z · (∂B_z/∂z) is possible. This is within standard Newtonian mechanics for a magnetic dipole but not for a purely mechanical gyroscope — it is the magnetism that provides the extra force component, not a violation of Newton's laws per se.
