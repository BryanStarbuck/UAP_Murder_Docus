## Section: 1919593582488650172.yaml — Control Experiment: Spinning Magnetic Levitation (Hamdi Ucar 2021)

**Context:** Drew conducted a control experiment to test the "spinning magnetic levitation" effect discovered by Hamdi Ucar in 2021. The experiment used a Dremel rotary tool to spin a permanent magnet at 5000 rpm.

**Key finding: permanent magnets are unsuitable for studying this phenomenon.**
The problem is paramagnetic attraction: when a spinning magnet is brought near a stationary magnet, the tight paramagnetic attractive force at short distances overwhelms any levitation effect. The spinning magnet does not levitate — it attracts (sticks) to the stationary magnet with increased force due to reduced average separation oscillations at high spin rates.

**From video transcription (13_1919592136674250752_video.mdx):**
- North Pole oriented vertically, spinning at 5000 rpm: stationary magnet continues to attract to spinning magnet — no levitation, same dipole-dipole attraction.
- Magnet reoriented horizontal (North Pole perpendicular to the spin axis), spinning at 5000 rpm: the stationary magnet spins quite rapidly (induced co-rotation), but the two magnets still attract — they do not float apart.
- Conclusion: "The idea that we can study this phenomenon using permanent magnets is incorrect."

**Required apparatus:** 3-phase copper-wire-coil electromagnets. These produce a rotating magnetic field without the permanent magnet's intrinsic dipole attraction problem. Additionally, a stability "pole" is required for spin stability of the rotor.

**Physical distinction:** Permanent magnets have static dipole fields with no phase control. 3-phase electromagnets produce a field that rotates in the XY-plane with controllable phase, frequency, and amplitude independently of any mechanical rotation, allowing clean separation of the rotating-field effect from static dipole-dipole interactions.

## Section: 1930622600667136104.yaml — Three Types of Magnetic Force in UFO Propulsion

Three distinct magnetic force types are enumerated:

**Force 1: Upward force of spinning magnetic flywheel**
The spinning magnetic flywheel (disc-magnet at high angular velocity) generates an upward Z-axis force. This can be captured using a silicone bead (the bead levitates in the field above the spinning magnet, demonstrating an upward force region). Mechanism: the combination of rapid rotation of the magnetic dipole and the geometry of the surrounding 3-phase field produces a net upward force on the magnet itself through a mechanism that is not simple static dipole repulsion.

**Force 2: Reverse spin of 3-phase wire coils**
The 3-phase wire coils that drive the flywheel experience a counter-rotation reaction by Newton's Third Law. This is purely an angular momentum conservation effect in the XY-plane. The coils spin in the direction opposite to the flywheel's spin. This force is not directly useful for lift but is a structural requirement: the coil assembly must be free to counter-rotate or be constrained against counter-rotation by bearings/frame.

**Force 3: Downward reaction producing full craft lift**
A third force acts on the overall vehicle. Drew describes it as "some kind of downward reaction to an upward spinning magnet, which may not affect the wire coils, but which lifts a complete UFO off the ground." This is the least well-understood force. It may represent a coupling between the spinning magnet's rising motion and the vehicle body that bypasses the wire coil assembly entirely — possibly through the magnetic field permeating the vehicle structure.

## Section: 1964917258494599207.yaml — Lorentz Force Magnetism: Anomalous Angular Momentum Non-Conservation

**Experiment 1 (video 13_1964912194107457536_video.mdx — angular momentum conservation baseline):**
A motor spinning a blade on a floating boat (frictionless). When blade reversal occurs, the boat counter-rotates to conserve angular momentum. Total L = 0 throughout. Standard result confirming conservation of angular momentum.

**Experiment 2 (video 13_1964912403545804801_video.mdx — Lorentz force anomaly):**
A large magnet (high inertia) and small wires (low inertia) in a circuit. When DC current is added, the wires rotate one direction and the disc rotates the other direction. The anomaly: the disc (much greater mass) rotates at approximately the same angular speed as the lightweight wires — opposite torques are not proportional to inertias as expected from Newton's 3rd Law (τ = Iα → for equal and opposite τ, lighter object should have higher angular acceleration).

Video transcript: "the wires are not enough mass to start the disc rotating in the opposite direction, yet the disc starts rotating in the opposite direction. Where are the torques interacting? This is not equal in opposite torques."

This is presented as evidence that the Lorentz force is not constrained by Newton's 3rd Law in the usual sense for rotational systems.

**Experiment 3 (video 13_1964913091323502592_video.mdx — mercury vortex, poor transcription):**
Liquid mercury in aluminum bowl spins when DC current is applied. No counter-spin reaction. This is the Faraday homopolar disk motor analog but using liquid metal. The absence of counter-rotation is anomalous for a system obeying standard Newton's 3rd Law for torques.

**Drew's physical mechanism for the anomaly:**

The reactive force (Newton's 3rd Law pair) would therefore point in a direction perpendicular to both the rotation plane AND the radial direction — i.e., the axial (Z) direction. This reaction force is therefore non-rotational and effectively "disappears" from any measurement of rotational momentum. Result: the rotational Lorentz interaction appears to violate Newton's 3rd Law for angular momentum because the reactive force escapes into the axial direction.

**Proposed extension to 3D helix:**
Apply 3-phase power to create a helical magnetic field (rotating field + axial field component simultaneously). The Lorentz force, which normally produces 2D planar rotation, would follow a 3D helix. The Z-axis component of this helical Lorentz force would produce net upward thrust — lift without a Newton's 3rd Law reaction in the rotational/translational sense.
