## Section: 1919593582488650172.yaml — 3-Phase Rotating Field Mathematics

**3-phase magnetic field components:**
Let the three coils be oriented 120° apart in the XY-plane, each driven by a phase-shifted sinusoidal current:

  I₁(t) = I₀ cos(ωt)
  I₂(t) = I₀ cos(ωt - 2π/3)
  I₃(t) = I₀ cos(ωt - 4π/3)

The resulting magnetic field at the center of the coil assembly rotates in the XY-plane:
  B_x(t) = B₀ cos(ωt)
  B_y(t) = B₀ cos(ωt - 2π/3) · sin(120°) = B₀ sin(ωt)

Combined:
  **B(t) = B₀[cos(ωt) x̂ + sin(ωt) ŷ]**

This is a field of constant magnitude B₀ rotating at angular frequency ω = 2πf. Key properties:
- |B(t)| = B₀ = constant (no magnitude oscillation, unlike single-phase)
- Phase velocity: ω rad/s
- For f = 10 Hz: ω = 62.8 rad/s → rotates 10 full circles per second
- For f = 30 Hz: ω = 188.5 rad/s → rotates 30 full circles per second

**Why 3-phase eliminates paramagnetic attraction:** The net static field (DC component) of a balanced 3-phase system is:
  ⟨B_x⟩_t = B₀⟨cos(ωt)⟩_t = 0
  ⟨B_y⟩_t = B₀⟨sin(ωt)⟩_t = 0

Zero time-averaged field → zero net static dipole force on a ferromagnetic rotor → no paramagnetic lock-in.

**Synchronous motor condition:**
The rotor (disc-magnet with dipole m) locks to the rotating field when:
  ω_rotor = ω_field = 2πf

Locking condition (torque balance):
  τ = m × B → τ_max = |m||B₀| (maximum torque at 90° phase lag)
  At synchronous operation: |τ_synchronous| = |m||B₀|sin(δ)
where δ is the torque angle (phase lag between rotor dipole and field). For stable operation: 0 < δ < 90°.

## Section: 1958799775534653808.yaml — Force/Energy Balance in Rising Magnet System

**Gravitational potential energy of rising magnet:**
  ΔE_grav = M_magnet · g · Δh

where Δh = height rise of magnet from initial position to top of pole (measured experimentally when 3-phase field drives it upward at 10 Hz).

**Scale reading (total system weight):**
  F_scale = (M_magnet + M_coils + M_frame) · g = M_total · g

Observed: F_scale does not change when magnet rises. This means the weight of the magnet on the scale is balanced by a force transmitted through the pole. The pole exerts an upward force F_pole on the magnet (supporting it against gravity AND providing the net upward impulse), and by reaction, the magnet pulls the pole (and attached coil assembly) downward with force F_pole. Net scale reading remains M_total · g.

The center of mass of the system:
  z_cm = (M_magnet · z_magnet + M_coils · z_coils) / M_total

For the scale reading to be unchanged, the center of mass must not have changed:
  Δz_cm = 0

This requires: M_magnet · Δz_magnet = -M_coils · Δz_coils
If Δz_magnet = +Δh (magnet rises Δh), then Δz_coils = -(M_magnet/M_coils)·Δh (coil assembly sinks by a small amount). The coil assembly sinks fractionally because M_coils >> M_magnet. The magnet rises Δh, and the system center of mass stays fixed.

**Piton mechanism energy equation:**
Add large coil C_large at elevation z₀ < z_coils, with attractive coupling F_attract to spinning magnet M_spin.

For the piton mechanism to produce net lift of the entire system:
  Work done by 3-phase + DC power sources > ΔE_grav(entire system)

The coupling energy between M_spin and C_large:
  U_coupling = -F_attract · (z_magnet - z_coil_bottom)  [attractive → negative potential]

As z_magnet increases (magnet rises), the coupling energy between M_spin and C_large changes, providing a force on C_large:
  F_on_C_large = -∂U_coupling/∂z_C_large = F_attract  (upward on C_large as M_spin rises above it)

The net upward force on C_large (and attached system):
  F_net_lift = F_attract - (M_system - M_magnet) · g

Lift condition: F_attract > (M_system - M_magnet) · g

This requires the attractive force between the spinning magnet and the large coil to exceed the weight of the entire system minus the magnet — a demanding requirement, but physically achievable with strong electromagnets.

## Section: 1964917258494599207.yaml — Angular Momentum and Lorentz Force Mathematics

**Conservation of angular momentum (standard system):**
  dL_total/dt = τ_external = 0  (no external torques)
  L_total = L_wheel + L_boat = constant = 0  [starting from rest]

For the wheel-boat system: if wheel gains ΔL_wheel = +L₁ (spin one way), boat acquires ΔL_boat = -L₁.

**Lorentz force standard derivation:**
For a charge q moving with velocity v in magnetic field B:
  F_Lorentz = qv × B

For a radial current density J_r in a field B_z ẑ:
  J_r = J_r r̂
  F = J_r × B_z ẑ = J_r · B_z (r̂ × ẑ) = J_r · B_z (-φ̂)

or

  F = J_r · B_z φ̂  (sign depending on current direction)

This is azimuthal (tangential) force → rotation in XY-plane. The associated torque on the current-carrying element:
  τ_wire = r × F = r · J_r · B_z ẑ  (axial torque → drives rotation)

By Newton's 3rd Law, the torque reaction on the B-field source (the magnet):
  τ_magnet = -τ_wire = -r · J_r · B_z ẑ  (opposite axial torque)

For equal and opposite torques with different moments of inertia:
  τ_wire = I_wire · α_wire  →  α_wire = τ/I_wire  (large angular acceleration, small I)
  τ_magnet = I_magnet · α_magnet  →  α_magnet = τ/I_magnet  (small angular acceleration, large I)

Prediction: α_wire >> α_magnet (wires spin much faster than magnet). 
Observation: both spin at approximately equal angular speeds — contradicts prediction.

**Drew's resolution — spin-orbit deflection of reactive force:**
The Lorentz force on the charge carrier involves an underlying process where the electron spin (ℏ/2) causes the radial electric force to deflect by 90°:
  F_radial → F_tangential (by electron spin coupling)
  [This is the standard Hall effect / Lorentz deflection mechanism]

For the reactive force, Drew argues the same 90° deflection applies:
  F_reactive_tangential → F_reactive_axial  (reactive force deflected out of XY-plane into Z-axis)

In tensor notation, if the action force is:
  F_action = q(v_r r̂ × B_z ẑ) = qv_r B_z φ̂

Then the reactive force is not:
  F_reaction = -qv_r B_z φ̂  (which would be equal and opposite tangential)

but rather:
  F_reaction ≈ F_z ẑ  (axial component, due to second application of spin-coupling deflection)

The angular momentum carried away:
  ΔL_lost = ∫ F_z · r_⊥ dt  (not captured as rotational angular momentum, exits as axial linear momentum × moment arm — but since it's purely axial, it contributes to linear momentum in Z, not angular momentum)

Net result for rotating system: angular momentum is not conserved because it is continuously converted to axial linear momentum via the spin-deflection of the reactive Lorentz force.

**Helical field extension:**
Standard 3-phase rotating field (XY-plane only):
  B_transverse(t) = B₀[cos(ωt) x̂ + sin(ωt) ŷ]

Add a Z-component with time-varying phase:
  B_z(t) = B_z · cos(ωt + φ_z)   [or as a separate helical component]

Combined helical field:
  B(t) = B₀[cos(ωt) x̂ + sin(ωt) ŷ] + B_z cos(ωt + φ_z) ẑ

The Lorentz force on a current loop in this helical field:
  F_total = ∮ I(dl × B)

For a horizontal current loop (current in XY-plane), in a helical field:
  F_horizontal = I · (dl × B_transverse)  →  azimuthal torque (as before)
  F_vertical = I · (dl × B_z ẑ component)  →  radial force in XY-plane

But for a helical current path (current flowing both azimuthally and axially):
  dF = I · [dl_φ φ̂ + dl_z ẑ] × [B_transverse + B_z ẑ]

The cross terms:
  dl_φ φ̂ × B_z ẑ = B_z dl_φ (φ̂ × ẑ) = B_z dl_φ (-r̂)  [radial force]
  dl_z ẑ × B_transverse = ẑ × B₀[cos(ωt) x̂ + sin(ωt) ŷ] = B₀ dl_z [cos(ωt)(ẑ×x̂) + sin(ωt)(ẑ×ŷ)]
                        = B₀ dl_z [cos(ωt) ŷ - sin(ωt) x̂]  [tangential force with Z-phase]

If the non-Newtonian reactive mechanism deflects the tangential force to axial:
  F_reaction_Z ≈ B₀ dl_z [cos(ωt) component in Z]

This gives a net time-averaged upward force component:
  ⟨F_Z⟩ = B₀ · I · ⟨dl_z · cos²(ωt)⟩ = B₀ · I · (dl_z/2)  [for appropriate geometry]

This is the proposed mathematical basis for helical Lorentz lift.

## Section: 2019233077084713230.yaml — Magnetostatic Torque Levitation (Ucar 2021) Mechanism

**Reference: Hamdi Ucar (2021) — levitation via magnetostatic torque.**

The magnetostatic torque on a magnetic dipole m in an external field B:
  τ = m × B

For a spinning dipole (rotor) with angular velocity ω_r ẑ, in a static external field B_ext:
  The precession of m about B_ext generates a time-varying τ

Ucar's finding: a spinning permanent magnet (rotating at ω_r) in the presence of a magnetostatic field configuration (not a rotating field — a static field) produces levitation. This is distinct from a conventional synchronous motor because the driving field is static, not rotating. The levitation arises from the gyroscopic coupling between the spinning magnet's angular momentum and the field-induced torque:

  Ω_precession = τ / L = (m × B_ext) / (I · ω_r)

When Ω_precession has a vertical component, the spinning magnet's center of mass experiences a net upward force — the precessing dipole couples to the gradient of B to produce a force:
  F_gradient = ∇(m · B)  [force on a dipole in a field gradient]

For a precessing m in a static non-uniform B_ext, ∇(m · B) has a non-zero time-averaged Z-component if the field has a vertical gradient and the precession is in a plane that is not horizontal.

Grok's interpretation confirmed by Drew: "3-phase system with a central spinning element, akin to Ucar's 2021 levitation via magnetostatic torque — potentially depicting stable lift through synchronized rotation."

**Current experimental implementation:**
Inner assembly: 3 coils in 3-phase configuration → rotating field B_inner(t)
Outer assembly: 6 coils in 3-phase configuration → rotating field B_outer(t)

If B_inner rotates at +ω and B_outer at -ω (counter-rotating fields):
  Net angular momentum of field: L_field = L_inner + L_outer → cancels if |L_inner| = |L_outer|

But the physical disc-magnets driven by each field carry angular momentum of opposite signs:
  L_rotor1 = +I₁ω₁ẑ
  L_rotor2 = -I₂ω₂ẑ

The interaction between counter-rotating magnetic fields and their respective rotors produces a combined force field with potential symmetry properties that may result in a net vertical force component — particularly through the ∇(m₁·B₂ + m₂·B₁) cross-coupling between the two rotor-field systems.

## Section: 1962126576889545030.yaml — Pixel-Scale Object Size Estimation

**Methodology for astronomical object size from CCD data:**
Let θ_pixel = angular size of one pixel (known from telescope optics)
Let d = distance to object (known or estimated)
Let N_bright = number of pixels spanned by bright central region

Then object angular diameter:
  θ_object = N_bright · θ_pixel

Physical diameter:
  D = d · θ_object = d · N_bright · θ_pixel   [for D << d, small angle]

Applied to 3I/ATLAS:
  d_CO₂_coma = known (from spectroscopic analysis)
  N_pixels_bright = measured (from slide comparison)
  Result: D = 20-40 km

This is a direct measurement, not resolution-limited (bright circle diameter in pixels exceeds the point-spread-function size of a point source). Consistent with Avi Loeb's estimate.
