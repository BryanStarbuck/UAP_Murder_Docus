## Section: 1919593582488650172.yaml — Paramagnetic Attraction Problem in Spinning Magnet Experiments

**Physical problem:** At short distances between a spinning permanent magnet and a stationary magnet, the paramagnetic (static dipole-dipole) attractive force dominates. The force between two magnetic dipoles scales as:

  F_dipole ∝ (μ₀/4π) · (m₁ · m₂) / r³   [for aligned dipoles at separation r]

At small r, this force becomes very large (cubic in r). When one magnet spins, the instantaneous dipole orientation varies, but at 5000 rpm (≈ 83 Hz), the field seen by the stationary magnet averages over the rotation cycle on the timescale of mechanical response. For neodymium magnets at millimeter-range separations, the average attractive force completely overwhelms any spin-induced levitation effect. The result is that the spinning magnet adheres to the stationary magnet with no observable levitation.

**Physical conclusion:** Permanent magnet-to-permanent magnet configurations are not useful for studying the Hamdi Ucar (2021) spinning levitation phenomenon. The experiment requires electromagnet stator coils with controlled phase to produce a field that rotates without carrying a permanent magnetic dipole of its own.

**Required phase control:** 3-phase AC electromagnets create a field vector that rotates in the XY-plane as:
  B_x(t) = B₀ cos(ωt)
  B_y(t) = B₀ cos(ωt - 2π/3)
  [third phase implicitly completes the 3-phase rotation]

This produces a rotating field of constant magnitude |B| = B₀√(3/2) (for 3-phase) with no net static dipole — eliminating the paramagnetic attraction problem.

**Stability requirement:** A "pole" is required for spin stability. This is a central axial shaft or constraint that prevents the rotor from precessing or tumbling, maintaining the spin axis along the Z-direction despite the gyroscopic and magnetic torques acting on the rotor.

## Section: 1940176113923498063.yaml — Gyroscope / Spin-Gravity Coupling

**Gyroscope physics baseline:**
A gyroscope spinning about its symmetry axis ẑ with angular momentum L = Iω·ẑ experiences, in a gravitational field g = -gẑ, a gravitational torque τ_g = r × (Mg) where r is the position of the center of mass relative to the pivot. For a standard gyroscope inclined from vertical, this produces precession:
  Ω_precession = Mgr / (Iω)   [precession rate]

As ω → ∞, Ω_precession → 0 (gyroscope becomes rigid against gravity).

**Drew's claim for spinning magnet:** A gyroscope "rises against gravity with increased SPIN." This is not standard gyroscope physics (a standard gyroscope does not rise; it precesses). Drew appears to claim that the spinning magnet, when driven by a 3-phase field and constrained by a pole, rises in the Z-direction as spin rate increases — going beyond gyroscopic precession into a regime where the magnetic-gravitational coupling produces actual vertical displacement. The experimental observation at 10 Hz (magnet rises to top of pole) supports this claim for the magnet itself, though the overall system weight does not change (see tweet 1958799775534653808).

**Proposed experimental implementation:** Disc-magnet or magnetic flywheel + 3-phase or 5-phase AC electromagnets → synchronous rotation at arbitrary frequency. The synchronous motor condition requires:
  ω_rotor = ω_field (synchronous rotation)
where ω_field = 2πf with f = AC supply frequency (controlled variable, range tested: 10-30 Hz).

## Section: 1964917258494599207.yaml — Non-Newtonian Lorentz Force Physics

**Angular momentum conservation (standard):**
For a closed system: dL_total/dt = 0 (no external torques).
In a system of motor + rotor on a frictionless boat (video 1):
  L_blade + L_boat = 0 always
  ΔL_blade = -ΔL_boat (conservation exactly satisfied)

**Lorentz force motor anomaly (video 2):**
Large magnet (moment of inertia I_large) + small wires (moment of inertia I_small << I_large) in circuit with DC current. When current flows:
  F_Lorentz = qv × B (on charge carriers in wire)
  Reaction (Newton's 3rd): Force on B-field source (the magnet) = -F_Lorentz on wires

Expected by Newton's 3rd Law: τ_magnet = -τ_wire
Since I_large >> I_small: α_magnet = τ_magnet/I_large << α_wire = τ_wire/I_small

Observed: both objects spin at approximately equal angular speeds in opposite directions — the mass asymmetry does NOT produce the expected speed asymmetry. This means the effective torque ratio does not equal the inertia ratio, implying an additional torque source or that the reactive torque is not equal and opposite to the action torque.

Drew's physical interpretation:
The Lorentz force F = qv × B acts on a charge moving radially (v_r) in field B_z:
  F_Lorentz = q·v_r × B_z = q·v_r·B_z·φ̂  (tangential, in XY-plane)
This is the force that produces rotation. Its Newton's 3rd Law reaction should be:
  F_reaction = -q·v_r·B_z·φ̂  (equal and opposite tangential force on the magnet)

But Drew argues the Lorentz force arises because of electron spin — the radial electrical force undergoes a 90-degree deflection due to spin-orbit coupling at the quantum level. The reactive force would then point in a direction that is 90 degrees from the tangential force, i.e., in the Z-direction:
  F_reaction ≈ F_z·ẑ  (axial, not tangential)

If the reaction is axial (F_z) rather than tangential (-F_φ), then:
- There is no tangential reactive torque on the magnet from the current interaction
- The apparent "torque" on the magnet comes from a different mechanism (possibly magnetic pressure balance)
- The axial reaction force F_z is undetectable in a typical rotation experiment (it just presses up or down on bearings)

**Consequence for Newton's 3rd Law:** The rotational interaction appears to violate Newton's 3rd Law because the reactive force is not rotational — it is axial. The angular momentum of the rotational system is not conserved because angular momentum leaks into the axial direction as linear momentum.

**Mercury vortex (video 3):** Liquid mercury (conducting fluid) in an aluminum bowl (conductor) with DC radial current applied in the presence of external B field:
  J_r × B_z = F_φ (azimuthal force → mercury spins)
Expected counter-spin reaction on the external circuit: not observed. Confirms: Lorentz force interaction in this geometry produces azimuthal motion without observable azimuthal reaction.

**Proposed 3D extension:**
3-phase power → helical B field: B(t) = B₀[cos(ωt)x̂ + cos(ωt - 2π/3)ŷ + B_z·ẑ]
The resulting Lorentz force on azimuthally flowing current follows a helical path. The non-Newtonian reaction (axial rather than tangential) combined with the helical geometry means the axial component of the Lorentz force has no equal/opposite reaction in the translational Z-direction. Net result: upward thrust without Newtonian reaction force downward.
