### The Core Claim

Eric Laithwaite (Imperial College London, 1974 Royal Institution Christmas Lectures) explicitly stated that Newton's Laws of Motion require extension:

> "So however distasteful it is, we now have to say that object has got something more than a mass... if we ever choose to spin it, it has another property, all of its own, which corresponds to the inductance of a coil. **This is the updating of Newton's Laws of Motion.** But I'm not saying that Newton's Laws of Motion are wrong. I'm really pointing out they are restricted to motion in straight lines, and to motion where there is no rate of change of acceleration."

**Formal statement:**

Newton's 2nd law:
```
F = dp/dt = m · a   [valid for translational motion only]
```

Laithwaite's extension (by analogy with electromagnetic inductance):

When a body rotates (spins), a new term appears:
```
F_total = m · a + F_gyro
```
where F_gyro is the gyroscopic reaction term — proportional to the rate of change of angular momentum direction, not magnitude.

For precession:
```
F_gyro = dL/dt|_{direction change only} = Ω_p × L = (τ/L) × L = τ  (τ as applied)
```
This appears circular, but Laithwaite's point is that the **gyroscopic term produces accelerations in directions orthogonal to the applied force** — something Newton's F = ma in a single direction cannot capture.

### Specific Anomalous Observation: Gyroscope Appearing Lighter

**Setup:** Gyroscope (8 lb, ~3.6 kg) spinning on a spring-mounted stand with measurable load cell. Under precession:

* Expected load (Newton): mg = 3.6 × 9.81 = 35.3 N
* Observed load: **less than 35.3 N** (not quantified precisely in lecture but clearly visible in spring reading)

> "I can make it rise. And as I do so, that spring says it weighs less."

**Conventional explanation (which Laithwaite contests):**
The precessing gyroscope redirects some of the gravitational force into centripetal force maintaining the precession orbit. The vertical support force is:
```
N = mg - m Ω_p² R_prec
```
where R_prec is the precession orbit radius. This accounts for some weight reduction without violating Newton.

**Laithwaite's beyond-Newton claim:**
The weight reduction exceeds what can be accounted for by the standard centripetal correction. The gyroscope acts as if part of its gravitational mass is "cancelled" by the spin — which standard mechanics does not allow.

**The no-centrifugal-force observation:**

An 8-pound spinning flywheel extended far beyond the bench edge:
```
Expected centrifugal force at distance R from support = m Ω_p² R
```
Observation: **zero detected centrifugal force**. The gyroscope does not topple, does not press outward, despite being massively out of balance.

> "Gyroscopes do not exhibit a new force. They show a **lack** of a force where there should have been one. That's why it was so hard to see, a lack of centrifugal."

Beyond-Newton content: centrifugal force is a real inertial effect in the rotating reference frame (pseudo-force) or equivalently, an actual reaction in the lab frame requiring centripetal support. Its apparent absence in a spinning gyroscope under precession is not explained by standard classical mechanics without invoking subtle cancellations that Laithwaite argues cannot fully account for the effect at scale.

When rotation is introduced, the full mechanical equation requires additional terms. Laithwaite frames this as the "updating of Newton's Laws" — not falsification, but **domain restriction with extension**.

**Formal analogy:**
```
Ohm: V = IR    (DC)          → Newton: F = ma           (translation only)
```

```
F_gyro = d/dt(Ω × L) = (dΩ/dt × L) + (Ω × dL/dt)
```

where Ω is the precession angular velocity and L is angular momentum. This term is **not zero** for rotating bodies and represents the beyond-Newton contribution.
