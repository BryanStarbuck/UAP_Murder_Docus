## Section: 13_1955044355808563200_video.mdx — Energy in Rotating Systems; Flywheel Energy Storage

**Laithwaite (1974) on gyroscopic energy:**

Kinetic energy of a spinning flywheel:
```
E_spin = (1/2) I ω²
```

For the 15 lb (≈ 6.8 kg) flywheel, radius ~0.15 m, at 5,000 rpm = 524 rad/s:
```
I ≈ (1/2)(6.8)(0.15)² = 0.077 kg·m²
E_spin ≈ (1/2)(0.077)(524)² ≈ 10,570 J ≈ 10.6 kJ
```

Laithwaite's demonstration: dropped onto rubber mat, this energy caused the flywheel to bounce to "half the height of the theatre" (estimated several meters). At height h:
```
E = mgh → h = E/(mg) = 10,570 / (6.8 × 9.81) ≈ 158 m
```
This is consistent with Laithwaite's claim of "about half the height of the theatre... there are large amounts of energy at stake."

**Swiss gyrobus energy storage:**
Laithwaite cited Swiss flywheel buses capable of:
* Driving a bus for half a day on stored rotational energy
* Regenerative braking: energy returned to flywheel on descent, effectively "pouring petrol back into the tank"
* This is a mature application of flywheel energy storage (no anomalous energy claims here — standard kinetic energy storage)

## Section: 1925730264941994181.yaml — Homopolar Generator and Alternator as UFO Energy References

Drew's note tweet:
> "What is an alternator? What is a homopolar generator?"

**Alternator (standard):**
An AC generator producing sinusoidal voltage via rotating magnetic flux linkage:
```
V(t) = N · dΦ/dt = N · B · A · ω · sin(ωt)
```
Maximum EMF: V_max = NBAω

**Homopolar generator (Faraday disk):**
A conducting disc rotating in an axial magnetic field generates a radial EMF:
```
EMF = (1/2) ω B r²
```
where ω = disc angular velocity, B = axial flux density, r = disc radius.

Key properties of the homopolar generator relevant to Drew's work:
* Produces **DC** output (no commutation needed)
* Very large current, very low voltage output for a given size
* The disc and axial magnetic field are the same geometry as Drew's spinning-magnet levitation device
* Homopolar generators have been proposed (e.g., in ORION project descriptions) as components of pulsed power systems for propulsion

Drew raises this in context of the Buga orb-sphere tests (Jaime and Fernando, Mexico City), suggesting the orb may operate on homopolar or alternator principles.
