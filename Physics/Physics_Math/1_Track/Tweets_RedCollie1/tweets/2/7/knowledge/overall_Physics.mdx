## Section: 13_1955044355808563200_video.mdx — Gyroscope Physics (Laithwaite 1974 Royal Institution)

### Precession

Applied torque τ on a spinning gyroscope does not produce angular acceleration in the direction of the torque. Instead:

```
τ = dL/dt
```

where **L** = angular momentum vector = I·ω·n̂ (I = moment of inertia, ω = spin angular velocity, n̂ = spin axis unit vector).

For a gyroscope with spin axis along x, torque applied about y:
```
τ_y = dL_x/dt → precession in xy plane
```

The angular velocity of precession Ω_p:
```
Ω_p = τ / L = τ / (I·ω)
```

Key experimental result (Laithwaite):
* Tripling the hanging mass M triples the precession angular velocity Ω_p
* This is consistent with τ = Mgr (torque from gravity at radius r) giving:
```
Ω_p = Mgr / (Iω)
```

### Nutation

When torque is applied rapidly (impulsively), the gyroscope does not immediately precess at constant Ω_p. Instead:
* Initial impulse produces **nutation** — a rapid wobbling superimposed on precession
* Nutation angle θ_n is determined by the impulse magnitude and the gyroscope's inertial tensor
* Nutation frequency ω_n = I_s·ω_s / I_t (spin inertia × spin speed / transverse inertia)

Laithwaite demonstrated:
> "You see it going up and down as it goes around. That process is called nutation. It is the result of an acceleration as well as a pure precession."

### Apparent Weight Reduction Under Precession

Experimental observation:
* An 8-pound gyroscope spinning on a spring-mounted stand with spring measuring downward force
* When the gyroscope is set into precession (by allowing it to torque against gravity), the spring reading decreases
* Laithwaite: "I can make it rise. And as I do so, that spring says it weighs less."

The weight reduction cannot be explained by standard mechanics: the full gravitational force (Mg) still acts on the gyroscope. Yet the measured force on the support is less than Mg.

Physical interpretation (as presented):
* The gyroscope's precession converts gravitational potential energy into angular momentum change at the bearing
* The **normal force from the support is partially replaced by the reaction to precession** rather than by simple compression of the spring
* This is not perpetual motion — energy accounting requires including kinetic energy of precession

### Key Gyroscope Scaling Results

* 15-16 pound gyroscope mounted from ceiling (no gimbal rings, only ceiling mount): maintains horizontal axis under gravity while precessing, orbits the ceiling mount point in a conical pendulum pattern
* Observation: "It is going to maintain its centre of mass on a horizontal plane, even though as a conical pendulum it appears to have raised"
* The orbit tightens as the gyroscope slows (decreasing L → increasing Ω_p for same gravity torque)
* At very slow spin speed, the gyroscope still maintains its axis orientation
