### 1. The Core Non-Classical Claim: Z-Axis Force from XY-Plane Rotation

The entire levitation thesis constitutes a departure from Newtonian mechanics. In Newtonian mechanics:
- Forces are applied externally (Newton's 3rd law: reaction mass required)
- A spinning disc in a rotating electromagnetic field experiences electromagnetic torque (tangential) and back-torque (through Lenz's law / induction), but no gravitational force
- No combination of electromagnetic fields and mechanical rotation produces a net gravitational force in classical mechanics or in general relativistic corrections to it

The claim that spin + 3-phase → Z-axis lift is explicitly non-Newtonian. There is no derivation from Maxwell's equations, Newton's laws, or general relativity that produces this result. This places the claim in the category of phenomena requiring extension of known physics.

Possible theoretical frameworks where this could be accommodated:
1. **Torsion-based theories of gravity** (Einstein-Cartan theory, Hehl-von der Heyde-Kerlick-Nester formalism): angular momentum density (spin density) couples to the spacetime torsion tensor T^μ_{νρ}, which modifies the geodesic equation and can produce forces without reaction mass in spinning matter
2. **Scalar-vector-tensor gravity extensions**: additional scalar or vector gravitational fields that couple to rotating electromagnetic fields
3. **Woodward-Mach effect**: transient mass fluctuations in accelerating matter (Woodward's Mach effect, based on the Abraham-Lorentz force and Mach's principle) — a spinning disc in a time-varying electromagnetic field has transient mass fluctuations that could produce net force
4. **Helical photon momentum**: circularly polarized photons carry angular momentum ħ per photon; a spinning magnetic system radiates circularly polarized electromagnetic radiation, which carries momentum — but this radiation reaction force is far too small at classical field strengths
