### 4. Homopolar / Faraday Disc — XY-Plane Magnetic Interaction

From 3_2006484721119932418.txt (image description of Faraday homopolar schematic):

- Annular conducting disc, inner radius R₁, outer radius R₂
- Axial magnetic field B_z (along Z-axis) — provided by permanent magnet
- Radial current I_in (applied from center) flows to I_out at rim
- Lorentz force on radial current carriers: F = I(dl × B)
  - dl is radial (r-hat direction)
  - B is axial (z-hat direction)
  - F = I dr (r̂ × ẑ) = I dr (-φ̂) → tangential force in -φ direction

Net effect: the Lorentz force on the radial current produces azimuthal (tangential) torque, spinning the disc about the Z-axis. This is pure electromagnetic torque without mechanical coupling — the defining property of the homopolar (unipolar) motor.

### 5. Corbino Disc — Variant Geometry

> "Slide 1 shows a crop picture from 2012 near Avebury, which illustrates the physics of a 'Corbino disc' (slide 2 from textbooks on Earth). Movie 3 shows a real (liquid) Corbino disc with spin."

The Corbino disc differs from the Faraday disc in that:
- The conducting medium is an annular disc (not a full disc), or implemented in liquid metal
- Current paths are radial only (not azimuthal brush-driven)
- The Lorentz torque is purely radial-cross-axial → azimuthal, same as Faraday disc
- Implementation in liquid metal allows the entire conducting medium to spin (no mechanical bearings needed for the conducting element itself)
