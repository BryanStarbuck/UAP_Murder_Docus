### Lorentz Force Counter-Spin — Key Magnetic Asymmetry

**Observation of angular momentum non-conservation under Lorentz drive:**
When DC current drives a disc magnet to spin via Lorentz force (F = IL × B, where I is current, L is conductor length, B is external magnetic field), and the supply wires are free to rotate:
- Disc magnet mass M ≈ 100 × wire mass m
- Despite this 100:1 mass ratio, both components spin at approximately equal angular velocities in opposite directions

**Classical prediction (violated):**
If angular momentum L = I·ω were conserved (I = moment of inertia, ω = angular velocity), then:
L_disc = I_disc · ω_disc = M·r²·ω_disc
L_wire = I_wire · ω_wire = m·r²·ω_wire

Conservation: I_disc · ω_disc = I_wire · ω_wire → ω_wire/ω_disc = I_disc/I_wire = M/m ≈ 100

But the observed ratio is ω_wire/ω_disc ≈ 1, not 100.

**Explanation invoked:**
The Lorentz force acts perpendicular to velocity at all times, therefore does no work (dW = F·ds = 0 when F ⊥ ds). This means the Lorentz-driven spin does not transfer conventional mechanical momentum in the collinear sense. The "reaction" to the Lorentz-driven disc spin is not a Newton's Third Law mechanical counter-force propagated along the Z-axis, but a transverse electromagnetic force that "leaks" angular momentum without generating a downward Z-axis thrust.

**Proposed device topology:**
"An upward 3-phase spin (clockwise) opposes a downward Lorentz spin (anti-clockwise)." The central magnet spins clockwise under 3-phase power (producing upward gyroscopic force). The outer ring or surrounding conductor spins anti-clockwise under Lorentz force (carrying the electromagnetic reaction without producing net downward mechanical force).
