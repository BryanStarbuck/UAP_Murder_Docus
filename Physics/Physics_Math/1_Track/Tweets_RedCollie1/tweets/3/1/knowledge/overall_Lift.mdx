## Section: 1919872892667625946.yaml — Primary Lift Mechanism: Lenz Levitation Unbalanced by Spin

Drew's named mechanism: "Lenz levitation unbalanced by spin"

Lenz levitation (conventional, without spin):
- A conducting disk (or plate) falls toward a fixed magnet below it
- The changing flux through the disk induces eddy currents: I_eddy = EMF/R = (dΦ/dt)/R
- These eddy currents create a magnetic field opposing the change (Lenz's law)
- The repulsive force between the eddy current's field and the external magnet decelerates the fall
- In the strong-field, high-conductivity limit: the disk "levitates" above the magnet
- This is conventional Lenz levitation — the disk hovers but does not climb upward net

Lenz levitation with AC driving (active levitation):
- The large flat wire coil below the spinning flywheel is driven with AC power at frequency ω_AC
- The AC field from the coil produces a time-varying flux through the flywheel (and vice versa)
- The mutual induction creates alternating attractive/repulsive forces at 2ω_AC
- With proper phase relationship, the time-averaged force can be upward (net lift)

The "unbalanced by spin" modification:
- The spinning magnetic flywheel rotating at ω_spin introduces an asymmetry in the flux distribution
- On one side of the flywheel (where flywheel rotation is in the same sense as the AC field rotation),
  the relative angular velocity is (ω_AC − ω_spin) — reduced coupling
- On the other side: (ω_AC + ω_spin) — increased coupling
- This Doppler-like asymmetry in coupling creates an unequal force distribution across the flywheel
- The net integrated force is non-zero and directed vertically (Z-axis) — this is the lift

The requirement: ω_spin must be large enough to produce a measurable asymmetry. Fast spin = strong
asymmetry = stronger lift. This is why Drew emphasizes "fast spin" repeatedly.

Quantitative estimate (symbolic):
  F_lift ~ k_mutual × I_coil² × ω_spin / ω_AC × (geometric factor)
  where k_mutual is the mutual coupling coefficient between coil and flywheel
