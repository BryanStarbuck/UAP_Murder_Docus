## Section: 1936371306309771275.yaml — 3-Phase Rotating Magnetic Field Drive System

Device configuration:
- Six small 3-phase wire coils: 3 above and 3 below a central flat magnetic flywheel
- Symmetry: 120° angular spacing of coil phases in each plane
- The coils produce a rotating magnetic field B_rot(t) in the XY-plane

For a balanced 3-phase system with angular frequency ω_s (supply frequency):
  B_x(t) = B_0 cos(ω_s t)
  B_y(t) = B_0 cos(ω_s t - 2π/3)  [phase B, 120° offset]
  Third coil at 240° offset

The superposition of these three phasors produces a spatially and temporally rotating magnetic
field of constant magnitude B_0, rotating at angular velocity ω_s:
  B_rot(t) = B_0 [cos(ω_s t) x̂ + sin(ω_s t) ŷ]

This rotating field acts as an induction motor stator, coupling to the permanent magnets embedded
in the perimeter of the flywheel ("beaded magnetic flywheel"). The torque on the flywheel:
  τ = μ × B_rot
where μ is the magnetic dipole moment of each perimeter magnet.

Fast spin condition: if ω_rotor ≈ ω_s (synchronous operation) or ω_rotor < ω_s (asynchronous/
induction operation with slip), the rotating field continuously accelerates the flywheel up to
maximum RPM limited by bearing friction and eddy current losses.

## Section: 1977842550921990209.yaml + 13_1977842470991060992_video.mdx — Gallium Spinning in Three-Phase Field

Experimental observation (video transcription):
- 60 mm magnet (gallium metal in a container)
- Three-phase field causes the gallium/magnet assembly to turn clockwise
- Reversing the sense of the gallium spin (clockwise vs. anti-clockwise) is achieved by changing
  the phase sequence of the three-phase supply

The three-phase field acting on a conducting liquid (gallium, σ ~ 3.7 × 10⁶ S/m at room temperature):
- The rotating B field induces eddy currents in the liquid: J_eddy = σ(E + v × B)
- The J_eddy × B interaction produces azimuthal body force on the liquid → liquid spins
- This is an electromagnetic stirrer / induction pump operating on liquid metal

Key observation: the direction of rotation is controllable by reversing the three-phase supply
sequence, meaning the angular momentum vector of the spinning liquid can be reversed electronically
without mechanical intervention.

## Section: 1964996766048387391.yaml + 13_1964996566814728192_video.mdx — Lorentz Force Demonstration: DC Magnet in Electrolyte

Experimental parameters (from video transcription):
- NdFeB permanent magnet: 80 mm diameter
- Electrolyte: salt water (NaCl solution, σ ~ 5-20 S/m depending on concentration)
- Applied potential: 50 V DC
- Tracer: green food dye

Configuration A: N pole of magnet pointing upward (B directed +ẑ)
- DC current density J flows radially in the electrolyte (electrode geometry determines radial vs.
  axial current path)
- Lorentz body force: f = J × B
- With J ~ r̂ (radial) and B ~ ẑ: f ~ r̂ × ẑ = -φ̂ (azimuthal, counter-clockwise)
- Observed: dye swirls counter-clockwise

Configuration B: S pole up (B directed -ẑ)
- Same J ~ r̂, but B ~ -ẑ: f ~ r̂ × (-ẑ) = +φ̂ (azimuthal, clockwise)
- Observed: dye swirls clockwise

This confirms the classical homopolar motor relationship and validates the experimental platform
for testing liquid-metal Lorentz rotation at higher conductivities.

## Section: page_021.json — Liquid Metal Homopolar vs. Permanent Magnet Homopolar

Permanent magnet homopolar motor (Barlow wheel / Faraday disk type):
- A conducting disk with embedded permanent magnets rotates about its axial center
- Current injected via brushes at center and rim
- Torque: τ = I_radial × B_axial × r (where r is the effective moment arm)
- Angular momentum: L = Iω_mech (where I is the moment of inertia of the disk)

Liquid metal homopolar motor:
- Conducting liquid (Ga, Hg, or Ni-bearing electrolyte) fills a circular cavity
- Current injected via brushes (at the rim, "lots of brush symbols" per crop picture instruction)
- The liquid itself rotates — the conducting fluid is also the rotating inertial element
- For a liquid disk of radius R, density ρ, thickness h, in field B_z with current I_total:
  Torque per unit volume: τ/V = (J_r)(B_z) = (I_total / 2πrh) × B_z
  Net torque: τ = ∫₀^R (I_total / 2πrh) × B_z × r × (2πr h dr) = (I_total × B_z × R²) / 2

The advantage of the liquid metal configuration: no mechanical friction in the rotating element
(only fluid viscosity), higher coupling efficiency, and the liquid acts simultaneously as electrical
conductor, rotating mass, and potential magnetic material (if ferrofluid or nickel solution used).

presence of nickel in the exhaust/coma is consistent with an operational H₂-based fuel cycle.

## Section: 1986602340439499222.yaml — Off-Axis Wobble of Central Spinning Magnet

"The central spinning magnet can wobble off-axis slightly, just as drawn in crops England 2020
(Ashmore Down)."

An off-axis wobble of a spinning magnet introduces a nutational component to the motion. For a
magnetic dipole μ precessing about the Z-axis with nutation angle θ:
- The time-varying component of the field at a fixed observation point has both:
  - A steady component: μ_z ẑ (axial, time-independent)
  - A rotating component: μ⊥ [cos(ω_p t) x̂ + sin(ω_p t) ŷ] (transverse, rotating at ω_p)
  - A nutational component: μ_nut [cos(ω_n t) x̂ + sin(ω_n t) ŷ] (at nutation frequency ω_n)

The off-axis wobble means the magnetic field experienced by any surrounding coil or conductor is
no longer simply periodic at one frequency. The spectrum of B(t) contains sidebands at ω_p ± ω_n.
These sidebands can couple to resonant modes of the receiving coil, potentially increasing the
energy transfer efficiency at specific combinations of spin, precession, and nutation frequencies.

Drew interprets the crop circle depiction of an offset central circle as instructional: the slight
offset of the central spinning element is intentional, not an error, and is part of the functional
design of the propulsion device.

## Section: 1943838561599729681.yaml — Perimeter Magnets on the 3-Axis Gyroscope Wheels

"All four rims of those wheels were full of eyes." — Ezekiel, interpreted as perimeter-mounted
magnets on each gyroscope wheel.

If each wheel of a 3-axis gyroscope carries N magnets arranged around its perimeter at radius R,
each with moment μ_i:
- As the wheel rotates at angular velocity ω, each magnet sweeps past a fixed point with period
  T = 2π/(Nω) — the magnetic field at any external point oscillates at frequency f = Nω/(2π)
- The multi-pole field from N magnets, at distance d from the rim, has spatial harmonics that
  fall off as (R/d)^(2n+1) for multipole order n
- For large N: the fundamental frequency is very high (f = Nω/2π), well into the RF range for
  fast rotation, creating a high-frequency oscillating field

This high-frequency oscillating magnetic field from a rotating perimeter magnet array is the
electromagnetic driver for induction in surrounding coils. The relationship between rotation speed
ω, magnet count N, and output frequency is:
  f_output = N × (ω / 2π)

For N = 100 magnets and ω = 1000 rpm = 104.7 rad/s:
  f_output = 100 × (1000/60) ≈ 1667 Hz

This is in the audio/low-RF range, suitable for driving resonant induction coils with high efficiency.
