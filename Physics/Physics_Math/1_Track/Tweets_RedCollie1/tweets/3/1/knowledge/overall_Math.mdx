## Section: 1919872892667625946.yaml — Gyroscopic Precession Equations

Steady precession of a spinning top in a gravitational field:

Angular momentum vector:
  L = I_spin × ω_spin    [kg·m²/s]
  where I_spin = moment of inertia about spin axis, ω_spin = spin angular velocity [rad/s]

Gravitational torque (about contact point):
  τ_grav = r_cm × F_grav = r_cm × (−mg ẑ)
  |τ_grav| = m g r_cm sin(θ)    [N·m]
  where θ = tilt angle from vertical, r_cm = distance from contact to center of mass

Precessional angular velocity (steady precession):
  Ω_prec = τ_grav / L_⊥ = (m g r_cm) / (I_spin ω_spin)    [rad/s]
  Direction: horizontal, perpendicular to both L and τ_grav (right-hand rule)

Nutation frequency:
  ω_nut = L / I_nutation = (I_spin ω_spin) / I_⊥
  where I_⊥ = moment of inertia about the contact-point nutation axis

Energy:
  T_rot = ½ I_spin ω_spin²    [J]
  T_prec = ½ I_eff Ω_prec²    [J] (effective)
  Total energy: E = T_rot + T_prec + T_nut + V_grav

Key relationship: as ω_spin → ∞, Ω_prec → 0 (fast-spinning tops precess more slowly) and
ω_nut → ∞ (nutation frequency increases with spin speed).

XY-plane dynamics (spin, precession) driving Z-axis output (rising):
  The time-averaged Z-position of center of mass changes due to the nutational oscillation
  combined with precession only when τ_grav ≠ 0. When gravity is replaced by τ_EM:
  Replace: m g r_cm → τ_EM_effective in the precession formula
  Ω_prec_EM = τ_EM / (I_spin ω_spin)

## Section: 1936371306309771275.yaml — 3-Phase Rotating Magnetic Field Mathematics

Balanced 3-phase coil system at angular supply frequency ω_s:
  B₁(t) = B₀ cos(ω_s t) x̂         [phase A]
  B₂(t) = B₀ cos(ω_s t − 2π/3) û₂  [phase B, 120° spatial and temporal offset]
  B₃(t) = B₀ cos(ω_s t − 4π/3) û₃  [phase C, 240° spatial and temporal offset]

Superposition (for coils at 0°, 120°, 240° spatial positions):
  B_total(t) = (3B₀/2)[cos(ω_s t) x̂ + sin(ω_s t) ŷ]
  = (3B₀/2) exp(i ω_s t) in complex phasor notation

Magnitude: |B_total| = 3B₀/2 = constant (time-independent magnitude)
Direction: rotates in XY-plane at angular frequency ω_s

Motor torque on flywheel magnet with moment μ:
  τ = μ × B_total(t)
  |τ| = |μ| |B_total| sin(α)    where α = angle between μ and B_total
  For synchronous operation (rotor follows field): α is the torque angle, maintained constant
  τ_max = |μ| × (3B₀/2)    at α = 90°

Power input to rotate flywheel:
  P = τ × ω_rotor

Induction motor slip:
  s = (ω_s − ω_rotor) / ω_s    [dimensionless, 0 ≤ s ≤ 1]
  Torque in induction regime: τ ∝ s·B²/(R₂), where R₂ = rotor resistance

## Section: 1964996766048387391.yaml — Lorentz Force Equations in Conducting Electrolyte

Lorentz force body force density in bulk conductor:
  f = J × B    [N/m³]
  where J = current density [A/m²], B = magnetic flux density [T]

For electrolyte (NaCl solution) with conductivity σ [S/m]:
  J = σ(E + v × B)    (Ohm's law in moving conducting medium)
  In low-velocity limit (v << c): J ≈ σE

Radial current geometry in cylindrical coordinates (z = axial, r = radial, φ = azimuthal):
  J = J_r r̂    (radial current injection)
  B = B_z ẑ    (axial magnetic field from permanent magnet)
  f = J_r r̂ × B_z ẑ = J_r B_z (r̂ × ẑ) = −J_r B_z φ̂

The azimuthal body force −J_r B_z φ̂ drives counter-clockwise rotation when J_r > 0, B_z > 0.

Reverse B → B_z < 0:
  f = −J_r B_z φ̂ → positive φ̂ → clockwise rotation

Net torque on fluid volume (disk of radius R, height h):
  τ_net = ∫₀^R ∫₀^{2π} ∫₀^h (f_φ · r) r dr dφ dz
        = ∫₀^R (−J_r B_z) · r · 2πr h dr
  If J_r = I_total / (2πr h):
  τ_net = −B_z I_total ∫₀^R r dr = −B_z I_total R²/2

Applied voltage 50 V, approximate current at typical resistance of salt water cell
~ few ohms → I ≈ 10–25 A. Field from 80 mm NdFeB magnet at contact: B_z ≈ 0.5–1 T.
Estimated τ_net ~ 1 N·m for R = 40 mm, I = 20 A, B = 0.5 T:
  τ = (0.5)(20)(0.04²)/2 = 0.008 N·m (at liquid surface scale of a small demonstration)

## Section: 2006531803658826141.yaml — DNA Supercoiling Mathematics Applied to Astrophysical Jet

White-Fuller-Calugareanu theorem:
  Lk = Tw + Wr    (linking number = twist + writhe)

For a closed curve (or effectively closed jet segment):
  Lk is a topological invariant (integer or half-integer)
  ΔLk = 0 → ΔWr = −ΔTw    (conservation)

Twist per unit length along the curve:
  tw = (1/2π) ∫ (dT/ds × T) · N ds    [turns per unit arc length]
  or equivalently via the Frenet-Serret torsion τ(s):
  Tw = (1/2π) ∫ τ(s) ds

Writhe:
  Wr = (1/4π) ∮∮ (dr₁ × dr₂) · (r₁ − r₂) / |r₁ − r₂|³    (Gauss double integral)

Frenet-Serret formulas for the jet trajectory r(s) (arc-length parameterized):
  T(s) = dr/ds    (unit tangent)
  κ(s) = |dT/ds|    (curvature)
  N(s) = (1/κ) dT/ds    (principal normal)
  B(s) = T × N    (binormal)
  dB/ds = −τ(s) N(s)    (torsion τ characterizes out-of-plane twisting)

For the 3I/ATLAS jet: fast core rotation imparts torsion τ to the ejected material. The
conservation of Lk forces the developing torsion in the jet to convert to writhe, producing
the macroscopically visible supercoiling (curling) of the anti-tail.

The rate of writhe generation:
  dWr/dt = −dTw/dt = −(1/2π) d/dt ∫ τ(s,t) ds

This is mathematically identical to the formula Drew derived for DNA supercoiling in JMB 1990,
though the physical system (astrophysical jet vs. DNA molecule) is entirely different.
