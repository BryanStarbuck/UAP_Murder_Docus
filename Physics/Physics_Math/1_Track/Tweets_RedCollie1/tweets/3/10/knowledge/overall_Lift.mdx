### Mechanism 2: Off-Axis 45° Disc Magnet Under Three-Phase Drive

**Two non-lifting and one lifting configuration**:

**Non-lifting (on-axis, θ = 0°, disc horizontal)**:
- Three-phase rotating field drives disc to spin in the horizontal plane about the Z-axis
- No gyroscopic response needed (disc is already aligned with field rotation plane)
- Result: pure in-plane spin, no vertical force

**Non-lifting (on-axis, θ = 45°, disc tilted)**:
- Three-phase field drives disc spin about disc normal AND exerts perpendicular torque attempting to align disc to horizontal
- Gyroscopic inertia resists the alignment torque
- Disc straightens up from 45° toward 0° — a pure rotation (precession toward horizontal alignment)
- No radial offset → no Z-component of gyroscopic reaction → no lift

**Lifting (off-axis, θ = 45°, disc tilted AND radially offset by r)**:
- Three-phase field drives disc spin
- Perpendicular torque τ_perp attempts to align disc
- Gyroscopic inertia resists τ_perp
- Gyroscopic reaction force F_gyr = τ_perp × L / |L|² has a Z-component because the offset breaks the axial symmetry
- Disc straightens up (rotates from 45° toward 0°) AND translates upward by H
- H ∝ r² · f

**The Paper Diagram at ~4:00 of video**:
Drew draws on paper the following diagram:
- Disc at 45°, offset by r from axis
- Three-phase coil axis (Z)
- Arrow showing "straightening motion" (rotation toward 0°) AND upward arrow (H)
- Label: "pushing against the vacuum makes it move upward"

The paper diagram is the primary schematic for understanding how XY-plane rotational dynamics (the three-phase rotating field in the horizontal plane) produce Z-axis (vertical) translational motion.

**Physical chain for lift**:

1. Input: Three-phase AC at frequency f, amplitude B₀, to three wire coils
2. Produces: Rotating magnetic field **B_rot(t)** of magnitude (3/2)B₀ at angular frequency ω = 2πf
3. Acts on: Disc permanent magnet with dipole moment μ at 45° tilt, offset by r
4. Produces: Spin torque (τ_spin = μ B₀, drives rotation about disc normal) + Alignment torque (τ_align = μ B₀ sin45° ≈ 0.707 μ B₀, attempts to tilt disc to horizontal)
5. Gyroscopic response: L = Iω_disc n̂; dL/dt = τ_align → precession
6. Precession with offset: ΔL resolved along Z → F_z = d(L_z)/dt
7. Output: Upward force F_z, lift height H ∝ r² · f

### Mechanism 3: Rising Disc in Stainless Steel Tube — Step 3

**Geometry**: 70 mm disc magnet inside stainless steel tube (non-magnetic, austenitic). 8 mm standoff tube, 14 mm inner hole. Three three-phase wire coils below.

**Observation**: With three-phase power applied (specific frequency not stated in this source), the disc magnet rises up through the stainless steel tube. Lift is confirmed experimentally.

**Physical interpretation**: The rotating field from the three coils below drives the disc magnet into clockwise rotation. The disc, spinning inside the tube, experiences the gyroscopic-vacuum coupling described in Mechanism 2. The stainless steel tube (non-magnetic) constrains radial motion but not axial (Z-direction) motion. The disc rises along Z.

**Critical material constraint**: Paramagnetic stainless steel (ferritic or martensitic grades) grips the spinning magnet too strongly through magnetic stiction — the inner magnet cannot rotate and therefore cannot rise. Only austenitic (non-magnetic) stainless prevents this. This is an experimentally confirmed material constraint on the lift mechanism.

### Mechanism 4: Counter-Rotating Flywheel — Step 4 (Enhanced Lift)

**Configuration** (see also overall_Magnetics.mdx for full details):
- Inner magnet: 70 mm disc, clockwise rotation (three coils below, UVW)
- Outer flywheel: plastic disc with 8 or 16 alternating-polarity permanent magnets, anti-clockwise rotation (six coils external, UVW-UVW in series)
- Teflon paper bearing surface (required — stainless steel ball-bearing race causes stiction)

**Lift enhancement**: The counter-rotating outer flywheel modifies the vacuum coupling of the inner magnet. The combined effect (spin + counter-spin) produces greater net upward force than the inner magnet alone.

**Jumping ring analogy**: Drew explicitly compares the mechanism to the "jumping ring from physics" — the electromagnetic induction demonstration where an AC-driven coil launches a conducting ring upward through the induced repulsion between the coil's field and the ring's induced-current field. In the jumping ring: F ∝ dI_coil/dt × I_ring ∝ f (frequency-dependent force). In the Prototype One: F ∝ coupling between clockwise inner field and anti-clockwise outer field, both driven by the same three-phase frequency.
