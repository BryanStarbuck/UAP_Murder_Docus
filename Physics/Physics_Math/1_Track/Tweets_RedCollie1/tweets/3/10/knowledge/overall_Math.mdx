### Equation 1: Lift Height vs. Offset and Frequency

The primary quantitative relationship stated by Drew, derived from crop circle "blueprints":

**h ∝ r² · f**

Definitions:
- h = lift height achieved by the spinning magnetic disc under three-phase excitation [meters]
- r = radial offset of the spinning disc magnet from the central symmetry axis of the three-phase coil assembly [meters]
- f = frequency of the three-phase AC supply [Hz]
- The proportionality constant is unspecified in this source — it would incorporate the disc magnetic moment μ, moment of inertia I, field amplitude B₀, coil geometry, and vacuum coupling constant

**Mathematical structure**: This is a power law of the form h = k · r^α · f^β where α = 2, β = 1. The second-order dependence on r (h ∝ r²) means:
- At r = 0 (disc on axis): h = 0 (no lift — confirmed experimentally, pure rotation only)
- Small offsets produce negligible lift (quadratic onset)
- Doubling r increases lift by factor of 4
- The f-linear dependence means doubling frequency doubles lift height

**Dimensional consistency**: For h [m] = k · r² [m²] · f [s⁻¹], the proportionality constant k has units [m / (m² · s⁻¹)] = [s/m]. The physical interpretation of k requires identifying the coupling mechanism: k ~ (μ · B₀) / (I · ω² · v_rise) where v_rise is a characteristic velocity of the rise process, or alternatively k ~ τ_gyro / (I · ω_rise) from the gyroscopic force-per-unit-offset.

**Derivation chain from physics** (Drew's description of the mechanism, translating to equations):

1. Rotating field torque on disc: τ_B = μ · B₀ · sin(angle) ≈ μ · B₀ (for 90° geometry)

2. Gyroscopic angular momentum: L = I · ω_disc = I · 2πf

3. Precession angular velocity: Ω_prec = τ_B / L = (μ · B₀) / (I · 2πf)

4. Precession velocity of disc center (off-axis by r): v_z = Ω_prec · r = (μ · B₀ · r) / (I · 2πf)

5. Lift height in time T: h = v_z · T = (μ · B₀ · r · T) / (I · 2πf)

Under this derivation, h ∝ r (not r²) for fixed T. To recover h ∝ r² · f, we need an additional r-dependence in the effective torque or T:

If T ∝ r (the rise time scales linearly with offset — larger offset requires longer time to straighten up), then h ∝ r² / f, which contradicts the stated h ∝ r² · f.

Alternatively, if the mechanism is not precession-driven translation but rather a force F_z ∝ r · f² (from some frequency-dependent vacuum coupling), then h = F_z · t²/(2m) ∝ r · f² · t² and for constant observation time h ∝ r · f². This does not reproduce r² · f either.

**Status**: The relationship h ∝ r² · f is presented as an empirical/alien-transmitted result, not yet confirmed by Drew's experiments. The theoretical derivation consistent with this scaling is not explicitly provided and remains an open problem in this source. The scaling suggests a coupling that is both quadratic in offset and linear in frequency — possibly indicative of a parametric resonance mechanism where r determines the mode amplitude and f determines the pumping rate.

### Equation 2: Spinor Phase Under Rotation

The SU(2) Lie group element for a rotation by angle φ about axis n̂:

U(φ, n̂) = exp(-iφ σ·n̂/2) = cos(φ/2)·I - i sin(φ/2)(σ·n̂)

where σ = (σ_x, σ_y, σ_z) are Pauli matrices. Under a 2π rotation:

U(2π, n̂) = cos(π)·I - i sin(π)(σ·n̂) = -I

The spinor acquires a phase of -1 under 2π rotation (double-valued representation of SO(3)). Under 4π rotation, U(4π) = +I — return to original state. This is the Möbius strip topology referenced by Drew.

**Right-hand rule emergence**: For a right-helicity electron (spin eigenstate of helicity operator ĥ = σ·p̂/|p|, eigenvalue +1/2), the spinor is:

χ_R = (cos(θ/2), sin(θ/2)·e^{iφ}) for momentum direction (θ,φ)

This has positive phase winding in the φ direction — the complex phase of χ_R increases with increasing azimuthal angle φ. When the electron moves in the +z direction, the induced magnetic field **B** circles in the +φ direction (i.e., counterclockwise when viewed from the direction of motion), which is exactly the right-hand rule: thumb along +z (current), fingers point in +φ direction.

**The standard Biot-Savart law**:

d**B** = (μ₀/4π) · I (d**l** × r̂) / r²

gives **B** circling counterclockwise around the current direction when viewed from the direction of current flow. Drew's claim is that this law (specifically the handedness, not just the functional form) follows necessarily from the right-handed spinor chirality of the electron.

### Equation 3: Gyroscope Precession Rate

Standard Euler equation for gyroscope precession (torque-free vs. torque-driven):

Torque-driven precession:
**dL/dt** = **τ** = **r** × m**g**

Precession angular velocity:
|Ω_prec| = |**τ**| / |**L**| = mgr / (Iω)

As ω decreases (spinning down): Ω_prec ∝ 1/ω increases → faster precession → wider cone → eventual fall.

**π relationship**: The precession orbit subtends 2π radians in one period T_prec = 2π/Ω_prec. The factor π appears explicitly in the orbit period and in the nutation frequency. The crop circle reference to π = 3.141592654 likely encodes either:
(a) The ratio of nutation frequency to precession frequency (which involves π in its exact form)
(b) The number of complete precession cycles before the gyroscope falls (proportional to ω₀/ω_friction and involving π)
(c) Simply the geometric relationship between the disc circumference and diameter of the precession orbit

### Equation 4: Three-Phase Rotating Field Phasor

Three-phase AC with phase angles 0°, 120°, 240° applied to three coils at spatial positions 0°, 120°, 240° (physical winding positions):

B_x(t) = B₀ cos(2πft)
B_y(t) = B₀ cos(2πft - 2π/3)
B_z_equiv(t) = B₀ cos(2πft - 4π/3)

Vector sum in the plane of the coils:
**B_rot(t)** = B₀ [cos(2πft) x̂ + cos(2πft - 2π/3) ŷ + cos(2πft - 4π/3) (−x̂−ŷ)/√2 + ...]

More precisely for a symmetric three-phase two-pole winding:
**B_rot(t)** = (3B₀/2) [cos(2πft) x̂ + sin(2πft) ŷ]

This is a vector of constant magnitude 3B₀/2 rotating at angular frequency ω = 2πf. The factor of 3/2 is the standard three-phase winding factor for a balanced, symmetric winding.

**For the six-coil UVW-UVW series winding** (outer flywheel drive):
The two three-phase sets in series double the effective ampere-turns, increasing field amplitude to approximately 3B₀ (twice that of a three-coil winding) while maintaining the same rotation rate ω = 2πf.

**Paradox resolution** (clockwise field → opposite rotations):
The three-phase field **B_rot** rotates clockwise in the frame of the coil winding (looking from above, i.e., from +Z). 

- Inner rotor (inside the coil assembly): The rotor sees the field rotating clockwise in its frame. It rotates clockwise to follow the field (positive alignment torque for a synchronous motor in same-direction mode). → Inner magnet rotates clockwise.

- Outer flywheel (outside the coil assembly): For a motor where the rotor surrounds the stator (external rotor motor), the torque coupling reverses direction — the outer rotor rotates anti-clockwise to follow the clockwise-rotating field from its external perspective. This is because for an external rotor, the spatial relationship between N-poles of the rotor and the rotating field requires the rotor to turn in the opposite sense to an internal rotor. → Flywheel rotates anti-clockwise.

Both use "clockwise" field rotation (same three-phase sequence), but produce opposite mechanical rotations because the geometry of N-pole alignment differs between internal and external rotor configurations.

### Equation 5: Spatial Harmonic Content of U-U-V-V-W-W Winding

For a ring of six coils at angular positions θ_k = k·π/3 (k = 0,1,2,3,4,5) carrying currents:

Standard U-V-W-U-V-W (60° interleaved):
I_k(t) = I₀ cos(2πft - k·2π/3)

The resulting spatial flux density field as a Fourier series in θ:
B(θ,t) = Σ_n B_n cos(nθ - 2πft) for fundamental n=1, higher harmonics n=5,7,...

U-U-V-V-W-W (paired adjacent, Drew's configuration):
Adjacent pairs carry same phase: I_0=I_1 (U), I_2=I_3 (V), I_4=I_5 (W)
This is equivalent to three two-coil groups, each spanning 60° of arc, at 120° separation.

The spatial harmonic content is modified: the 2-pole (n=1) fundamental spatial harmonic may have reduced amplitude if the paired coils partially cancel the n=1 term, while the 4-pole (n=2) or 6-pole (n=3) harmonics may be relatively enhanced. The exact harmonic content depends on the coil geometry (pitch, width) and the specific grouping.

The consequence for rotating field speed: a p-pole-pair field rotates at ω_mechanical = 2πf/p. Higher pole-pair harmonics produce a field that rotates more slowly, producing different torque-speed characteristics on the spinning permanent magnet rotor. This may be the physical basis for the "never done before" experimental findings — the higher-harmonic rotating field component produces a different force signature on the spinning magnet than the standard fundamental rotating field.
