### Gyroscopic Rise: Z-Axis Elevation Against Gravity

**Observed phenomenon** (experimental, multiple independent confirmators):
A spinning gyroscope or tippe-top, when placed on a surface under gravitational
field g, undergoes a spontaneous upward displacement in Z — it rises.

For the tippe-top specifically:
- Initial state: spinning on its rounded base, center of mass BELOW pivot
- Final state: spinning on its stem, center of mass ABOVE pivot (inverted)
- Energy: this inversion RAISES the center of mass by Δz > 0
- Source of energy for lift: kinetic energy of spin (KE_spin converts to ΔPE_grav)
- The rise occurs against gravity: it is NOT ballistic flight but a continuous
  Z-axis displacement while maintaining spin

The tippe-top inversion requires friction with the surface AND angular momentum
(spin). The physics involves both the gyroscopic term and the frictional torque.

For a tippe-top of radius R, CoM offset ε from geometric center:
Equations of motion (Routh's equations for rolling with slip):
I₁ω̇₁ = (I₃ - I₁)ω₂ω₃ + Mgε sin(θ) + F_friction × R
...
The rise of the CoM: d(Mgz_cm)/dt > 0 while KE_spin decreases (d(½I₃ω₃²)/dt < 0)

**Drew's question for space (no external g):**
In free space with no gravity, the tippe-top mechanism cannot operate (friction
requires a surface; surface normal force is provided by gravity). The claim:
some other mechanism (magnetic field coupling) could substitute for gravity to
provide the normal force, enabling a "magnetic tippe-top" effect in space.

### Lenz Levitation (Non-Spinning): Confirmed Null Baseline

**Experimental result**: Ordinary Lenz levitation WITHOUT spin:
- No anti-gravity effect
- Upward and downward forces cancel exactly

Physical analysis:
For an AC solenoid (axis vertical, Z) below a horizontal conducting disc:
Force per unit area on disc: f_z = (J × B)_z
Integrated over disc: F_z = ∫∫ (J × B)_z dA

For sinusoidal drive at frequency f:
B_z(z,t) = B₀(z) cos(2πft)
J_θ(r,t) = J₀(r) sin(2πft)  [eddy currents lag B by 90°]

F_z = ∫∫ J_θ × B_r dA - ∫∫ J_r × B_z dA

At the levitation equilibrium height z₀, the time-averaged force:
<F_z> = Mg (weight support)
<F_z²> contributions: some upward, some downward; NET = Mg only

There is no net upward force BEYOND weight support — the Lenz force cannot lift
the disc higher than the equilibrium while maintaining the same AC drive level.
To achieve higher levitation, power must be increased.

**Conclusion from Drew's experiment**: spin = 0 → lift = 0 (net). This establishes
the baseline physics for the null case.

### Lenz Jumping Ring: Observed Lift Force

Experimental data point:
- 80 mm diameter disc (smaller): jumps "a long way" → higher lift force
- 100 mm diameter disc (larger): jumps "a little bit" → lower lift force

For the Lenz jumping ring, the lift impulse (not steady lift) is:
J_z = ∫F_z dt ≈ I_eddy · ΔΦ · N / R_disc_eff

where:
- I_eddy = peak eddy current
- ΔΦ = flux change during current pulse
- N = effective number of turns
- R_disc_eff = effective resistance of eddy current paths

The smaller disc achieves greater height h = J_z²/(2M²g):
h ∝ J_z²/M²

Since M ∝ R² (same thickness), h ∝ J_z²/R⁴
The observed result (smaller disc → greater h) requires J_z(80mm) > J_z(100mm)
by more than the mass ratio (M_100/M_80)² = (100/80)⁴ ≈ 2.44 times.

This is a non-trivial result about the geometry dependence of Lenz levitation forces.
