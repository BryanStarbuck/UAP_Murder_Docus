### Angular Momentum and Gyroscopic Precession Equations

**Angular momentum of a symmetric rigid rotor:**
L = I₃ω₃ ê₃

where:
- I₃ = axial moment of inertia (about spin axis)
- ω₃ = angular velocity about spin axis (spin rate)
- ê₃ = unit vector along spin axis

**Torque from gravity on a precessing gyroscope:**
τ = r_cm × F_gravity = r × (−Mg ẑ)

In polar form for tilt angle θ from vertical:
|τ| = Mgr sin(θ)
Direction: horizontal, perpendicular to the plane containing L and ẑ

**Precession rate (Euler):**
Ω_p = |τ|/(|L| sin θ) = Mgr/(I₃ω₃)

Note: Ω_p is independent of tilt angle θ (cancels). This is the steady-state
result for fast spin (gyroscopic approximation: I₃ω₃ >> I₁Ω_p).

**Nutation (small oscillations superimposed on precession):**
Θ̈ + [(I₃ω₃/I₁)² - (Mgr/I₁)cos θ_0] · (θ - θ_0) = 0

Nutation frequency: ω_nut ≈ I₃ω₃/I₁ (for fast top)

**Laithwaite anomaly claim (experimental, not derivable from above):**
d(p_total)/dt ≠ F_applied - F_gravity during gyroscope rise
i.e., momentum is NOT conserved in the standard form when the gyroscope rises.
This is the claimed deviation from Newton's 2nd Law for the composite system.

Vector check that Drew invites: Compute all force vectors during the lift:
F_hand_up + F_gravity + F_reaction = m·a_gyro + m_body·a_body

If F_reaction ≈ 0 (Laithwaite's claim), then:
F_hand_up + F_gravity = m·a_gyro only
→ F_hand_up = m(a_gyro + g)

The controversy: Laithwaite claimed F_hand_up was LESS than m(a_gyro + g),
implying the gyroscope was lighter than expected. This has not been reproduced
under controlled conditions with strain gauge measurement.

### 3-Phase Rotating Magnetic Field: Mathematical Structure

**3-phase current set (120° phase displacement):**
I_A(t) = I₀ cos(ωt)
I_B(t) = I₀ cos(ωt - 2π/3)
I_C(t) = I₀ cos(ωt - 4π/3)

**Resulting field from coils at 0°, 120°, 240° azimuth:**
B_x(t) = B₁[cos(ωt)·cos(0°) + cos(ωt - 2π/3)·cos(120°) + cos(ωt - 4π/3)·cos(240°)]
        = B₁ · (3/2) cos(ωt)

B_y(t) = B₁[cos(ωt)·sin(0°) + cos(ωt - 2π/3)·sin(120°) + cos(ωt - 4π/3)·sin(240°)]
        = B₁ · (3/2) sin(ωt)

Combined: B(t) = (3B₁/2)[cos(ωt) x̂ + sin(ωt) ŷ]
→ Constant magnitude rotating field: |B| = 3B₁/2

**Lenz force on aluminium disc (static disc, no spin):**
Induced EMF: ε = -dΦ/dt = -πR²_disc · dB_z/dt

For field rotating in XY-plane, B_z = 0 → no net EMF from pure XY rotation
for a horizontal disc. The Lenz levitation requires a Z-component (vertical coil
below the disc, not rotating field). These are two distinct configurations.

**Force on spinning rotor in rotating field (synchronous frame):**
In the frame rotating at ω (field angular velocity):
B_rotating = B₀ ẑ' (appears static)
Rotor at angular velocity Ω_rotor: slip angular frequency = ω - Ω_rotor

At synchronous (Ω_rotor = ω): no relative motion → no induced torque
(analogous to synchronous motor at no-load)

At Ω_rotor < ω (sub-synchronous): induction torque acts to accelerate rotor
At Ω_rotor > ω (super-synchronous): induction torque decelerates rotor

The claimed anomalous Z-force would appear at specific Ω_rotor/ω ratios.

### Jumping Ring (Lenz Levitation) Size Dependence

Video data (13_1936372506547388416):
- Power: V = 160 V, isolation transformer
- Disc 1: R = 50 mm, t = 3 mm → Area A₁ = π(0.05)² = 7.85 × 10⁻³ m²
- Disc 2: R = 40 mm, t = 3 mm → Area A₂ = π(0.04)² = 5.03 × 10⁻³ m²
- Result: Disc 2 (smaller) jumps HIGHER

Force model for pulsed Lenz launching:
F_z = (1/2)I²_eddy · dM/dz

where M = mutual inductance between solenoid and disc, I_eddy = eddy current.

dM/dz increases as disc moves away (mutual inductance drops with distance).
I_eddy ∝ V_induced/R_disc_resistance ∝ (dΦ/dt)/R_eff

For smaller disc (same thickness): R_eff is higher (smaller circumference for
current loops), but V_induced is also lower (less flux). 

More precisely:
Φ through disc = ∫B_z · dA over disc area
For B_z(r) from solenoid, if disc radius < solenoid radius:
Φ ∝ R²_disc (disc area limited)
EMF = -dΦ/dt ∝ R²_disc · ω · B₀

Resistance of eddy current loop of radius r in disc:
R_loop ≈ ρ/(2πt) · 2π/r (per unit length) ∝ 1/(r·t)
where ρ = resistivity

I_eddy ∝ EMF/R_loop ∝ R²_disc · r · t

The force F ∝ I²_eddy / (R_loop) leads to complex R dependence.
Numerical result suggests intermediate-size disc maximizes F/m.

### Tilted Magnet Geometry: Coupling XY Rotation to Z Force

**Rotating magnetic dipole on tilted plane:**
Let tilt angle of magnet plane from horizontal = α
Dipole magnitude = μ

Dipole moment components during rotation at Ω:
μ_x(t) = μ sin(α) cos(Ωt)
μ_y(t) = μ sin(α) sin(Ωt)
μ_z = μ cos(α)  [constant]

Time derivatives (for radiation/induction coupling):
dμ_x/dt = -μΩ sin(α) sin(Ωt)
dμ_y/dt = +μΩ sin(α) cos(Ωt)
dμ_z/dt = 0

The transverse (XY) components represent a circularly polarized rotating dipole.

**Interaction with external field gradient ∂B_z/∂z:**
Force on dipole in non-uniform field:
F_z = ∇_z(μ · B) = μ_z · ∂B_z/∂z + μ_x · ∂B_x/∂z + μ_y · ∂B_y/∂z

Time-averaged (over one rotation period):
<F_z> = μ cos(α) · ∂B_z/∂z + (μ sin(α)/2) · [∂B_x/∂z · cos(φ) + ∂B_y/∂z · sin(φ)]

where φ is the phase offset between rotor and stator field. This reduces to:
<F_z> = μ cos(α) · ∂B_z/∂z + (μ sin(α) · |∂B_⊥/∂z| /2) · cos(φ - φ₀)

For α = 0 (no tilt): <F_z> = μ · ∂B_z/∂z (standard dipole force, no XY coupling)
For α ≠ 0 and φ ≠ π/2: additional term contributes to Z-force from XY-plane rotation

**Maximum additional Z-force at α = 45°:**
d<F_z>/dα = -μ sin(α)·∂B_z/∂z + (μ cos(α)/2)·|∂B_⊥/∂z|·cos(φ-φ₀) = 0
At ∂B_z/∂z = 0 (no vertical gradient in stator): max at α = π/2 (horizontal tilt)
At finite ∂B_z/∂z: optimization gives tan(α_opt) = |∂B_⊥/∂z|/(2∂B_z/∂z)

The "slanted plane" of the inner magnet optimizes this coupling.

### 5-Phase Rotating Magnetic Field: Fourier Decomposition

**5-phase current set (72° phase displacement):**
I_k(t) = I₀ cos(ωt - 2πk/5),  k = 0, 1, 2, 3, 4

**Coils placed at azimuthal angles φ_k = 2πk/5:**

B_x(t) = B₁ Σ_{k=0}^{4} cos(ωt - 2πk/5) · cos(2πk/5)
B_y(t) = B₁ Σ_{k=0}^{4} cos(ωt - 2πk/5) · sin(2πk/5)

Using identity: cos(A-B)cos(B) = (1/2)[cos(A-2B) + cos(A)]

B_x(t) = (B₁/2) Σ_{k=0}^{4} [cos(ωt - 4πk/5) + cos(ωt)]
        = (5B₁/2) cos(ωt)  [since Σ cos(4πk/5) = 0 for k=0..4]

B_y(t) = (5B₁/2) sin(ωt)

Result: B(t) = (5B₁/2)[cos(ωt) x̂ + sin(ωt) ŷ]
Magnitude: |B| = 5B₁/2 (uniform rotation, same as 3-phase but 5/3× larger for same B₁)

**Spatial field harmonics (for 5-fold wound stator):**
For a winding with pole-pair number p, the spatial distribution has Fourier components
at: p, 5p±2, 5p±4, ... (due to 5-fold winding symmetry)

For p=1 (2-pole machine equivalent with 5-phase):
Harmonics at: 1, 3, 7, 9, 11, 13, ... (eliminating 5th and multiples)
Advantage over 3-phase (harmonics at 1, 5, 7, 11, ...): the 5th harmonic is
eliminated in 3-phase but the 5-fold winding also eliminates 5th harmonic and
has a different harmonic content.

**Relevance**: If the UAP rotor has a 5-fold magnetic symmetry (5 poles), resonant
coupling occurs at Ω_rotor = ω (fundamental) with force coupling enhanced by the
absence of specific spatial harmonics that would otherwise create torque ripple.

### Spinning Magnetic Levitation: Force Balance with Spin Term

**Generalized force balance for spinning magnetic rotor:**
In Z: F_magnetic_Z + F_gravity = m·a_z

Classical (no spin): F_magnetic_Z = F_Lenz ≈ 0 (net, for symmetric geometry) → no lift
With spin (Ω ≠ 0): additional term F_spin(Ω) enters:
F_spin = f(Ω, B, ∂B/∂z, I, geometry)

Drew's experimental program: determine the functional form of F_spin(Ω) —
specifically, does it scale as Ω, Ω², or some other power, and what is its
direction relative to the gravitational field vector.

If F_spin ∝ Ω² · (∂B/∂z): then a critical spin rate Ω_c exists above which
F_spin > Mg (full levitation without thrust reaction):
Ω_c = √[Mg / (k · ∂B/∂z)]  where k is a geometry-dependent coupling constant
