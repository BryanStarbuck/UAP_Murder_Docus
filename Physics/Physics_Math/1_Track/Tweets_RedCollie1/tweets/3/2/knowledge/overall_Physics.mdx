### Lenz Effect (Classical) vs. Spinning Magnetic Levitation

**Classical Lenz Effect (experimental confirmation)**:
- Aluminium disc above AC-driven solenoid → induced eddy currents → Lenz repulsion
- Equilibrium condition: F_Lenz(z₀) = Mg (levitation height z₀)
- Force expression: F_Lenz = (μ₀/4π) · σ · ω² · (dΦ/dz)² · A_disc · δ
  where σ = conductivity, δ = skin depth, Φ = flux through disc
- At z₀: stable levitation, NO net upward displacement beyond balance point
- Drew experimental result: "upward and downward forces cancel" — confirmed null

**3-Phase Coil Rotating Field**:
Two sets of 3-phase wire coils producing rotating magnetic field B_RMF(t):
B_x(t) = B₀ cos(ωt)
B_y(t) = B₀ sin(ωt)
|B_RMF| = B₀ = constant (no magnitude oscillation)

The rotating field interacts with:
(a) The eddy currents in a stationary conducting disc → standard Lenz geometry
(b) A spinning magnetized rotor → gyromagnetic coupling

The key transition: when rotor spin rate Ω_rotor synchronizes with (or is a
rational multiple of) ω_drive, resonant coupling occurs. Drew's claim is that
in this resonant or near-resonant spinning regime, a net Z-force emerges.

### Gyroscope Apparent Mass Reduction: Laithwaite's Experiment

Video transcription describes the critical experiment:
"This is the most remarkable experiment I think that I've ever done."
"I'm not sure if it can, but now we've balanced it, and we've balanced it
about its centre of mass. So if this gyro transfers its mass centre to the pivot,
it should stay balanced."
"Let those who say I cannot make a body appear lighter than it is, come and pull
holes in that one."

Physical interpretation:
- A gyroscope balanced about its center of mass should, by classical mechanics,
  remain balanced at the pivot — this is trivial (any object balanced at its CoM
  remains balanced statically).
- The anomalous claim: when SPINNING, the gyroscope's apparent inertial weight
  measured at the pivot changes (becomes less than Mg_apparent).

The physics of inertial mass modification:
Standard inertial mass: m_i in F = m_i a
Gravitational mass: m_g in F = m_g g
Equivalence principle: m_i = m_g (tested to 1 part in 10¹³)

If a spinning gyroscope appears lighter, one of three effects:
1. **Woodward/Mach effect**: Transient mass fluctuations arise when a body
   is simultaneously accelerating and having its internal energy changed:
   δm = -(4πGρ/3c²) · U/c²  [Sciama's formulation]
   A spinning gyroscope undergoing precession (angular acceleration) while
   converting rotational KE → gravitational PE during rise could satisfy
   these conditions.
2. **Gravitomagnetic effect**: Rotating mass generates a gravitomagnetic
   field B_grav analogous to magnetic field of rotating charge:
   ∇ × B_grav = -(4G/c²)J_mass
   where J_mass = ρv is mass current density. For a rapidly spinning rotor,
   B_grav couples to the gravitational field: an upward-rising gyroscope
   experiences an additional force F_grav = m v × B_grav.
3. **Gyromagnetic ratio of spinning matter**: Spinning neutral matter still
   has a gyromagnetic ratio due to orbital and spin angular momenta of nuclei.
   The coupling of L = Iω to external B fields: F_z = ∂(μ·B)/∂z
   For μ proportional to L: F_z = (γ·Iω)·∂B_z/∂z
   This is a real, classical coupling — but typically tiny for macroscopic rotors.

### Tilted Inner Magnet Plane as Lift Generator

Question: "Is the inner magnet carried on a slanted plane for any specific reason?"
Drew: "That is how it generates 'lift' (like for a spinning 'top')."

Physical mechanism:
A disc magnet on a tilted plane within a spinning housing creates a time-varying
magnetic dipole moment as it rotates:
- Let the tilt angle be α from the horizontal (XY-plane)
- Dipole moment: μ = μ₀[sin(α)cos(Ωt) x̂ + sin(α)sin(Ωt) ŷ + cos(α) ẑ]
  where Ω is the spin angular velocity of the housing

The time derivative: dμ/dt = μ₀Ω sin(α) [-sin(Ωt) x̂ + cos(Ωt) ŷ]

This creates a rotating transverse magnetic moment, analogous to a circularly
polarized magnetic dipole emitter. The radiation reaction from this oscillating
dipole couples back to the spinning rotor.

For an external coil/stator field B_ext, the interaction energy:
U = -μ · B_ext = -μ₀B_ext[sin(α)cos(Ωt-φ)]

Averaged over one rotation, if there is a phase offset φ ≠ 0, π:
<U> = -(μ₀B_ext/2)sin(α)cos(φ) ≠ 0

This non-zero average interaction energy creates a force on the rotor system
in the Z-direction, proportional to sin(α) — the tilt angle of the magnet plane.

The tilted plane geometry is thus the mechanism for coupling XY-plane rotation
to Z-axis force — analogous to how gyroscopic precession couples horizontal
angular momentum to vertical force.

- Embedding in ℝ³: M = {(cos(t)(R + s·cos(t/2)), sin(t)(R + s·cos(t/2)), s·sin(t/2)) : t ∈ [0,2π), s ∈ [-w,w]}
