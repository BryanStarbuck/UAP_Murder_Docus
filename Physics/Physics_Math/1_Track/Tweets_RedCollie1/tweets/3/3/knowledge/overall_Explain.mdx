### Spinning Gyroscope Apparent Weight Reduction — Experimental Observation

A heavy spinning gyroscope, when lifted while spinning at high angular velocity, is reported by the
handler (Sydney University demonstration) to feel "incredibly light" compared to lifting it at rest.
The handler states: "when it's spinning, it honestly felt like it was just wanting to go up by itself"
and "it felt like I was not putting in the amount of force that you think to lift that kind of weight."

The beneficial output here is a perceived and measurable reduction in the gravitational load required
to elevate a spinning mass. Drew's explanation bridges this to a 90-degree force-vector rotation:
the downward gravitational force F_g acting on the spinning gyroscope is not simply opposed by a
vertical lifting force, but instead is "transferred" (converted) 90 degrees into a horizontal
precessional torque. The net effect for the vertical (Z-axis) load is reduction in perceived weight
and inertial resistance along the lift axis.

The chain of reasoning connecting the XY-plane dynamics (precession) to the Z-axis output (reduced
apparent weight, effective lift tendency) is as follows:

1. Gravitational force vector: F_g = -mg ẑ acts on the far end of the gyroscope arm.
2. Without spin: lifting requires exerting F_lift = +mg ẑ, which the handler confirms is "big effort."
3. With spin (angular momentum L = Iω ẑ', where ẑ' is the spin axis): the gravitational torque
   τ = r × F_g is not dissipated as downward acceleration of the rotor but instead drives precession
   in the XY plane. The angular momentum vector precesses about the vertical with precession rate
   Ω_p = (mgr) / (Iω), where r is the distance from pivot to center of mass.
4. The key claim is that this conversion of gravitational torque into XY-plane precessional motion
   reduces the effective Z-axis gravitational load: the weight "appears" reduced because the
   gravitational work is being done in producing horizontal rotation rather than requiring vertical
   support force.
5. Beneficial output: Z-axis lift tendency emerges from XY-plane spin dynamics without direct
   upward thrust force.

Drew explicitly labels this mechanism a "Lorentz kind of force for gravity" — a gravitational analog
of the Lorentz force, where a transverse force (precession) results from a velocity-perpendicular
(spin angular momentum) interaction with a field (gravity). The Lorentz force in EM: F = q(v × B).
The gravitational analog proposed: the gravitational "force" on a spinning body is redirected by
the cross-product geometry of angular momentum and gravitational field, producing transverse motion
rather than direct vertical acceleration.

Historical precedent: Eric Laithwaite, 1974 Royal Institution Christmas Lectures, London, demonstrated
anomalous gyroscopic behavior that mainstream physics academics rejected. Drew notes "he was despised
by the other academics in England for it." Laithwaite's demonstrations are now available on YouTube

inertial resistance in other directions. This anisotropy of inertial mass is the direct bridge
