### Core Governing Equation — "Transitional Magnetism" UFO Height Formula

From Drew's 2021 dream notes:

  ω = h / a                                    ... (1)

Symbols:
- ω: angular frequency [rad/s] — rotation rate of the spinning disc or magnetic gyroscope
- h: height [m] — vertical displacement (altitude) achieved by the flying disc
- a: area [m²] — cross-sectional area of the flying disc or device

Rearranged to solve for height:
  h = ω · a                                    ... (2)

For a circular disc of radius R:
  a = π R²
  h = ω · π R²                                 ... (3)

Dimensional check of (1): [rad/s] = [m] / [m²] = [1/m]
The dimensions do not balance dimensionally in SI units. This implies the formula is either:
(a) a proportionality with a dimensional constant k: ω = k · h / a, where k has units [rad · m / s]
(b) normalized quantities (e.g., h and a in specific units that make the formula dimensionally
    consistent in a non-SI system)
(c) a phenomenological relation where the constant of proportionality carries the physical coupling
    between spin and gravitational/vacuum interaction

Drew's statement: "Fast angular rotation ω may create an upward force to give height h. The height h
of a flying disc is proportional to angular frequency ω × area a." This is consistent with reading
(c): the formula encodes the scaling law, not the absolute magnitude.

The implied force scaling:
If F_lift must overcome gravity: F_lift ≥ mg (for weight mg), and the achieved height under
net force F_net = F_lift - mg over time t is:
  h = ½ (F_net/m) t²  (kinematic, constant force)

For F_lift ∝ ω · a:
  F_lift = C · ω · a  (where C is a coupling constant with units [kg/(m·s)])
  F_net = C · ω · a - mg
  h = ½ (C · ω · a / m - g) t²                ... (4)

Note: equation (2) h = ω · a is the steady-state relation (perhaps terminal altitude in equilibrium
between lift force and gravity), not the kinematic trajectory. At equilibrium:
  C · ω · a = mg
  → ω_hover = mg / (C · a)                     ... (5)
At angular frequencies above ω_hover, the device rises. This establishes ω_hover as the minimum
rotation rate for levitation, inversely proportional to disc area a.

### Gyroscopic Precession Equations — Gravity-to-Precession Force Conversion

Standard gyroscopic precession (XY-plane dynamics):

Torque from gravity on gyroscope arm:
  τ = r × F_g = r × (-mg ẑ)                   ... (6)

where r is the vector from pivot to center of mass of spinning rotor.
If r = r x̂ (arm horizontal):
  τ = r(-mg)(x̂ × ẑ) = rmg ŷ                   ... (7)

Angular momentum of spinning rotor:
  L = I ω ẑ'    (ẑ' = spin axis direction)     ... (8)

where I = moment of inertia of rotor about spin axis [kg·m²], ω = spin angular velocity [rad/s].

Euler's equation for precession:
  dL/dt = τ
  L Ω_p = τ
  Ω_p = τ / L = (mgr) / (Iω)                  ... (9)

Precession angular velocity Ω_p [rad/s]:
- Proportional to gravitational acceleration g and arm length r
- Inversely proportional to moment of inertia I and spin rate ω
- Higher spin rate → slower precession (more stable gyroscope)
- The precessional motion is in the horizontal plane (XY plane)

Key result: the gravitational torque τ (which would cause the gyroscope to fall if not spinning)
is entirely converted into precessional angular velocity Ω_p. The apparent vertical load is
reduced because the gravitational work goes into maintaining horizontal precession, not downward
acceleration.

### Lorentz Force Analogy — Mathematical Structure

Standard Lorentz force (electromagnetic):
  F_EM = q (v × B)                             ... (10)

where q = charge [C], v = velocity [m/s], B = magnetic field [T].
F_EM is perpendicular to both v and B — a 90-degree force redirection.

Proposed "Lorentz force for gravity" (Drew's conceptual extension):
  F_gravity_effective = (angular momentum) × (gravitational field equivalent)

Formally, the precession equation:
  τ = dL/dt = Ω_p × L                         ... (11)

where Ω_p × L gives the rate of change of L (the precession). This has the same cross-product
structure as the Lorentz force. The gravitational field g plays the role of B:
  τ = m (r × g)    analogous to    F = q (v × B)   ... (12)

The structural analogy:
| EM Lorentz | Gravitational Analog (Drew) |
|---|---|
| charge q | mass m |
| velocity v | arm vector r |
| magnetic field B | gravitational field g |
| force F perpendicular to v,B | torque τ perpendicular to r,g |
| deflects trajectory | converts downward fall to precession |

The cross-product structure in both cases produces a 90-degree force redirection. Drew's claim is
that this 90-degree redirection in the gravitational case has physical consequences for the
effective weight and lift, just as the Lorentz force in EM redirects particle trajectories.

### Inertial Mass Tensor — Direction-Dependent Inertia in Spinning Bodies

Standard (Newtonian/GR) inertial mass: scalar m_i, isotropic:
  F = m_i · a_vector   (for any direction of a)     ... (13)

Drew's claimed anisotropic inertial mass for rapidly spinning disc:
  F = m_ij · a_j    (tensor, indices i,j ∈ {x,y,z}) ... (14)

Claimed values:
- m_tangential (along precessional orbit, XY plane tangential): m_tangential → 0 (near zero)
- m_other (other directions): m_other > m_0 (increased, m_0 = rest mass)

The inertia tensor for a spinning body in standard rigid-body mechanics:
  I_ij (moment of inertia tensor) = ∫ρ(r)(r²δ_ij - r_i r_j) dV    ... (15)

This is anisotropic for non-spherical bodies (disc, rod, etc.), but in standard mechanics this
governs rotational inertia, not the translational inertial mass m. The translational inertial mass
remains scalar m.

Drew's claim is that rapid spin couples the rotational inertia tensor to the translational inertial
mass, effectively making translational inertia direction-dependent. This would require a
coupling term in the equations of motion:

  m_eff(θ) = m_0 + ΔI(θ) · ω² / c²    (schematic form)   ... (16)

where ΔI(θ) is the direction-dependent rotational inertia correction, ω is spin rate, and c² is a
normalizing constant. At low ω, m_eff → m_0 (standard). At high ω, m_eff becomes direction-dependent.

This effective mass anisotropy directly challenges Einstein's Equivalence Principle, which requires:
  m_gravitational = m_inertial (scalar, isotropic, independent of internal state)    ... (17)

If (16) holds, then m_inertial is NOT a scalar and IS dependent on internal state (spin rate ω),
violating (17).

The measured brightness signal B(t) = B_star(t) + B_ATLAS(t) where B_star(t) is atmospheric
scintillation ("twinkling") — stochastic noise — and B_ATLAS(t) may contain a periodic component.

Fourier decomposition:
  B(t) = Σ_n [A_n cos(nω₀t) + B_n sin(nω₀t)]    ... (18)

or equivalently in terms of the power spectral density:
  S(ω) = |∫B(t) e^(-iωt) dt|²                     ... (19)

Atmospheric scintillation spectrum is dominated by low-frequency turbulence (Kolmogorov turbulence
power spectrum: S_atm(ω) ∝ ω^(-8/3) for frequencies in the inertial range). A discrete periodic
component from ATLAS rotation or pulsed emission would appear as a sharp peak in S(ω) at ω = ω_rot,
distinguishable from the smooth atmospheric background.

Observed brightness variation: factor of 3 to 10 over a few minutes (from tweet 1990322068869816757
parent context). If periodic, the rotation period T_rot = 2π/ω_rot is of order minutes.

This is standard signal analysis — Drew identifies it as the correct approach to determine whether
the brightness variations are purely atmospheric or intrinsic to the object.

working small model as a starting point.
