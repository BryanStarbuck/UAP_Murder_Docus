### Gyroscopic Force Vector Rotation — "Lorentz Force for Gravity"

Experimental context: Sydney University demonstration of a heavy spinning gyroscope being lifted
over the head with one hand. Handler reports the gyroscope feels "incredibly light" while spinning.

Drew's physics explanation:

**Step 1: Classical gravitational force on gyroscope**
A downward gravitational force F_g acts on the far end of the heavy spinning gyroscope arm.
When NOT spinning, this force makes the gyroscope very difficult to lift (large lifting force required).

**Step 2: Force vector rotation under spin**
When the gyroscope spins, the downward vector-force for gravity gets "transferred" 90-degrees
sideways into a horizontal vector-force for rotary precessional motion.

Formally: torque τ = r × F_g (where r is the arm vector from pivot to rotor center of mass,
F_g = -mg ẑ) drives precession of the angular momentum vector L = Iω.

Precession rate: Ω_p = τ / L = (mgr) / (Iω)

The precessional motion is in the XY plane (horizontal), not the Z-axis (vertical).

**Step 3: Consequence for Z-axis dynamics**
The heavy spinning gyroscope "wants to turn naturally around his body and head, due to the force
of downward-to-horizontal converted gravity." The gravitational work is channeled into horizontal
rotation, leaving less net downward force available as weight.

**Step 4: Analogy to spinning top under compressed air**
"Just like for the sideways motion of a spinning top, when we spray it with compressed air."
External force applied to a spinning top produces precessional motion, not simple deflection.

**Step 5: Analogy to Lorentz force**
"Or for the sideways motion of spinning electrons in a N-S magnetic field, when we apply an
electric force perpendicular to the N-S axis. That is called the 'Lorentz force'."

The Lorentz force: F = q(v × B)
For a charge q moving with velocity v in magnetic field B, the force is perpendicular to both
v and B. The direction is given by the cross product.

**Drew's proposed extension — "Lorentz kind of force for gravity":**
By analogy: for a rotating mass (gyroscope) in a gravitational field g, when a force is applied
(gravity F_g), the response is perpendicular to both the angular momentum L and the applied force.
This is mathematically identical to the precessional equation:

  dL/dt = τ = r × F_g

but Drew's conceptual framing elevates this to a "Lorentz-like" cross-product interaction between
gravity and spin, where the gravitational force on a spinning body is systematically redirected
perpendicular to its original direction.

**Proposed application:** "This 'Lorentz force for gravity' may relate in some way to how UFOs
or orb-spheres fly, and we should work in that direction next."

Historical credit: Eric Laithwaite, 1974 London, first demonstrated and publicized this effect
in a controversial Royal Institution lecture. Reference: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JRPC7a_AcQo

**Apparent weight reduction:**
Diverting the gravitational force reduces the apparent "gravitational weight" of the spinning disc.
The disc turns around a central rod with little force required (low inertia in the precessional
direction).

**Inertial mass anisotropy (key non-Newtonian claim):**
"Its 'inertial mass' also becomes direction-dependent due to rapid spin."
- Along the precessional circle (tangential to the precessional orbit): "There is little to no
  inertia as it turns in a large circle about that central rod."
- In other directions (e.g., along the spin axis, or radially): "inertia of the spinning disc
  is greatly increased."

This is a direct observational claim: the inertial mass tensor of the spinning body becomes
anisotropic. In standard Newtonian mechanics and GR, inertial mass is a scalar (isotropic).
The claimed effect is:

  m_inertial → m_ij (tensor, direction-dependent)

where m_ij ≠ δ_ij · m_0. Specifically:
- m_tangential (precessional direction) → 0 (effectively)
- m_other directions → > m_0 (increased)

**Equivalence Principle challenge:**
Drew states: "So much for Einstein's Equivalence Principle."
The Equivalence Principle (EP) states that gravitational mass m_g equals inertial mass m_i for
all bodies regardless of composition or internal state. The claimed direction-dependence of
inertial mass in a spinning body, if experimentally confirmed, would constitute an internal-state
dependence of m_i, violating the EP.

Note: standard physics does not predict this violation. The classical gyroscope effect (precession)
does not reduce actual inertial mass; it redistributes torque. Drew's claim is that the effective
inertial resistance to specific motions is genuinely reduced, not merely redirected.

### 3I/ATLAS Coma Composition — CO₂ Sublimation from Interstellar Object

"Carbon dioxide, the sublimation of dry ice after long travel through interstellar space."

Physical process: CO₂ at interstellar temperatures (T ~ 10-20 K in dense molecular clouds,
T ~ 2.7 K in diffuse ISM from CMB) is frozen solid. Upon entering the inner solar system and
receiving solar radiation flux F = L_☉/(4πr²), the surface temperature rises:

  T_surface ≈ (L_☉ / (16πσr²))^(1/4) · (1 - A)^(1/4)

where A is albedo, σ is Stefan-Boltzmann constant. At distances of several AU, T reaches the
CO₂ sublimation temperature (~195 K at 1 bar, lower at vacuum — CO₂ sublimes at ~120-150 K in
space conditions), producing the observed coma.

The preference for CO₂ over H₂O ice outgassing (water sublimates at ~170-200 K at vacuum
relevant distances) implies the coma begins farther from the Sun than for a typical comet,
consistent with a CO₂-rich or CO₂-dominated surface composition.
