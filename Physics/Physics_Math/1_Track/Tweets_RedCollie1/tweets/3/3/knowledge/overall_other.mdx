Context: reply to tweet about LAD1 (Leukocyte Adhesion Deficiency type 1) gene therapy trial

Drew's specific claim: lentiviruses used as gene therapy vectors can cause cancer as a side effect
via insertional mutagenesis. This is a documented clinical risk: lentiviral vectors integrate
their cargo DNA semi-randomly into the host genome. If insertion occurs in or near a proto-oncogene
promoter, it can dysregulate gene expression and trigger oncogenic transformation.

Historical examples: early retroviral gene therapy trials (X-SCID trials, 1990s-2000s) produced
leukemia in several patients due to insertional mutagenesis near the LMO2 proto-oncogene.
Lentiviral vectors (used in later trials including CAR-T cell therapy and LAD1 treatment) are
considered safer than gamma-retroviral vectors because they preferentially integrate into the
bodies of active genes rather than near promoters, but the risk is not zero.

This tweet content is biomedical, not UAP physics.

  to Jupiter

- Spectrochemistry: shedding nickel and cyanide (CN), NOT iron — anomalous elemental composition
  for a standard rocky or metallic interstellar body

common (from photodissociation of HCN and other nitriles by solar UV), but the absence of iron
