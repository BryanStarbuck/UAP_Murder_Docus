## Section: 1940698415371018587.yaml / note_tweet — Lorentz Force Analogy

**Lorentz force statement**:
- When ions of nitrogen and oxygen (or electrons) pass over a N or S magnetic field, due to a strong electric force from plus to minus, they never reach the opposite electrode but curve 90 degrees in another direction: the "Lorentz force."
- Mathematical form: **F** = q(**v** × **B**), where **v** is particle velocity, **B** is magnetic field vector.
- In the XY-plane context: if **v** is in the x-direction and **B** is in the z-direction, the Lorentz force is in the y-direction — a 90-degree redirection in the XY-plane.

**Analogy to gyroscopic behavior**:
- Just as the Lorentz force redirects a moving charge 90 degrees relative to both **v** and **B**, the gyroscopic precession torque redirects the downward gravitational force 90 degrees into rotary motion (about the Z-axis).
- The structural parallel: v ↔ angular momentum **L** of gyroscope; **B** ↔ gravitational torque; Lorentz force direction ↔ precession angular velocity direction.

## Section: 13_1940693448664784896_video.mdx — Arc Deflection Experiment

**Experimental description**:
- A ring magnet is placed with North Pole facing up between two electrodes carrying an electric arc (DC power).
- Observation: the arc flame is deflected strongly to one side (right).
- When the ring magnet is flipped (South Pole facing up), the arc deflects to the opposite side.
- This demonstrates Lorentz force deflection of the current-carrying plasma in the arc: **F** = I(**dl** × **B**), where I is arc current, **dl** is the current element direction, **B** is the magnetic field of the ring magnet (axial, Z-direction with ring magnet N-up or S-up).
- With N-up: **B** points up (Z), current in arc flows horizontally (X), force = X × Z = -Y → arc deflects to -Y.
- With S-up: **B** points down (-Z), force = X × (-Z) = +Y → arc deflects to +Y.
- XY-plane force from Z-axis magnetic field: standard Lorentz force configuration.

**Weight reduction observation**:
- During the video, a gyroscope (or spinning magnetic device) is described as feeling "incredibly light" while spinning, compared with its static weight.
- "When it's spinning, it honestly felt like it was just wanting to go up by itself."
- "I was not putting in the amount of force that you think to lift that kind of weight."
- This is a qualitative subjective report of Z-axis apparent weight reduction during spin, consistent with Laithwaite's 1974 observations.
