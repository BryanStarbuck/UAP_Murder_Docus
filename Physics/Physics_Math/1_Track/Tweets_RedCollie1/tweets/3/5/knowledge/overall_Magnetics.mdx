**Lorentz Force — Magnetic Component and Its Key Property:**

The magnetic component of the Lorentz force:

    F_mag = q(v × B)

does no work on a charged particle because F_mag is always perpendicular to v (the particle's velocity vector). Consequently:

    W = ∫ F_mag · ds = ∫ F_mag · v dt = 0

because F_mag ⊥ v implies F_mag · v = 0 at every instant.

**Physical consequence for propulsion:** Because no work is done by the magnetic component, there is no back-reaction by Newton's 3rd law in the conventional energy-transfer sense. Drew invokes this as the theoretical basis for why Lorentz-force-based propulsion evades conventional reaction constraints.

**Spinning electrons as example of same general principle:**

Spinning electrons exhibit gyromagnetic behavior: their magnetic dipole moment μ is proportional to their spin angular momentum S:

    μ = g_s · (e/2m_e) · S = g_s · μ_B · (S/ℏ)

where g_s ≈ 2.002 (electron g-factor), μ_B = eℏ/2m_e (Bohr magneton). Under an external magnetic field B, the spin precesses at the Larmor frequency:

    ω_L = (g_s · e / 2m_e) · B

Drew invokes spinning electrons as one of the three canonical examples of the general spin-based principle that "underlies all of our modern electromagnetic devices."

**Gyroscopic Precession Under 3-Phase Magnetic Torque:**

"While gyroscopic inertia resists changes to the spin axis, it doesn't prevent it entirely. When a torque is applied, any gyroscope will exhibit precession, which is rotation of the spin axis around a different axis."

Precession equation for a gyroscope under applied torque τ:

    τ = dL/dt

where L = Iω (angular momentum, I = moment of inertia, ω = spin angular velocity). For torque τ perpendicular to L:

    |Ω_precession| = τ / L = τ / (Iω)

The precession angular velocity Ω_precession is the rate at which the spin axis rotates around the torque axis.

**Application to UFO propulsion:** If the applied torque comes from the 3-phase rotating magnetic field (varying in both amplitude and phase), then Ω_precession can be modulated to steer the spin axis. The spin axis direction determines the thrust vector.

Also observed in the lab: "An example of PRECESSION in the laboratory, caused by gyroscopic inertia plus 3-phase magnetism" (tweet id 1952830817907917279).
