**Lorentz Force — Exact Formulation:**

The full Lorentz force on a charge q moving with velocity v in electric field E and magnetic field B:

    F = q(E + v × B)

The magnetic component alone:

    F_B = q(v × B)

**Zero-work property:**

    P_B = F_B · v = q(v × B) · v

Using the vector identity (A × B) · A = 0 for any vectors A, B:

    (v × B) · v = 0   ∀ v, B

Therefore:

    P_B = 0   (identically)

The magnetic force does zero power (zero work per unit time) on any charged particle, at every instant, regardless of speed or field strength.

**Consequence for Newton's 3rd law:** Since no energy is transferred from the field to the particle via the magnetic component, no back-EMF or equal-and-opposite energy reaction is induced. This is Drew's key theoretical justification for Lorentz-force propulsion being free of the conventional back-reaction constraint.

**Gyroscopic Precession — Exact Equations:**

For a symmetric spinning top (or gyroscope) with spin angular momentum L = Iω:

    τ = dL/dt

For gravitational torque on a gyroscope with center of mass displaced from pivot by r, and gravitational acceleration g:

    τ_grav = r × (−mg ẑ) = mgr (−ẑ × r̂) = mgr θ̂  [horizontal, azimuthal]

Since τ is horizontal and L is along the spin axis (initially tilted), the precession equation:

    Ω_p × L = τ

gives:

    Ω_p = τ / (L sin θ) = mgr / (Iω sin θ)   [in direction ẑ, if τ is azimuthal]

For small tilt angle θ → the precession rate increases as spin rate ω decreases (gyroscope spins slower → precesses faster → more wobble).

**Analogy to Lorentz force:** The structural analogy Drew draws:

    F_Lorentz = q(v × B)   [force ⊥ to both v and B]
    τ_gyro = r × mg         [torque ⊥ to both r and g → precession ⊥ to both L and τ]

In both cases, the output vector is perpendicular to the two input vectors — the cross-product structure is identical.

    J_r = σ E_r

Under axial magnetic field B_z = B ẑ, the Lorentz body force per unit volume:

    f = J × B = (σ E_r r̂) × (B ẑ) = σ E_r B (r̂ × ẑ) = σ E_r B (-φ̂)

The azimuthal body force density f_φ = -σ E_r B drives fluid rotation in the -φ̂ direction (clockwise viewed from above for J in +r̂ direction with B in +ẑ).

Total torque on a disc of inner radius a, outer radius b, thickness h:

    T = ∫∫∫ r · f_φ dV = -∫_a^b ∫_0^{2π} ∫_0^h σ E_r(r) B r² h dφ dr

This scales as r³ — larger disc radius gives much more torque.

**Precession Rate Under 3-Phase Magnetic Torque:**

Applied torque from 3-phase rotating magnetic field on a disc magnet with magnetic moment μ = M V (magnetization M times volume V) in rotating field B(t):

    τ_mag = μ × B(t)

For a sinusoidally rotating field B(t) = B_0(cos(ωt) x̂ + sin(ωt) ŷ):

    τ_mag = M V × B_0[cos(ωt), sin(ωt), 0]

The maximum torque (when μ is perpendicular to B) is:

    |τ_max| = M V B_0

Precession rate of gyroscope under this torque:

    Ω_precession = |τ| / |L| = M V B_0 / (I_disc · ω_spin)

Increasing either ω_spin (disc spin rate) or I_disc (moment of inertia, i.e., larger disc) reduces Ω_precession for a given magnetic torque.

    N_coils = 3 × k,   k ∈ {2, 3, 4, 5, 6}

    n_s = 120f / P   [RPM]
