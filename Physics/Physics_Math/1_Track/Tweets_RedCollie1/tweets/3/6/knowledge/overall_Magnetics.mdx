### Spinning Lorentz Force — Magnetic Coupling Between XY Rotation and Z-Axis

**Named mechanism:** "Spinning Lorentz force"

**Standard Lorentz force:** F = q(E + v × B) acts on a point charge q. For a magnetic moment m in non-uniform field: F = ∇(m · B).

**Drew's "spinning Lorentz force" interpretation:**
When a charged or magnetically polarized body undergoes simultaneous spin (ω_spin) and precession (ω_prec), the velocity of each constituent charge element has contributions from both motions. The Lorentz force on each element is:
F_element = q(v_spin × B + v_prec × B)

The v_spin × B terms, summed over the body, cancel for a symmetric body in a uniform external B-field (net force = 0, but there is a net torque producing precession — this is standard gyro-EM coupling).

The v_prec × B terms, summed over the body, can produce a net force if the precession velocity v_prec has a component parallel to B (violating the condition that all v_prec components lie in the XY plane). Drew's implicit claim is that this net force has a non-zero Z component — i.e., the "spinning Lorentz force" converts XY-plane rotational motion into Z-axis lift.

**This claim requires:** either a non-uniform B-field (∂B/∂z ≠ 0) which is standard physics and known to produce force on a magnetic moment, OR a new coupling not present in classical electromagnetism.

**Assessment:** Drew does not specify which. The gradient-force interpretation (F = ∇(m · B) with spatially varying B) is entirely within Maxwell's framework and does not require new physics. The stronger claim (vacuum coupling) goes beyond it.
