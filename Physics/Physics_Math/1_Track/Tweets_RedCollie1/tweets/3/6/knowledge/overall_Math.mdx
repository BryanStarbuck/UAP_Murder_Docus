### Spinning Lorentz Force — Mathematical Decomposition

**Standard Lorentz force on point charge:**
F = q(E + v × B)

**Velocity decomposition for spinning precessing body:**
For a charge element dq at position r in a body with:
- Spin: ω_s (along body symmetry axis n̂)
- Precession: ω_p (along vertical Z-axis)
- Center of mass position: R(t)

Velocity of charge element:
v = V_cm + ω_p × R + ω_s × r

where V_cm = velocity of center of mass (assume zero for hovering body).

**Lorentz force on charge element:**
dF = dq [(ω_p × R) × B + (ω_s × r) × B]

**Integrated over whole body:**
First term (precession contribution):
F_prec = Q_total · (ω_p × R) × B  (Q_total = net charge of body)

For an electrically neutral body: Q_total = 0 → F_prec = 0. No net force from precession alone in uniform B.

Second term (spin contribution):
F_spin = ∫ dq (ω_s × r) × B

For a spherically symmetric charge distribution: F_spin = 0 (cancels by symmetry).

**Conclusion from standard physics:**
Standard Lorentz force gives F_net = 0 for a neutral body, regardless of spin or precession, in a uniform external B.

**Drew's "spinning Lorentz force" claim implies:**
Either:
(a) The body carries net charge, so Q_total ≠ 0 and F_prec ≠ 0 (possible but not stated), OR
(b) The external B is non-uniform (∂B/∂z ≠ 0), and the magnetic moment m = ∫ (r × J) dV gives a force F = ∇(m · B) ≠ 0 (standard physics, possible with designed field gradient), OR
(c) A non-electromagnetic vacuum coupling produces force on the precessing body beyond what Maxwell's equations permit.

Drew does not resolve which case applies. The "spinning Lorentz force" remains a named phenomenological claim without closed-form derivation in the source material.
