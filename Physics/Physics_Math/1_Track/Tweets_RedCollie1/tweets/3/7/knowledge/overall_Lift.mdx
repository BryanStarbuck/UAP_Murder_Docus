### Z-Axis Levitation via Magnetic Focal Point and Spinning Magnet

**Core mechanism for Z-axis lift:**

Step 1 — Static levitation (hovering):
A large ring magnet/coil below creates an axial magnetic field with gradient ∂B_z/∂z. A spinning disc magnet placed above the ring finds a "focal point" at height z = z_f where:

  F_z_magnetic(z_f) = m · ∂B_z/∂z|_{z_f} = 0

Gravity is balanced by this magnetic force at z_f:

  m · ∂B_z/∂z|_{z_f} = M · g   →   equilibrium levitation

The spin is essential: Earnshaw's theorem forbids stable static equilibrium for a non-spinning magnet. The gyroscopic angular momentum provides the dynamic stabilization that Earnshaw's theorem denies to static systems (Levitron principle).

Step 2 — Active Z-axis thrust (upward translation):
When the spinning magnet rises from z_f, the new focal point z_f' > z_f because the field gradient at higher z has a different magnitude. If the new equilibrium point is always ahead of the current position, the magnet continuously chases a moving equilibrium — generating sustained upward thrust.

**The focal point migration argument:**
At height z_f, the equilibrium is maintained. As the craft rises by dz, the magnetic force balance shifts to a new z_f' = z_f + δ > z_f. The craft is now below its new equilibrium and continues to rise. Drew proposes this creates "a theoretical basis for forward space travel (thrust) where there is no gravity."

Note: This argument requires that the equilibrium point shift (δ > 0 for each increment of rise), which depends on the specific field geometry of the ring magnet. For a simple current loop, the equilibrium point above a repulsive magnet does not migrate upward indefinitely — the force goes to zero at large z. Drew's proposal implicitly requires a more complex field geometry or an active control mechanism that adjusts the ring coil current as the craft rises.

### Gyroscope Mechanism: Redirecting Gravitational Force to Horizontal Plane

**Observed in video (13_1940911383069020160_video.mdx):**
- A spinning gyroscope placed on a tabletop remains vertical rather than falling — gyroscopic rigidity.
- A spinning gyroscope "balances on a string" — apparent levitation, center of mass suspended at string contact point.
- When spin ceases, the gyroscope falls — confirming the stabilizing force is purely gyroscopic.
- Can be "lifted with a string looped around one end" — the gyroscope exhibits no tendency to tip when spun.

**Physical chain from horizontal dynamics to Z-axis support:**
The gravitational torque τ = r × Mg acts horizontally (perpendicular to L). Instead of producing angular acceleration in the direction of the torque (which would cause the gyroscope to fall), it produces precession in the direction perpendicular to both L and τ:

  Ω_p = (r × Mg) / |L| = (r × Mg) / (I·ω)

This horizontal precession redirects the gravitational energy input into rotational kinetic energy of the precession, rather than into downward translational kinetic energy. The gyroscope's center of mass does not accelerate downward — it moves in a horizontal circle.

**Extension to magnetic driving (proposed experiment):**
With a wire coil below the spinning gyroscope providing upward magnetic force F_mag = m·∂B/∂z:

  Net axial equation: M·z̈ = F_mag(z) - M·g

If F_mag > Mg: net upward acceleration → lift (anti-gravity condition)
If F_mag = Mg: hover (zero net axial force, identical to magnetic focal point equilibrium)

The magnetic coil is proposed to supply exactly this additional vertical force, cooperating with gyroscopic precession to achieve stable levitation.

### 3-Phase Spin → Z-Axis Rise: Wobbling Magnet Mechanism

**Direct statement of lift mechanism:**
"The 3-phase spin of a wobbling magnet, against its surrounding vacuum, will cause it to be 'pushed up' along a central pole. In the same way that a 'spinning top' rises."

**Spinning top rise analogy:**
A precessing top in a gravitational field: the precession is maintained at constant height if there is no friction. With a small amount of friction, the precession rate decreases, and the top's axis nutates to lower angles — the top "sleeps" and eventually falls. However, for tops on smooth surfaces (low friction regime near the spin-down point), the top rises — the "tippe top" effect: as spin slows, the contact dynamics push the center of mass upward before the top falls.

For the magnetically driven gyroscope:
- 3-phase electromagnetic driving maintains ω (spin rate) against resistive losses
- The Z-axis magnetic gradient provides the upward restoring force
- The precessing magnet "wobbles" against the vacuum (no mechanical friction) — the wobble represents nutation
- This wobble/nutation, combined with the axial gradient force, produces helical upward motion

**Coupling equation:**
The full equation for the magnetically driven gyroscope includes:
1. Translational Z-axis: M·z̈ = F_z(z) - Mg
2. Rotational (spin): I·ω̇ = τ_drive - τ_dissipation
3. Precessional: Ω_p = τ_perp / (I·ω)
4. Nutational: involves the full Euler equations for rigid body motion in an axisymmetric potential

For sustained lift (z̈ > 0): F_z(z) > Mg, satisfied at z < z_f where the magnetic force exceeds gravity.

### Spinning Lorentz Force as Z-Axis Force Generator

The context: "What is a spinning Lorentz force?"

For a current-carrying conductor rotating at angular frequency ω in a static magnetic field B:
- In the lab frame, the conductor moves with velocity v = ω × r
- The motional EMF drives radial currents: ε = ∮ (v × B) · dl
- These radial currents in the axial field B_z produce azimuthal Lorentz forces f = J_r × B_z·ẑ = J_r·B_z·φ̂

If instead the system has axial symmetry and the total angular momentum of the rotating current system is considered, the interaction between the spinning current distribution and the external field can produce a net axial force via:

  F_z = ∂(L_em)/∂z   (gradient of electromagnetic field angular momentum)

This is the electromagnetic analog of the magnetic gradient force on a dipole, but for a distributed current system. Drew's "spinning Lorentz force" likely refers to this Z-axis component of the Lorentz force that results from XY-plane rotation coupled to an axial magnetic field.
