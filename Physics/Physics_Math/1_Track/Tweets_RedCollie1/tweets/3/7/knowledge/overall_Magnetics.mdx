  dL/dt = τ

This causes precession: the gyroscope's spin axis rotates in a horizontal plane (XY-plane) rather than falling under gravity. The gravitational "downward" force is converted into a "sideways" (horizontal) force producing uniform precession. The gyroscope does not fall.

Drew's analogy: gravity is like a debt to the bank. The gyroscopic precession is like borrowing from another source (angular momentum) to pay the debt, so gravity "no longer pulls you down."

  F_z = m · ∂B_z/∂z

Drew plans to test both DC and AC configurations. For AC current the time-averaged force will depend on the phase relationship between the oscillating field and the spinning gyroscope's orientation.

- 18 wire coils: 3-phase with 6 per phase

This is an axial-flux topology: the magnetic field produced by the stator coils is directed along the rotation axis (Z-axis), rather than radially as in conventional radial-flux motors. In an axial-flux motor, the torque is generated by the interaction between the axial stator field and the permanent magnets (or induced currents) in the rotor.

**3-phase rotating field:**
For a 3-phase system with N poles per phase, the stator coils are energized with currents:

  I_A(t) = I_max · cos(ωt)
  I_B(t) = I_max · cos(ωt - 2π/3)

These produce a rotating magnetic field vector that sweeps through 360° in one cycle period T = 2π/ω, driving the rotor (spinning magnet or disc) at the synchronous speed ω_s = ω/p (where p is the number of pole pairs).

**UFO application:**

By Newton's Third Law, as the 3-phase coils drive the central magnet clockwise, the coils themselves experience an equal and opposite anti-clockwise force (from 2034917623348199465.yaml). This reaction torque acts on the outer rim/hull structure, driving counter-rotation.
