### Ring Magnet Focal Point: Axial Magnetic Equilibrium

A large ring magnet (or a current-carrying ring coil) produces an axially symmetric magnetic field. On the symmetry axis (Z-axis), the field has the form:

  B_z(z) = (μ_0 · m) / (4π) · 2/(z³ + ...) [far field approximation for a magnetic dipole]

More precisely for a thin ring of radius R carrying magnetic surface charge density σ_m:

  B_z(z) = (μ_0 · σ_m · R) / (2) · z / (R² + z²)^(3/2)

The axial field gradient ∂B_z/∂z changes sign at some z = z_f above (or below) the ring plane. At z_f, a small permanent magnet on the axis experiences:

  F_z = m_z · ∂B_z/∂z = 0   (equilibrium)

with ∂²(F_z)/∂z² < 0 (stable equilibrium in z) if the geometry satisfies Earnshaw's theorem exception for spinning objects (Earnshaw's theorem forbids static equilibrium for non-spinning magnetic objects, but rotating magnets can be stabilized by gyroscopic rigidity).

Drew calls this a "focal point" and explicitly invokes the Lagrange point analogy. In celestial mechanics, Lagrange points L1–L5 occur where the gravitational forces of two massive bodies and the centrifugal force in the rotating frame combine to zero net force. The magnetic focal point is the electromagnetic analog: the ring magnet's repulsive force on the spinning disc at short range and the attractive force at longer range combine to produce a net-zero axial force at z_f.

**Claimed height:** z_f ≈ 100 mm above the large wire coil for the experimental scale described.

### Gyroscope Physics: Conversion of Gravitational Torque to Horizontal Precession

The gyroscope equation of motion:

  dL/dt = τ_ext

where L = I·ω is the angular momentum (I = moment of inertia about spin axis, ω = spin angular velocity), and τ_ext is the external torque.

For a gyroscope with spin axis initially vertical, a gravitational torque τ = r × Mg acts horizontally (perpendicular to L). The result is:

  Ω_precession = τ / |L| = (M·g·r) / (I·ω)

The spin axis precesses horizontally (in the XY-plane) at angular rate Ω_precession. The key phenomenological result (confirmed in video transcription 13_1940911383069020160): the gyroscope does NOT fall. The downward gravitational force is "converted" to sideways precession. The gyroscope "balances on a string" and appears to levitate.

**Physical interpretation Drew uses:**
The gravitational debt is "paid" by borrowing from the angular momentum. The downward force is redirected horizontally, leaving no net downward acceleration of the center of mass (in the precessing reference frame, to leading order in the approximation |ω| >> Ω_precession).

**Experimental confirmation (video):**
- Spinning gyroscope resists tilting/rotating (rigidity in space)
- Stands vertical on tabletop due to gyroscopic stabilization force
- Balances on a string — apparent levitation
- When spin stops: restoring force disappears; gyroscope falls normally
