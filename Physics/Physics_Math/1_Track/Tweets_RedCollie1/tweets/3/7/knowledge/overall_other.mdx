### Archimedean Spirals in Crop Formations: Geometric/Mathematical Content

Drew identifies a recurring geometric motif — the Archimedean spiral — in crop formations attributed to "Quetzal" (a specific crop artist entity Drew tracks).

**Archimedean spiral definition:**
In polar coordinates (r, θ):
  r(θ) = a + b · θ

where:
- a = starting radius offset
- b = rate of radial increase per radian
- The arc length between successive turns is constant: Δr = 2π·b (uniform spacing)

**Distinction from logarithmic/equiangular spiral:**
A logarithmic spiral: r = a · e^(b·θ) — the spacing between turns increases with radius (Fibonacci, nautilus shell, galaxy arms).
The Archimedean spiral has constant inter-turn spacing — it is a mechanical spiral, not a growth spiral.

**Occurrence in UAP physics context:**
Archimedean spiral geometry appears in:
1. Flat coil winding design (the 3-phase flat coils Drew proposes have Archimedean spiral wire paths)
2. Vortex flow patterns (the Birkeland current spiral, tornado cross-section)

### 3I/ATLAS Interstellar Object: Physical Characterization

Drew discusses the interstellar object 3I/ATLAS (detected 2025):

- Bright, round core with diameter 20–40 km (estimated by comparison to known CO2 coma size)
- Large brown cloud of dust and carbon dioxide (not water ice — different from typical comets)
- Bright orange anti-tail on the left (anti-tail points toward the Sun, indicating sunward-driven particles — fine dust or gas pushed by radiation pressure in the anti-solar direction)
- Long blue tail (gas/plasma tail pointing anti-sunward, driven by solar wind)

Drew suggests the 20 km "shiny surface" implies an artificial origin — too bright for a comet nucleus of that size. Natural comet nuclei have very low albedo (~0.03–0.08); a 20 km "shiny" object would require an albedo much higher, consistent with Drew's hypothesis that 3I/ATLAS is an artificial (spacecraft) origin.

**Note on anti-tail physics:**
An anti-tail forms when larger dust particles, less susceptible to radiation pressure than fine particles, orbit near the comet's path and are seen as a sunward extension from Earth's perspective when Earth crosses the comet's orbital plane. The observation of a bright orange anti-tail is normal for comets with substantial dust component; it does not require any non-standard physics.
