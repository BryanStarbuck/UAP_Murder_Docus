**Mercury spinning in a magnetic field — Lorentz force mechanism**

Drew provides a mechanistic explanation for why liquid mercury spins when subjected to a combined DC current and external magnetic field. The full note_tweet text:

"Mercury spins in a magnetic field due to what is called a 'Lorentz force'. Basically we apply a strong DC current from in-to-out or vice-versa. The 'force' of that electric field gets turned by 90 degrees, to make the mercury spins, because all of the nuclear and electron spin motions 'line up' north-south, parallel to an applied magnetic field. Such fast spin motions turn the electric force by 90 degree, just like for blowing air onto a spinning top."

**Mechanism decomposition:**

1. External static magnetic field B applied (orientation: north-south axis).
2. DC current J applied radially (in-to-out or out-to-in through the mercury volume).
3. Nuclear and electron spin angular momenta align parallel to B (spin polarization under external field — standard Zeeman-type alignment).
4. The Lorentz force F = q(v × B) acts on current-carrying charge carriers; with J radial and B axial, the cross product produces an azimuthal force, driving rotation about the symmetry axis.
5. Drew frames the 90-degree rotation of the electric force as analogous to gyroscopic precession: "blowing air onto a spinning top" redirects the momentum by 90 degrees.

The analogy to gyroscopic precession is non-standard in classical Lorentz force treatment. Drew implies that the collective alignment of nuclear and electron spin angular momenta (bulk spin polarization) under B amplifies or modifies the Lorentz azimuthal torque, not merely through classical F = qv × B but through a spin-mediated torque transfer. This is a speculative extension beyond standard MHD.

**Relation to UAP propulsion:** Drew presents mercury-spin under Lorentz force as a laboratory analog of UAP magnetic propulsion systems — the same 90-degree force-rotation principle is claimed to underlie UAP field drive.

**5-phase vs. 3-phase rotating field comparison:**

A 3-phase rotating field has 3 discrete stator poles at 120° spacing; temporal phase separation = 120°. The resulting rotating B-field has 6-fold symmetry in one electrical cycle. A 5-phase rotating field has 5 stator poles at 72° spacing; temporal phase separation = 72°. The resulting rotating B-field has 10-fold symmetry per cycle, producing significantly lower spatial harmonic content and more uniform field rotation. If UAP propulsion depends on the smoothness and uniformity of field rotation (e.g., for coupling to space-time or for maintaining stable gyromagnetic resonance), 5-phase would be theoretically superior to 3-phase.

**Physics of magnetic Lenz repulsion at high frequency:**

Lenz's law states that induced currents oppose the change in flux that created them. At very high AC frequencies (500 kHz range), the skin depth in conductive media δ = √(2ρ/ωμ) becomes extremely small, and induced eddy currents in any conductive medium become very large. In ionized gas (plasma), the conductivity σ can be high enough at 500 kHz Tesla-coil frequencies to produce significant induced currents. The magnetic pressure P_B = B²/(2μ₀) from these induced currents produces a Lenz repulsion — a force expelling the conducting plasma from regions of high field gradient.

Drew notes the effect only appears at very high frequencies (≥ ~500 kHz), not at low frequencies. This is consistent with the frequency-dependent nature of inductive coupling: at low frequencies, the inductive impedance jωL is small, induced currents are small, and Lenz repulsion is negligible. At 500 kHz, inductive coupling becomes dominant.

**Connection to Tesla's claim about ground conductivity:** At high AC frequencies, the displacement current term ε₀(∂E/∂t) in Maxwell's equations becomes significant even in poorly conducting media. Tesla's observation that earth becomes highly conductive at high frequencies is consistent with modern understanding of radiofrequency ground propagation and displacement current effects.

**Relevance to UAP propulsion:** If a UAP generates a high-frequency magnetic field at its boundary layer, Lenz repulsion of ionized atmospheric plasma could contribute to lift or thrust. Drew presents this as an "ion rocket" mechanism — directional Lenz repulsion of ionized gas provides reaction thrust without propellant mass (the ion medium is environmental).

**Gyroscopic precession and 90-degree force redirection**

"At low spin, the top precesses in a horizontal plane, because the downward force of gravity has been spin-shifted by 90 degrees from 'down' to 'sideways'."

This describes the standard gyroscopic precession result: for a spinning top with angular momentum L along its spin axis, an applied torque τ = r × F_gravity produces precession at angular velocity Ω_p = τ/L (for L >> precession effects). The gravitational torque, instead of producing a downward tipping, is redirected by 90 degrees (by the cross-product geometry of the precession equation dL/dt = τ) into a horizontal circular precession.

The 3-phase power is the mechanism for adding angular momentum to the rotor, driving the transition from low-spin (horizontal precession) to high-spin (vertical rise), which Drew claims produces the anti-gravity force.

**3-phase power application:** Three-phase AC current through coils arranged at 120° spacing produces a rotating magnetic field at the supply frequency. This rotating field can couple inductively to a ferromagnetic rotor to increase its spin speed — this is the operating principle of a 3-phase induction motor. Drew is applying the same principle to a free-spinning gyroscopic rotor to increase its angular momentum and trigger the hypothesized gravity-opposing vertical rise.
