### Apparatus: 6-Coil Ring, U-U-V-V-W-W Wiring

The experimental coil assembly consists of six wire coils arranged in a ring, wired in the sequence U-U-V-V-W-W. In standard 3-phase electrical notation:
- Phase U: two coils at positions separated by 60°
- Phase V: two coils at 60° offset from U pair
- Phase W: two coils at 60° offset from V pair

The three phases (U, V, W) are driven with currents offset by 120° in time:
> I_U(t) = I_0 cos(ωt)
> I_V(t) = I_0 cos(ωt − 2π/3)
> I_W(t) = I_0 cos(ωt − 4π/3)

This produces a rotating magnetic field whose spatial angle φ(t) = ωt in the plane of the coil ring. The field is not simply oscillating — it is spatially rotating, sweeping through 360° per electrical cycle. This is the operating principle of AC induction motors and is standard in three-phase machinery.

### Helical Field Structure

Drew explicitly states: "Those 3-phase wire coils create a HELICAL magnetic field, through which a permanent magnet can rise as it spins, always upward against the force of gravity."

The claim of a helical field structure is physically interpretable as follows: the combination of the rotating azimuthal component (from the 3-phase rotation) and the axial component (from the coil geometry along the Z-axis of the ring bore) produces a composite field vector that traces a helix as a function of both azimuthal angle and axial position. A magnet precessing about the Z-axis within this helical field would experience a net upward force if its precession is phase-matched with the field rotation — analogous to how a corkscrew-threaded bolt moves upward when rotated in the correct direction through a helical groove.

The helical field analogy is significant because it implies a topological matching condition:
- The field helix has a pitch determined by (ω_field, coil geometry)
- The spinning magnet has a precession frequency ω_prec
- Net upward transport requires ω_prec ≈ ω_field (resonance condition)

This is consistent with the video observation that levitation occurs within a specific frequency window (~20–34 Hz) and fails outside it.

### Pole Orientation Independence — Critical Magnetic Anomaly

**Observed:** Both N-up and S-up orientations of the permanent magnet produce identical upward force.

**Classical prediction (violated):** In classical dipole-field theory, the axial force on a magnetic dipole **m** in a field **B** is:
> F_z = (m · ∇)B_z = m_z (∂B_z/∂z)

For N-up (m_z > 0) in a field with ∂B_z/∂z > 0: F_z > 0 (upward)
For S-up (m_z < 0) in the same field: F_z < 0 (downward)

The observation that both orientations give upward force violates this classical prediction. This could be explained by:
1. **The dominant force is not axial dipole-gradient but gyroscopic:** The spinning magnet's angular momentum **L** = I**ω** couples to the field through spin-orbit-like interaction, and the direction of **L** is the same regardless of which magnetic pole faces up (because L depends on rotation direction, not magnetic orientation). If the drive direction of rotation is maintained (same 3-phase phase sequence), the angular momentum direction is fixed and the "upward" force follows from the gyroscopic precession, not from magnetic polarity.
2. **Alternating field:** The rotating 3-phase field has no preferred axial polarity — it rotates in a plane — so it cannot discriminate between N-up and S-up orientations in a time-averaged sense.

### 3-Phase Rotation Direction Independence

**Observed:** Reversing the rotation direction of the 3-phase field (phase sequence reversal: U-V-W → U-W-V) does not change the direction or magnitude of the upward force on the magnet.

**Implication:** The force is not due to a chiral interaction between the field's rotational chirality and the magnet's spin chirality. If it were, reversing the field rotation would reverse the force. The independence of both field rotation direction and magnet orientation from the direction of the upward force is a strong experimental constraint: the force magnitude is determined by some scalar property of the interaction, not by a vector chirality.

Possible mechanisms consistent with both symmetry observations:
- The upward force is proportional to (ω_spin)², which is chirality-independent
- The force arises from a magnetic pressure gradient or radiation pressure from the rotating field, not from directional torque transfer

### Lorentz Force in Mercury / Conducting Liquid (1986927009604509972.yaml)

The Lorentz force mechanism for liquid-metal or conducting-fluid rotation:

> **F** = q**v** × **B**   (single-charge form)
> **f** = **J** × **B**   (volumetric form, **J** = current density, A/m²)

Application to mercury-spin analogy described by Drew:
- Strong DC current applied from in-to-out (radial direction): **J** = J_r r̂
- Ambient magnetic field **B** = B_z ẑ (axial, from permanent magnet or external coil)
- Resulting Lorentz force: **f** = J_r B_z (r̂ × ẑ) = J_r B_z (-φ̂)

The cross-product r̂ × ẑ = -φ̂ means the force is azimuthal (tangential), producing rotation of the conducting fluid. This is the mechanism Drew identifies as causing mercury to spin when radial DC current is applied through an axial magnetic field.

**Extension to UFO model:** In the proposed "third component" experiment (13_1980840868627058688_video.mdx), the central stainless-steel dowel carries electric current (**J** = J_z ẑ). The rotating 3-phase field provides an azimuthal/toroidal **B** component. The Lorentz interaction **J** × **B** = J_z ẑ × B_φ φ̂ = J_z B_φ (ẑ × φ̂) = J_z B_φ r̂ would produce a radial force, potentially modifying the pressure distribution inside the apparatus and creating an asymmetric force balance. Whether this breaks the F_up = F_down symmetry that prevents net external lift has not yet been measured.

### Rotating Field Geometry (13_1980840868627058688_video.mdx)

From the video transcription: "When we turn on the three-phase power, it will make a rotating magnetic field... it will go around those three-phase coils clockwise."

The rotating magnetic field in the plane of the coil ring:
> **B**(t) = B_0 [cos(ωt) x̂ + sin(ωt) ŷ]

This is the standard 3-phase rotating field expression. The magnitude |**B**| = B_0 is constant; only the direction rotates. The angular frequency ω = 2πf where f is the electrical supply frequency (varied experimentally from ~10 to 34 Hz in the lab).

### Gyroscope Analogy (1980841049368195176.yaml)

The referenced tweet (1980840769926963669) states: "a fast-spinning magnet acts like a gyroscope, and thus is..." (truncated). The gyroscope analogy is central to Drew's physical model. A spinning gyroscope precesses under an applied torque τ:
> **τ** = d**L**/dt = **Ω** × **L**

where **Ω** is the precession angular velocity and **L** = I**ω**_spin. The torque is provided by the interaction between the spinning magnetic dipole and the gradient of the rotating 3-phase field. The precession manifests as a continuous upward drift rather than a simple precession in a plane, because the field itself is rotating and the net torque has a persistent axial component.

For a magnetic dipole **m** in a time-varying field **B**(t):
> **τ** = **m** × **B**(t)

When **B**(t) rotates at the same frequency as the magnet's spin, the torque is not zero on average — it can have a sustained component driving the magnet along the Z axis (up or down depending on resonance phase).
