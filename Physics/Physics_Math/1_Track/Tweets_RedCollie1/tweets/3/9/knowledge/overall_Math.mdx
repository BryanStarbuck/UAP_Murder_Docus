### Equation Set 1: Newton's Third Law Applied to Spinning Magnet + Coil System

Let:
- F_up = upward force on spinning permanent magnet (Z-axis, +ẑ direction)
- F_down = downward reaction force on 3-phase coil assembly (Z-axis, −ẑ direction)
- W_magnet = weight of permanent magnet = m_mag · g
- W_coils = weight of coil assembly = m_coils · g
- W_scale = apparent weight registered by scale under entire device

**Observed relations (experimental):**

> F_up = F_down   (equal and opposite internal forces)

> W_scale = (m_mag + m_coils) · g = const   (invariant under drive activation)

> ΔW_scale = 0   when spinning magnet drive is ON

These equations express that the spinning-magnet/helical-field interaction is an internal force pair within the combined system. No momentum is transferred to the external reference frame (the scale/ground).

**Derived implication:**

Net external impulse on system per unit time = 0:
> d(p_system)/dt = F_external = W_scale − (m_mag + m_coils)g = 0

The system is in static equilibrium in the external frame despite large internal Z-axis forces.

### Equation Set 2: Condition for Net External Lift (The Open Problem)

For a "UFO drive" to work, we need:
> F_up > F_down   (net upward force on vehicle frame)

Or equivalently, we need to break the symmetry F_up = F_down by providing an external momentum sink. Drew identifies this as requiring a "third component." Mathematically, this would appear as:

> F_net = F_up − F_reaction_on_vehicle = ε > 0

where F_reaction_on_vehicle < F_up because some fraction of the reaction is directed to the "third component" rather than to the coil platform.

## Source: 1931675077818667148.yaml — Gyroscopic Precession Mathematics

### Gyroscope Precession Under Gravitational + Magnetic Torque

For a spinning permanent magnet with:
- Moment of inertia I (about spin axis)
- Spin angular frequency ω_spin
- Angular momentum L = I · ω_spin (along spin axis, ẑ assumed)
- Mass m, gravitational force F_g = mg downward

Classical gyroscope precession angular velocity about vertical:
> Ω_prec = τ / L = (r × F_g) / (I · ω_spin)

For a symmetric top precessing about the vertical:
> Ω_prec = m · g · d / (I · ω_spin)

where d = distance from pivot to center of mass.

**Extended to magnetic case:**

When the magnet is also experiencing a magnetic torque τ_mag = m_dipole × B_rotating, the effective precession includes a magnetic term:

> Ω_prec^{eff} = (τ_grav + τ_mag) / L = [m·g·d + |m_dipole||B|sin(α)] / (I·ω_spin)

where α = angle between magnetic dipole moment and rotating field.

**Resonance condition for sustained upward drift:**

The upward Z-axis drift of the magnet (as opposed to simple azimuthal precession) requires that the rotating field's angular frequency ω_field match the natural precession frequency of the spinning magnet:

> ω_field = Ω_prec^{eff}

At this resonance, the field continuously feeds energy into the precession, converting the precession into net Z-axis translation rather than closed circular motion.

**Frequency window from experiment:**
- Lower bound: ~10–20 Hz (onset of levitation)
- Upper bound: ~34 Hz (loss of coupling in the experimental run documented in 13_1959450870925713409_video.mdx)

Width of resonance window: ~14 Hz, centered around ~22–27 Hz in that run.

## Source: 1986927009604509972.yaml (referenced tweet) — Lorentz Force Mathematics

### Lorentz Force on Current-Carrying Conductor in External Magnetic Field

Single-particle Lorentz force:
> **F** = q(**E** + **v** × **B**)

For a conductor with volume current density **J** [A/m²]:
> **f** = **J** × **B**   [N/m³]

### Mercury Spin Geometry (Cylindrical Coordinates r, φ, z)

Given:
- Radial DC current density: **J** = J_r r̂   (current flows radially outward)
- Axial magnetic field: **B** = B_z ẑ   (field along axis)

Cross product:
> **f** = J_r r̂ × B_z ẑ = J_r · B_z · (r̂ × ẑ)

In cylindrical coordinates: r̂ × ẑ = −φ̂

Therefore:
> **f** = −J_r · B_z · φ̂

For J_r > 0 (outward current) and B_z > 0 (upward field):
> **f** is in the −φ̂ direction (clockwise rotation when viewed from +z)

To reverse spin direction: either reverse current direction (J_r → −J_r) or reverse field (B_z → −B_z).

**Dimensional analysis:**
- [J_r] = A/m²
- [B_z] = T = kg/(A·s²)
- [J_r · B_z] = A/m² · kg/(A·s²) = kg/(m²·s²) = N/m³   ✓

### Axial Current in Central Dowel + Rotating Field

Given:
- Axial current in dowel: **J** = J_z ẑ
- Azimuthal (toroidal) component of rotating 3-phase field: **B** = B_φ φ̂

Cross product:
> **f** = J_z ẑ × B_φ φ̂ = J_z · B_φ · (ẑ × φ̂)

In cylindrical coordinates: ẑ × φ̂ = −r̂

Therefore:
> **f** = −J_z · B_φ · r̂   (radially inward for J_z > 0, B_φ > 0)

**Physical interpretation:** The Lorentz force on the current-carrying axial dowel in the azimuthal component of the rotating field produces a radially inward pressure (magnetic pressure). If the field is rotating and B_φ has a time-varying sign, the time-averaged radial force may be non-zero due to correlation between J_z and B_φ(t).

### Lorentz Force with Liquid Metal and Rotating Field

If liquid metal (LM) fills the bowl around the central dowel, induced currents in the LM from the rotating field interact with the field itself:

Induced current density in LM from time-varying rotating field:
> **J**_{ind} = σ · (**E**_{ind})   where σ = electrical conductivity of LM

Induced electric field from rotating **B**:
> **E**_{ind} = −∂**A**/∂t   where **A** is the magnetic vector potential of the rotating field

The resulting body force on the LM:
> **f**_{LM} = **J**_{ind} × **B**_{total}

This force distribution is non-uniform (depends on position within the bowl) and could produce a net Z-axis pressure differential if the geometry is asymmetric about the midplane.

## Mathematical Relationships Between Key Quantities

### Resonance Condition (Spinning Magnet / Helical Field)

> ω_field = Ω_prec = m_g · g · d_CM / (I_mag · ω_spin)

Solving for the spin frequency needed for levitation at a given field frequency:
> ω_spin = m_g · g · d_CM / (I_mag · ω_field)

For ω_field ~ 2π × 25 rad/s ≈ 157 rad/s, this gives a constraint on the magnet's spin angular velocity needed to maintain resonance.

### Scale Reading vs. Internal Force

> W_scale(t) = (m_mag + m_coils) · g − F_net_external(t)

Experimental observation: W_scale(t) = const = (m_mag + m_coils) · g

Therefore: F_net_external(t) = 0 for all t during operation.

This is the mathematical statement of the "no net lift" result that Drew acknowledges and identifies as the barrier to a functional UFO drive.

### Force Required for "Third Component" to Break Symmetry

If the third component redirects a fraction η of the reaction force away from the coil platform to an external reference:
> F_net = F_up − (1 − η) · F_up = η · F_up

For F_up ~ 10–20 N and η → 1 (perfect redirection):
> F_net → 10–20 N

This is sufficient to levitate a small mass (1–2 kg) against Earth's gravity (g ≈ 10 m/s²): F_lift ≥ m · g ≥ 1 × 10 = 10 N. The system in principle has enough raw internal force to achieve this if the symmetry can be broken.
