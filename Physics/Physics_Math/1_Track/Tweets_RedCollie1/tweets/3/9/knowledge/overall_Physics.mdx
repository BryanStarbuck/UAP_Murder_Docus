## Source: 1931675077818667148.yaml (note_tweet.text) — Gravity-Physics of Spinning Permanent Magnet

### Problem Statement

Dr. Drew frames his investigation as studying the "gravity-physics" of a spinning permanent magnet interacting with a 3-phase wire coil ring. This phrasing — "gravity-physics" — signals that the target phenomenon lies in the intersection of gravitational dynamics and magnetic dynamics, not classical electromagnetism alone.

### Physical System

- Permanent ring magnet: 70 mm outer diameter, 14 mm hole diameter, 3 mm thickness
- Coil ring: 6 coils, 3-phase (U-U-V-V-W-W wiring)
- Degrees of freedom: Z-axis translation (up/down), spin about Z-axis

### Dynamical Equations (Classical Framework, Extended by Observation)

Under classical mechanics, the forces on the spinning magnet in the Z direction are:

> F_z^{net} = F_magnetic(B, m, z) + F_gravity + F_gyroscopic

where:
- F_gravity = −mg (downward, g ≈ 9.8 m/s²)
- F_magnetic(B, m, z) = (m·∇)B_z (axial dipole-gradient force)
- F_gyroscopic = coupling between spin angular momentum and field-induced torque gradient

The observed result: the magnet rises (F_z^{net} > 0) regardless of the sign of (m·∇)B_z, meaning the gyroscopic term dominates and produces upward force even when classical magnetic force would be expected to be downward (reversed pole orientation). This is the non-classical aspect of the reported result.

### Reaction Force (Newton's Third Law Applied to Closed System)

> F_coils↓ = −F_magnet↑ = −F_up

Total external force on the system:
> F_external = F_up + F_down = 0

This is classical in form but produces an interesting result: the internal anti-gravity-like force cannot be harvested as net external force without a mechanism to preferentially anchor one component (coils) to an external reference frame.

### Force Magnitude

Reported maximum upward force of spinning magnet: **1–2 kg-force** (approximately 10–20 N). This is not a trivially small effect — it represents a substantial force for a laboratory-scale permanent magnet.

### Frequency-Dependent Resonance Window

From video transcriptions (13_1959450870925713409_video.mdx):
- Stable levitation observed from ~20 Hz to ~34 Hz
- Below ~10–20 Hz: insufficient coupling (magnet doesn't rise)
- Above ~34 Hz in this run: instability, magnet falls

This resonance window implies a matching condition between:
- The magnet's spin frequency (set by 3-phase drive frequency)
- The magnet's natural gyroscopic precession frequency ω_prec = τ/L = (m × B)/Iω

At resonance, ω_drive ≈ ω_prec, and sustained torque transfer occurs. Above resonance, the magnet cannot precess fast enough to track the rotating field and loses coupling.

## Source: 1986927009604509972.yaml — Lorentz Force Physics (Mercury Spin)

### Lorentz Force Framework

The Lorentz force on a charge carrier:
> **F** = q(**E** + **v** × **B**)

For a charge distribution with current density **J** = nq**v** (n = carrier density, q = charge, **v** = drift velocity):
> **f** = **J** × **B**   [N/m³, body force density]

### Mercury Spin Geometry

Applied to a conducting disk or cylinder of mercury:
- DC radial current applied: **J** = J_r(r) r̂
- Axial magnetic field: **B** = B_z ẑ
- Lorentz force: **f** = J_r B_z (r̂ × ẑ) = −J_r B_z φ̂

The negative sign in the standard right-hand coordinate system means the force is in the −φ direction for positive J_r and B_z, i.e., the mercury spins in the clockwise direction when viewed from above (for B_z pointing upward and radial current flowing outward).

Reversing either J_r or B_z (but not both) reverses the rotation direction. This is the basis for controllable rotation direction.

Drew notes this is due to "all of the nuclear and electron spin" — referring to the fact that the Lorentz force acts on all charge carriers, including bound electrons in the nuclear environment of liquid mercury atoms, not just free conduction electrons. The bulk effect is the macroscopic body force on the mercury fluid as a whole.

## Source: 13_1959450945341075456_video.mdx — Gyroscopic Rise: Orientation Control Experiment

### Physical Test of Mechanism

The experiment tests whether the upward force is due to:
- **(Hypothesis A) Classical magnetic repulsion:** A specific pole (N or S) is repelled upward by the rotating field. Prediction: reversing the magnet would produce downward force.
- **(Hypothesis B) Gyroscopic spin dynamics:** The angular momentum of the spinning magnet couples to the rotating field independent of pole orientation. Prediction: both orientations produce upward force.

**Observation:** "Instead it rises like a top" in both orientations.

**Conclusion:** Hypothesis B is supported. The physics is gyroscopic, not classical magnetic repulsion/attraction.

The "spinning top" analogy is exactly correct for the following reason: a spinning top does not fall under gravity because the gravitational torque τ = r × F_gravity causes precession rather than tipping, as long as ω_spin >> ω_prec. The analogy with the magnet is:
- Gravity = gravity
- Spinning top = spinning permanent magnet
- Floor reaction (for top) = rotating field reaction (for magnet), providing the upward precession-sustaining force

The magnet "falls" when the drive frequency moves outside the resonance window (as seen at 34 Hz in the frequency sweep video), analogous to a top falling when its spin slows below the critical angular momentum needed to maintain stable precession.
