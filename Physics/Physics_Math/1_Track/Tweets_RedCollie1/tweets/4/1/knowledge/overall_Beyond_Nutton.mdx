Explicit claim: "I have done MANY experiments which show that a 'spinning magnet' will rise without causing any downward back-reaction by Newton's 3rd Law!"

This would violate Newton's Third Law as applied to the interaction between the spinning magnet and the field source. Standard analysis:

  F_on_magnet = grad(m · B)

Newton's 3rd Law: F_on_source = -F_on_magnet

1. The force on the source coil is directed horizontally (not downward), making it appear that there is no downward reaction even though there is a reaction
2. The force on the source coil is transferred to the structure via magnetic coupling without translating to a net ground-level force
3. A genuinely non-Newtonian mechanism is at work

Drew's proposed resolution: "Any 3rd Law back-reaction for a spinning magnet would create a force perpendicular to the pole on which it is rising, so we would not expect to see a 3rd Law reaction downward against the ground."

  dL/dt = tau

The linear force (not torque) on the spinning magnet from the field gradient is:
  F_z = m_eff * (dB_z/dz)

The back-reaction on the coil from the magnet is:

This back-reaction IS downward in standard theory. The claimed absence of this reaction would be a genuine violation of Newton's Third Law for the linear force.

2. Spin-orbit coupling in quantum mechanics: for spinning charges, there is a velocity-dependent magnetic interaction (Breit equation correction to Coulomb force). However, this is a quantum effect scaling as (v/c)^2 and negligible for macroscopic speeds.
