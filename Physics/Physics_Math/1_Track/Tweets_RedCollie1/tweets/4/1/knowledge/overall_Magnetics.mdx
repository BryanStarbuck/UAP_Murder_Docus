* Result: the net magnetic field vector rotates continuously in the xy-plane, completing one revolution per electrical cycle (60 Hz → one revolution per 1/60 second)

Bloch wall placement requirement:
A Bloch wall (domain wall) is the transition region between two magnetic domains of opposite orientation within a ferromagnetic material. In the context of the electromagnet ring: the boundary between the N-pole region and S-pole region of adjacent electromagnets forms an analog of a domain wall. By placing a ring magnet precisely at this N/S boundary (Bloch wall position):
* The ring magnet experiences net zero tangential force (it sits at the symmetry point)
* It does not spin up with the rotating field
* It remains stationary while providing a fixed magnetic reference for the rising inner rotor

The Bloch wall in a physical ferromagnet has a characteristic width delta_B given by:

  delta_B = pi * sqrt(A / K)

where A is the exchange stiffness constant (J/m) and K is the anisotropy energy density (J/m^3). For typical NdFeB magnets, delta_B ~ 3-5 nm. In the macroscopic electromagnet context, the analog "Bloch wall" position is the geometric midpoint between adjacent coil faces.

### Single-Phase Flat Coil for Lenz Levitation — Spin-Unbalanced Configuration

The large, round, flat 1-phase wire coil placed above the six 3-phase outer coils creates Lenz levitation. Classical Lenz levitation mechanics:

* The 1-phase coil carries alternating current I(t) = I_0 * sin(omega*t)
* It produces a time-varying magnetic flux through any conducting or magnetized object above it
* By Faraday's law: EMF = -d(Phi)/dt, induced currents oppose the change in flux
* By Lenz's law: the net force on the induced-current object is repulsive (upward for an object above the coil) — analogous to eddy-current levitation

Standard Lenz levitation (without spin): The levitation force F_Lenz is:

  F_Lenz = (mu_0 * omega^2 * sigma * delta * A^2 * I_0^2) / (4*pi * d^4)

approximately, where sigma is the conductivity of the levitated object, delta is its skin depth, A is the coil area, d is the separation distance. This force is purely vertical and symmetric.

Spin-unbalanced Lenz levitation (the proposed UAP mechanism): When the object being Lenz-levitated is simultaneously spinning, the spin breaks the azimuthal symmetry of the eddy current distribution. The induced currents in a spinning disc are no longer purely concentric — they develop a helical component due to the combination of the AC driving field and the mechanical rotation. This asymmetry is proposed to produce an enhanced net upward force beyond what standard Lenz theory predicts.
