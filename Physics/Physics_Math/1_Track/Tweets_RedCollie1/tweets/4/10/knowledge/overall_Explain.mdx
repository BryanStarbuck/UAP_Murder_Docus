### Off-Axis Magnet Spin with 3-Phase Power — Bridge to Levitation Output

**Claim (experimental, Dr. Horace Drew):** A 60 mm permanent disc magnet with a 20 mm central bore, driven off-axis by a 3-phase AC power supply, rises upward on its central pole. The observed output is net upward displacement against Earth's gravitational field — i.e., reduction of apparent weight and intermittent free-fall excursions producing oscillating scale readings of 0 to −50 grams at approximately 20 Hz oscillation frequency.

**Mechanism bridge:** The 3-phase rotating magnetic field (generated by three spatially offset wire coils, each driven 120° out of phase in time) couples to the permanent magnet's dipole moment. The torque on the magnet spins it. The off-axis geometry (bore hole displacing the center of mass from the rotation axis) introduces precession-like dynamics. The observed rise is reported as actual upward displacement against gravity, not merely buoyancy or spring oscillation.

**What the physics of the output requires:** For the magnet to rise steadily against gravity, the net vertical (Z-axis) force component from the rotating magnetic interaction must satisfy:

    F_z > m_magnet * g

where m_magnet is the magnet mass and g = 9.81 m/s². The experimental observation is that the magnet "did rise up strongly" but with oscillating, noisy motion, producing oscillating scale readings (0 to −50 grams below zero). A reading of 0 on the scale when the magnet is above it indicates the magnet has fully decoupled from gravitational loading on the scale — net lift condition. A reading of −50 g indicates the magnet is in free-fall and the scale is measuring the release impulse upward.

**Scale measurement interpretation:** The scale below measures force transmitted through the pole to the base. When the 3-phase-driven magnet rises freely (free-fall upward), normal force on the scale drops to zero. When the magnet falls back (restoring force of the field), it overshoots and registers negative (pulling scale upward). This oscillation at ~20 Hz is consistent with a driven mechanical resonance between the rotating magnetic torque and the magnet's gravitational restoring force.

**Bridge to UFO levitation hypothesis:** Drew explicitly states this experiment is a step toward UFO propulsion. The bridge is: a rotating magnetic field (produced by 3-phase coils) creates a spinning internal rotor; the spinning rotor produces a net vertical force in a manner not fully explained by classical electromagnetism. The "missing third piece" he identifies is the step from observed intermittent rise (oscillating weight loss) to sustained steady levitation.

### Spin/Counter-Spin Asymmetry — The Core Puzzle

**Observation from eyewitness account (video transcription):** A UFO in operation causes the grass below to rotate clockwise while the outer body of the craft spins counter-clockwise. The craft does not spin downward (no downward displacement) despite the counter-clockwise outer spin.

**Bridge explanation:** In classical mechanics, angular momentum conservation requires that a counter-spinning outer shell must produce a reaction torque. By Newton's third law, an outer shell spinning counter-clockwise while an inner rotor spins clockwise — against a fixed reference frame — requires that some external torque source provide the differential. In the absence of such a source, the net angular momentum of the system about the vertical axis (Z-axis) must be conserved. If inner and outer moments of inertia are matched appropriately:

    I_inner * ω_inner + I_outer * ω_outer = constant

The "secret puzzle" Drew identifies is: why does the counter-spinning outer section of a UFO not spin downward (i.e., why does counter-rotation not produce downward thrust or downward displacement)?

**Bridge to lift:** Drew interprets the asymmetry as pointing to a physics mechanism where the counter-spinning outer component interacts with an external field (Earth's magnetic field, gravitational field, or zero-point field) such that the downward reaction is absent. The external field absorbs the reaction torque, leaving only the upward force component.

**Experimental model:** 6 wire coils + 1 three-phase power supply → inner magnet spins in one direction, outer region spins in opposite direction. Matches crop circle schematic drawings cited by Drew.

**Problem identified:** A central spinning disc magnet on a pole wobbles noisily due to gyroscopic precession and magnetic torque instability.

**Solution:** A magnetic flywheel placed concentrically outside the central disc magnet, with 4 magnets attracting the central disc magnet edge-to-edge, holds the central disc magnet flat (suppresses tilt/wobble) while still allowing spin.

**Bridge to lift output:** If the stabilized central disc magnet rises against Earth's gravity field, it may drag the outer flywheel (with its 4 counter-spinning magnets) upward as well. The stabilization mechanism is therefore a prerequisite for achieving coherent lift — without it, the wobble dissipates the spin energy into mechanical noise rather than directed vertical force.

**Mechanical coupling:** The edge-to-edge magnetic attraction between central magnet and outer flywheel magnets creates a radial constraint force. As the inner magnet rises, the radial constraint acts at an angle, converting part of the vertical displacement into a tangential pull on the flywheel, which begins to move upward. This is the proposed mechanism for the entire assembly rising together.
