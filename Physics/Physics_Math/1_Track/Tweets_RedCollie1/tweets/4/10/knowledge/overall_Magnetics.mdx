### Homopolar Motor Spin — Galinstan and Mercury Vortex Context

**Homopolar motor demonstration:** Video shows a permanent magnet spinning at 10 V applied voltage while hovering over additional magnets, with two electrical brush contacts. When power is removed, the magnet bounces and magnetically repels from the stacked magnets below, demonstrating field-mediated levitation at low current.

**Physics of homopolar motor:**
The homopolar (unipolar) motor operates by the Lorentz force on current-carrying conductors in a magnetic field:

    F = I (L × B)

In the disc/ring geometry, current I flows radially from the center to the rim (or rim to center) through a conducting disc or ring magnet. A static axial magnetic field B (along Z-axis) produces a tangential (azimuthal) Lorentz force F_θ on each radial current element dI, resulting in a net torque about the Z-axis:

    τ = ∫ r × (I dL × B) 

For uniform axial field B_z and radial current I over annular conductor from inner radius r_1 to outer radius r_2:

    τ = (1/2) I B_z (r_2² - r_1²)

This is the classical homopolar motor torque. The spin is continuous (no commutation needed) and the output is pure rotational motion in the XY-plane.

**Device parameters:**
- Central magnet: 60 mm outer diameter, 20 mm central bore, permanent disc magnet

- Spin direction: the pole counter-spins relative to the magnet (pole spins opposite to the magnet's rotation direction — consistent with reaction torque in the support structure)

- Scale reading: oscillates between 0 and −50 grams at ~20 Hz — indicating oscillating lift force superimposed on the magnet weight

**3-Phase rotating field geometry:**
Three wire coils spaced 120° apart (azimuthally) in the XY-plane, each driven by a phase-shifted sinusoidal current:

    I_1(t) = I_0 sin(ωt)
    I_2(t) = I_0 sin(ωt + 2π/3)
    I_3(t) = I_0 sin(ωt + 4π/3)

Each coil produces a magnetic field at the center that oscillates in time. The superposition of the three fields produces a resultant field that rotates uniformly in the XY-plane at angular frequency ω with constant magnitude:

    B_resultant = (3/2) B_0 [rotating vector of constant amplitude in XY-plane]

This is the same principle as a 3-phase AC induction motor stator. The rotating resultant field exerts a continuous torque on the permanent disc magnet, driving it to spin.

**Off-axis condition:** With the magnet center of mass offset from the rotation axis (due to the 20 mm bore), the rotating field interaction is no longer purely symmetric about the Z-axis. The field gradient across the magnet's dipole produces a net force with both horizontal and vertical components. The vertical component F_z varies at twice the rotation frequency (second harmonic of ω), producing the observed ~20 Hz oscillation (if the drive frequency ω/2π ≈ 10 Hz, then F_z oscillates at ~20 Hz).

### Spin / Counter-Spin: Inner CW, Outer CCW — The Asymmetry Problem

**Experimental model description:**
- 6 wire coils driven by 3-phase AC power
- Central permanent disc magnet spins in one direction (inner rotor) — this also causes grass/compass below to rotate clockwise
- Outer part of UFO body (or outer flywheel) spins counter-clockwise

**Observed asymmetry:**
The outer section spins counter-clockwise but does NOT spin downward (does not descend). In classical mechanics, a counter-spinning outer shell should carry angular momentum in the −Z direction (if inner carries +Z), but there is no reason from classical mechanics alone why counter-rotation of an outer shell would produce upward force. The puzzle is therefore:

Why does the counter-spinning outer section remain elevated (or rise) rather than descending?

**Possible resolution — magnetic field handedness:**
The rotating magnetic field of the inner 3-phase-driven magnet creates a helical magnetic flux pattern (field lines spiraling upward along Z with azimuthal rotation). The outer counter-spinning flywheel magnets interact with this helical flux. The relative motion between the counter-spinning flywheel and the helical field of the inner magnet creates a Lenz's law induction effect that opposes the counter-spin. The reaction to this opposing force is upward (in the direction the helical flux is propagating — along +Z).

This is analogous to the screw symmetry argument: a nut threaded on a rotating helix advances axially. The outer counter-spinning section "threads" along the helical magnetic flux of the inner rotating magnet and is advanced upward.

### Magnetic Flywheel Stabilization — Edge-to-Edge Attraction

**Configuration:**
- Central disc magnet: spinning on a pole under 3-phase drive
- Outer flywheel: 4 permanent magnets, edge-to-edge magnetic attraction to central disc magnet

**Magnetic stabilization mechanism:**
The 4 outer flywheel magnets each attract the edge of the central disc magnet. For a disc magnet of radius R and thickness t, the edge field is a complex multipole superposition. The dominant interaction at edge contact is dipole-dipole:

    U_edge = -(μ_0 / 4π) [3(μ_1·r̂)(μ_2·r̂) - μ_1·μ_2] / r³

arranged such that edge-to-edge attraction (flux closure through the edge) stabilizes the disc in the horizontal plane.

With 4 magnets at 90° spacing attracting the disc edge, the restoring torque in the tilt direction (away from horizontal) is:

    τ_restoring ∝ 4 * (dU_edge/dθ_tilt)

This produces a strong restoring torque toward the horizontal plane, suppressing wobble.

**Counter-spin relationship:** The 4 outer flywheel magnets will be dragged into counter-rotation relative to the central disc by the rotating field leakage from the 3-phase coils, or by the direct magnetic coupling from the spinning central disc (reluctance torque). The outer flywheel counter-spin is thus a natural consequence of the edge coupling, not a separately driven mode.
