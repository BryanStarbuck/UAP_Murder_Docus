### 3-Phase Rotating Field Mathematics

**Phase-offset sinusoidal currents:**

    I_k(t) = I_0 sin(ωt + (k-1) * 2π/3),   k = 1, 2, 3

**Resultant rotating field at center (superposition):**

    B_x(t) = B_coil [sin(ωt) + sin(ωt + 2π/3)cos(2π/3) + sin(ωt + 4π/3)cos(4π/3)]
           = (3/2) B_coil cos(ωt + π/2)
           = (3/2) B_coil sin(ωt + π/2)

More directly, using phasor/complex notation:

    B̃ = B_coil [1 + e^{i·2π/3} + e^{i·4π/3}] = 0 (static part cancels)
    B̃_+ = (3/2) B_coil e^{iωt}   [positive rotating component]
    B̃_− = 0                        [no counter-rotating component for balanced 3-phase]

In real notation:
    **B**(t) = (3/2) B_coil [cos(ωt) x̂ + sin(ωt) ŷ]

**Amplitude:** B_eff = (3/2) B_coil = (3/2) (μ_0 N I_0)/(2 r_coil) for single-layer coil

**Torque on permanent magnet (dipole moment m, tilt angle θ from xy-plane, lag angle δ):**

    τ_z = m sin(θ) B_eff sin(δ)

where δ is the angular lag between magnet's azimuthal orientation and the rotating field.

**Steady-state rotation condition:** The magnet follows the field at frequency ω with a constant lag δ determined by:

    τ_z = τ_friction + τ_damping
    m sin(θ) B_eff sin(δ) = c_f ω + c_d ω

where c_f is friction coefficient and c_d is magnetic damping coefficient.

### Off-Axis Vertical Force — Second Harmonic

**Off-axis displacement:** Bore hole displaces effective magnetic center by r_offset from rotation axis.

**Position of effective dipole center:**
    x_cm(t) = r_offset cos(ωt + φ_0)
    y_cm(t) = r_offset sin(ωt + φ_0)

**Vertical force from field gradient:**
The vertical (Z-axis) component of force on a magnetic dipole **m** in a nonuniform field is:

    F_z = ∇_z (m · B) = m_z (∂B_z/∂z) + m_x (∂B_x/∂z) + m_y (∂B_y/∂z)

For the rotating field above a 3-phase stator, the field has a Z-gradient:
    ∂B_z/∂x ≡ G_xz, ∂B_z/∂y ≡ G_yz (field gradient tensor components)

Substituting the off-axis position:
    F_z(t) = m_z [G_xz x_cm(t) + G_yz y_cm(t)]
           = m_z r_offset [G_xz cos(ωt + φ_0) + G_yz sin(ωt + φ_0)]

This oscillates at ω (single frequency). However, if the magnet is also rotating at ω in the XY-plane (its dipole moment has a rotating transverse component):
    m_x(t) = m_⊥ cos(ωt + α)
    m_y(t) = m_⊥ sin(ωt + α)

then cross terms produce a second-harmonic contribution at 2ω:
    F_z(t) ~ m_⊥ r_offset [product of two cosines at ω → sum at 2ω and DC]

The DC component of F_z represents a time-averaged vertical force (net lift or suppression of weight).
The 2ω component explains the 20 Hz oscillation at 10 Hz drive.

**Net lift condition (time-averaged):**
    <F_z> = (1/2) m_⊥ r_offset G (cos(α − φ_0))

where G is the appropriate gradient tensor component. For net lift, cos(α − φ_0) > 0, meaning the magnet's transverse dipole must be in phase with the field gradient encountered during the orbit.

### MHD Vortex — Body Force Equations

**Ohm's law in a moving conductor:**
    **J** = σ(**E** + **v** × **B**)

**Lorentz body force per unit volume:**
    **f** = **J** × **B** = σ(**E** + **v** × **B**) × **B**

**Expanded in cylindrical coordinates (r, θ, z):**
For azimuthal flow v = v_θ θ̂ in axial field B = B_z ẑ:

    **v** × **B** = v_θ θ̂ × B_z ẑ = v_θ B_z r̂

    **J** = σ(E_r r̂ + v_θ B_z r̂) = σ(E_r + v_θ B_z) r̂

    **f** = **J** × **B** = σ(E_r + v_θ B_z) r̂ × B_z ẑ = σ(E_r + v_θ B_z) B_z (−θ̂)

Wait — r̂ × ẑ = −θ̂, so the azimuthal body force is:

    f_θ = −σ(E_r + v_θ B_z) B_z

For this to drive the vortex (positive azimuthal acceleration), we need f_θ > 0 (in the +θ̂ direction), so:
    E_r + v_θ B_z < 0, i.e., the motional EMF opposes the external E_r

Alternatively, for an inward E_r (E_r < 0, pointing −r̂, i.e., field toward center):
    f_θ = σ|E_r| B_z > 0   (positive θ̂ drive)

This is the E × B pinch drive for the MHD vortex.

**Azimuthal equation of motion for fluid ring:**
    ρ Dv_θ/Dt = f_θ − (viscous dissipation) − (centrifugal pressure gradient)
    ρ (∂v_θ/∂t + v_r ∂v_θ/∂r + v_θ/r ∂v_θ/∂θ − v_r v_θ/r) = f_θ − η ∇²v_θ

In steady state (∂/∂t = 0) with negligible radial flow (v_r = 0):
    η ∇²v_θ = −f_θ = σ(E_r + v_θ B_z) B_z

This is a balance between viscous diffusion and electromagnetic body force — the MHD Hartmann-layer balance.

### E × B Drift — Azimuthal Spin of 3I/ATLAS Coma

**E × B drift velocity (exact, for single particle):**
    **v**_drift = (**E** × **B**) / |**B**|²

For **E** = E_r r̂ and **B** = B_z ẑ:
    **E** × **B** = E_r r̂ × B_z ẑ = E_r B_z (r̂ × ẑ) = −E_r B_z θ̂

    **v**_drift = (−E_r B_z / B_z²) θ̂ = −(E_r / B_z) θ̂

For E_r > 0 (field pointing outward), the drift is in the −θ̂ direction (clockwise when viewed from +z).
For E_r < 0 (field pointing inward), drift is in +θ̂ (counter-clockwise).

This drift is:
- Independent of particle mass and charge sign (both electrons and ions drift the same direction)
- Proportional to E_r/B_z
- Purely azimuthal (no radial or axial component in uniform fields)

**Drift-induced spin rate (angular velocity):**
    Ω_drift = v_drift / r = (E_r / B_z) / r

For a 1/r² Coulomb field: E_r = Q/(4πε_0 r²), and a dipole field B_z ≈ μ_0 m/(2πr³) on axis:
    Ω_drift = [Q/(4πε_0 r²)] / [μ_0 m/(2πr³)]
            = [Q/(4πε_0 r²)] * [2πr³/(μ_0 m)]
            = Q r / (2ε_0 μ_0 m)
            = Q r c² / (2m)

where c² = 1/(ε_0 μ_0). This grows linearly with r — outer gas rings spin faster than inner rings, consistent with a differentially rotating coma observed as a spiral arm structure.

### Newton's 3rd Law — Orbital Momentum Transfer

**Conservation of total momentum (center-of-mass frame):**
    p_total = m_1 v_1 + m_2 v_2 = const

**Impulse from thruster ejection:**
    m_1 Δv_1 = −m_2 Δv_2   (Newton's 3rd, impulse-momentum theorem)

**Vis-viva equation for pre/post-ejection orbit of m_1:**
    v_1² = G M_sun (2/r − 1/a_1)

Post-ejection, m_1 has a new semi-major axis a_1'. The change in orbital energy:
    ΔE_1 = G M_sun m_1 (1/a_1 − 1/a_1') / 2

**For m_2 (ejected body), initial conditions:**
At ejection position **r**_0, the ejected body has velocity:
    **v**_2 = **v**_1 + Δ**v**_2

New orbit of m_2 is fully determined by (**r**_0, **v**_2) and the two-body problem with M_sun.

**Specific energy of m_2 orbit:**
    ε_2 = |**v**_2|²/2 − G M_sun / |**r**_0|

**If ε_2 < 0:** bound orbit (ellipse). Semi-major axis: a_2 = −G M_sun / (2 ε_2)
**If ε_2 > 0:** unbound orbit (hyperbola)
**If ε_2 = 0:** parabolic trajectory (escape with zero terminal velocity)

**Specific angular momentum of m_2 orbit:**
    **L**_2 = **r**_0 × **v**_2

Semi-latus rectum: p_2 = L_2² / (G M_sun)
Eccentricity: e_2 = sqrt(1 + 2 ε_2 L_2² / (G M_sun)²)

The ejected body's orbital plane and period can be computed entirely from these initial conditions.

### 5-Phase Rotating Field Mathematics

**Phase-offset currents (5-phase):**
    I_k(t) = I_0 sin(ωt + (k-1) * 2π/5),   k = 1..5

**Resultant rotating field:**
    **B**(t) = (5/2) B_coil [cos(ωt) x̂ + sin(ωt) ŷ]

(for balanced 5-phase, the resultant amplitude is (5/2) times the single-coil contribution — same principle as 3-phase but stronger for the same coil count)

**Spatial harmonic matching with outer flywheel:**
Outer flywheel: 10 magnets, 5 N-S pairs, spatial period Λ = 2π R_flywheel / 5 (where R_flywheel is flywheel radius).
Fundamental spatial wavenumber: k_spatial = 5 / R_flywheel

5-phase rotating field: temporal frequency ω, fundamental spatial Fourier component in azimuth is also k=5 for 5-coil geometry.

**Synchronous condition:** The flywheel rotates synchronously (without slip) when the rotating field's spatial wave velocity matches the flywheel surface speed:
    v_surface = ω_flywheel * R_flywheel = ω / k_spatial = ω R_flywheel / 5
    ω_flywheel = ω / 5   [synchronous flywheel speed for 5-phase, 5-pair drive]

Compare 3-phase with 6-coil (3 N-S pairs) outer: ω_flywheel = ω / 3.
The 5-phase system provides slower flywheel rotation for the same drive frequency — higher torque, lower speed (more poles = lower synchronous speed, higher torque density).

### Edge-to-Edge Magnetic Stabilization — Tilt Restoring Torque

**Dipole-dipole interaction potential:**
    U = (μ_0 / 4π) [(**m**_1 · **m**_2)/r³ − 3(**m**_1 · r̂)(**m**_2 · r̂)/r³]

For face-to-face alignment (attraction), edge-to-edge alignment of two bar magnets (both pointing the same transverse direction):
    U_edge = −(μ_0 m²) / (4π r³)   [attraction]

**Restoring torque for tilt θ from horizontal:**
If the central disc tilts by angle θ, the edge distance to one flywheel magnet decreases (r → r − δr(θ)) and to the opposite one increases:
    δr(θ) ≈ (R_disc + R_magnet) sin(θ) ≈ (R_disc + R_magnet) θ   for small θ

The differential in potential energy:
    δU = (μ_0 m²/4π) [1/(r−δr)³ − 1/(r+δr)³] ≈ (μ_0 m²/4π) · 6δr/r⁴

Net restoring torque (for N_outer = 4 flywheel magnets at 90° spacing, two pairs of opposite magnets contributing):
    τ_restoring = −2 · dU/dθ |_{θ=0} ≈ −2 (μ_0 m²/4π) · 6(R_disc + R_magnet)/r⁴ · θ

This is a Hookean (linear) restoring torque: τ = −k_tilt θ, with:
    k_tilt = (3 μ_0 m²/π) (R_disc + R_magnet) / r⁴

The natural tilt oscillation frequency:
    ω_tilt = sqrt(k_tilt / I_disc)

where I_disc is the moment of inertia of the central disc about its diameter. The system is stable for all oscillation frequencies above zero.

### Homopolar Motor Torque — Multiple Ring Magnets

**Torque for N_ring ring magnets providing total axial field B_z_total at radial current path:**
    τ = (1/2) I B_z_total (r_outer² − r_inner²)

For 6 magnets with identical geometry and aligned polarity:
    B_z_total = 6 B_single_z

    τ = (1/2) I · 6 B_single_z · (r_2² − r_1²)
      = 3 I B_single_z (r_2² − r_1²)

**Power delivered:**
    P = τ · ω_motor = 3 I B_single_z (r_2² − r_1²) · ω_motor

**EMF generated (homopolar generator mode):**
    EMF = (1/2) B_z_total ω_motor (r_2² − r_1²)
        = 3 B_single_z ω_motor (r_2² − r_1²)

**Note on hexagonal symmetry:** The 6-magnet hexagonal arrangement (60° spacing) creates a field with 6-fold azimuthal symmetry. Any test particle at radius r within this array experiences a field:
    B_z(r, θ) = B_0_z + B_6 cos(6θ + φ_0)

The leading term B_0_z (azimuthally uniform) drives the homopolar torque; the B_6 hexapole term creates a 6-fold modulation that could lock (synchronize) the rotation of a 6-pole rotor magnetically.
