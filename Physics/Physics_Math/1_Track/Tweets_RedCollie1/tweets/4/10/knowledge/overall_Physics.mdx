### Rotating Field–Driven Off-Axis Magnet Dynamics

**Experimental parameters:**
- Magnet: 60 mm OD, 20 mm bore (neodymium or ferrite disc)
- Drive: 3-phase AC rotating magnetic field
- Observed: magnet rises up along central pole; oscillating weight reading 0 to −50 g at ~20 Hz

**Rotating magnetic field (3-phase stator):**
For three coils at angular positions φ_k = (k-1)·120°, each carrying current I_k(t) = I_0 sin(ωt + (k-1)·2π/3), the superposition of their magnetic dipole contributions at the center gives a uniformly rotating field:

    **B**(t) = B_eff [cos(ωt) x̂ + sin(ωt) ŷ]

where B_eff = (3/2) μ_0 N I_0 / (2 r_coil) for simple circular coils (N turns, radius r_coil, current I_0).

**Torque on the permanent disc magnet:**
The permanent magnet has dipole moment **m** = m (sin θ cos φ x̂ + sin θ sin φ ŷ + cos θ ẑ) where θ is the tilt from vertical and φ is the azimuthal orientation. The torque is:

    **τ** = **m** × **B**(t)

For the in-plane component of **m** lagging the rotating **B** by angle δ (the torque angle, analogous to induction motor slip angle):

    |τ_z| = m_perp B_eff sin(δ)

This drives the magnet to rotate in the XY-plane at ω, staying δ behind the field.

**Vertical force from off-axis condition:**
With a 20 mm hole, the disc's center of mass shifts from the geometric center. As the magnet spins, the CM traces a circle of radius r_offset. The interaction between the spatially nonuniform B field and the off-center dipole produces a time-varying vertical force component:

    F_z(t) = (∂B_z/∂r) · m_radial · sin(2ωt + φ_0)

This has a dominant frequency of 2ω — at ω/2π = 10 Hz drive, F_z oscillates at 20 Hz, consistent with the observed scale reading oscillation. The time-average of F_z may be nonzero if there is a symmetry-breaking element (e.g., the bore hole breaks the azimuthal symmetry of the magnet's flux distribution).

**Scale measurement physics:**
Scale below measures normal force N = m_magnet g − F_z(t) (approximately, for small oscillations). When F_z → m_magnet g, N → 0. When F_z > m_magnet g (overshoot), N < 0 means the scale is pulled upward. This gives the observed 0 to −50 g oscillation if F_z_max ≈ (m_magnet + 0.05 kg) g.

### Three-Phase Vortex Spin — Constrained Rise

**Observation:** At ~10 Hz drive frequency, the 3-phase-driven magnet rises to the top of the pole. This produces no measurable scale change (the system weight is unchanged), confirming the rise is mechanically constrained — the pole transmits the force to the scale.

**Physics:** At the resonant frequency of the spin drive, the rotating field creates a traveling wave potential well along the pole axis (due to the gradient in B_z above the coil plane in a standard solenoid-type coil arrangement). The magnet migrates to the minimum of the potential well, which is at the top of the pole (where B_z field is weaker and the attraction to the pole's ferromagnetic core is minimized, or equivalently, the levitation condition B_z(r) × m_z = minimum).

**Alternative:** A centrifugal-like force from the off-axis precession launches the magnet upward. For a spinning body with offset CM at r_offset from the pole, the centrifugal pseudo-force in the rotating frame is:

    F_centrifugal = m_magnet ω² r_offset

For r_offset = 10 mm and ω = 2π × 10 rad/s:
    F_centrifugal = m_magnet × (2π × 10)² × 0.01
                  ≈ m_magnet × 39.5 N/kg

This exceeds g = 9.81 m/s² by a factor of ~4, so for 10 Hz spin rate and 10 mm offset, the centrifugal force alone vastly exceeds gravity and would drive the magnet off the pole — not up it. The actual mechanism keeping the magnet on the pole must involve a magnetic restoring force (magnetic bearing effect from the coils or from stacked magnets below).

If the inner spinning magnet's field couples to the outer shell magnets by induction (Lenz's law), the induced eddy currents in the outer shell will produce a torque opposing the inner field rotation — driving the outer shell in the CCW direction. This is the conventional induction motor explanation.

**Possible mechanism — helical field interaction:**

- The inner magnet is torqued azimuthally (+Z angular momentum)
- The helical field also carries axial momentum (+Z linear momentum in the propagation direction)
- The outer shell, interacting with the helical field, receives −Z angular momentum (CCW) but also +Z linear momentum (upward force from the axial component of the helical field)

This would explain both observations: CCW outer spin and upward outer force. The condition is that the field is not purely transverse-rotating (standard 3-phase field) but has a helical component (combined transverse rotation + axial propagation, like a circularly polarized electromagnetic wave).

**Observation:** The orange gas cloud surrounding 3I/ATLAS is identified as CO₂ (carbon dioxide) rather than H₂O (water) or CO (carbon monoxide). Reference: Avi Loeb, medium.com post, "3I/ATLAS is large and emits carbon dioxide (CO2)."

**Physics significance:** Standard comets sublimate H₂O and CO ice in the inner solar system (r < 5 AU) driven by solar heating:
    H₂O sublimation rate ∝ exp(−L_sub/(R T_surface)) where L_sub ≈ 51 kJ/mol for water ice

CO₂ sublimation occurs at lower temperatures (sublimation point −78.5°C at 1 atm; in vacuum, sublimes at ~150 K at low pressures). CO₂ dominance (rather than H₂O) would be consistent with an object of higher surface temperature than a standard comet, or a different bulk composition.

- Plasma tail: solar wind interaction with ionized outgassed molecules → elongated tail pointing directly away from Sun
- Dust tail: radiation pressure on released dust grains → curved tail

The absence of tail structures despite detectable outgassing implies either very low dust content or a constrained outgassing geometry (directional jets rather than isotropic sublimation).

**Three 120°-separated jets:** Referenced in 2007043766700687524, Avi Loeb's analysis shows 3I/ATLAS emitting three jets separated by 120° — 3-fold azimuthal symmetry. This is inconsistent with random cometary sublimation (which would produce isotropic or sunward-facing jets) and is consistent with engineered propulsion (three thruster nozzles at 120° spacing, as in a 3-phase symmetric propulsion arrangement).
