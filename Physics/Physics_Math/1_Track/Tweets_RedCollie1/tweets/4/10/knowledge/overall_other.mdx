### R.E. Dickerson — DNA Structural Biology (Non-Physics Context)

**Content:** Drew's Caltech Ph.D. advisor, Richard E. Dickerson, recently passed away. Drew memorializes him by posting Dickerson's X-ray crystallography analyses of A-form, B-form, and Z-form DNA. The artwork was created by illustrator Irving Geis (a signed print), not by computer.

**Scientific context (structural biology, not UAP physics):**
- A-DNA: right-handed double helix, 11 base pairs per turn, found in dehydrated conditions or RNA-DNA hybrids
- B-DNA: right-handed double helix, ~10.5 base pairs per turn, the predominant biological form (Watson-Crick, 1953)
- Z-DNA: left-handed double helix, 12 base pairs per turn, found in alternating purine-pyrimidine sequences under high salt or negative supercoiling

**Dickerson's contribution:** Dickerson and Drew published the first high-resolution crystal structure of a B-DNA dodecamer (the "Dickerson-Drew dodecamer," CGCGAATTCGCG, 1980, Journal of Molecular Biology). This was the first atomic-resolution view of DNA in solution-like conditions, confirming many structural parameters of B-DNA and revealing sequence-dependent structural variation.

**Relevance to Drew's background:** Drew (as a Caltech PhD student under Dickerson) worked on DNA crystallography and X-ray diffraction, which provides the scientific foundation for his pattern recognition in crop circles — he applies the same mathematical analysis of repeating geometric structures to both DNA and crop formations.

## Section: 1959781056200253723.yaml
### 3I/ATLAS — CO₂ Outgassing and Non-Comet Classification

**Content:** Drew argues that the orange gas cloud around 3I/ATLAS is CO₂ (not H₂O or CO), and therefore 3I/ATLAS is NOT an "icy comet." Key argument: no tail is present despite CO₂ outgassing.

**Astronomical context (standard cometary science):**
- Standard icy comets: H₂O, CO, CO₂ as primary volatiles; H₂O dominates in the inner solar system (inside the "snow line" at ~3 AU)
- CO₂ sublimation temperature (at low pressure in vacuum): ~150 K, lower than H₂O (~180 K) — active farther from the Sun
- CO₂-dominated coma: consistent with a CO₂-rich composition or warmer-than-expected surface conditions

**No tail analysis:** A standard cometary tail (plasma + dust) forms when:
- Solar wind picks up ionized outgassed molecules → plasma tail (anti-sunward, straight)
- Radiation pressure on dust grains → dust tail (curved, anti-sunward with curvature from emission timing)

Absence of tail despite CO₂ outgassing requires either: no dust content, or directional (non-isotropic) outgassing jets that do not produce the diffuse dust cloud needed for a tail.
