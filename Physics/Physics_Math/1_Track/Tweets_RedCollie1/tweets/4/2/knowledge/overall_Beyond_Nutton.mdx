### Spin/Counter-Spin Magnetic Levitation — Beyond Classical Electromagnetism

**Earnshaw's Theorem (1842):** No static arrangement of point charges or magnetic monopoles can produce a stable equilibrium for a test charge or monopole in 3D space. - A spinning magnet is not in a static configuration; it has angular momentum L = Iω

- This is the same physics as the Levitron spinning top (commercial toy demonstrating stable levitation of a spinning magnet above a magnet base)

This coupling mechanism — where the ring magnet (stator) is dragged upward by the precessing rotor's focal point — is not a standard result in classical electromagnetism.

**Chain of reasoning:**
1. Ring magnet creates focal point at height z_f above its bore
2. Spinning floater magnet of moment m_f is placed at z_f
3. Spinning floater precesses upward: z(t) = z_f + (Ω_p · r_orbit) · t (at constant precession rate)
4. As floater rises, the focal point (equilibrium of floater in ring field) must shift to track it
5. The focal point shift requires the ring magnet field to reconfigure — which requires the ring magnet to physically move upward
6. The coupling force F_coupling = -∂U/∂z_ring = m_f · ∂B_z(z_f)/∂z_ring pulls the ring magnet upward
7. If F_coupling > M_ring · g, the ring magnet rises

This is a dynamical feedback mechanism with no direct Newtonian analogue. The "action at a distance through the focal point potential" is mediated by the magnetic field, which itself obeys Maxwell's equations (classical), but the specific dynamical trajectory (spinning floater causing ring ascent) requires the combined treatment of gyroscopic dynamics + time-varying magnetic field topology.
