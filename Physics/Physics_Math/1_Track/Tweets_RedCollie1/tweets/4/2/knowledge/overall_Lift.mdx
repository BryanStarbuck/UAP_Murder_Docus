### 3-Phase Coils Above Ball Produce Stronger Upward Force

Drew observes: "the upward force against that 'ball' would be much stronger, if we were to place those 3-phase wire coils above the 'ball' rather than below."

Physical explanation: a rotating magnetic field above the ball, with north-pole field directed downward toward the ball, would induce an upward attractive force on the ball (if the ball is ferromagnetic or contains a permanent magnet with its north pole facing up). The gradient force F = ∇(m · B) is maximized when the field source is close to the ball and above it, because the gradient |dB/dz| is largest just below the coil array and decreases with distance.

For lift: placing the rotating coils above the rotor ball and orienting them to attract the rotor upward is more efficient than pushing from below, consistent with how induction motors work (the rotor is pulled along by the rotating field above/around it, not pushed from behind).
