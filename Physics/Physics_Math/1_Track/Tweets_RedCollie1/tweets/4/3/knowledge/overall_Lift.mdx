### Magnetic Ring Focal Point as "Invisible Tabletop" — Z-Axis Levitation Mechanism

**Core claim:** The focal point of a ring magnet creates an invisible "tabletop" on which
a spinning magnetic top can rise. This is the proposed primary lift mechanism.

**Magnetic field gradient force for a magnetic dipole:**
  F_z = μ_z × ∂B_z/∂z

where μ_z is the Z-component of the magnetic dipole moment of the spinning top and
∂B_z/∂z is the axial gradient of the ring magnet's field.

**For levitation condition (equilibrium against gravity):**
  F_z = mg
  μ_z × ∂B_z/∂z = mg
  ∂B_z/∂z = mg / μ_z

**Earnshaw's theorem bypass via spinning (gyroscopic stabilization):**
Earnshaw's theorem states: A static arrangement of magnetic poles cannot produce a stable
equilibrium for a magnetic body by static fields alone. The gradient force F_z = μ_z ∂B_z/∂z
can balance gravity at one point, but the equilibrium is unstable to lateral displacement
(∂²B_z/∂x² + ∂²B_z/∂y² + ∂²B_z/∂z² = 0 by ∇²B = 0 in free space, so if ∂²B_z/∂z² < 0
at equilibrium, then ∂²B_z/∂x² + ∂²B_z/∂y² > 0, meaning lateral instability).

A spinning top with large angular momentum L = Iω provides gyroscopic rigidity. The tipping
instability mode (lateral tilt) has a characteristic frequency:
  ω_instability ~ √(κ_restoring / I_tilt)
where κ_restoring is the restoring stiffness from field + gyroscopic coupling.

For large enough ω (fast spin), the gyroscopic term dominates and the instability rate
is suppressed below the decay rate. This is the same principle as a spinning levitating
top (Levitron toy) but at the scale and power level needed for propulsion.

**Proposed "rise" beyond equilibrium:** When the 3-phase AC driving power is increased,
the spinning rate ω increases. By:
  Ω_p = τ / (Iω)
increasing ω decreases the precession rate. The effective coupling between spin rate,
precession rate, and the axial force balance can be tuned to achieve net upward displacement
(rise) — the "spinning rise-precession" of the crop-circle diagram.

### Magnet Rising Through Stainless Steel Tube Under 3-Phase Power

**Experimental configuration:**
- Permanent magnet inside stainless steel tube (Z-axis oriented)
- Outer ring of small permanent magnets driven by 3-phase power at angular velocity +ω₁
- Second outer ring of small permanent magnets driven by separate 3-phase supply at −ω₁

**Force analysis:**
The first outer ring creates a rotating B field at +ω₁ in the XY-plane, experienced by the
inner magnet as a rotating external field. The inner magnet (if it can rotate) will tend to
synchronize with the rotating field (synchronous motor behavior). If constrained to translate
only (tube prevents rotation), the rotating field exerts a time-averaged force.

For a magnetic dipole m in a rotating field B(t):
  B_rot(t) = B₀[cos(ω₁t) x̂ + sin(ω₁t) ŷ]

The torque on the magnet:
  τ = m × B_rot

For a magnet with Z-oriented moment m = m_z ẑ:
  τ = m_z ẑ × B₀[cos(ω₁t) x̂ + sin(ω₁t) ŷ] = m_z B₀[cos(ω₁t) ŷ − sin(ω₁t) x̂]

This torque is perpendicular to Z — it tries to tilt the magnet into the XY-plane.
The counter-rotating second ring produces the opposite torque. The two together produce
a net torque:
  τ_net = τ_ring1 + τ_ring2

If the two rings rotate at exactly opposite velocities (ω₁ and −ω₁), the instantaneous
torques partially cancel for the tipping mode, but the time-averaged force on Z-translation
(from gradient coupling) may be nonzero due to broken symmetry in the Z-direction.

**Proposed lift mechanism:** The asymmetric interaction of the two counter-rotating fields
with the axial field gradient of the tube configuration produces a net upward force (Fz > 0).
This is not derivable from simple first-order field theory and represents the "never seen
before" element of the claimed device.

**Stainless steel tube role:** Non-magnetic (austenitic stainless steel μ_r ≈ 1.003),
so it does not shield or significantly distort the magnetic field. It constrains the
inner magnet to Z-axis translation only, preventing lateral displacement or rotation,
forcing the field interaction to produce only Z-axis force output (lift).
