### Corbino Disc: Lorentz Force Torque Equation

**Force per unit volume:**
  f = J × B

**In cylindrical coordinates, Corbino geometry:**
  J = J_r(r) r̂
  B = B_z ẑ
  f = J_r B_z (r̂ × ẑ) = −J_r B_z φ̂

**Current density for total current I through disc of thickness h:**
  J_r(r) = I / (2πrh)

**Torque per unit volume about Z-axis:**
  dτ_z/dV = r |f_φ| = r J_r B_z = r × [I / (2πrh)] × B_z = I B_z / (2πh)

This is independent of r — each radial shell contributes equally to the torque per unit
height. Integrating over disc volume (r₁ to r₂, height h):
  τ_z = ∫_{r₁}^{r₂} ∫₀^h [I B_z / (2πh)] × 2πr dr dz
      = I B_z × ∫_{r₁}^{r₂} r dr
      = I B_z × (r₂² − r₁²)/2

**Net disc torque (exact):**
  τ_disc = (I B_z / 2)(r₂² − r₁²)

**Precession/spin relation for the Corbino disc as a gyroscopic element:**
If the Corbino disc is also spinning about its Z-axis with angular momentum L = I_moment × ω,
then the azimuthal electromagnetic torque τ_disc acts to accelerate this rotation:
  dL/dt = τ_disc
  I_moment (dω/dt) = (I_current B_z / 2)(r₂² − r₁²)
  Angular acceleration: α = (I_current B_z)(r₂² − r₁²) / (2 I_moment)

### Three-Phase Rotating Magnetic Field — Analytical Form

Three coils at angles 0°, 120°, 240° in the XY-plane, carrying currents:
  I₁(t) = I₀ cos(ωt)
  I₂(t) = I₀ cos(ωt − 2π/3)
  I₃(t) = I₀ cos(ωt − 4π/3)

Each coil produces a dipole field in its own direction (n̂₁, n̂₂, n̂₃). Superposing:
  n̂₁ = x̂
  n̂₂ = cos(120°)x̂ + sin(120°)ŷ = −½x̂ + (√3/2)ŷ
  n̂₃ = cos(240°)x̂ + sin(240°)ŷ = −½x̂ − (√3/2)ŷ

Net field in XY-plane at center:
  B(t) = K[I₁n̂₁ + I₂n̂₂ + I₃n̂₃]
  B_x(t) = K[I₀cos(ωt) + I₀cos(ωt−2π/3)(−½) + I₀cos(ωt−4π/3)(−½)]
           = K I₀ (3/2) cos(ωt)    [using trigonometric identity]
  B_y(t) = K[0 + I₀cos(ωt−2π/3)(√3/2) + I₀cos(ωt−4π/3)(−√3/2)]
           = K I₀ (3/2)(−sin(ωt)) [completing the derivation]

  B(t) = (3KI₀/2)[cos(ωt) x̂ − sin(ωt) ŷ]

This is a vector of constant magnitude (3KI₀/2) rotating at angular frequency ω in the
XY-plane — the rotating magnetic field used in induction motors and proposed by Drew as
the XY-plane driver of magnetic-spin levitation.

**Field magnitude:** |B| = 3KI₀/2 (constant, independent of t — pure rotation)
**Angular velocity:** Ω = ω rad/s (same as current frequency)

### Gyroscopic Precession Rate Equations

**Standard precession rate:**
  Ω_p = τ / L = (mgr sinθ) / (Iω)

where:
  m = mass of top
  g = gravitational acceleration
  r = distance from pivot to center of mass
  θ = tilt angle from vertical
  I = moment of inertia about spin axis
  ω = spin angular velocity

**Rise rate (proposed, non-standard):**
If an additional upward force F_up acts on the spinning top (e.g., from magnetic field
gradient), the effective torque includes:
  τ_eff = τ_gravity − F_up × r_⊥

where r_⊥ is the effective moment arm of the upward force. The net precession rate
becomes:
  Ω_p = (mgr sinθ − F_up r_⊥) / (Iω)

When F_up × r_⊥ = mgr sinθ, the precession rate drops to zero and the top "hovers" in
precession equilibrium. When F_up × r_⊥ > mgr sinθ, the top precesses upward (rises).
This is the proposed "rise-precession" mechanism.

**Nutation frequency** (small oscillation about steady precession):
  ω_nutation = Iω / (I_nutation moment)

Nutation is the fast wobble superimposed on slow precession; in the proposed device,
controlling nutation damping may be required for stable levitation.

Source: Experimental data from Drew's lab (page_033.json)

  L_c = I × ω_c = 1.76 × 10⁻⁴ × 157 ≈ 0.0277 kg·m²/s = 27.7 × 10⁻³ N·m·s

**Precession rate at critical angular momentum:**

At ω_c = 25 Hz, the precession rate drops to ~0.04 Hz — very slow precession, effectively

  Where Ω_tipping is the natural tipping rate in the absence of gyroscopic stabilization.
