**How this could be reconciled with known physics:**
The closest known effect is the gyroscopic force-free precession being "rectified" by the rotating field into a net vertical displacement. This is analogous to resonant excitation in driven oscillators. However, "always rises" regardless of phase suggests a more robust mechanism than resonant phase matching.

**Pole symmetry claim:**
"Both North and South poles of a permanent magnet spin and rise with the same force, and in the same direction."

In conventional magnetic levitation, opposite poles experience opposite forces (N is attracted to S, repelled from N). The fact that both poles produce the same rise force in the same direction is a strong signature that the mechanism is NOT a simple magnetic gradient force (F = ∇(m · B)), which would be pole-dependent. This suggests the mechanism involves the angular momentum vector of the spinning magnet rather than its magnetic moment direction — the angular momentum L = Iω ẑ is the same regardless of which pole is "up."

However, Drew is using this not just as a curiosity but as a direct analog to his magnetic levitation mechanism, suggesting the same physics (gyroscopic rise against gravity) applies to his spinning magnet in a 3-phase field.
