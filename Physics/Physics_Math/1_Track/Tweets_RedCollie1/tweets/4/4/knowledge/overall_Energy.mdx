### Homopolar Motor Energy Chain

For the homopolar motor driving the liquid-metal spin:

Input: DC electrical energy (P_in = V × I, where V is the terminal voltage and I is the radial current through the liquid metal).

Energy conversion path:
P_in → P_Lorentz (mechanical work done by J × B on the conducting fluid)
→ KE_rotation = (1/2) I_fluid ω_vortex² (rotational kinetic energy of the vortex)
→ [coupling to 3-phase field] → F_rise × Δz (gravitational potential energy gained by the rising system)

Losses at each step:
- Ohmic dissipation in the liquid metal: P_ohmic = J² / σ × Volume (dominant energy loss)
- Viscous dissipation in the fluid: P_viscous ∝ η ω² (shear stress dissipation)
- Eddy current losses in the permanent magnets exposed to the rotating field

### Energy Distribution in the 3-Wire / 6-Wire Coil System

The full system from tweet 2042518945157238885:
- 3 central wire coils: U-V-W power → drives inner disc magnet at ω_inner.
- 6 outer wire coils: U-V-W-U-V-W power → drives outer 4-magnet flywheel at −ω_outer.

**Total electrical power input:**
P_total = P_inner_coils + P_outer_coils
= (3/2) × I_inner² × R_inner + (3/2) × I_outer² × R_outer

where R_inner and R_outer are the coil resistances and the factor 3/2 comes from 3-phase balanced loading.

**Mechanical power output:**
P_mech_inner = τ_inner × ω_inner
P_mech_outer = τ_outer × ω_outer

**Where the energy goes:**
At steady state, P_input = P_mech + P_losses. The mechanical power goes into maintaining the counter-rotating angular velocities against friction and drag. Any lift force F_z × v_z (where v_z is any vertical velocity of the rising magnet) is an additional mechanical power output.

**Experimental note (video 13_2042517002548580352):** "No noise" at 30 Hz operation — implies low mechanical losses (quiet operation = low vibration = low friction losses). This is physically significant: low acoustic/vibration energy output means most input electrical energy goes into the magnetic field interactions and rotational kinetic energy.

### Mercury vs. Galinstan: Energy Implications of Working Fluid Choice

Mercury (Hg):
- Electrical conductivity: σ_Hg ≈ 1.04 × 10⁶ S/m (lower than galinstan).
- Density: ρ_Hg ≈ 13,534 kg/m³ (much higher than galinstan, ≈6,440 kg/m³).
- Higher density → greater moment of inertia for same volume → more angular momentum per unit volume.
- Higher density → greater inertial force in the vortex (centrifugal pressure builds up more strongly).

Galinstan:
- Electrical conductivity: σ_galinstan ≈ 3.46 × 10⁶ S/m (3.3× higher than mercury).
- Density: ρ_galinstan ≈ 6,440 kg/m³.
- Higher conductivity → more efficient Lorentz force coupling → more torque per amp of applied current.
- Lower density → less angular momentum per unit volume, but easier to spin up (less rotational inertia to overcome during acceleration).

**Energy efficiency trade-off:** Galinstan requires ~3.3× less current for the same force density as mercury, meaning ~3.3× less ohmic heating loss (P_ohmic ∝ J²/σ) for the same mechanical output. This makes galinstan energetically superior despite its lower density, unless the higher angular momentum of mercury is specifically required.

Drew's choice of galinstan is thus both regulatory (mercury is banned in Australia) and energetically rational.

### 3I/ATLAS and the Nickel Coating: Material Energy Considerations

Drew reports: "Another new photo November 17! GREEN for its nickel coating." (tweet 1990609334712348761, referring to his prototype with a nickel-coated component.)

In tweet 1993954836573004049, Drew discusses 3I/ATLAS (the interstellar object), noting a "large green nickel coma" and "yellow jets" closer to a "bright white core."

**Material energy relevance:**
Nickel (Ni) is ferromagnetic at room temperature (Curie temperature: 358°C). A nickel coating on a rotating component would contribute to the magnetic field configuration:
- Ferromagnetic coating increases permeability of the surface, concentrating magnetic flux.
- Rotating ferromagnetic material generates an alternating magnetic field externally (similar to a rotating magnet), contributing to the total rotating field interaction with the permanent magnet rotor.
- Nickel plating changes the surface work function (relevant if the component is involved in electron emission or electrostatic effects).

**Energy implication:** The green color of nickel oxide (NiO) or nickel plating under certain oxidation conditions is noted, but the primary relevance is the ferromagnetic property of Ni contributing to the magnetic field configuration of the prototype.
