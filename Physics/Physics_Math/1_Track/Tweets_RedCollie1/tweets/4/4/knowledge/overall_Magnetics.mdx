### 3-Phase Helical Magnetic Field: The Core Propulsive Field Configuration

Drew's core propulsion concept centers on the interaction between a spinning permanent magnet and a 3-phase rotating magnetic field. The terminology and geometry:

**3-phase wire coils:**
- 6 outer wire coils arranged in a ring (or 3 inner coils driving the central magnet, and 6 outer coils driving the outer flywheel magnets — see tweet 2042518945157238885).
- Phased U-V-W (120° electrical phase separation between coils) for the inner driving coils.
- U-V-W-U-V-W phasing for the outer 6-coil ring.
- This arrangement produces a rotating (helical in 3D) magnetic field in the XY-plane — identical in principle to the stator field of a 3-phase induction motor or synchronous motor.

**The rotating stator field:**
In a 3-phase system with spatial coil separation of 120° and temporal phase separation of 120°, the resultant field B_rotating sweeps the XY-plane at the angular frequency ω_field = 2πf, where f is the drive frequency. The field magnitude is constant: |B_rotating| = (3/2) × B_peak for a balanced 3-phase system.

**Interaction with spinning permanent magnet:**
The permanent magnet acts as the rotor. When it spins at angular frequency ω_magnet, the relative angular velocity between the rotor and the rotating field determines the "slip" in the induction-motor analogy: ω_slip = ω_field − ω_magnet. For synchronous operation (ω_slip = 0), the magnet rotates at exactly the field frequency.

**The RISE property:**
Drew claims that when the permanent magnet spins within the 3-phase rotating field, it rises against gravity along the +Z axis. Both magnetic poles participate equally. The mechanism is distinct from:
- Conventional magnetic levitation (which requires opposing poles and careful position control).
- Earnshaw's theorem violation (static field configurations cannot levitate a permanent magnet stably in free space) — Drew's system uses a dynamic rotating field, not a static configuration, which is not subject to Earnshaw's theorem in its classical form.

### Homopolar Motor as Spin Driver

Drew proposes the initial spin is generated by a homopolar motor, "probably made of liquid-metal, with a permanent magnet inside." The homopolar motor (Faraday motor) principle:

- A conductor (here: liquid metal, likely galinstan) is placed in a static magnetic field B_0 oriented along the Z-axis (from the permanent magnet).
- A radial current I_r flows from center to periphery (or vice versa) through the conducting liquid.
- The Lorentz force F = I_r × B_0 acts tangentially (in the XY-plane), generating a spin torque τ = r × F.
- The resulting rotation is a function of the radial current magnitude and the static field strength: τ = (B_0 I r²) / 2 for a uniform current distribution.

**Galinstan as the liquid metal conductor:**
Galinstan (gallium 68.5 wt%, indium 21.5 wt%, tin 10 wt%; melting point ≈ −19°C) is used because:
- It is liquid at room temperature (unlike mercury, which is toxic and increasingly regulated).
- It has substantial electrical conductivity (~3.46 × 10⁶ S/m) enabling efficient Lorentz-force-driven vortex.
- It is non-toxic relative to mercury.
- Its density (~6.44 g/cm³) provides a meaningful inertial mass for the vortex.

**Lorentz force spin in the galinstan vortex experiment (video 13_1968862743961980928):**
- 11 permanent magnets arranged in a ring around the galinstan pool.
- DC current from a 12V battery applied across the galinstan pool (radial current configuration — "from red to black").
- The galinstan spins clockwise under the Lorentz force.
- Reversing the DC polarity ("red to black, reverse DC polarity") reverses the vortex spin to counter-clockwise.
- This demonstrates polarity-controlled spin direction — a direct experimental result.

The Prototype device (spin/counter-spin, 6-wire-coil version):

**Experimental frequencies (video 13_2042517002548580352):**

- Inner disc magnet: ~30 Hz clockwise.
- Total relative angular velocity between inner and outer: ω_rel = ω_inner + ω_outer ≈ 2 × 30 Hz = 60 Hz (since they spin in opposite directions).
- Drew notes: "a relative 2 × 30 Hz."

At 25 Hz outer ring, the device begins operating. The inner magnet "tries to go up, but the magnets outside won't let it go up." This is a direct experimental observation of a vertical (Z-axis) force on the inner magnet induced by the magnetic interaction.

**The angular momentum asymmetry claim:**
"The angular momentum due to counter-spin will always be greater than that due to spin."
This requires |L_outer| > |L_inner|, where L = Iω (moment of inertia × angular velocity). If the outer flywheel of 4 magnets has a greater moment of inertia I_outer than the inner disc magnet I_inner (likely due to the larger radius of the outer ring), then even at equal |ω|, |L_outer| > |L_inner|.

The claimed consequence: "Likewise the angular reaction by Newton's Third Law to counter-spin will always be greater than that due to spin." This angular reaction asymmetry is offered as the mechanism to overcome the force-compensation problem.
