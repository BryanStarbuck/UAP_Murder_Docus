### Gyroscopic Precession: Full Mathematical Structure

**Euler's Equations for Rigid Body Rotation:**

For a rigid body with principal moments of inertia I_1, I_2, I_3 about body-fixed axes (x', y', z'):

I_1 (dω_1/dt) − (I_2 − I_3) ω_2 ω_3 = τ_1
I_2 (dω_2/dt) − (I_3 − I_1) ω_3 ω_1 = τ_2
I_3 (dω_3/dt) − (I_1 − I_2) ω_1 ω_2 = τ_3

For a symmetric top (I_1 = I_2 = I_⊥, I_3 = I_∥, spin axis = z'):

I_⊥ (dω_1/dt) − (I_⊥ − I_∥) ω_2 ω_3 = τ_1
I_⊥ (dω_2/dt) − (I_∥ − I_⊥) ω_3 ω_1 = τ_2
I_∥ (dω_3/dt) = 0 (torque-free component along spin axis → ω_3 = ω_spin = const.)

**Precession angular velocity (space-fixed frame, under gravity):**

Ω_precession = (M g r_cm) / (I_∥ ω_spin)

where:
- M = total mass of the gyroscope
- g = gravitational acceleration (9.81 m/s²)
- r_cm = distance from pivot point to center of mass
- I_∥ = moment of inertia about spin axis
- ω_spin = spin angular velocity (rad/s)

Units check: [Ω_precession] = (kg × m/s² × m) / (kg·m² × rad/s) = (kg·m²/s²) / (kg·m²/s) = rad/s ✓

**Nutation frequency for a symmetric top (torque-free):**

Ω_nutation = (I_∥ − I_⊥) / I_⊥ × ω_spin

For a flat disc: I_∥ = MR²/2, I_⊥ = MR²/4:

Ω_nutation = (MR²/2 − MR²/4) / (MR²/4) × ω_spin = [(MR²/4) / (MR²/4)] × ω_spin = ω_spin

Result: For a flat disc, nutation frequency equals the spin frequency. The disc magnet used in Drew's experiment (a flat cylindrical permanent magnet) will have nutation oscillations at its spin frequency — important for stability analysis.

For a thin disc gyroscope under gravity with nutation, the full solution involves:

Θ(t) = Θ_0 + A cos(ω_nut t + φ_0)

where Θ is the tilt angle from vertical, A is the nutation amplitude, and φ_0 is the initial phase.

### Angular Momentum in the Spin/Counter-Spin System

**Angular momentum of each rotor:**

L_inner = I_inner × ω_inner ẑ (clockwise = +ẑ convention)

L_outer = I_outer × (−ω_outer) ẑ (counter-clockwise = −ẑ)

where I_inner = (1/2) M_inner R_inner², I_outer ≈ M_outer R_outer² (thin ring approximation)

**Total angular momentum:**

L_total = L_inner + L_outer = (I_inner ω_inner − I_outer ω_outer) ẑ

**Condition for angular momentum asymmetry (Drew's claim: |L_outer| > |L_inner|):**

I_outer ω_outer > I_inner ω_inner
M_outer R_outer² × ω_outer > (1/2) M_inner R_inner² × ω_inner

At equal frequencies (ω_outer = ω_inner = ω):
M_outer R_outer² > (1/2) M_inner R_inner²

This is satisfied when the outer ring of 4 magnets has greater mass × radius² than (1/2) × inner disc mass × inner disc radius². Given that R_outer >> R_inner in Drew's device (the outer ring is at larger radius), this inequality is easily satisfied.

**Experimental values (video 13_2042517002548580352):**
ω_inner = 2π × 30 ≈ 188.5 rad/s (clockwise)
ω_outer = 2π × 30 ≈ 188.5 rad/s (counter-clockwise)

Relative angular velocity:
ω_rel = ω_inner + ω_outer = 2π × 60 ≈ 376.99 rad/s

Net L_total direction: −ẑ (if outer dominates), magnitude: (I_outer − I_inner) × 2π × 30

### Lorentz Force on Conducting Liquid: Mathematical Framework

**Lorentz force density (per unit volume):**

f = J × B

where:
- J = σ(E + v × B): current density (A/m²), σ = electrical conductivity of galinstan
- B: magnetic field from the ring of permanent magnets (T)
- v: velocity of the conducting fluid (m/s)
- E: applied electric field from the DC battery

**For the static (v = 0) initial condition, the force density is:**

f_initial = J₀ × B = σE × B

Direction: tangential (azimuthal), generating spin torque.

**Torque on the galinstan vortex:**

τ = ∫_V (r × f) dV = ∫_V r × (J × B) dV

For a cylindrical geometry with radial current and axial field, J = J_r r̂ and B ≈ B_z ẑ:
J × B = J_r B_z (r̂ × ẑ) = −J_r B_z φ̂ (tangential)

r × (J × B) = r × (−J_r B_z φ̂) = r r̂ × (−J_r B_z φ̂) = −J_r B_z r ẑ

τ_total = −∫_V J_r B_z r dV ẑ (negative ẑ for clockwise with B in +z direction and radially outward current)

**Magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) steady-state spin:**

When the galinstan is rotating at ω_vortex, the back-EMF opposes further acceleration. The steady-state spin rate is:

ω_steady ∝ (I_applied × B_ring) / (η × R_vortex)

where η is the effective viscous resistance (dynamic viscosity of galinstan ≈ 2.04 × 10⁻³ Pa·s at 20°C) and R_vortex is the vortex radius.

### 3-Phase Rotating Field: Mathematical Representation

For a 3-phase coil system with phase separations of 120° spatially and temporally:

B_U(φ, t) = B_peak cos(φ) cos(ωt)
B_V(φ, t) = B_peak cos(φ − 2π/3) cos(ωt − 2π/3)
B_W(φ, t) = B_peak cos(φ − 4π/3) cos(ωt − 4π/3)

Resultant rotating field:
B_total(φ, t) = B_U + B_V + B_W = (3/2) B_peak cos(ωt − φ)

This is a single rotating vector of constant magnitude (3/2) B_peak, rotating at angular frequency ω in the φ-direction (XY-plane). The constant magnitude is a key property — unlike a single-phase pulsating field, the 3-phase rotating field has no pulsation.

**Force on a permanent magnet in this rotating field:**

For a magnetic dipole m = m ẑ (spin axis of permanent magnet along ẑ) in a rotating field B_ext(t) in the XY-plane:

τ = m × B_ext(t)

If the magnet's equatorial plane has a component aligned with B_ext, this produces a torque in the XY-plane (driving spin) plus, through gyroscopic coupling, a torque component along the ẑ-axis (producing precession/rise).

### Transistor / MOSFET: Mathematical Operation (Signal Amplification)

Drew states the transistor amplifies via a "gate switch." For a BJT transistor in the active (linear amplification) region:

I_C = β × I_B

where I_C is the collector current (large), I_B is the base current (small control current), and β (DC current gain) is typically 50–300.

For a MOSFET:
I_D = (μ_n C_ox W / 2L)(V_GS − V_th)² (saturation region)

where V_GS is the gate-source voltage (the "voltage control"), V_th is the threshold voltage, μ_n is the electron mobility, C_ox is the gate oxide capacitance per unit area, W/L is the width-to-length ratio of the channel.

**Relevance to 3-phase drive:** In the 3-phase inverter controlling Drew's coils, MOSFETs switch the U, V, W phases. Each phase current through the coils is:

I_phase(t) = I_peak × sin(ωt + θ_phase)

The rotating magnetic field is the superposition of these three sinusoidal current-driven fields.

### Crop Circle Formation: Microwave Energy Deposition Equations

The microwave heating hypothesis (Levengood/Taylor/Kaku):

**Power absorbed per unit volume in plant tissue:**

P_abs = (σ + ωε'') |E|² / 2

where:
- σ: ionic conductivity of plant tissue (S/m)
- ω = 2πf: microwave angular frequency
- ε'': imaginary part of relative permittivity (dielectric loss)
- |E|: electric field magnitude (V/m)

For plant stem tissue with high water content, ε'' is significant in the GHz range (water absorbs strongly at ~2.45 GHz — the standard industrial microwave frequency).

**Temperature rise:**

ΔT = P_abs × t / (ρ × c_p)

where ρ is tissue density, c_p is specific heat capacity, t is exposure time. For rapid localized heating (stem bending without breakage):

t << τ_thermal_diffusion = d² / (4α)

where d is the stem diameter and α = k/(ρ c_p) is the thermal diffusivity of plant tissue. This ensures the heating is localized to the stem cross-section before it can conduct away — causing rapid softening/bending.

**The vortex knot pattern at crop circle centers:** stems twist and converge at the formation center. This requires the energy source to spiral inward, consistent with a rotating electromagnetic beam or plasma vortex with angular momentum. The central convergence geometry suggests: the field had non-zero angular momentum at the formation center, implying a rotating rather than static source.
