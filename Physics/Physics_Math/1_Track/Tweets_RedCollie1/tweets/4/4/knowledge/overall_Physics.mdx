### Gyroscopic Precession: The Physical Analog for Anomalous Rise

Drew repeatedly invokes the classical gyroscope as the conceptual entry point. The standard physics:

**Gyroscope precession equation:**

τ_grav = dL/dt = Ω_precession × L

The precession angular velocity:

Ω_precession = (M × g × r_cm) / (I × ω_spin)

Direction: Ω_precession is perpendicular to both L and r_cm × g, causing the spin axis to sweep out a cone (precession cone).

This is because as the top precesses, nutation (the oscillatory perturbation of the precession) can couple into vertical motion if the pivot allows vertical displacement.

### Force Compensation: Newton's Third Law in the Spinning Magnet System

The core physics puzzle Drew identifies: "A complete system of spinning magnet, and 3-phase wire coils, seems to induce a DOWNWARD force of equal magnitude" to the upward rise force.

This is explicitly a Newton's Third Law (action-reaction) statement. If the spinning magnet experiences an upward force F_rise = +F_z, the 3-phase coil system (or the ground to which it is fixed) experiences a reaction force of −F_z (downward).

**The spinning-top analog:** Drew writes: "we can think of the rising force of a spinning top, which presses down onto a tabletop with precisely the same force as by which it rises."

This is a statement about the mechanical ground reaction: a spinning top in precession exerts a downward force on its pivot/support equal to the upward component it would have if the precession were converted to linear motion. The "force compensation" issue means the NET force on the complete system (magnet + coil assembly + support) measured on a scale is zero — only local redistribution of forces within the system.

**Engineering consequence:** To achieve flight, the coils must be co-located with (or rigidly attached to) the spinning magnet in a self-contained system. The downward reaction must be redirected into aerodynamic or field-mediated thrust rather than into the ground.

### Gyroscope Geometry: Precession and Nutation Physics

The image documents a CGI spinning top showing:
- Disc tilted ~30° from horizontal.
- Spin axis precessing around the vertical.
- Nutation: small rapid oscillations of the axis about the precession cone.

**Nutation frequency:** For a torque-free symmetric top (Euler's equations):
ω_nutation = (I_spin − I_perp) / I_perp × ω_spin

where I_spin is the moment of inertia about the spin axis and I_perp is the moment of inertia about a perpendicular axis.

For a flat disc: I_spin = MR²/2, I_perp = MR²/4, so:
ω_nutation = (MR²/2 − MR²/4) / (MR²/4) × ω_spin = (1/4 / 1/4) × ω_spin = ω_spin

For a flat disc, the nutation frequency equals the spin frequency — the disk "wobbles" at its own spin rate. This is relevant to Drew's spinning disc magnet, which is geometrically similar to a flat disc gyroscope.
