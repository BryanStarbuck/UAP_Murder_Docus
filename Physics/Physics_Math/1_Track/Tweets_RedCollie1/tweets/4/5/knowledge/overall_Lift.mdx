**Core experimental result:** A ring permanent magnet (100 mm OD, 13 mm bore, 8 mm rod) placed inside a three-phase rotating magnetic field rises axially — achieving lift along the Z-axis — exclusively when oriented with the South (blue) pole pointing upward. The North (red) pole orientation does not produce sustained lift.

**Kinematic proof that lift is not mechanical screw motion:**
1. The 13 mm central hole in the plastic magnet insert was pre-drilled in both directions (both CW and CCW thread-cutting directions), eliminating any right-hand or left-hand thread structure.
2. At the moment of maximum ascent, the rod and cork are nearly stationary (not spinning).
3. The video observation: "It's a thrust motion. Up with thrust and not spin."

These three independent observations establish that the Z-axis displacement is a genuine electromagnetic body force (thrust) rather than any mechanical screw, Archimedes' effect, or torque-to-translation coupling. This is fundamental: it distinguishes a novel electromagnetic lift mechanism from known rotary-to-linear conversion.

**Rotation symmetry of the lift force:**
Both clockwise and anti-clockwise rotation of the driving three-phase field produce the same upward force for South-up orientation. This demonstrates the lift mechanism is independent of field handedness — it is a property of the axial gradient structure of the three-phase field and the polarity orientation of the test magnet, not of the rotational direction.

**Coupled counter-rotation observation:**
The ascending magnet pole rotates in the opposite direction to the spinning action below it. This spontaneous angular momentum segregation — inner rising pole counter-rotating relative to the base — is a key feature. It implies the device naturally partitions angular momentum into opposing axial components as a byproduct of the lift-generating dynamics.
