### Polarity-Selective Axial Force in a Rotating Magnetic Field

**Experimental result (observational, not theoretical derivation):**
A ring permanent magnet (100 mm OD, 13 mm hole, 8 mm rod) spinning in a three-phase rotating magnetic field experiences a net axial (Z-direction) translational force only when the South (blue) pole is oriented upward. The North (red) pole orientation does not produce this force.

**Physical reasoning:**
For a magnetic dipole **m** in a spatially varying field **B**, the force is:
  **F** = ∇(**m** · **B**)

When the magnet spins in the rotating field, the time-averaged interaction energy ⟨**m** · **B**⟩ depends on the relative orientation of **m** and the spatial gradient of **B**. The three-phase coil geometry creates a rotating field with axial gradient structure. For the South-up orientation, the gradient projects favorably onto the axial direction, producing a net +Z force. For the North-up orientation, the geometry is such that this axial force is suppressed or reversed.

**The thrust-not-spin observation:**
The rod and cork are nearly stationary while the magnet rises. This eliminates torque-to-translation coupling mechanisms (Archimedes screw, mechanical thread, gyroscopic precession-induced translation). The Z-axis force is therefore an electromagnetic body force, not a mechanical constraint force. In classical electrodynamics, body forces on magnetized media in non-uniform fields are given by the Kelvin force density:
  **f** = μ₀ (**M** · ∇)**H**
where **M** is magnetization and **H** is the applied field. The integrated Kelvin force over the magnet volume gives the net body force. Drew's result suggests this body force has a net Z-component for South-up but not North-up orientation.

**Rotation-independence of the axial force:**
Both clockwise and anti-clockwise rotation of the driving field produce the same axial force for South-up orientation. This symmetry implies the axial force mechanism is not dependent on the handedness of the field rotation, but only on the polarity of the test magnet relative to the axial gradient of the field.

**Counter-rotation relationship:**
"The upward pole spin-motion for blue-up permanent magnets is always opposite to spin-motion in the magnet below." This is a systematic angular momentum inversion. If the lower magnet has angular momentum +L_z, the rising pole acquires −L_z. Conservation of total angular momentum is maintained within the field-magnet system; the field (driven by the external power supply) absorbs the differential angular momentum.

### 4D Spacetime Model of Electron Magnetism

**The standard physics:**
Moving charges produce magnetic fields (Biot-Savart / Ampere's law). Permanent magnets arise from the net magnetic moment of unpaired electron spins (Bohr magneton μ_B = eℏ/2m_e).

**Drew's extended 4D model:**
"Magnetism is not 'created' when current electrons move through a wire. It was always there, but such motions did not intersect our 3-D space xyz, until the electron moved through a wire."

Physical content: the electron's full trajectory exists in a 4D spacetime manifold (xyzt). The magnetic field is a geometric property of this 4D trajectory — it is the component of the electromagnetic field tensor F_μν that is "off-diagonal" in space-time mixing terms. When an electron moves through a wire (acquires a velocity component along x or y), the Lorentz transformation mixes E and B fields. In the rest frame of the lab (but not the electron), the transformed fields contain a magnetic component:

  B = γ(B₀ - v × E/c²)
  
For an electron at rest in the wire's rest frame, B₀ ≈ 0 but when it moves with velocity v, the transformed field includes contributions from E₀ (the Coulomb field of the electron), yielding a non-zero B in the lab frame. Drew interprets this as "the magnetic field was always there in 4D" — the Lorentz-transformed magnetic component pre-existed in the 4D structure; current flow changes which component projects onto 3-space.

**Connection to E = mc²:**
"The 'wave' motion of an electron was likewise always there, even when we see it as a 'particle'. Hence its huge energy at rest of E = mc²."

In de Broglie–Bohm theory, the electron's pilot wave has frequency ν = mc²/h even in the rest frame. Drew interprets the rest mass energy as the energy of this perpetual internal wave motion in 4D spacetime. This is not standard textbook interpretation but is consistent with Schrödinger's Zitterbewegung: the rapid oscillatory motion of a Dirac electron at velocity ±c with frequency ω = 2mc²/ℏ.

**Right-hand rule from 4D chirality:**
"Many billions of small right-handed motions likewise add to give a 'right-hand rule' for magnetism on a large scale." The macroscopic right-hand rule of electromagnetism is, in this model, the statistical average of the right-handed chirality of electron trajectories in 4D spacetime. The chirality is an intrinsic property of the electron (spin = ℏ/2, right-handed spinor).

**The key physics claim:**
"What is truly remarkable here, is that we can get fast, powerful, clockwise spin, as well as fast, powerful, anti-clockwise counter-spin, from the SAME 3-phase, clockwise rotating magnetic field!"

Physical basis of the counter-rotation: The outer ring of eight NS-NS-NS-NS alternating-polarity magnets on a zirconia bearing interacts with the rotating field in a manner analogous to an induction motor operating in the slip regime. The rotating field induces eddy currents and/or exerts a periodic force on the alternating-polarity array that can — depending on the number of pole pairs and the field rotation speed — produce net torque in the direction opposite to field rotation (asynchronous / slip-dependent behavior).

**Proposed modification of Newton's Third Law:**
"We might expect two, counter-rotating, magnetic discs to have strong and novel effects on Newton's Third Law of action-reaction."

In standard mechanics, Newton's Third Law requires that F₁₂ = −F₂₁. For two co-axial spinning magnetic discs with opposite spin angular momenta, the electromagnetic stress tensor T_μν between them contains off-diagonal space-time terms (Poynting vector stress). These terms can produce a net electromagnetic momentum in the space between the discs even when the mechanical momentum sums to zero. The Maxwell stress tensor:
  T_ij = ε₀(E_i E_j - ½δ_ij E²) + (1/μ₀)(B_i B_j - ½δ_ij B²)
contributes a radiation pressure that is, in principle, asymmetric between the two rotating systems if the field configuration is not time-reversal symmetric. The counter-rotating arrangement breaks time-reversal symmetry in the composite field, potentially creating a net axial component of T_ij that acts on the bounding surfaces of the system.

### Gyroscopic Stabilization of the Rising Magnet

"A fast-spinning magnet acts like a gyroscope, and thus is [stable in orientation]."

Gyroscopic torque: τ_gyro = L × Ω, where L = Iω is the spin angular momentum and Ω is the precession angular velocity. For a rapidly spinning magnet, L is large, and the gyroscopic stiffness resists any torque that would tilt the spin axis. This allows the magnet to maintain its pole orientation (South-up) as it rises through the varying field, ensuring the polarity-selective force condition is maintained throughout the ascent. The gyroscopic stabilization is thus a prerequisite for sustained axial propulsion: without it, the magnet would precess and lose the South-up orientation that generates the force.

**Observation of wobble at low spin rates:**
At low spin rates (low L), precession is observable ("the spinning and wobbling (rising) magnet"). As spin rate increases, the gyroscopic term dominates and wobble diminishes, and the axial displacement begins. This suggests a threshold spin rate below which the South-up orientation is not maintained and above which stable axial thrust occurs.
