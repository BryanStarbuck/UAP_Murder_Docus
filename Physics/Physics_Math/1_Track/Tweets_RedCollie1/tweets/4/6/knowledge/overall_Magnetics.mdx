## Section: 1998543870406365686.yaml — MHD Lorentz Drive on Conductive Fluid

**Experimental Setup:**
- Permanent magnet (or electromagnet) with field B directed along Z-axis (vertical, either up or down depending on pole orientation).
- Conductive fluid above the magnet: salt water used in tabletop demonstration; galinstan (Ga-In-Sn eutectic, σ ≈ 3.46 × 10⁶ S/m) proposed as operational fluid.
- Applied voltage: 50 V DC (demonstration); 40–50 kV DC (proposed operational scale).
- Electrodes immersed in fluid to drive current density **J** in the radial or tangential direction.

**Lorentz Body Force on Fluid:**

The volumetric Lorentz body force on the conducting fluid is:

**f** = **J** × **B**

where:
- **J** = current density vector (A/m²) in the conducting fluid
- **B** = magnetic flux density vector (T) from the permanent magnet

For a radial current density **J** = J_r r̂ and an axial magnetic field **B** = B_z ẑ:

**f** = J_r B_z (r̂ × ẑ) = J_r B_z (-φ̂) = -J_r B_z φ̂

This azimuthal force drives the fluid into rotation in the XY-plane (azimuthal direction). The sign of rotation (clockwise or counter-clockwise when viewed from above) depends on the sign of J_r and the direction of B_z (i.e., which pole of the magnet faces upward: North vs. South).

**Pole-Reversal Experiment** (video 13_1998542927191298048_video.mdx):
- 80 mm neodymium permanent magnet with central rod (13 mm hole, 8 mm rod per companion YAML context).
- With N pole facing up: conductive dye (green food dye in salt water) swirls counter-clockwise (CCW) at 50 V.
- Magnet flipped so S pole faces up: dye swirls clockwise (CW) at 50 V.

This is a direct experimental demonstration of:

**Ω_fluid ∝ sign(B_z) × sign(J_r)**

The reversal of the magnetic pole orientation reverses B_z → −B_z, which reverses the azimuthal Lorentz force, reversing the rotation direction. Experimental result: confirmed unambiguously in tabletop experiment.

**3-Phase Wire Coil Addition:**

Once the conductive fluid (or a secondary magnet) is in azimuthal rotation, Drew proposes adding three wire coils in 3-phase AC configuration. The rotating magnetic field from the 3-phase coils:

B_rotating(x,y,t) = B_0 [cos(ωt) x̂ + cos(ωt - 2π/3) ŷ + cos(ωt - 4π/3) (x̂ × ŷ)]

(for a 2-pole 3-phase winding, the rotating field sweeps the XY-plane at angular frequency ω = 2πf, where f = AC supply frequency, typically 50 or 60 Hz).

The claim is that the interaction between this rotating XY-plane field and the already-rotating conductive fluid/magnet produces an additional Z-axis force component (vertical lift). The physical mechanism proposed is analogous to how a 3-phase induction motor generates torque, but the claimed output is translational rather than rotational.

## Section: 1912108899605598494.yaml — "Upward Pole" Force in 3-Phase Magnetic Field

The referenced parent tweet (1912108172971790596) states: "When using a 100 mm permanent-magnet (13 mm hole, 8 mm rod) as a spinning object in a 3-phase magnetic field, we see 'upward pole' forces only for the blue 'South' orientation."

Key physical observations:
- Magnet geometry: 100 mm diameter toroid/disc permanent magnet, 13 mm central hole, 8 mm axial rod for spin stabilization.
- The magnet is placed inside or near a 3-phase rotating magnetic field.
- "Upward pole" forces: a net Z-axis (vertical) force component is observed.
- Crucially: this "upward pole" force is **orientation-dependent** — observed only when the South pole faces upward, not when the North pole faces upward.

This orientation-dependence of the Z-axis force is a significant claim. In a uniform magnetic field, a magnetic dipole experiences a force only in the presence of a field gradient: **F** = ∇(**m** · **B**). In a 3-phase rotating field configuration, the field topology is not uniform; it has spatial structure. The interaction between the spinning magnetic moment vector (precessing as the magnet spins) and the rotating external field vector can produce time-averaged forces that depend on the relative orientation of the magnet's dipole moment and the direction of rotation.

The role of the central rod: Drew states (tweet 1920987068525658515): "the rod is needed for spin stability." The rod through the central hole of the toroidal/donut-shaped magnet provides the mechanical axle for gyroscopic stabilization — without it, the spinning magnet would precess chaotically rather than maintaining a well-defined spin axis, preventing the observation of the orientation-dependent Z-axis force.

## Section: 1959735926789415048.yaml — AC Electromagnet as Alternating Lorentz Force Generator

**Device Configuration (Drew's modified Farnsworth fusor):**

Slide 2 configuration: 40 kV DC power supply + DC electromagnet
- Static magnetic field B_DC along Z-axis.
- Plasma ions in the fusor's interior experience one Lorentz force: F = qv × B_DC.
- Ions spiral in helical orbits around B-field lines (Larmor orbits): r_L = mv⊥/(|q|B), where m is ion mass, v⊥ is velocity component perpendicular to B, q is ion charge.
- This is "just one Lorentz force" as stated in the tweet.

Slide 3 configuration: 40 kV DC + AC electromagnet
- B_AC(t) = B₀ cos(ωt) where ω = 2π × 50 rad/s (50 Hz AC).
- The AC field alternates the direction of B between +Z and −Z at 50 Hz.
- When B is in +Z direction: positive ions spiral clockwise (CW) in the XY-plane.
- When B is in −Z direction: positive ions spiral counter-clockwise (CCW) in the XY-plane.
- The alternation occurs 50 times per second → ions alternate CW and CCW Larmor orbits at 50 Hz.
- "Now we see two Lorentz forces, which alternate in time clockwise versus anti-clockwise, 50 times per second."

The key result: the CCW-spiraling ions (during negative B half-cycle) and CW-spiraling ions (during positive B half-cycle) collide head-on when their orbits converge at the device center. This is geometrically similar to a colliding-beam configuration but achieved by temporal alternation of the confinement field rather than two separate beam lines.

**Observed outcome:** A "white region" at the central convergence zone — indicating extremely high local temperature (thermal plasma luminescence in visible or near-UV range) while the rest of the device remains only warm. This is a localized fusion-relevant temperature condition.

**Mechanism:** The alternating Lorentz force converts the DC-driven inward electrostatic acceleration (from the fusor's −40 to −50 kV central cathode) into a combination of inward radial motion (from the electrostatic field) and alternating azimuthal orbital motion (from the AC magnetic field), resulting in periodic head-on collisions of inward-moving ions at the center.

**Notation summary:**

F_electrostatic = qE_r r̂ = q(V/r) r̂ (inward, centripetal)
F_Lorentz_AC = q(v × B_AC(t)) = q(v_r × B₀cos(ωt) ẑ) = qv_r B₀cos(ωt) φ̂

The total force has:
- Radial component driving ions inward to fusion conditions.
- Time-varying azimuthal component driving clockwise/counter-clockwise Larmor circulation alternation.

At the crossing point (device center), ions arriving from opposite azimuthal directions during each half-cycle collide with head-on velocity components → elevated nuclear reaction cross-section.
