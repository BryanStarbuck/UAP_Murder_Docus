## Section: 1998543870406365686.yaml — Lorentz Force on Conducting Fluid

### Primary Equation

**f** = **J** × **B**

Components:
- **f**: volumetric body force density [N/m³] on conducting fluid
- **J**: current density vector [A/m²]; for radial electrode geometry: **J** ≈ J_r r̂
- **B**: magnetic flux density [T]; for axial permanent magnet with N pole up: **B** ≈ B_z ẑ (B_z > 0)

### Cross Product Evaluation (cylindrical coordinates)

r̂ × ẑ = −φ̂

Therefore:
**f** = J_r r̂ × B_z ẑ = J_r B_z (r̂ × ẑ) = −J_r B_z φ̂

For J_r > 0 and B_z > 0: **f** is in the −φ̂ direction (clockwise rotation when viewed from above, in standard right-hand convention with ẑ pointing up).

For J_r > 0 and B_z < 0 (magnet flipped, S pole up): **f** is in the +φ̂ direction (counter-clockwise rotation).

This matches the experimental observation (video 13_1998542927191298048): pole reversal reverses rotation direction.

### Ohm's Law in Moving Conductor

**J** = σ(**E** + **v** × **B**)

where:
- σ: electrical conductivity [S/m]
- **E** = −∇V: electric field from applied DC voltage V
- **v**: fluid velocity vector [m/s]
- **v** × **B**: motional EMF term (back-EMF as fluid gains velocity)

At steady state (force balance between driving Lorentz force and viscous drag):

Viscous drag (simplified, for cylindrical Couette flow):
τ_viscous = η (dv_φ/dr) ≈ η Ω R / δ [approximation for thin fluid layer]

Driving azimuthal force:
F_φ = J_r B_z per unit volume

Steady state: J_r B_z = η Ω_fluid / (scale length)

→ Ω_fluid ∝ (J_r B_z) / η

### Rotation Direction Mapping (Experimental)

| B_z direction | Rotation (viewed from above) | Corresponds to pole |
|---|---|---|
| B_z > 0 (N pole up) | CCW | North |
| B_z < 0 (S pole up) | CW | South |

(Note: sign convention depends on current direction; experimentally verified reversal confirmed in 80 mm neodymium magnet test at 50 V)

## Section: 1941067166998315300.yaml — Gyroscope Precession Mathematics

### Angular Momentum of Spinning Body

**L** = I ω n̂

where:
- I: moment of inertia about spin axis [kg·m²]
- ω: angular velocity of spin [rad/s]
- n̂: unit vector along spin axis

### Precession under Applied Torque

dL/dt = **τ**

For a gyroscope supported at a pivot, with center of mass at position **r** from pivot, gravitational torque:

**τ** = **r** × (−M g ẑ) = M g r (r̂ × (−ẑ)) = M g r φ̂  [varies with orientation]

In the simplest case (spin axis horizontal, pivot at one end):

|**τ**| = M g r

Precession angular frequency:

Ω_p = |**τ**| / |**L**| = M g r / (I ω)

Precession direction: horizontal, perpendicular to both **L** (spin axis) and **τ** (torque direction), resulting in horizontal revolution of the spin axis around the vertical.

### Relevance to UAP Propulsion

If the spinning magnetic element has I ω >> M g r / Ω_p_desired, then the precession frequency is slow and the element maintains its spin axis orientation effectively — gyroscopic rigidity.

The proposed mechanism: by applying an electromagnetic torque **τ_EM** through the 3-phase coil system that exactly cancels **τ_gravity** at all times, the gyroscope neither falls nor precesses — it maintains its orientation while the interaction with the rotating field produces the Z-axis force. This is the hypothesized "upward pole" force in operation.
