## Section: 1937003484433481960.yaml — Conductive Disc AC Lenz Levitation

Drew states: "a permanent disc magnet shows very little AC Lenz levitation" — implying that a conductive disc DOES show significant AC Lenz levitation.

### AC Lenz Levitation (Electromagnetic Levitation) Physics:
An oscillating magnetic field B(t) = B_0 cos(ωt) ẑ above a conductive disc induces eddy currents in the disc by Faraday's law:
EMF = -dΦ_B/dt = -d/dt[B_0 cos(ωt) A] = B_0 A ω sin(ωt)

where A = area of disc face.

Induced eddy currents J_eddy(t) ∝ sin(ωt) create a magnetic field that (by Lenz's law) opposes the change in flux — i.e., the eddy current's magnetic field pushes back against the applied AC field.

The time-averaged repulsive force (electromagnetic levitation force):
<F_z> = -(μ₀/4π) ∫∫ [J_eddy(t) × B_ext(t)] · ẑ dV dt / T

For a thick disc with conductivity σ and thickness d << skin depth δ = √(2/(μ₀ σ ω)):
<F_z> ∝ σ d ω B₀² A

This force is directed upward (away from the AC field source) — this is AC electromagnetic levitation, also known as the "Lenz's law levitation" or "magnetic braking levitation."

### Why a permanent magnet disc levitates poorly:
A permanent magnet disc has a fixed internal magnetization. When subjected to the external AC field, the opposition is primarily from the magnet's static field, not from induced currents. The AC flux induces little eddy current because the magnet's highly permeable interior suppresses the skin-depth penetration of AC flux. Result: low eddy-current density → low Lenz levitation force.

A conductive (non-magnetic) Al or Cu disc has high σ, low μ_r ≈ 1, and full skin-depth penetration at the relevant frequencies → strong eddy currents → strong Lenz levitation force.

### Implication for energy:
The levitation force is sustained by the AC power input to the coil system — it is not anomalous energy generation. However, the levitation represents mechanical work done by the electromagnetic field against gravity: W_grav = M_disc g h (where h is levitation height). This energy comes directly from the AC electrical input. The ratio W_grav/P_AC is the "lift efficiency" — the fraction of electrical input power converted to gravitational potential energy of the levitated disc.

For high-conductivity materials at optimal frequency (when disc radius r ≈ skin depth δ), levitation efficiency can be high.
