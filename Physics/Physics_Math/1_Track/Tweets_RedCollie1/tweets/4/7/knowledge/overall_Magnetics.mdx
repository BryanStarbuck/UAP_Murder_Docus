## Section: 1920988158734266684.yaml — Ring Magnet Focal Point and Spinning Magnet Levitation

Drew identifies the "focal point" of a ring magnet as a key experimental discovery. A ring magnet is a toroidal or annular permanent magnet with a hole along its axis. The axial magnetic field of a ring magnet has a specific spatial geometry:

- On the axis of symmetry (z-axis), the field is directed axially.
- In the XY plane (radial plane through the center), the field has both radial and axial components depending on position relative to the ring's inner hole.
- The "focal point" Drew refers to is presumably the axial position above or below the ring at which the field lines converge — i.e., the point of maximum axial field strength or the node at which the diverging and converging field line topology transitions. This is a non-trivial geometric property of a finite-thickness ring magnet, not present in an idealized dipole.

A "spinning magnet held above and below at its focal point" (from tweet description of fourth crop-circle image): the configuration involves a magnet constrained at its focal point(s) on both faces — the z-axis entry and exit points of the concentrated axial field. This creates a boundary condition for the spinning magnet's precession axis, anchoring the spin axis to the magnetic focal geometry rather than to an arbitrary mechanical support.

Three-phase power referenced in crop circle imagery:
- One crop circle image explicitly "shows '3-phase power'" in the context of UFO levitation.
- 3-phase power produces a rotating magnetic field when applied to three spatially displaced coils at 120-degree angular intervals. The rotating field vector in the XY plane sweeps continuously: B(t) = B_0[cos(ωt) x̂ + cos(ωt - 2π/3) ŷ + cos(ωt - 4π/3) ẑ] — though in a 3-phase induction configuration the third phase typically closes in a third spatial coil, producing |B_rotating| = (3/2)B_0 at constant magnitude rotating in the XY plane at angular frequency ω.

UFO rises by SPIN: Two crop circle images are described as showing "how a UFO rises up by SPIN" — the mechanism being that a fast-spinning magnetic element (gyroscope-like) develops an apparent upward force vector.

## Section: 1937003484433481960.yaml — Six 3-Phase Coil Architecture (Crop Circle Blueprint)

Referenced tweet (parent: 1936728406512795694) describes the recurring crop-circle blueprint:

Architecture:
* Six 3-phase wire coils around the outside — this is a 6-coil, 3-phase induction motor stator configuration producing a rotating magnetic field in the XY plane (azimuthal rotation). A standard 3-phase motor uses 3 coils at 120-degree spacing; six coils can be wired as two sets of 3-phase windings (at 60-degree spacing), producing a more uniform rotating field with reduced spatial harmonics.
* One large, round, flat wire coil (1-phase) placed just above them — this produces a predominantly axial (Z-direction) field component. If driven at AC, it creates an oscillating axial field. If combined with the rotating XY field from the outer coils, the total field vector traces a helical or conical path, depending on phase relationship.
* A small rotor in the middle (requires conductive aluminum or copper disc, NOT a permanent magnet disc).

Conductive disc requirement:
- A conductive (non-ferromagnetic) aluminum or copper disc responds to the rotating magnetic field through eddy current induction (Lenz's law effect in the AC motor context, equivalent to the induction motor squirrel-cage rotor principle).
- The eddy currents induced in the disc interact with the external rotating field to produce a torque that drags the disc toward synchronous rotation with the field.
- Torque on the disc: τ = -(∂W/∂θ)|_{I=const} where W is the stored magnetic energy — or more directly, τ ∝ Im[χ(ω)] × |B_ext|^2 where χ(ω) is the complex susceptibility of the conducting disc to the rotating field, with Im[χ] > 0 providing the drag torque.
- A permanent disc magnet instead "shows very little AC Lenz levitation" because a permanent magnet already has a fixed internal field and resists inductive coupling differently — its magnetization cannot be driven by the external AC field in the same drag-torque manner.

## Section: 1968945832247574953.yaml — Spinning Vortex in Conductive Liquid (Magnetohydrodynamic Demonstration)

Experiment: Green food dye added to pool salt water (conductive electrolyte), DC current applied, placed over a magnet below.

Physical mechanism (magnetohydrodynamic — MHD):
- The external magnet (permanent magnet below the vessel) provides axial magnetic field B_z pointing upward through the liquid.
- DC current I passes through the saline liquid, establishing current density J in the liquid (radially if the electrodes are at center and rim; axially if between top and bottom plates).
- Lorentz body force per unit volume: f = J × B
- For radial current J = J_r r̂ and axial field B = B_z ẑ: f = J_r B_z (r̂ × ẑ) = -J_r B_z φ̂ — an azimuthal (tangential) force driving the liquid into rotation.
- This is the principle of a liquid-metal MHD pump or the Faraday disk motor operating with a conductive liquid rotor.
- The resulting "spinning vortex" is a macroscopic flow field in the liquid.

Connection to UFO crop-picture imagery: Drew states that "that spinning vortex shows up in many UFO-related crop pictures" — implying the crop formations encode the MHD spinning vortex as a representation of the UFO propulsion field topology.

Referenced parent tweet (1968863383266148583) describes a prior experiment: a "spinning vortex of the liquid metal 'galinstan'" (gallium-indium-tin eutectic alloy, melting point ~ -19°C, liquid at room temperature, highly electrically conductive: σ ≈ 3.46 × 10^6 S/m at 20°C). Eleven "thin" magnets around the outside provide the external field for the galinstan experiment. The galinstan vortex is described as the "second critical step" toward making a UFO-propulsion drive.

Apparatus description from image analysis:
- Glass bowl (~25-30 cm diameter) containing shallow conductive liquid (electrolyte/saline).
- Central copper rod/electrode descending to center of bowl (central current terminal).
- Three or four yellow tape contact squares at the bowl rim (outer current return terminals).
- Permanent magnet below the bowl (axial field B_z, not shown in image).

- Label "N2" on bowl base (magnet configuration identifier in experimental series).

- Current flows radially outward: J = J_r r̂ from central copper electrode through conductive liquid to outer rim contacts.

- Lorentz body force: f = J × B = J_r r̂ × B_z ẑ = -J_r B_z φ̂ (azimuthal, driving liquid rotation).

- Power input: P = IV (electrical), P_mechanical = τ × ω (mechanical rotation of liquid).

Relationship to Drew's broader work: this experiment demonstrates the Lorentz force as a mechanism for converting radial current flow (XY-plane current component) combined with axial magnetic field (Z-axis) into azimuthal (tangential, XY-plane) mechanical rotation — the same force decomposition claimed to underlie UFO propulsion.
