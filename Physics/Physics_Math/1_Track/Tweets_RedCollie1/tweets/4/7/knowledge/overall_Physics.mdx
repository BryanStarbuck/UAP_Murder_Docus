### The Upward Force from Spinning Magnets

Claim: A fast-spinning magnet (or magnetic flywheel) produces a strong upward force by "pushing against space" in the manner of a spinning gyroscope.

Standard gyroscope physics for reference:
- Angular momentum: L = I × ω (where I = moment of inertia, ω = angular velocity vector)
- Torque-precession relation: τ = dL/dt = Ω_prec × L
- Precession angular velocity: Ω_prec = τ/|L| = (r × mg)/|L|

For a spinning magnet in a magnetic field (not just gravitational), the effective torque τ includes magnetic torque τ_mag = μ × B (where μ = magnetic dipole moment). The precession behavior is therefore modified by the field geometry.

Drew's claim that spinning fast imparts an upward force "like a spinning gyroscope" references the gyroscopic resistance to changes in orientation — but the directionality of any net vertical force requires breaking the axial symmetry of the force environment, which the proposed Lorentz force conversion achieves.

### Newton's Third Law and Its Proposed Circumvention

Standard Newton's Third Law: for every action force F_AB on body B by body A, there is an equal and opposite reaction force F_BA = -F_AB on body A by body B.

Problem for levitation: a spinning magnet producing upward force F_up necessarily produces downward force F_down = -F_up on the craft/support structure. Net force on the combined system = 0.

Proposed resolution: Convert F_down into a Lorentz force.

The Lorentz force on a charged particle: F = q(E + v × B)
The Lorentz force on a current element: dF = I(dl × B)

Key property exploited: The Lorentz force is NOT a Newtonian contact force between two bodies. It is a field-mediated force in which the reaction is expressed as a change in the electromagnetic field momentum (Poynting vector), not as a direct mechanical force on a discrete body. Maxwell stress tensor mediates the momentum exchange between matter and field.

Momentum balance in electrodynamics (Poynting theorem for momentum):
∂g/∂t + ∇·T = -f_mech

Where:
- g = electromagnetic field momentum density = (1/c²)(E × H) = S/c²
- T = Maxwell stress tensor T_ij = ε_0(E_i E_j - ½δ_ij E²) + (1/μ_0)(B_i B_j - ½δ_ij B²)
- f_mech = mechanical force density on matter

If the downward reaction force is entirely expressed as ∂g/∂t (change in electromagnetic momentum flux), it need not appear as a reaction force on the craft structure. This is Drew's claimed mechanism — the "downward" force is absorbed into the electromagnetic field rather than reacting mechanically on the airframe.

### Experimental Evidence: Broken Spin Angular Momentum Conservation

Observation: DC current applied to disc magnet + surrounding thin wires.
- Disc mass M_disc ≈ 100 × M_wires (100:1 mass ratio, approximately)
- Both disc and surrounding wires spin at approximately the same angular speed |ω_disc| ≈ |ω_wires| in opposite directions.

Classical prediction: If equal and opposite torques τ act on disc (moment of inertia I_disc) and wires (moment of inertia I_wires) for equal time Δt:
- Angular momentum gained by disc: ΔL_disc = τ × Δt
- Angular momentum gained by wires: ΔL_wires = τ × Δt (equal magnitude)
- But ΔL = I × Δω, so Δω_wires/Δω_disc = I_disc/I_wires ≈ M_disc r²/(M_wires r²) = M_disc/M_wires ≈ 100

Classical prediction: wires should spin ~100 times faster than disc for equal torque.
Observed: approximately equal speeds.

Conclusion: classical torque-reaction balance is violated. The Lorentz force produces a "peculiar sideways action" that distributes angular impulse in a manner inconsistent with I × Δω conservation for the isolated mechanical subsystem.

Drew's interpretation: spin angular momentum m × v × r is NOT conserved "due to a peculiar sideways action of the Lorentz force."

Note for PhD-level scrutiny: In standard electrodynamics, the total angular momentum (mechanical + electromagnetic field angular momentum L_field = r × (E × B)/c²) IS conserved. The apparent violation is of the mechanical-subsystem-only conservation — the "missing" angular momentum has been transferred to the electromagnetic field. Drew's observation is consistent with this interpretation, even if his framing is in non-technical language.

### Horizontal and Vertical Dynamics

XY-plane (horizontal) dynamics:
- Rotating magnetic field (3-phase or 5-phase) drives azimuthal spin of central magnetic disc — horizontal rotation in the XY plane.
- Lorentz forces on radial current elements in the disc produce azimuthal (tangential) forces — horizontal.
- Eddy currents in conductive disc (induction motor effect) produce drag torque in the XY plane.
- Spin angular momentum vector L points in the Z direction (vertical) for a horizontally spinning disc.

Z-axis (vertical) dynamics:
- Gyroscopic precession couples horizontal rotation to vertical force via τ = dL/dt.
- For τ applied horizontally, L (vertical), precession Ω_prec = τ/|L| is horizontal — no net vertical force from standard precession alone.
- To get net vertical force: requires either (a) tilting L from vertical (angled precession coupling force to vertical component) or (b) using anti-precession to generate an upward reaction (as in the 13_2030107101071298568 video transcription).
- Drew's proposed Lorentz conversion: the "downward" mechanical reaction force (Z-direction, opposing the upward gyroscopic output) is expressed as an electromagnetic Lorentz force absorbed back into the field, leaving only the upward mechanical component.

Physics analysis:
- Normal gyroscopic precession: spinning wheel with L = Iω; under gravitational torque τ = r × F_grav (horizontal torque), precession occurs in horizontal plane. The reaction torque on the support structure is -τ (downward gravitational torque absorbed through support).
- Anti-precession: forcing the spinning wheel in the direction OPPOSITE to its natural precession. This requires applying a torque in the direction +τ_prec against the natural precessional motion.
- During anti-precession, the gyroscope pushes back against the applied force in a direction that is neither parallel nor antiparallel to the applied force — it pushes "sideways" relative to the forcing direction. This is the torque-free precession (Euler angle coupling) effect.
- The "apparent violation" of Newton's Third Law: the reaction to the anti-precessional input force is expressed as a different type of motion (rotation of the support frame in a perpendicular direction) rather than as a direct equal and opposite linear force. From the experimenter's mechanical perspective, the apparent reaction appears in an unexpected direction and magnitude.

This anti-precession effect is the physical phenomenon underlying Drew's "spin / counter-spin" model for UFO levitation — the coupling between upper (spinning magnet) and lower (magnetic flywheel) wheels where the induced motion of the lower wheel appears disproportionate to the applied forcing of the upper wheel.
