### Physical Energy Analysis
Two counter-rotating disc halves, each with moment of inertia I, rotating at angular velocity Ω:
$$KE_{total} = 2 \times \frac{1}{2}I\Omega^2 = I\Omega^2$$

This kinetic energy is stored in the spin. The claim is that this rotational kinetic energy can be extracted to power the craft systems. The mechanism of extraction is unspecified but could include:

**1. Relative rotation EMF (homopolar generator mode):**
If the two counter-rotating discs carry magnets on their outer edges, the relative angular velocity between them is 2Ω. Any conducting circuit spanning both discs would have an EMF induced by the relative motion:
$$\mathcal{E} = B \cdot v_{rel} \cdot L = B \cdot (2\Omega r) \cdot L$$

where r is the radius and L is the active length. For magnets on the rim at radius R, the induced EMF per unit length of the circuit:
$$\mathcal{E}/L \approx 2B\Omega R$$

**2. Beating magnetic flux:**
The magnets on the outer rim of the upper disc (rotating CW) and lower disc (rotating CCW) periodically align and anti-align. The time-varying flux through any pickup coil between the discs produces an AC EMF:
$$\mathcal{E}_{pickup} = -N\frac{d\Phi}{dt} = -N\frac{d(B_{upper}+B_{lower})}{dt}$$

The frequency of this beating is proportional to the sum of the two rotation rates.

**3. Reluctance machine principle:**
As pairs of magnets on opposite discs align and misalign, the magnetic circuit reluctance varies periodically. This variation drives a pulsating torque which, if mechanically coupled to a generator, produces electrical power.

The critical energy accounting question: if the rotational energy is being extracted to power the craft, the discs should gradually slow down unless the energy is replenished. The ET claim (implicit) is that the propulsion mechanism itself replenishes the rotational energy from the environmental energy source (ambient gravitational field? zero-point field? external RF?). This would constitute an over-unity or energy-harvesting configuration.

## Source: 2009400813479940460.yaml — Capacitive Power Control: Energy Storage and Reactive Power

Drew highlights the advantage of AC/3-phase over DC:
> "Control of current using capacitors rather than (hot) resistors"

The energy physics: a resistor dissipates energy as heat (P = I²R — irreversible). A capacitor stores energy in an electric field (E_cap = ½CV²) and returns it to the circuit each half-cycle — no dissipation in an ideal capacitor.

For an AC Lenz levitation system, reactive power management is critical:
$$S = P + jQ$$

where S is complex power (VA), P is real power (W), Q is reactive power (VAR). The inductance of the levitation coil stores Q as magnetic energy; a capacitor can be used to cancel this reactive power, reducing the required source current:

$$Q_C = -Q_L \Rightarrow \omega C = \frac{1}{\omega L}$$

At resonance (ω² = 1/LC), the reactive power circulates entirely within the LC circuit without burdening the power supply. This allows the levitation coil to operate at much higher field amplitude for a given supply current — equivalent to a force amplification from the resonance Q-factor.

If the resonant Q is large (Q = ω₀L/R >> 1), the reactive power circulating in the LC tank is Q times the real power consumed. The field amplitude and hence the Lenz levitation force scale with the reactive power, not the real power:

$$F_{Lenz} \propto B^2 \propto I^2 \propto (Q \cdot I_{supply})^2$$

At Q = 100 (achievable in a well-designed LC resonator), the force scales as 100² = 10,000 times the square of the supply current — a massive efficiency gain. This is the energy physics rationale for using AC/3-phase with capacitive tuning in a practical UAP propulsion system.
