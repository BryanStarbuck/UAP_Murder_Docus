### Classical Lenz Levitation
The "jumping ring" phenomenon: an aluminum conducting ring placed above a wire coil wrapped around iron, driven by AC current. The changing magnetic flux through the ring induces an EMF (Faraday's law):

$$\mathcal{E} = -\frac{d\Phi_B}{dt}$$

The induced current in the ring interacts with the external magnetic field to produce a repulsive force (Lenz's law) directed upward. For an AC-driven coil, the ring is levitated against gravity. The force is:

$$F_{Lenz} = \frac{(\text{induced current}) \times B_{external}}{\text{geometry factor}}$$

The upward Lenz force on the ring is matched by an equal and opposite downward force on the coil (Newton's 3rd law) — no net momentum is transferred to the Earth-craft system as a whole.

### Force Alteration by Spin
Drew's prior experimental result: when the aluminum disc conductor is given **rapid spin**, the downward and upward forces for AC Lenz levitation are "altered substantially." This is the key magnetic anomaly:

- The symmetry between F_up and F_down is broken by the spin

The candidate mechanism: eddy currents in a spinning disc have a Coriolis-like deflection — in the rotating frame of the disc, Coriolis forces act on charge carriers, modifying the current path and hence the force geometry. In the lab frame, the rotating disc carries charge in helical paths (superposition of the linear induction-driven current and the rotational motion), changing the force vector orientation relative to the external B-field.

### 3-Phase Spin Drive Proposal
