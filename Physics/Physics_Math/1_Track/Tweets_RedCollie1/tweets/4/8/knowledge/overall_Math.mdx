# Mathematical Equations and Relationships

## Source: 1912265953964064934.yaml — Gyroscopic Precession and Constrained Angular Momentum

### Gyroscopic Precession Equation
For a spinning body with angular momentum **L** = I**ω** subject to gravitational torque **τ** = **r** × m**g**:

$$\frac{d\mathbf{L}}{dt} = \boldsymbol{\tau} = \mathbf{r} \times m\mathbf{g}$$

The precession angular velocity:
$$\boldsymbol{\Omega}_{prec} = \frac{\tau}{L\sin\theta} = \frac{mgr}{I\omega\sin\theta}$$

where θ is the angle between **L** and the vertical. Precession is in the horizontal plane; vertical drift requires additional coupling terms (nutation damping, friction).

### Constrained System Energy Redistribution
In the Lagrangian formulation, let q₁ = Z-coordinate of spinning magnet, q₂ = Z-coordinate of pole assembly:

With constraint f(q₁) = 0 (magnet Z-displacement blocked), the Lagrange multiplier λ:

$$\frac{\partial L}{\partial q_1} - \frac{d}{dt}\frac{\partial L}{\partial \dot{q}_1} + \lambda \frac{\partial f}{\partial q_1} = 0$$

$$\frac{\partial L}{\partial q_2} - \frac{d}{dt}\frac{\partial L}{\partial \dot{q}_2} = 0$$

The constraint force -λ(∂f/∂q₁) is the reaction force that, if it has a component projecting onto the q₂ coordinate, drives the pole upward. The force amplification observed (F_pole >> F_free_spin) indicates the Lagrange multiplier λ is large — the constraint is energetically "stiff" relative to the free-spin degree of freedom.

## Source: 1926182246303625330.yaml — Rotating Magnetic Field: Balanced 3-Phase Field Vector

### 3-Phase Rotating Field Superposition
Three balanced phase currents:
$$I_1 = I_0 \cos(\omega t)$$
$$I_2 = I_0 \cos(\omega t - 2\pi/3)$$
$$I_3 = I_0 \cos(\omega t - 4\pi/3)$$

Coils at azimuthal positions φ₁ = 0, φ₂ = 2π/3, φ₃ = 4π/3, each producing a field along their coil axis:

$$B_x = kI_1\cos(0) + kI_2\cos(2\pi/3) + kI_3\cos(4\pi/3)$$
$$B_y = kI_1\sin(0) + kI_2\sin(2\pi/3) + kI_3\sin(4\pi/3)$$

Evaluating explicitly:
$$B_x = kI_0\left[\cos(\omega t) + \cos(\omega t - 2\pi/3)\cos(2\pi/3) + \cos(\omega t - 4\pi/3)\cos(4\pi/3)\right]$$

Using trigonometric sum identities, the result for the balanced 3-phase case:
$$B_x(t) = \frac{3}{2}kI_0\cos(\omega t)$$
$$B_y(t) = \frac{3}{2}kI_0\sin(\omega t)$$

The resultant field magnitude:
$$|\mathbf{B}| = \frac{3}{2}kI_0 = \text{constant}$$

The field **rotates** at angular frequency ω with constant amplitude (3/2)kI₀. This is the canonical rotating magnetic field (RMF). The constant amplitude is the advantage of 3-phase over single-phase (which produces a pulsating field).

### Homopolar EMF
For a conducting disc of radius R rotating at angular velocity Ω in axial field B_z:

$$\mathcal{E}_{homopolar} = \frac{1}{2}\Omega B_z R^2$$

Power generated (for current I flowing radially):
$$P = \mathcal{E} \cdot I = \frac{1}{2}\Omega B_z R^2 I$$

Torque on disc (reaction to driving):
$$\tau = \frac{P}{\Omega} = \frac{1}{2}B_z R^2 I$$

## Source: 1937008130501808472.yaml — Lenz Levitation Force Equations

### Induction and Force
Faraday induction in conducting ring:
$$\mathcal{E}_{ring} = -\frac{d\Phi}{dt} = -\frac{d(B \cdot A)}{dt}$$

For AC coil with current I_coil = I₀sin(ωt), axial field at ring location:
$$B_{coil}(t) = \frac{\mu_0 N I_0}{2r}\sin(\omega t) \cdot f_{geometry}$$

Induced ring current (for ring with self-inductance L_r and resistance R_r):
$$I_{ring}(t) = \frac{\omega M I_0}{\sqrt{R_r^2 + (\omega L_r)^2}}\cos(\omega t + \phi)$$

where M is mutual inductance between coil and ring, φ = arctan(ωL_r/R_r).

Upward force (time-averaged):
$$\langle F_z \rangle = \langle I_{ring} \rangle \oint \frac{\partial B_z}{\partial z} dA = \frac{\omega M^2 I_0^2}{2(R_r^2 + \omega^2 L_r^2)} \frac{\partial B_z}{\partial z}$$

### Rotation-Modified Current Distribution
In a spinning disc (angular velocity Ω) carrying eddy currents, the effective Ohm's law in the rotating frame:

$$\mathbf{J} = \sigma(\mathbf{E'}) = \sigma(\mathbf{E} + \mathbf{v}_{spin} \times \mathbf{B})$$

where **v**_spin = Ω × **r** is the rotational velocity at position **r**. The additional term σ(**v**_spin × **B**) = σ(Ω r B_z) in the azimuthal direction adds to the Lenz-driven radial current, creating a helical (spiral) current distribution.

The force on this modified current:
$$d\mathbf{F}_{spin} = \mathbf{J}_{spin} \times \mathbf{B} = \sigma(\mathbf{v}_{spin} \times \mathbf{B}) \times \mathbf{B}$$

Expanding using the BAC-CAB rule:
$$(\mathbf{v}_{spin} \times \mathbf{B}) \times \mathbf{B} = \mathbf{v}_{spin}(\mathbf{B}\cdot\mathbf{B}) - \mathbf{B}(\mathbf{v}_{spin}\cdot\mathbf{B})$$

For B = B_z ẑ and v_spin in the XY plane (v_spin · B = 0):
$$\mathbf{F}_{spin}/V = \sigma\Omega r B_z^2 \hat{v}_{spin} \cdot \hat{r} \cdot (\hat{r} \times \hat{z}) = \text{azimuthal force}$$

This azimuthal force does not directly produce Z-axis force in first order. The Z-axis force modification arises from second-order effects: the azimuthal current modifies the ring's magnetic moment, which modifies the interaction with the axial gradient of the external field.

## Source: 1926182246303625330.yaml — MHD Equations for Galinstan Vortex

### Magnetohydrodynamic Governing Equations

**Continuity (incompressible):**
$$\nabla \cdot \mathbf{u} = 0$$

**Navier-Stokes with Lorentz body force:**
$$\rho\left(\frac{\partial \mathbf{u}}{\partial t} + (\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}\right) = -\nabla p + \mu\nabla^2\mathbf{u} + \mathbf{J}\times\mathbf{B} + \rho\mathbf{g}$$

**Ohm's Law for moving conductor:**
$$\mathbf{J} = \sigma(\mathbf{E} + \mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{B})$$

**Faraday's Law:**
$$\nabla \times \mathbf{E} = -\frac{\partial\mathbf{B}}{\partial t}$$

**Ampere's Law (quasi-static, neglecting displacement current):**
$$\nabla \times \mathbf{B} = \mu_0 \mathbf{J}$$

**Induction Equation** (eliminating E and J):
$$\frac{\partial\mathbf{B}}{\partial t} = \nabla\times(\mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{B}) + \frac{1}{\mu_0\sigma}\nabla^2\mathbf{B}$$

### Magnetic Reynolds Number
$$R_m = \mu_0\sigma U L$$

For galinstan: σ = 3.46 × 10⁶ S/m, U ~ 0.1-1 m/s (estimated vortex speed), L ~ 0.05-0.1 m (container radius):
$$R_m = (4\pi\times10^{-7})(3.46\times10^6)(0.3)(0.07) \approx 0.09$$

Low R_m regime: the fluid-induced magnetic field is much smaller than the applied field. The induction equation simplifies to:
$$\frac{\partial\mathbf{B}}{\partial t} \approx \frac{1}{\mu_0\sigma}\nabla^2\mathbf{B}$$

The field diffusion time:
$$\tau_{diff} = \mu_0\sigma L^2 \approx (4\pi\times10^{-7})(3.46\times10^6)(0.07)^2 \approx 21 \text{ ms}$$

For ω/(2π) = 50 Hz, the field oscillation period is 20 ms — comparable to τ_diff. This means the applied field partially penetrates the galinstan but not fully, creating a skin-effect-modified force distribution.

## Source: 2030159714965942404.yaml — Electromagnetic Angular Momentum in Rotating Field

### EM Angular Momentum Density
$$\mathbf{l}_{EM} = \epsilon_0 (\mathbf{E}\times\mathbf{B}) = \frac{1}{c^2}(\mathbf{E}\times\mathbf{H})$$

For the rotating magnetic field (B rotates in XY plane) with any associated electric field component E_z (from Faraday's law applied to the time-varying B):

$$\frac{\partial B_x}{\partial t} = -\omega B_0\sin(\omega t) \Rightarrow (\nabla\times\mathbf{E})_z = \omega B_0\sin(\omega t)$$

The total EM angular momentum:
$$\mathbf{L}_{EM} = \epsilon_0\int\mathbf{r}\times(\mathbf{E}\times\mathbf{B})dV$$

For a rotating field with angular frequency ω, L_EM points along the Z-axis (the rotation axis). When this EM angular momentum interacts with the mechanical angular momentum of a spinning conductor **L**_mech, the combined system conserves total angular momentum:

$$\frac{d}{dt}(\mathbf{L}_{mech} + \mathbf{L}_{EM}) = \boldsymbol{\tau}_{external}$$

Changes in L_EM can drive changes in L_mech, including its Z-component direction and magnitude. This is the formal basis for the claim that a rotating magnetic field can produce Z-axis mechanical force on a spinning body.

## Source: 1998627362544734560.yaml — Counter-Rotation Beat Frequency Equations

For two fields rotating at frequencies ω₁ and ω₂ = -ω₁ (counter-rotation):

$$\mathbf{B}_1 = B_0[\cos(\omega_1 t)\hat{x} + \sin(\omega_1 t)\hat{y}]$$
$$\mathbf{B}_2 = B_0[\cos(-\omega_1 t)\hat{x} + \sin(-\omega_1 t)\hat{y}] = B_0[\cos(\omega_1 t)\hat{x} - \sin(\omega_1 t)\hat{y}]$$

Sum:
$$\mathbf{B}_{total} = 2B_0\cos(\omega_1 t)\hat{x}$$

This is a **linearly oscillating** (pulsating) field in the x-direction — a standing wave rather than a rotating wave. The sum of two equal and opposite rotating fields gives a linearly polarized oscillating field.

For **unequal amplitudes** (A₁ ≠ A₂):
$$\mathbf{B}_{total} = (A_1+A_2)\cos(\omega_1 t)\hat{x} + (A_1-A_2)\sin(\omega_1 t)\hat{y}$$

This is an **elliptically polarized** rotating field — the resultant traces an ellipse. The eccentricity (ratio of major to minor axis) = (A₁+A₂)/(A₁-A₂).

For a conducting body interacting with this elliptically rotating field, the force is not constant in azimuth — it has a periodic component at 2ω₁ and a mean component that depends on the ellipticity. This mean component could produce a steady Z-axis force if the field geometry is right.

## Source: 1987609666449494366.yaml — Orbital Mechanics: Hohmann-Type Transfer

### Orbital Speed
$$v_{circ} = \sqrt{\frac{GM}{r}}$$

Earth's surface: v_circ ≈ 7.9 km/s. At r = R_E + 400 km ≈ 6778 km: v_circ ≈ 7.67 km/s.

### Capture from Hyperbolic Trajectory
For an object arriving from interstellar space on a hyperbolic orbit with excess velocity v_∞:
$$v_{periapsis} = \sqrt{v_\infty^2 + v_{esc}^2}$$

where v_esc = √(2GM/r) at periapsis distance r.

For capture into circular orbit at r: required ΔV:
$$\Delta V = v_{periapsis} - v_{circ} = \sqrt{v_\infty^2 + \frac{2GM}{r}} - \sqrt{\frac{GM}{r}}$$

Energy of burn: ΔE = ½m[(v_periapsis)² - (v_circ)²]. "A few seconds of thrust" implies high specific impulse and thrust. For ΔV ~ 10 km/s and burn time τ ~ 10 s: required deceleration a = ΔV/τ = 1000 m/s² ≈ 100g. This is far beyond any known chemical or ion propulsion — only the proposed UAP propulsion (anti-gravity / EM coupling) could achieve this.

## Source: 2009400813479940460.yaml — Information Theory: Quaternary vs Binary

### Shannon Channel Capacity
Binary (M=2) channel:
$$C_2 = B\log_2(2) \cdot \frac{1}{1+\text{err}} = B \text{ (bits/s, in ideal case)}$$

Quaternary (M=4) channel:
$$C_4 = B\log_2(4) = 2B \text{ (bits/s)}$$

For same physical bandwidth B, quaternary encoding doubles the spectral efficiency. The required SNR for quaternary to achieve BER equivalent to binary:
$$\text{SNR}_{4-QAM} = \text{SNR}_{BPSK} + 3 \text{ dB}$$

The 3 dB penalty in SNR is offset by the 2× bandwidth efficiency. For interstellar communication systems with very limited bandwidth (narrow laser channels or tight radio beams), the 2× capacity gain far outweighs the SNR penalty.

Drew's "ECR" encryption claim: in a 4-symbol system, each symbol carries log₂(4) = 2 bits. The keyspace for an n-symbol message grows as 4ⁿ = 2^(2n) — equivalent to doubling the key length in binary. For n = 512 quaternary symbols, keyspace = 4^512 = 2^1024, which is already cryptographically secure by current standards. The ET system may use even longer keys.
