### Claim: Spin-Enhanced Axial Force on Magnetic Pole ("NEW PHYSICS" label)

**Statement**: "Up the pole with spin? NEW PHYSICS." Video confirms: "You can't bring it
down. Very strong up the force."

**What standard physics predicts**: For a permanent magnet (magnetic dipole moment m)
on a vertical pole in an external magnetic field B(z), the translational force in Z is:

  F_z = m · ∂B_z/∂z                                    ... (1)

This is independent of the spin rate ω of the magnet about Z. A spinning dipole in a
uniform or smoothly varying field precesses (Larmor precession at ω_L = γ·B) but does
not experience an enhanced translational force due to its spin. Therefore equation (1)
is the complete Newtonian/Maxwell prediction: no spin dependence.

**Claimed deviation**: The force when spinning is reported to be dramatically stronger
("you can't bring it down") compared to the static case, implying:
  F_z_actual >> F_z = m · ∂B_z/∂z                      ... (2)

**Chain of reasoning toward a non-Newtonian explanation**:
  Step 1: If the magnet is spinning, its magnetic moment vector m can be thought of as
    having a rapidly precessing transverse component (if not perfectly aligned with B),
    but the time-averaged m in steady precession is still along B — so the time-averaged
    force should still be ∇(m·B).
  Step 2: However, if the spinning produces oscillating eddy currents or induced fields
    in the pole material (if the pole is conducting), there could be an additional
    Lenz-law force. But this would generally oppose motion, not enhance upward force.
  Step 3: If the spin creates a dynamic magneto-mechanical resonance between the magnet
    and the pole field geometry, the effective m could be enhanced beyond the static value.
    This would require a mechanism not in classical electrodynamics.
  Step 4: Drew labels the observation "new physics" because none of the standard
    mechanisms in steps 1–3 predict the magnitude of the observed force enhancement.

**Status**: Experimental claim by Drew. Not yet reproduced or published in peer-reviewed
literature. If confirmed, represents evidence for spin-translational coupling in magnetic
systems beyond Maxwell electrodynamics.

### Claim: Counter-Rotating Magnetic Disks Produce Anomalous Sustained Repulsion

**Statement**: "NOT IN ANY TEXTBOOK OR JOURNAL" — two counter-rotating disks with

**What standard physics predicts**: For two counter-rotating disks with N_m magnets each
at angular velocity ±ω (counter-rotation), if the disks are mechanically independent
(no coupling), the relative phase θ_rel(t) = 2ωt sweeps uniformly through all values.
The time-averaged axial force:

  <F_z> = -(1/T) ∫₀^T (dU/dd) dt                      ... (3)

where U(θ_rel, d) = U_0(d) + U_{N_m}(d)·cos(N_m·θ_rel) + ...

The oscillatory terms average to zero → <F_z> = -dU_0/dd (the angle-averaged
interaction force). This can be non-zero (repulsive or attractive depending on the
overall flux geometry) but is not the resonance-locked repulsion Drew observes.

**Claimed deviation**: The disks dwell preferentially in repulsion alignment and move
apart in reproducible "jumpy steps." The time-averaged force is not simply -dU_0/dd
but exceeds it toward the repulsion direction.

**Chain of reasoning toward beyond-Newtonian explanation**:
  Step 1: The mechanical coupling via the shared rod imposes a constraint on the
    relative phase — the disks cannot pass freely through each other's strong attraction
    zones without transferring momentum to the rod.
  Step 2: When the disks encounter an attraction zone while counter-rotating, they snap
    together (observed: "they snap together really close"). This snap event is a
    dissipative collision that absorbs the kinetic energy of mutual approach.
  Step 3: But when they are at a repulsion zone while counter-rotating, they are pushed
    apart, and their kinetic energy in the axial direction is increased.
  Step 4: The asymmetry between the snap-together (dissipative, energy absorbed by rod/
    frame) and push-apart (elastic, kinetic energy gained by disks) could produce a
    net ratcheted repulsion — a rectification of the oscillating magnetic force.

This rectification would be a consequence of the mechanical nonlinearity (dissipation
at attraction, elasticity at repulsion) rather than a violation of energy conservation.
Whether this constitutes "beyond Newtonian" or merely "poorly understood mechanical
resonance" is the open question. Drew's framing as "bizarre, unexplained results" and
"not in any textbook or journal" asserts it is genuinely novel.

**Central claim** (most explicit statement of beyond-Newtonian physics in this dataset):
  "Spinning one wheel (say a large base-wheel) will cause another spinning wheel (say a

**What Newton's laws predict**: For any macroscopic system, the center of mass
theorem requires:
  d²(Σ m_i · r_i)/dt² = Σ F_external                  ... (4)

If the only external force in Z is gravity (F_ext,z = -M_total · g) and the support
structure exerts a normal force N in Z, then for a steady state:
  N = M_total · g  (system doesn't accelerate upward as a whole)  ... (5)

For the upper wheel to rise (z_upper increases), either:
  (a) N increases (more force from the support structure, transmitted to the ground), or
  (b) The system center of mass remains stationary while z_upper rises → some other
      component must descend.

Drew claims neither (a) nor (b) applies — "no downward Newtonian action-reaction."
If literally true, the system's center of mass accelerates upward without an external
upward force, violating equation (4) applied to Z.

**Standard-physics resolution**: The downward reaction almost certainly appears in the
bearing forces transmitted through the base-wheel shaft to the support structure and
ground, even if the visible external motion appears entirely horizontal (torque applied

motor-driven systems: the "reaction" to the rotating output shaft torque is a reaction
torque on the motor casing, transmitted to the frame, transmitted to the floor.

**Drew's hypothesis**: The claimed novelty is that in this specific coupled-gyroscope
configuration, the vertical impulse to the environment is zero or negligibly small —
i.e., the force-balance is genuinely different from a motor-driven lift. If confirmed
by force-plate measurement (which Drew proposes to do), this would constitute evidence
for a momentum-non-conserving process in the Z direction for this specific mechanical
system — genuinely beyond Newtonian mechanics.

**The critical experiment**: Drew proposes to measure whether applying a base-wheel
torque causes the upper spinning wheel to rise, and simultaneously whether there is a
detectable downward force on the support structure. This is the experimental test that
would definitively determine whether the claimed beyond-Newtonian coupling is real.

**Chain of logical steps toward UFO application**:
  1. Crop formations (multiple, since at least 1999) depict spinning-wheel-causes-lift
     geometry as alleged ET-provided engineering diagrams.

  4. The proposed critical experiment tests the inverse: base-wheel torque → upper-wheel
