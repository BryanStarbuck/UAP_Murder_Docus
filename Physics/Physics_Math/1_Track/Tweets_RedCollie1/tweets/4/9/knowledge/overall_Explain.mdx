### Ring-Magnet Focal-Point Levitation: From Device Geometry to Macroscopic Force Output

The core claim is that a ring magnet with a sufficiently large central bore, paired with
a floater magnet whose diameter is equal to or smaller than that bore diameter, produces
a stable focal-point equilibrium in the vertical (Z) axis. This is not mere magnetic
repulsion; the floater simultaneously experiences outward repulsion from the bore axis and
inward attraction toward the outer annular mass of the ring magnet. The superposition of
these two opposing force gradients along the radial (r) coordinate produces a potential
minimum — a confinement well — that localizes the floater at a stable hover position.

**Bridging statement**: The output here is stable passive levitation — Z-axis support
against gravity — generated purely by a static magnetic configuration in the XY plane
(the ring geometry). The ring geometry in the XY plane determines whether the radial
force profile has a stable zero crossing that supports load in Z. There is no external
power input to the levitation force once the geometry is set.

**Geometric constraint for focal-point formation (experimental result, not textbook):**
- Ring magnet outer diameter: d_ring (e.g., 140 mm or 145 mm)
- Ring bore diameter: d_bore (e.g., 80 mm or 60 mm)
- Floater diameter: d_floater

Condition for focal-point formation:
  d_floater <= d_bore

When d_floater > d_bore: no focal-point forms; only pure repulsion (or pure attraction
depending on pole orientation) occurs along the bore axis. The transition is sharp — a
floater of 80 mm on a ring with a 60 mm bore shows only repulsion with no inward
attraction, whereas a 60 mm floater on the same ring (d_floater = d_bore) still shows
focal-point behavior.

**Materials tested (experimental):**
- Ferrite ring magnet: 145 mm OD, 80 mm bore. Floaters from 40 mm to 100 mm tested.
  Focal point confirmed for d_floater in range [40, 80] mm; lost for d_floater = 100 mm.
- Neodymium ring magnet: 100 mm OD, 60 mm bore. Floaters from 40 mm to 80 mm tested.
  Focal point confirmed for d_floater in range [40, 60] mm; lost for d_floater >= 70 mm.

**Claimed advancement beyond standard magnetics texts**: Dr. Drew states explicitly that
this focal-point behavior "is not in any textbook." The observation that a floater magnet
can be simultaneously repelled from the bore center and attracted toward the outer
annular region — producing a stable equilibrium — represents a functional regime of
magnetic field topology not covered in standard electrodynamics curricula at the level
of Jackson or Griffiths, which address multipole fields of idealized geometries but do
not map the mesoscale focal-topology of real ring-plus-floater configurations with the
bore constraint.

**Connection to UAP propulsion hypothesis**: Drew frames this focal-point levitation as
a candidate primitive mechanism underlying UAP hover capability. The implication is that
a craft using a large ring-magnet analog in its base plane could sustain a secondary
spinning floater-magnet configuration at a stable focal point, decoupling vertical
support from thrust vectoring.

### Gyroscopic Spin Coupling to Base-Wheel Rotation: Proposed Mechanism for Reaction-Free Lift

Drew describes a laboratory experiment probing the physics of "UFO levitation." The
observation is:

- Lowering a spinning (upper) wheel causes a large base-wheel to rotate anti-clockwise.
- Raising the same spinning wheel causes the large base-wheel to rotate clockwise.

The proposed inverse experiment: apply an external torque to the base-wheel (making it
rotate anti-clockwise or clockwise), and observe whether the upper spinning wheel
consequently rises or descends without any direct vertical force being applied to it.

**Bridging statement**: If the inverse coupling holds — i.e., if base-wheel clockwise
torque produces upward displacement of the upper spinning wheel — then vertical lift
(Z-axis displacement) is generated from horizontal spin-torque input (XY-plane angular
momentum exchange). The claimed novelty is that this mechanism involves no
Newtonian action-reaction pair in the vertical direction:

  "Such a novel lifting motion will not cause any downward Newtonian action-reaction,
  because all of the force is applied as a horizontal spin-torque to the large base-wheel."

This would constitute a violation of the naive expectation from Newton's third law applied
to the vertical axis, if the input torque is entirely horizontal and the output is vertical
displacement. The mechanism is framed as the hidden operating principle illustrated in
multiple crop-circle diagrams showing spinning-wheel-causes-lift configurations.

**Relation to gyroscopic dynamics**: In classical mechanics, a spinning gyroscope under
applied torque precesses perpendicular to both the spin axis and the torque axis (the
gyroscopic equation dL/dt = τ). Drew's experiment may be probing a configuration where
the precession response to an applied horizontal base torque produces vertical displacement
of a coupled upper rotor — a non-obvious consequence of angular momentum conservation in
a mechanically coupled multi-body system. Whether the claimed "no downward reaction"
is genuinely non-Newtonian or is Newtonian but non-obvious (net vertical impulse
transferred to bearings/ground) is the critical open question motivating the proposed
experiment.
