### Passive Magnetic Levitation via Ring-Magnet Focal Point

**Mechanism**: A floater magnet suspended above a ring magnet at a stable focal point
is passively levitated against gravity in the Z direction. The Z-axis force balance:

  F_z_magnetic(r*, z*) + F_z_gravity = 0
  m_f · ∂B_z/∂z|_{r*,z*} = -M_f · g                  ... (1)

where m_f is the magnetic moment of the floater, M_f is its mass, g is gravitational
acceleration downward.

This is the condition that the magnetic gradient force upward equals the gravitational
pull downward at the focal point height z*. Since the ring magnet is a static permanent
magnet, this levitation requires no power input — it is purely static energy stored in
the magnetic field configuration.

**Why the geometry matters for lift**: The ring geometry (as opposed to a simple bar
magnet or horseshoe magnet) is essential because:
  1. The bore creates a region where the axial field B_z has a local maximum along the
     axis (Helmholtz configuration with the two pole faces), or more precisely, a
     specific gradient profile that supports both radial confinement (XY) and axial
     levitation (Z) simultaneously.
  2. The outer annular ring provides an attractive restoring force in r (toward r*),
     while the bore repulsion provides the upward B_z gradient force.

**Spin stabilization of the Z position**: When the floater spins about Z:
  L_z = I_floater · ω_spin                             ... (2)

The gyroscopic stiffness resists tipping perturbations. If the floater tilts by angle δθ
from vertical, it experiences both the magnetic restoring torque τ_mag and the gyroscopic
resistance to tilting. The effective stability condition for the combined magnetic +
gyroscopic system is:
  τ_mag / δθ + I_floater · ω_spin² / δθ² > 0          ... (3)

(schematic — not the exact form, but indicating that spin adds positive stiffness to the
stabilization against tipping that would otherwise cause the floater to flip and snap
into the ring magnet face.)

**Practical observation**: The floater spins freely at the focal point. This is "spinning
magnetic levitation." The spinning and the levitation reinforce each other: the spin
maintains orientation stability while the focal-point magnetic geometry maintains
positional stability.
