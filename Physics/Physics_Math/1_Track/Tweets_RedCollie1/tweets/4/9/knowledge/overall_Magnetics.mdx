### Ring-Magnet / Floater-Magnet Focal-Point Topology

**Experimental system**: A ring magnet (all-North-pole orientation in the axial
direction) and a floater magnet (also North-pole axially), with the floater placed above
the bore opening of the ring.

**XY-plane field topology**: The ring magnet produces a radially symmetric field in the
XY plane. At the bore center (r = 0), the field from the inner bore wall is repulsive
for a same-pole floater. Moving radially outward toward the outer annular mass of the
ring magnet (r increasing from r_bore/2 toward r_ring_outer), the field becomes
attractive because the outer annular surface dominates.

**Consequence**: There exists a radius r* such that the net radial force on the floater
changes sign:
  F_r(r < r*) < 0  (repulsive, pointing outward from bore center)
  F_r(r > r*) > 0  (attractive, pointing inward toward bore axis)

This zero crossing r* defines the focal circle in the XY plane. In the axial (Z)
direction, the geometry similarly produces a stable height h* above the ring face where
the floater equilibrates — combining the XY focal confinement with Z-axis magnetic
levitation (supplemented by or balanced against gravity).

**Geometric rule (experimentally established, not from textbooks)**:
  Stable focal point exists IFF:    d_floater <= d_bore

  d_bore = inner hole diameter of ring magnet
  d_floater = diameter of floater magnet

Tested ring-floater pairs:
  Ferrite ring 145 mm OD / 80 mm bore:
    d_floater = 40 mm: focal point confirmed
    d_floater = 50 mm: focal point confirmed
    d_floater = 60 mm: focal point confirmed
    d_floater = 70 mm: focal point confirmed (strong repulsion + strong inward pull)
    d_floater = 80 mm (= d_bore): focal point confirmed (strong both ways)
    d_floater = 100 mm (> d_bore): focal point absent; pure repulsion, no inward pull

  Neodymium ring 100 mm OD / 60 mm bore:
    d_floater = 40 mm: focal point confirmed
    d_floater = 50 mm: focal point confirmed
    d_floater = 60 mm (= d_bore): focal point confirmed
    d_floater = 70 mm (> d_bore): repulsion only, no focal point
    d_floater = 80 mm (> d_bore): orientation-dependent: one orientation repulsion only,
      other orientation attraction only; no focal point in either case

**Spinning magnetic levitation**: When the floater magnet is set spinning (rotational
axis vertical, i.e., spin axis = Z), the spinning introduces gyroscopic stabilization on
top of the magnetic focal confinement. Drew terms this "free-floating spinning magnetic
levitation." The tweet states:
  "All ring magnets will create a stable focal point nearby, if the floater magnet is no
  larger than an empty centre of the ring magnet. This then becomes a free-floating
  point to allow spinning magnetic levitation."

The spin adds angular momentum L = I·ω (where I is the floater moment of inertia about
Z, ω is its angular velocity) which resists tilting perturbations via the gyroscopic
torque τ_gyro = ω × L, supplementing the purely static magnetic confinement.

**Status**: Experimental result. Not covered in standard magnetostatics textbooks
(Drew's explicit claim). No analytical derivation provided in the tweets; pure empirical
characterization of the focal-point condition.

Tweet text: "Up the pole with spin? NEW PHYSICS."

This describes a spinning magnet or magnetic assembly on an axial pole that exhibits
an anomalously strong upward force ("up the pole") when spinning, compared to static
conditions. The word "fierce" and the operator's inability to push it down suggest that
the Z-axis force magnitude is substantially larger when the magnet is spinning than when
static. This is consistent with a gyromagnetic effect or a spin-Magnus-like coupling
between rotational velocity and the external magnetic field, but Drew frames it as "NEW
PHYSICS" — i.e., the magnitude or character of the effect exceeds what classical
electrodynamics would predict for a spinning magnetic dipole in a static external field.

In standard electrodynamics, a rotating magnetic dipole does not experience a net
translational force in a uniform field (only a torque); it can experience a force only
in a field gradient (F = ∇(m·B)). The claim of an anomalously "fierce" upward force
that "you can't bring down" implies either: (a) the gradient coupling is much stronger
than expected, or (b) there is a dynamical amplification of the force when the magnet
spins that has no classical analog.

**Connection to XY/Z dynamics**: The spin axis here is vertical (Z), and the enhanced
force is also vertical (Z). The mechanism by which XY-plane rotation produces enhanced
Z-axis force is the key unknown. This is precisely the coupling Drew is attempting to
characterize experimentally across multiple tweets.

### Counter-Rotating Magnetic Flywheels: Anomalous Repulsion Resonance

**Device**: Two DC motors (~12–20 V), each driving an aluminium disk carrying 6 permanent
magnets arranged in alternating North-South (NS-SN-NS) pattern around the periphery.
Both disks slide along a common rod.

**Static behavior**:
  - At certain rotational alignments: very strong mutual attraction (F_attract >> 0)
  - Rotated slightly: very strong mutual repulsion (F_repel >> 0)
  The force is a periodic function of relative rotational angle θ, oscillating between
  strong attraction and strong repulsion with periodicity 360°/6 = 60° (6 magnets per disk).

**Dynamic behavior (counter-rotation experiment)**:
  When both disks spin in opposite directions (disk 1 clockwise, disk 2 anti-clockwise),
  the two disks spontaneously adopt a mutual repulsion configuration and steadily move
  apart from one another in "jumpy little steps." The relative phase angle θ_rel(t) = 
  (ω_1 + ω_2)·t (since they spin oppositely, the relative angular velocity is ω_1 + ω_2)
  sweeps through all values, but the disks statistically dwell in and reinforce the
  repulsive alignment rather than averaging to zero net force.

  This is described as a "resonance effect": the mechanical coupling between the disks
  (via the shared rod) preferentially phase-locks the relative angle at or near the
  repulsion maxima during counter-rotation, leading to a net time-averaged repulsion
  rather than zero net force.

**Contrasted with AC-coil experiment**:
  Replacing one disk with a fast-spinning permanent-magnet disk and the other with an
  AC magnetic coil: the fast-changing AC field causes attraction (not repulsion). This
  is attributed to normal electromagnetic induction — the rapidly alternating external
  field induces eddy currents whose interaction with the permanent magnet produces net
  attraction.

  A stationary copper coil (no power, just spinning disk nearby) produces repulsion of
  the disk — correctly attributed to Lenz's law eddy currents induced in the copper by
  the alternating field of the spinning disk's magnets. This is standard induction.

**Claimed anomaly**: The resonance-type sustained repulsion between two counter-rotating
permanent-magnet disks is described as "NOT IN ANY TEXTBOOK OR JOURNAL" and as "bizarre,
unexplained results." The mechanism by which counter-rotation preferentially selects the
repulsion phase alignment (rather than producing a time-averaged zero force) is not
explained by simple superposition of static magnetic forces and requires a dynamical
analysis of the coupled rotors with shared-rod compliance.

**Inspiration**: UFO contactee Norman Paulsen reported seeing two magnetic flywheels
spinning in opposite directions in a craft. Drew's experiment is a direct laboratory
attempt to investigate the physical consequences of this geometry.

**Homopolar generator physics**: A conducting disk rotating at angular velocity ω in an
axial magnetic field B (parallel to the rotation axis, i.e., both along Z) generates an
electromotive force (EMF) given by:

  EMF = (1/2) · ω · B · r²

where r is the disk radius. The current flows radially outward (or inward) from the
rotation axis to the rim, and is extracted via brushes at the axis and at the rim. This
is a DC generator with no commutator, no alternating phases — pure DC from a symmetric
rotating geometry.

  - Homopolar component: generates steady radial current with axial B field (axial force)

**Experimental material**: Drew ordered $1000 of galinstan (a liquid metal alloy:

### Tesla Homopolar Motor: DC Current Flow Geometry in UAP Physics Context

Tweet: "Those are curved lines of DC current flow like in Tesla's homopolar motor."

The referenced tweet (1998543870406365686) describes a Czech Republic 2019 crop picture
allegedly drawn in response to a woman's question to an alien contact about how UFOs fly.

**Homopolar motor principle**: In Tesla's homopolar motor, DC current flows radially
through a conducting disk rotating in an axial magnetic field. The Lorentz force on
radial current carriers:
  F = I · (dl × B)
where dl is the radial current element and B is the axial field, produces a tangential
(azimuthal) force — i.e., a torque — that drives rotation. For a radial current element
at radius r with current I_r and axial field B_z:
  dF_azimuthal = I_r · B_z · dr

The total torque τ = ∫₀^R I_r · B_z · r dr.

In the UAP context, Drew is claiming that the crop formation depicts the exact current-flow
geometry of a homopolar motor/generator as the underlying electromagnetic circuit of the
craft's propulsion system.

**Curved (rather than straight radial) current trajectories**: Standard homopolar motors
have straight radial current flow in the disk. "Curved lines of DC current flow" suggest
either: (a) the current path is not purely radial but helical or spiral due to the
geometry of the current-injection electrodes, or (b) the relevant geometry is a rotating
spherical or hemispherical conductor rather than a flat disk, causing the geodesic
current paths to be great-circle arcs rather than straight lines.

### 3-Phase Coil UFO Model: Rotating Magnetic Field Generation

Referenced tweet describes: "a new, 10-minute video of a simplified model by which to
study UFO physics. The new model uses just 6 wire coils and one 3-phase power supply."

A 3-phase coil arrangement with 6 coils (2 per phase, 120° apart) driven by a balanced
3-phase supply generates a rotating magnetic field B(t) in the XY plane:

  B_x(t) = B_0 · cos(ωt)
  B_y(t) = B_0 · cos(ωt - 2π/3) · sin(2π/3) + B_0 · cos(ωt + 2π/3) · sin(2π/3)

For a properly wound and balanced 3-phase winding with angular symmetry, the resultant
field magnitude is constant: |B| = (3/2)·B_0, rotating at frequency ω in the XY plane.
This is identical to the operating principle of a 3-phase AC induction motor stator.
