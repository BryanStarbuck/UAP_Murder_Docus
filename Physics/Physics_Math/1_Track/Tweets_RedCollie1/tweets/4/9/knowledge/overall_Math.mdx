### Focal-Point Stability Condition: Geometric Inequality

The experimentally determined condition for a stable magnetic focal point in the
ring-magnet / floater-magnet system:

  CONDITION:  d_floater ≤ d_bore                        ... (1)

where:
  d_floater = diameter of floater (secondary) magnet [mm]
  d_bore    = inner bore diameter of ring magnet [mm]

Physical meaning of (1): the floater must fit inside the ring bore for the radial
force field to have a stable zero crossing that confines the floater. When (1) is
violated (d_floater > d_bore), the force is monotonic (all-repulsive or all-attractive)
and no confinement exists.

### Potential Energy Landscape for Focal-Point System

For a magnetic dipole m_f (floater) in the field B_ring(r, z) produced by a ring magnet
with bore radius R_bore = d_bore/2, the interaction energy is:

  U(r, z) = -m_f · B_ring(r, z)                        ... (2)

In cylindrical coordinates (r, φ, z) with the ring magnet centered at z = 0:

  B_ring(r, z) = B_r(r, z) r̂ + B_z(r, z) ẑ          ... (3)

(azimuthal symmetry → B_φ = 0 for a ring magnet with symmetric polar alignment)

The radial force on the floater:
  F_r = -∂U/∂r = m_f · ∂B_z/∂r  (for axially-aligned dipole)   ... (4)

The axial force:
  F_z = -∂U/∂z = m_f · ∂B_z/∂z                        ... (5)

The focal point (r*, z*) is defined by:
  F_r(r*, z*) = 0  →  ∂B_z/∂r|_{r*,z*} = 0           ... (6a)
  F_z(r*, z*) = 0  →  ∂B_z/∂z|_{r*,z*} = 0           ... (6b)

Stability requires:
  ∂F_r/∂r|_{r*,z*} < 0  →  ∂²B_z/∂r²|_{r*,z*} < 0   ... (7a)
  ∂F_z/∂z|_{r*,z*} < 0  →  ∂²B_z/∂z²|_{r*,z*} < 0   ... (7b)

Note: equations (7a) and (7b) cannot both hold simultaneously for a magnetostatic field
in free space (since ∇²B_z = 0 in free space → ∂²B_z/∂r² + (1/r)∂B_z/∂r + ∂²B_z/∂z²
= 0 for the z-component in cylindrical coordinates away from sources). This is the
mathematical statement of Earnshaw's theorem. The system evades this by relying on:
  (a) the boundary region near the bore wall (not free space — the ring magnet material
      provides the equivalent of a source term that locally satisfies the inequality
      in a restricted volume), and
  (b) gyroscopic stabilization from the spinning floater supplementing the static field.

### Gyroscopic Stabilization of Floater Angular Orientation

A spinning floater magnet with moment of inertia I_spin and angular velocity ω_spin
has angular momentum:
  L_spin = I_spin · ω_spin ẑ                           ... (8)

Under a perturbing torque τ_perturb (e.g., from magnetic field misalignment), the
gyroscopic response is precession rather than tipping:
  dL/dt = τ_perturb                                     ... (9)
  Ω_precess = |τ_perturb| / |L_spin|                   ... (10)

For sufficiently large ω_spin (fast spin), Ω_precess → small, meaning the floater
resists tipping. This is the gyroscopic stabilization that allows the focal-point
equilibrium to be practically achievable despite Earnshaw constraints.

### Homopolar Generator EMF

The EMF generated by a conducting disk of radius R rotating at angular velocity ω in
an axial field B_z:

  EMF_homopolar = (1/2) · ω · B_z · R²                 ... (11)

Derivation: a radial element dr at radius r moves with tangential velocity v_t = ω·r.
The motional EMF contribution from this element:
  dEMF = (v_t × B_z) · dr = ω · r · B_z · dr           ... (11a)

Integrating from 0 to R:
  EMF = ∫₀^R ω · r · B_z dr = (1/2) · ω · B_z · R²    ... (11b)

The radial current density in the disk (assuming uniform conductivity σ_disk):
  J_r(r) = σ_disk · (EMF - IR_internal) / (2πr·t)      ... (12)

where t is the disk thickness and R_internal is the internal resistance.

The brush contacts extract this radial current at r = 0 (axis brush) and r = R (rim
brush). The "eye-shaped" lines in the crop formation are identified as these brush
contact symbols.

### Three-Phase Rotating Magnetic Field (XY-Plane)

For a 3-phase winding with N_phases = 3, angular symmetry φ_k = 2πk/3 (k = 0, 1, 2),
driven with balanced currents I_k(t) = I_0 · cos(ωt - 2πk/3):

The field components at the center:
  B_x(t) = Σ_k B_0 · cos(ωt - 2πk/3) · cos(φ_k)
          = (3/2) · B_0 · cos(ωt)                      ... (13a)

  B_y(t) = Σ_k B_0 · cos(ωt - 2πk/3) · sin(φ_k)
          = (3/2) · B_0 · sin(ωt)                      ... (13b)

Resultant:
  |B_xy(t)| = (3/2) · B_0  = constant                  ... (13c)
  Phase angle: arctan(B_y/B_x) = ωt                    ... (13d)

The field rotates in the XY plane at angular frequency ω with constant magnitude
(3/2)·B_0. For N-phase system with N ≥ 3:
  |B_xy| = (N/2) · B_0  = constant                     ... (14)

### Combined Homopolar + Multi-Phase Field

If both a 3-phase rotating field (XY) and a homopolar axial field (Z) are
simultaneously present:

  B_total(r, t) = [(3/2)B_0·cos(ωt)] x̂ + [(3/2)B_0·sin(ωt)] ŷ + B_z·ẑ  ... (15)

This is a helical field: the XY components rotate at frequency ω while the Z component
is constant. The magnitude:
  |B_total| = sqrt[(3/2)²·B_0² + B_z²] = constant     ... (16)

The Poynting vector for this configuration (neglecting the near-field reactive component)
has both a rotational component (associated with the XY rotation) and an axial component
(associated with B_z if there is an associated E_φ from Faraday induction of the
time-varying B_xy).

### Gyroscope Coupling Equations: Proposed Z-Lift from XY Torque

**Standard precession equations** (Euler's equations for a rigid body):

  I_1 · dΩ_1/dt - (I_2 - I_3)·Ω_2·Ω_3 = τ_1          ... (17a)
  I_2 · dΩ_2/dt - (I_3 - I_1)·Ω_3·Ω_1 = τ_2          ... (17b)
  I_3 · dΩ_3/dt - (I_1 - I_2)·Ω_1·Ω_2 = τ_3          ... (17c)

For an axisymmetric gyroscope (I_1 = I_2 ≠ I_3) with spin along ẑ (Ω_3 = ω_spin >> Ω_1,Ω_2):

Precession rate about ẑ:
  Ω_prec = τ_⊥ / (I_3 · ω_spin)                       ... (18)

where τ_⊥ is the component of torque perpendicular to the spin axis.

**Drew's proposed inverse coupling** (not yet experimentally confirmed):

Let τ_base = external horizontal torque applied to base wheel (in XY plane, about Z):
  τ_base = τ_z ẑ

Claimed coupling to vertical displacement z_upper:
  F_z_upper ~ C · τ_z                                  ... (19)

where C is a coupling coefficient (units: [m/(N·m·s)] or equivalent) that must arise from
the mechanical coupling between the base wheel and the upper spinning wheel via a
common support structure.

The Lagrangian of the coupled two-wheel system (schematic, not fully derived by Drew):

  L = (1/2)I_base·θ̇_base² + (1/2)I_upper·θ̇_upper² + (1/2)M_upper·ż_upper²
      + U_magnetic(θ_base, θ_upper, z_upper)
      - M_upper·g·z_upper                              ... (20)

The coupling term ∂U/∂z_upper ∂U/∂θ_base ≠ 0 in general if the magnetic or mechanical
linkage between the two wheels depends on both θ_base and z_upper. Drew's claim is that
this cross-derivative is non-zero and large enough to produce macroscopic vertical motion
from horizontal torque input.

**Newtonian consistency question**: If F_z_upper > 0 (upward) and no downward force is
transmitted to the environment, this violates conservation of momentum in Z. The
resolution requires checking whether the reaction appears in the bearing forces on the
base wheel support structure (transmitted to ground). Drew's claim ("no downward
Newtonian action-reaction") would need to be verified by force-plate measurement.

### Counter-Rotating Disk Magnetic Interaction Energy

Two disks, each with N_m magnets, separated by distance d, with relative angle θ_rel:

  U(θ_rel, d) = Σ_{i,j} U_dipole-dipole(m_i, m_j, r_ij)   ... (21)

For parallel-axis dipoles (both disk normals along ẑ), the dipole-dipole interaction:
  U_ij = (μ_0/4π) · [m_i·m_j/r_ij³ - 3(m_i·r̂_ij)(m_j·r̂_ij)/r_ij³]   ... (22)

For the periodic arrangement (N_m = 6 magnets per disk), U is periodic in θ_rel with
fundamental period 2π/N_m = 60°. In Fourier series:
  U(θ_rel, d) = U_0(d) + U_6(d)·cos(6θ_rel) + U_12(d)·cos(12θ_rel) + ...  ... (23)

For counter-rotation: θ_rel(t) = 2ω·t + θ_0

Time-averaged U (free disks, no coupling):
  <U> = U_0(d)  (all oscillatory terms average to zero)   ... (24)

Hence <F_axial> = -d/dd<U> = -dU_0/dd, which is a non-zero DC repulsion or attraction
depending on the sign of dU_0/dd. The U_0(d) term is the angle-averaged interaction
energy and is equivalent to the force between two uniformly magnetized rings at
separation d — which is indeed non-zero.

However, Drew's observation of the "resonance" phase-locking suggests that

maxima, which are potential energy maxima in θ — i.e., the equilibrium is unstable in θ
for free disks but stabilized by the resonance). The resonance condition would be:
  6ω_resonance ≈ ω_mechanical_coupling  (the frequency at which mechanical coupling
                                         enforces θ_rel lock)    ... (25)

### Spinning Coma: Coriolis Force on Ejecta

For a rotating nucleus of angular velocity ω (about ẑ), an ejecta particle with
velocity v_ejecta in the rotating frame experiences:
  a_Coriolis = -2 · ω × v_ejecta                       ... (26)

The apparent curved trajectory of ejecta in the rest frame traces a spiral pattern with
angular offset from the radial direction:
  δφ ≈ arctan(2ω · r_ejecta / v_ejecta)               ... (27)

where r_ejecta is the distance from the nucleus at time of observation. For a rapidly
spinning body, this spiral deviation can be large and would appear as "spin-waves" in
the coma when observed as a time-overlap of frames — which is exactly what Drew describes.

The "offset plane of spin" from the expected cometary tail direction would require the
rotation axis to be misaligned with the Sun-comet radial vector. For a natural comet,
the rotation axis is generally fixed by its formation angular momentum and can be at any
angle. But if the offset is anomalously large or time-varying, it could indicate active
torquing of the rotation axis — consistent with an engineered object.
