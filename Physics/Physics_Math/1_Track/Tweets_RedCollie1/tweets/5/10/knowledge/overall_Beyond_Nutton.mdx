### Source: Negative Scale Reading During Downward Phase of Oscillating Spinning Magnet

**The beyond-Newtonian observation:**

Newtonian prediction during free-fall (magnet falling under gravity):
- The magnet is in free fall → it exerts zero force on the scale below (the scale reads zero during free fall of the magnet — normal behavior).
- Maximum anomaly expected classically: W_scale = 0 (free fall) or W_scale = W_static (resting).
- W_scale CANNOT go NEGATIVE in classical mechanics if the only interaction is gravitational: the falling magnet cannot push upward on the scale below it via gravitational interaction alone.

**Observed:** W_scale = -(15 to 20) grams-force during the downward phase.

**What negative scale reading means physically:**
The device is actively EXERTING AN UPWARD FORCE on the scale from above. This is impossible under Newtonian gravity alone: gravity pulls downward; a falling object in free fall exerts no force on an object below it via gravity; a falling object connected to a rope/spring could pull up on a pulley system but that is not the case here.

**Possible non-Newtonian explanations:**

1. Spin-gravity coupling (Mashoon effect / gravitomagnetic analog):
In General Relativity, rotating masses create a gravitomagnetic field (frame-dragging). The spinning magnet, with its large magnetic moment, may couple to Earth's gravitomagnetic field in a way that generates a repulsive force during the downward phase:
F_GM ∝ (μ_magnet × L) · B_gravitomagnetic

This is a GR effect but predicted to be extremely small for laboratory masses. The observed effect (≈ 15-20 gram-force) is far larger than any GR gravitomagnetic prediction for a laboratory magnet.

2. Quantum vacuum radiation pressure asymmetry:
As the spinning magnet accelerates downward, the Unruh effect predicts that an accelerating observer sees a thermal bath with temperature T_Unruh = ℏ a / (2π c k_B), where a is the acceleration. For a = 10 m/s² (modest laboratory acceleration), T_Unruh ≈ 4 × 10⁻²⁰ K — negligibly small. This cannot explain the observed force at laboratory scale by many orders of magnitude.

3. Electromagnetic interaction with the scale mechanism:
If the falling spinning magnet creates a time-varying magnetic flux through the scale's internal components (load cell, spring mechanism), induced eddy currents could create an upward Lenz force on the magnet, transferred to the scale. This would be a mundane electromagnetic artifact rather than a gravitational anomaly.

4. Drew's working hypothesis: Gyroscopic spin-gravity interaction
The spinning magnet's angular momentum L = Iω creates a coupling to the local gravitational field that is not captured by Newtonian gravity or standard GR for this scale. The downward motion of a spinning object with angular momentum parallel to the local g vector creates a precessional reaction force with an upward component.

**Chain of reasoning (Drew's framework):**
1. Classical mechanics: W_scale = 0 during free fall. (Prediction.)
2. Observation: W_scale = -W_magnet during fall. (Result — exceeds classical prediction by 2× in wrong direction.)
3. Magnitude of anomaly: comparable to weight of magnet itself — not a tiny perturbation.
4. Reproducibility: confirmed in multiple experiments by Drew.
5. Historical parallel: Laithwaite gyroscope weight anomaly (different manifestation, same class of anomaly since 1974).
6. Conclusion: Spinning objects interact with gravity in ways not predicted by Newtonian mechanics (and apparently also not by standard GR at this scale).
