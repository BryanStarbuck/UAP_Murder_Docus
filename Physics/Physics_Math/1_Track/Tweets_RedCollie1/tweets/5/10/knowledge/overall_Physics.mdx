- The weight during fall is: W_fall = -(15 to 20) g × (9.81 m/s²) ≈ -0.15 to -0.20 N (force).
- The weight during rise: W_rise ≈ 0 (classical expectation would be +W_magnet during inertial rise — the anomaly is that the scale reads ZERO, not the positive value expected from inertial reaction).

**Time-averaged weight:**
Let W_static = static weight of magnet at rest.
Let T = period of oscillation = 1/20 Hz = 50 ms.
During rise (half-period T/2): apparent weight W_rise ≈ 0.
During fall (half-period T/2): apparent weight W_fall ≈ -W_static.

⟨W⟩ = (1/T) × [∫₀^{T/2} W_rise dt + ∫_{T/2}^{T} W_fall dt]
     = (1/T) × [0 × T/2 + (-W_static) × T/2]
     = -W_static / 2

**Result:** Time-averaged apparent weight of the entire device ≈ -W_magnet / 2.

**Anti-gravity metric:** The time-averaged weight reduction equals half the static magnet weight — a net upward effect without propellant consumption.

- This claim is experimental (qualitative comparison only — no precise thrust/power quantification given in source material).

**Physical anomaly requiring explanation:** 
During the rise phase, classical Newtonian mechanics (Newton's 3rd Law) predicts the scale should read W_static + m_magnet × a_rise (where a_rise > 0 is the upward acceleration of the magnet). The observation of W_rise ≈ 0 violates this prediction. Possible explanations considered in the literature:
1. Magnetic field interaction with the scale's own internal components (electromagnetic shielding artifact).
2. True gravitational decoupling during spin — the spinning magnet's interaction with the local gravitational field is modified by its angular momentum (non-Newtonian spin-gravity coupling).
3. Measurement artifact (scale response time too slow to track 20 Hz oscillation → reads averaged force, not instantaneous).

### Source: Axial Flux Motor Configuration — Physics Context

**Axial flux vs. radial flux:**
- Radial flux (conventional motor): magnetic flux crosses the air gap in the radial direction (perpendicular to shaft axis). B-field is horizontal if shaft is vertical.
- Axial flux (Drew's device): magnetic flux crosses the air gap parallel to the shaft axis (Z-direction). B-field is vertical if the disc is horizontal.

In axial flux configuration:
- The rotor is a disc (not a cylinder).
- The stator coils are wound with their axes parallel to the rotation axis.
- The torque is produced by the interaction of the axial B-field from the stator with the radial current in the rotor disc.
- Torque per unit volume is higher for axial flux configurations at low rotational speeds, which is relevant for the high-torque/low-speed spinning needed for the gyroscopic anti-gravity effect.

**Connection to "upward-axial flux" claim:**
The 9-coil array with "upward-axial flux" (2017450212500099374.yaml) is an axial flux stator. The upward direction of the flux means B_z > 0 (pointing in the +Z direction). The rotor (disc magnet) placed above the stator experiences this upward B-field, and the interaction between the stator's rotating field and the rotor's permanent magnetic moment drives the rotor to spin in the XY-plane.
