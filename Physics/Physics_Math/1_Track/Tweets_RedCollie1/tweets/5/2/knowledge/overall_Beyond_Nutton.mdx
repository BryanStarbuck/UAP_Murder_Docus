"Two counter-rotating magnetic fields seem to repel one another over a great distance."

F ~ μ₀ m₁ m₂ / (4π r⁴)   (force between dipoles → falls as r⁻⁴)

originate from a longer-range interaction. Possible mechanisms beyond static field theory:

1. Radiation reaction: counter-rotating dipoles radiate electromagnetic energy at the rotation
   frequency ω_rot. The radiation produces a radiation pressure that falls as r⁻² (far-field).
   For two counter-rotating sources at frequency ω:
   P_radiated = (μ₀ ω⁴ m²) / (6π c³)
   This radiative coupling falls more slowly with distance than static dipole coupling.

2. Spin-spin coupling (quantum mechanical, anomalous at classical scale):
   E_coupling = −J S₁ · S₂
   In condensed matter, exchange coupling (Heisenberg J) is short-range. For macroscopic rotating
   magnets, no equivalent classical coupling is predicted.

3. Torsion field (proposed exotic): some beyond-standard-model proposals (Shipov, Heim) suggest
   that rotating bodies generate torsion fields falling as r⁻² rather than r⁻⁴. This is not
   mainstream physics.
