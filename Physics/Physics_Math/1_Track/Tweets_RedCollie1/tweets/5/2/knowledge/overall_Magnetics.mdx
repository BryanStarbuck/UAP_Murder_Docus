- When floater diameter ≤ hole diameter of the ring: the floater magnet simultaneously experiences
  (a) repulsion from the central axis and (b) attraction toward the focal annular zone inward

floater's radial field spread exceeds the hole, the inward restoring force is overwhelmed.

- 145 mm ferrite ring (80 mm hole): focal point with 40, 50, 60, 70, 80 mm floaters; no focal point
  with 100 mm floater (larger than hole)
- 100 mm NdFeB ring (60 mm hole): focal point with 40, 50, 60 mm floaters; no focal point
  with 70, 80 mm floaters

static magnetic phenomenon requiring explanation beyond standard Earnshaw-based arguments.

Relation to Z-axis dynamics: The stability against the Z-axis displacement (gravity) is achieved

### Gyroscopic Precession Under Gravity: Laithwaite Experiment

Laithwaite (Imperial College, 1974 Royal Institution Christmas Lecture, suppressed):
"We will hang a weight from the inner ring so that gravity and not I shall put on the torque.
And there you see a precession. Not the sort of movement you really expected because you are
accustomed in higher mathematics to a torque producing an acceleration. Here a torque produces
pure [precession]."

This directly states the departure from standard classical mechanics: in a gyroscope under torque,
the response is precession (angular displacement perpendicular to both the torque and spin axis),
NOT angular acceleration in the direction of the torque. The equation of motion is:

τ = dL/dt

where L = Iω (angular momentum vector). For a spinning gyroscope, if τ is applied along x̂,
the precession is in ŷ, not along x̂. This is fully classical (Euler equations) but
counterintuitive. The rate of precession:

Ω_p = τ / L = τ / (I ω)

Laithwaite's claim of "anti-gravity" effects (reduction in apparent weight) remains
experimentally controversial. Drew cites Laithwaite's 1974 findings as showing "interesting
physical correlations" relevant to UFO levitation, though acknowledges it did not directly
solve the levitation problem.
