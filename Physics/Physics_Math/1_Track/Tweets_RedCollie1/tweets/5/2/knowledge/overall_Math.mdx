d_floater ≤ d_hole (ring)

where:
- d_floater = diameter of the floater (probe) magnet
- d_hole = inner hole diameter of the ring magnet

This inequality defines the boundary condition for the coexistence of axial repulsion and radial
confinement. It is a geometric constraint on the field topology rather than a force equation.

### Gyroscopic Precession Equations

Gyroscope angular momentum:
L = I ω ẑ     (spinning about ẑ with moment of inertia I and angular speed ω)

Applied torque (e.g., gravity on offset mass):
τ = r × F = r × (−mg ẑ)     (torque about the pivot)

Euler equation (no dissipation):
τ = dL/dt

For L = L ẑ and τ = τ x̂:
τ x̂ = L (dφ/dt) ŷ   (precession in the ŷ direction)

Precession angular velocity:
Ω_p = dφ/dt = τ / L = τ / (I ω)

Key mathematical relationship: Ω_p ∝ τ / ω — precession rate decreases as spin speed increases.
As ω → ∞, Ω_p → 0 (gyroscopic stiffness).
As ω → 0, Ω_p → ∞ (nutation, tumbling — the quasi-static limit).

Energy partition:
T_spin = ½ I ω²
T_precession = ½ I_p Ω_p²   where I_p = moment of inertia about precession axis

Potential energy of suspended weight:
ΔPE = m g Δh = m g R sin(α) ΔΦ

where R = distance from pivot to weight, α = angle of precession axis from vertical, ΔΦ =
precession angle traversed. This must equal work input W_in.

Laithwaite's observed anomaly (experimental claim, not mathematically derived): ΔPE > W_input
(i.e., the energy gain in potential energy exceeds the mechanical work input). The mathematical
resolution requires full accounting of T_precession, which Laithwaite may have omitted.

### 3-Phase AC Rotating Magnetic Field: Mathematical Description

Three-phase voltage waveforms (balanced, positive sequence):
V_A(t) = V₀ cos(ω_e t)
V_B(t) = V₀ cos(ω_e t − 2π/3)
V_C(t) = V₀ cos(ω_e t − 4π/3)

Current in coils (assuming resistive loads, in-phase with voltage):
I_A(t) = I₀ cos(ω_e t)
I_B(t) = I₀ cos(ω_e t − 2π/3)
I_C(t) = I₀ cos(ω_e t − 4π/3)

Coil A aligned with x̂, coil B at 120°, coil C at 240°:
B_A(t) = B₀ cos(ω_e t) x̂
B_B(t) = B₀ cos(ω_e t − 2π/3) (−½ x̂ + (√3/2) ŷ)
B_C(t) = B₀ cos(ω_e t − 4π/3) (−½ x̂ − (√3/2) ŷ)

Summing:
B_total(t) = (3/2) B₀ (cos(ω_e t) x̂ + sin(ω_e t) ŷ)

This is a uniformly rotating field of constant magnitude (3/2) B₀ in the XY-plane, rotating at
angular velocity ω_e (the electrical supply frequency). For ω_e = 2π × 6 rad/s (6 Hz, as
observed in the video 13_2036250409845792773), the field completes one revolution every 1/6 s.

Inner magnet coupling: a permanent magnet placed in this field experiences a torque:
τ = m × B_total(t)

where m is the magnetic dipole moment of the permanent magnet. This torque drags the permanent
magnet into co-rotation with the field (synchronous motor behavior), providing the inner-ring
spin drive.

### Angular Momentum Cancellation in Counter-Rotating System

Inner element (disc magnet): angular momentum L_inner = I_inner ω_inner (direction: +ẑ assumed)
Outer element (liquid metal channel): L_outer = I_outer ω_outer (direction: −ẑ)

Total angular momentum:
L_total = L_inner + L_outer = I_inner ω_inner ẑ − I_outer ω_outer ẑ

Cancellation condition:
L_total = 0 ↔ I_inner ω_inner = I_outer ω_outer

This means the moments of inertia and spin rates must be matched. If both elements are driven by
the same 3-phase supply frequency but the inner element has a different moment of inertia than the
outer element, they will spin at different angular velocities (different numbers of pole pairs in
the motor analogy), requiring frequency or gear ratio adjustment to achieve cancellation.

Physical purpose of L_total = 0:
1. No net gyroscopic torque on the craft — the craft can reorient freely without gyroscopic
   resistance
2. No net reaction torque transmitted to the airframe (no torque stall)
3. Possible anomalous field configuration at the interface where opposing angular momenta cancel

### Force Calculation Intractability: Rapid Counter-Rotating Fields

Drew explicitly states: "We cannot calculate the forces anyway, due to such rapid, counter-spinning
motions."

This is a statement about the breakdown of quasi-static approximations. In standard magnetostatics,
forces between magnets are computed from the static field energy U:

F = −∇U     where U = −m · B(r)

This assumes B is static on the timescale of force evaluation. When the magnets are rapidly
rotating, B(r, t) is time-varying, and the appropriate framework is:

F(t) = ∫ [J(r', t) × B(r, t)] d³r'     (Lorentz force on current distribution)

or equivalently the Maxwell stress tensor:

T_ij = ε₀(E_i E_j − ½ δ_ij E²) + (1/μ₀)(B_i B_j − ½ δ_ij B²)

Both of these become intractable analytically for rapidly counter-rotating dipole configurations
because the fields interact at the rotation frequency and all harmonics thereof, producing
non-trivial time-averaged forces that cannot be reduced to simple closed-form expressions.

Drew's claim: the net force (including any Z-axis levitation force) can only be determined
experimentally in this regime, not theoretically from current tools.
