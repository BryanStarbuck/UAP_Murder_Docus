### Three-Phase Electromagnetic Drive — Upward Force Production

The central bridging claim in this batch is the experimental demonstration that a three-phase rotating magnetic field, applied via a set of three-phase electromagnetic coils to a ring magnet with a central aluminum rod, produces a measurable upward thrust force on that rod exceeding 1 kilogram at low drive frequencies.

**The mechanism chain bridging XY-plane rotation to Z-axis thrust:**

1. Three-phase AC coils (3 coil assemblies at ~120° angular separation in the XY plane) are energized with 3-phase AC power at a controlled frequency f (Hz).
2. The rotating magnetic field produced in the XY plane drives a ring magnet (100 mm diameter, 13 mm hole, mounted on an 8 mm aluminum rod) into axial spin about the Z-axis.
3. As the ring magnet spins, a precessional or gyromagnetic interaction between the spinning magnet's dipole moment and the rotating field drives the central aluminum rod upward along the Z-axis — the pole is "driven upward by precession from the center" (per tweet 2030409779731153070).
4. The output force registered on a scale below is downward (reaction force to upward pole thrust): 350 g at onset → 500 g → 700 g at 15 Hz → 800 g → 900 g → ~1000 g (1 kg) at 17 Hz.
5. Input power at peak: 80 V, 3 A (3-phase AC), f = 17 Hz → P_input = 80 × 3 = 240 W (per-phase voltage×current; total 3-phase power depends on power factor and winding configuration, but the stated operating point is 80 V / 3 A / 17 Hz).
6. Control experiment (magnet flipped, south/red pole upward): magnet rises but NO force change registered on scale — demonstrating the force is orientation-dependent and not a trivial magnetic attraction artifact.

**Significance:** The production of ~1 kg upward force from a benchtop device using only 3-phase AC at 17 Hz, with no mechanical screws or conventional reaction mass, constitutes — if reproducible — a thrust-generation mechanism that is not explained by standard electromagnetic force models (Lorentz force on current-carrying conductors, simple magnetic attraction/repulsion). The effect is orientation-dependent (pole direction determines whether force is produced), pointing to a precessional or gyromagnetic coupling mechanism as the operative physics.

### Ring Magnet Focal Point — Paramagnetic Iron Requirement

For the ring magnet to develop a "focal point" (a convergent field geometry required for the axial precessional drive effect):
- Attractive forces around the outside of the magnet must balance against repulsive forces from the central hole.
