**Earnshaw's theorem (standard form):**
No configuration of static electric charges, magnetic poles, or static gravitational masses can be in stable equilibrium under the combined action of those forces. Formally: any potential energy function V(r) satisfying Laplace's equation (∇²V = 0) cannot have a local minimum in free space.

**How the experimental system circumvents Earnshaw:**
1. The field is not static — it rotates at ω = 2πf.
2. Gyroscopic stabilization: the spinning magnet's angular momentum provides restoring forces in the transverse (XY) directions that are absent in the static case, effectively adding a velocity-dependent term to the potential that can produce a local minimum.
3. The effective potential in the rotating frame includes centrifugal (ω²r²) and Coriolis (2ω × v) terms that modify the stability analysis.

In the rotating frame: V_eff(r) = V_static(r) − (1/2)I_ring ω² + V_Coriolis

A local minimum of V_eff can exist even where V_static has no local minimum, provided ω is large enough. This is the mathematical basis for the Paul trap (rotating saddle potential for ion trapping) and for the stability of spinning magnetic tops near magnets — the "levitron" effect. Drew's device operates on a related but more powerful principle.
