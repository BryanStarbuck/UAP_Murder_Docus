### Three-Phase Rotating Magnetic Field — Drive Architecture

**Device geometry:**
- Three electromagnetic coil assemblies positioned at approximately 120° angular separation in the XY plane
- Ring magnet: 100 mm outer diameter, 13 mm central hole, mounted on 8 mm aluminum rod
- Magnet material: not specified, but the focal-point requirement (see 1921368126412726679) suggests partial paramagnetic iron content
- Drive signal: variable-frequency 3-phase AC from a variable frequency drive (VFD)

**Rotating field generation:**
A 3-phase AC system with phase currents I_U(t) = I₀ cos(ωt), I_V(t) = I₀ cos(ωt − 2π/3), I_W(t) = I₀ cos(ωt − 4π/3) applied to spatially separated coils at 0°, 120°, 240° produces a rotating magnetic field vector in the XY plane:

B_xy(t) = B₀ [cos(ωt) x̂ + sin(ωt) ŷ]

where ω = 2πf, f = drive frequency in Hz. At f = 17 Hz, ω = 2π × 17 ≈ 106.8 rad/s.

The magnitude |B_xy| = B₀ (constant), rotating at angular velocity ω in the XY plane. This is the standard symmetric 3-phase rotating field of induction motor theory.

**Interaction with ring magnet:**
The ring magnet's permanent dipole moment m aligns with B_xy and follows it, resulting in mechanical rotation at synchronous speed Ω_mech = ω/p_pairs (where p_pairs = number of pole pairs; for a simple ring magnet, p_pairs = 1, so Ω_mech = ω = 2πf).

At f = 17 Hz: Ω_mech = 2π × 17 ≈ 106.8 rad/s ≈ 1020 RPM (synchronous, no slip).

**XY-plane dynamics → Z-axis force (anomalous result):**
Standard electromagnetic theory predicts no net axial (Z-axis) force on a freely spinning magnet from a rotating field in the XY plane when the magnet axis is constrained along Z. The observation of ~1 kg upward reaction force on the scale is anomalous from a standard Maxwell-Lorentz standpoint. Drew attributes it to precessional mechanics: the spinning magnet (angular momentum L = Iω along Z) interacts with a torque component to produce precession, and under the constrained geometry (rod bounded above by a board), this precession manifests as upward axial force.

**Experimental force data (measured):**
- f < threshold: no force change
- f such that pole reaches constraint board: 350 g
- Increasing frequency: 500 g → 700 g (at 15 Hz) → 800 g → 900 g → 1000 g (at 17 Hz)
- Power off: force returns to zero
- Control (magnet flipped, S-pole up): no force generated

The orientation-dependence of the effect (N-pole must be the rising pole, not S-pole) indicates a magnetic polarity dependence in the force generation mechanism, consistent with a coupling between magnetic moment orientation and the angular momentum vector's precession direction.

### Ring Magnet Focal Point — Field Topology for Axial Force Convergence

**Condition for focal point:** The ring magnet must develop a field topology where the external attractive field lines around the magnet's periphery are balanced against the repulsive flux emerging from the central hole, creating a convergent focal region along the Z-axis.

For a uniformly axially magnetized ring magnet with inner radius r₁, outer radius r₂, thickness t, axial magnetization M:

The on-axis field H_z(z) = (M/2) × [z/√(z² + r₂²) − z/√(z² + r₁²) − (z−t)/√((z−t)² + r₂²) + (z−t)/√((z−t)² + r₁²)]

This expression has a maximum on-axis and falls off at large z. For a focal-point geometry, one requires a secondary maximum or field convergence above the ring, which is NOT achieved by uniform magnetization alone.

**Paramagnetic iron component requirement:** Introducing paramagnetic iron (μ_r ≈ 1 + χ_m, χ_m ~ 10⁻⁵ for pure iron in paramagnetic state but effectively ~ 200–5000 in soft ferromagnetic state) into the magnet composition allows the external pole pieces to redistribute flux such that a focal convergence point forms along the axis. This is the classical solenoid/pole-piece focusing arrangement in particle physics: shaped iron poles concentrate the field axially. In Drew's ring magnet, the paramagnetic iron fraction performs the equivalent function.

**Referenced phenomenon:** "Mainstream physics 2022: 'Magnets should not hover when they are close together.' Yet akin to what Galileo said in 1600: 'Still they hover!'" — Drew is claiming experimental observation of magnet hovering that contradicts standard magnetic levitation analysis (Earnshaw's theorem: static magnetic fields cannot stably levitate a permanent magnet). The focal-point ring geometry may create conditions where the Earnshaw constraint is locally violated by dynamic effects (rotation introduces gyroscopic stability absent in purely static configurations).

### Dual Counter-Rotating Ring Architecture — Magnetic Field Configuration

**Inner ring (3-coil, clockwise field):**
Three compact wire coils placed at center just below the inner ring, powered with 3-phase AC producing a clockwise-rotating B field (viewed from above, i.e., from +Z). Inner ring magnets (N-S alternating) align with and follow the rotating field → inner ring rotates clockwise at Ω_inner = 2πf_inner.

**Outer ring (opposing rotation):**
Coil count for outer ring: N_outer ∈ {6, 9, 12, 15, 18} — multiples of 3 compatible with 3-phase winding.

Method A — Anti-clockwise field: Second set of outer coils fed with phase sequence U-W-V (reversed phase order) instead of U-V-W, producing an anti-clockwise rotating B field → outer ring magnets rotate counter-clockwise.

Method B — Polarity inversion: Outer coils also fed with clockwise U-V-W phase sequence, but outer ring magnets assembled with reversed polarity relative to inner ring. The torque on a magnet in a rotating field is τ = m × B. With inverted polarity, m → −m, so τ → −τ, causing the outer ring to spin opposite to the field direction (counter-clockwise relative to field direction, which is still clockwise in lab frame but mechanically counter-clockwise relative to inner ring).

**Net magnetic topology:** Inner ring rotating CW at Ω₁, outer ring rotating CCW at Ω₂ (or with Ω₂ = Ω₁ in Method B relative to field but −Ω₁ relative to inner ring) → the combined field from both rotating rings produces a spatially complex, time-varying B(r,θ,z,t) field that cannot be reduced to a simple rotating dipole. The gradient ∂B/∂z between rings may produce axial (Z-direction) Maxwell stress tensor components:

T_zz = (1/2μ₀)(B_z² − B_r² − B_θ²)

suggesting that the axial component of B² integrated over the appropriate surface could generate a net upward (or downward) stress — a possible mechanistic bridge to lift force.

### 9-Coil / 12-Magnet Winding Configuration

**Wiring topology: UVW-UVW-UVW**
Nine coils, wired as three groups of three (each group carrying U, V, W phases respectively). With 12 N-S alternating magnets:
- Number of pole pairs p = 12/2 = 6
- Synchronous mechanical speed Ω_s = 2πf / p = 2πf / 6
- At f = 17 Hz: Ω_s = 2π × 17 / 6 ≈ 17.8 rad/s ≈ 170 RPM

This is a fractional-slot winding with 9 coils / 12 poles = 3/4 slots per pole per phase — a common configuration in permanent magnet motor design that minimizes cogging torque (important for smooth rotation of the magnet ring).

**Large central magnet — precession-driven upward displacement:**
Once the outer ring (12 magnets) is spinning stably under 9-coil drive, a large central magnet placed at the center is "driven upward by precession from the center." The physical model: the spinning outer ring acts as a gyroscope coupled magnetically to the central magnet. The central magnet's dipole is torqued by the rotating field of the spinning outer ring, causing it to precess. With the rod constrained geometrically (bounded above), precession manifests as axial (Z-axis) thrust.

- Motion: fast-spinning (rotation inferred from morphology/brightness variation)
- Anomaly: comet tails do NOT normally turn green (reference: UNSW 2021 study confirming 90-year-old observation); 3I/ATLAS coma AND tail are both green — indicating an anomalous nickel-ion source mechanism

**Magnetic shield physics (hypothesis):**
A rotating shell of ionized gas (plasma) with charge density ρ_q and angular velocity ω_plasma generates a toroidal magnetic field B_φ and, via the Biot-Savart contributions of the rotating surface charge, also a dipole-like poloidal field. For a spherical rotating shell of surface charge density σ and angular velocity ω:

B_interior = (2/3)μ₀ σ ω R (uniform, along rotation axis)

This is analogous to Earth's magnetosphere but with nickel plasma rather than ionized oxygen/nitrogen.
