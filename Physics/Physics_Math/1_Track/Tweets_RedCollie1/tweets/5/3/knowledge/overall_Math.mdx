### 3-Phase Rotating Field — Core Equations

**Phase currents (balanced 3-phase system):**

I_U(t) = I₀ cos(ωt)
I_V(t) = I₀ cos(ωt − 2π/3)
I_W(t) = I₀ cos(ωt − 4π/3)

where ω = 2πf, f = drive frequency (Hz), I₀ = peak current amplitude.

**Rotating field vector in XY plane:**

B_xy(t) = B₀ [cos(ωt) x̂ + sin(ωt) ŷ]

|B_xy| = B₀ = constant (magnitude fixed, direction rotates at ω)

Angular velocity of field rotation: ω_field = 2πf

**Synchronous mechanical speed of magnet (1 pole pair):**

Ω_mech = ω / p_pairs = 2πf / 1 = 2πf

At f = 17 Hz: Ω_mech = 2π × 17 = 34π ≈ 106.8 rad/s ≈ 1020 RPM

**Operating point (stated):**
- V = 80 V (3-phase)
- I = 3 A
- f = 17 Hz
- F_z = 1000 g = 9.81 N (upward axial force)
- Apparent input power: P = V × I = 80 × 3 = 240 W (single-phase equivalent; 3-phase total power depends on winding configuration)

## Section: Gyroscopic / Precessional Mechanics

**Angular momentum of spinning ring magnet:**

L = I_ring × Ω_spin ẑ

I_ring = (1/2) m_ring (r_o² + r_i²)

For ring magnet: m_ring ≈ 50–100 g, r_o = 50 mm = 0.05 m, r_i = 6.5 mm = 0.0065 m:

I_ring ≈ (1/2)(0.075)(0.0025 + 0.0000423) ≈ 9.5 × 10⁻⁵ kg·m²

At f = 17 Hz: L = I × Ω = 9.5 × 10⁻⁵ × 106.8 ≈ 1.01 × 10⁻² N·m·s

**Precessional rate under applied torque τ:**

Ω_precession = τ / L = τ / (I_ring × Ω_spin)

**Axial force from constrained precession:**
F_z = τ / (ℓ sin θ)

where ℓ = moment arm (effective rod length), θ = angle between angular momentum vector and constraint.

**Torque required to produce observed F_z = 9.81 N:**
τ = F_z × ℓ = 9.81 × ℓ_eff

For ℓ_eff ≈ 0.1 m (half rod length): τ ≈ 0.98 N·m

**Consistency check — precession rate:**
Ω_p = τ/L = 0.98 / 0.010 ≈ 98 rad/s

This is comparable to Ω_spin = 107 rad/s → Ω_p / Ω_spin ≈ 0.92. When precession rate approaches spin rate, the simple Euler equation breaks down and nutation coupling must be included:

(d²ψ/dt²) I₁ + Ω_spin (I₃ − I₁)(dφ/dt) = τ_external

where ψ = precession angle, φ = spin angle, I₁ = transverse moment of inertia, I₃ = axial moment of inertia.

### Ring Magnet On-Axis Field (Exact Expression)

For an axially magnetized ring magnet with magnetization M (A/m), inner radius r₁, outer radius r₂, thickness t, the on-axis field at distance z from the face:

H_z(z) = (M/2) × [ z/√(z² + r₂²) − z/√(z² + r₁²) − (z−t)/√((z−t)² + r₂²) + (z−t)/√((z−t)² + r₁²) ]

B_z(z) = μ₀ H_z(z) (in air, outside the magnet)

**Focal point condition:** d²B_z/dz² = 0 at a point z_f > t (above the magnet face) and dB_z/dz > 0 for z < z_f, dB_z/dz < 0 for z > z_f — a local maximum of B_z on axis, creating a potential well for a diamagnetic particle or a potential "hill" for a ferromagnetic element. The existence and position of z_f depends on the ratio r₁/r₂ and t/r₂; paramagnetic pole pieces modify the boundary conditions, shifting z_f.

**Paramagnetic iron permeability:**
μ_r = 1 + χ_m

For soft iron (ferromagnetic in practice): μ_r ~ 10³–10⁴ (not paramagnetic in the strict quantum mechanical sense, but Drew uses "paramagnetic" to indicate a magnetically soft, high-permeability material needed to focus flux without permanently retaining magnetization opposite to the drive field).

**Balance condition for hovering:**
For static levitation (which Drew claims is observed): the upward magnetic pressure force on the floating magnet must equal its weight:

∫(m · ∇B) dV = m_g g

(m · ∇B) = m_z (∂B_z/∂z)

m_z × (∂B_z/∂z)|_{z=z_hover} = m_g g

This requires a specific field gradient ∂B_z/∂z = m_g g / m_z at the hover point. For a permanent magnet with moment m_z = M × V_magnet, the required gradient can be computed. Earnshaw's theorem states this cannot be achieved with purely static fields for unconstrained degrees of freedom — but with dynamic (rotating) fields, gyroscopic stabilization provides the missing restoring force in the transverse directions.

### Dual Counter-Rotating Ring — Combined Field Decomposition

**Inner ring field:**
B_inner(r,t) = B_i(r) cos(Ω_i t + φ_i(r))

**Outer ring field (counter-rotating, Ω_o = −Ω_i):**
B_outer(r,t) = B_o(r) cos(−Ω_i t + φ_o(r)) = B_o(r) cos(Ω_i t − φ_o(r))

Wait — since Ω_o = −Ω_i, the outer field written in complex notation:

B_outer(r,t) = Re[B̃_o(r) e^{−iΩ_i t}]

**Sum (in complex phasor notation, z-component):**

B_z,total(r,t) = Re[B̃_i(r) e^{+iΩ_i t} + B̃_o(r) e^{−iΩ_i t}]

**Decomposition into sum and difference frequencies:**

B_z,total = |B_i+B_o*|cos(φ₊)/2 + |B_i−B_o*|cos(2Ω_i t + φ₋)/2

(where B_o* is the complex conjugate of B_o in the phasor representation)

The **DC (time-independent) term**: B_static(r) = Re[(B̃_i + B̃_o*)/2]

This static component has a non-zero spatial gradient ∂B_static/∂z capable of exerting sustained force on any magnetic material. The 2Ω_i oscillating term averages to zero force on the slow timescale of mechanical motion.

**Maxwell stress tensor axial component:**

T_zz = (1/2μ₀)(B_z² − B_r² − B_θ²)

Net upward force on a surface S enclosing the interior:

F_z = ∮_S T_zz dA = ∮_S [(1/2μ₀)(B_z² − B_r² − B_θ²)] dA

For a symmetric arrangement where the counter-rotating fields produce B_z(inner region) > B_r, B_θ → T_zz > 0 → net upward force. This is the Maxwell stress mechanism proposed (implicitly) for levitation in Drew's model.

### 9-Slot / 12-Pole Winding Parameters

**Pole pairs:** p = 12/2 = 6

**Slots per pole per phase:** q = N_slots / (2 × p × m) = 9 / (2 × 6 × 3) = 9/36 = 1/4

**Synchronous speed:**
Ω_s = ω_drive / p = 2πf / 6

At f = 17 Hz: Ω_s = 2π(17)/6 = 17.8 rad/s = 170 RPM

**Winding factor (9-slot, 12-pole concentrated winding):**
k_w = k_d × k_p ≈ 0.866

(For concentrated single-layer winding, this is a standard tabulated value for the 9/12 combination.)

**Air-gap flux density fundamental:**
B₁ = k_w × (4/π) × (μ₀ NI) / (2g)

For N turns per coil, I = 3 A, g = air gap:
B₁ = 0.866 × (4/π) × (4π × 10⁻⁷ × N × 3) / (2g)

= 0.866 × (4/π) × (μ₀ × 3N) / (2g)

≈ (3.3 × 10⁻⁶ N) / g [T, with g in meters]

## Section: Homopolar Generator (2042735857673945396.yaml)

**Faraday disc EMF:**

ε = ∫₀^R (v × B) · dr = ∫₀^R ωr B_z dr = (1/2) ω B_z R²

**Power output (with external resistance R_ext):**

I_gen = ε / R_ext

P_gen = ε² / R_ext = (ω B_z R²)² / (4 R_ext)

**Torque required to maintain spin against generator back-torque:**

τ_back = (B_z² R⁴ ω) / (4 R_ext μ₀) (order of magnitude)

This back-torque is subtracted from the drive torque of the 3-phase coils; the net propulsive torque is:

τ_net = τ_drive − τ_back − τ_friction

**Nickel emission wavelength (atomic):**
Ni I: 5475.43 Å (green, visible)
Ni⁺: multiple UV-visible transitions

**C₂ Swan band (normal comet coma green):**
d³Πg → a³Πu, band head at 5165 Å (λ ≈ 516.5 nm, green)

**Difference:** C₂ green is from molecular fluorescence; Ni/Ni⁺ green is from atomic/ionic emission. Both produce green but by different spectroscopic mechanisms. The tail composition of 3I/ATLAS showing Ni⁺ ion emission is inconsistent with any known cometary process.

**Ni/Fe solar abundance ratio:**
(Ni/Fe)_sun ≈ 0.057 by number
(Ni/Fe)_chondrite ≈ 0.055 by number
(Ni/Fe)_3I/ATLAS >> (Ni/Fe)_sun → factor unknown, but described as anomalously Ni-rich, Fe-poor

**Gyroradius of Ni⁺ ion in magnetosphere:**
r_g = m_Ni v_⊥ / (qB)

m_Ni = 58 × 1.67 × 10⁻²⁷ kg ≈ 9.7 × 10⁻²⁶ kg

r_g(Ni⁺) = (9.7 × 10⁻²⁶ × 4 × 10⁵) / (1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ × 10⁻⁸) ≈ 2.4 × 10⁸ m ≈ 0.34 R_Earth

This gyroradius (~0.34 Earth radii) is smaller than typical solar wind proton gyroradii, meaning nickel-ion plasma is better confined per tesla of field than proton plasma — lending credence to Drew's claim that a Ni-ion magnetosphere is more effective per unit field strength than an oxygen/nitrogen-ion one.

## Section: Rotating Charged Shell Magnetic Field (1994181682417815679.yaml)

**Uniformly charged rotating spherical shell (coma model):**
Surface charge density σ, radius R_coma, angular velocity ω_plasma about Z-axis:

Interior field (uniform): B_int = (2/3) μ₀ σ ω_plasma R_coma (along ẑ)

Exterior field: dipole with moment m = (4π/3) σ ω_plasma R_coma³

**For nickel ion plasma with number density n_Ni⁺ ions/m³:**
σ_eff = q × n_Ni⁺ × shell_thickness × R_coma = e × n × Δr × R_coma

The shielding effectiveness scales as B_int ∝ σ ω R, so higher spin rate ω_plasma → stronger interior shielding field.
