**Operating point:**
- Voltage: 80 V (3-phase)
- Current: 3 A

**Standard physics expectation:** A rotating magnetic field driving a ring magnet into spin about the Z-axis should produce no net axial force via the Lorentz force (F = q(v × B)) on the magnet's material because the net force on a magnetic dipole in a uniform field is F = ∇(m · B). With the rotating field uniform across the magnet volume and the magnet spinning synchronously, ∇B ≈ 0 at the magnet position → F_z ≈ 0 by standard analysis. The observed 1 kg force is anomalous under this analysis.

**Control experiment conclusion:** The force is polarity-dependent (N-pole upward → force; S-pole upward → no force), confirming it is not a simple scale artifact or electromagnetic attraction between device and scale. The polarity dependence is consistent with a gyromagnetic or precessional mechanism where the sign of angular momentum relative to magnetic moment orientation determines the direction of the force.

## Section: Precessional Mechanics — Gyroscopic Force from Spinning Magnetic Dipole

**Gyroscopic precession framework:**
A spinning ring magnet with moment of inertia I and angular velocity Ω_spin has angular momentum:

L = I Ω_spin ẑ (for spin about Z-axis)

An applied torque τ in the XY plane causes precession at rate:

Ω_precession = τ / L = τ / (I Ω_spin)

For a free gyroscope, precession is orbital (L rotates in XY plane). For a constrained gyroscope (rod bounded above by a board), the precessional motion cannot proceed freely; instead, the constraint converts precessional torque into axial force:

F_z = τ / (R_constraint × sin θ)

where R_constraint is the effective radius at which the constraint acts and θ is the angle between L and the constraint direction. This is the mechanism Drew implicitly invokes: "spin of that magnet will drive the pole outward" (upward when constrained by the board).

**Quantitative relationship:** The observed F_z ~ 1 kg = 9.81 N at f = 17 Hz implies a torque τ = F_z × R ≈ 9.81 × (rod length / 2). With a ~200 mm rod, τ ≈ 0.98 N·m. The moment of inertia of a 100 mm ring magnet (mass ~50–100 g) about its axis: I ≈ (1/2)m(r_o² + r_i²) ≈ (1/2)(0.075)(0.05² + 0.0065²) ≈ 9.5 × 10⁻⁵ kg·m². At Ω_spin = 2π × 17 ≈ 107 rad/s: L = I × Ω ≈ 9.5 × 10⁻⁵ × 107 ≈ 0.010 N·m·s. Precession rate needed to sustain 0.98 N·m torque: Ω_p = τ/L = 0.98/0.010 ≈ 98 rad/s ≈ 937 RPM — which is comparable to the spin rate, suggesting the system is operating near or in the nutational regime where simple precession analysis breaks down and more complex gyromagnetic/nutation dynamics apply. This is a key finding: the device may operate in a regime where simple gyroscope precession and spin-orbit-like coupling between the rotating magnetic field and the spinning magnet's angular momentum are intertwined.

### Dual Counter-Rotating Ring Physics

**Phase sequence reversal for counter-rotation:**
Standard 3-phase motor theory: synchronous speed direction is determined by the phase rotation sequence U-V-W (positive sequence → clockwise in XY plane) vs. U-W-V (negative sequence → counter-clockwise). By applying positive sequence to inner coils and negative sequence to outer coils:

Ω_inner = +2πf / p_inner (clockwise)
Ω_outer = −2πf / p_outer (counter-clockwise)

If p_inner = p_outer = p: |Ω_inner| = |Ω_outer| = 2πf/p.

**Counter-rotating magnetomechanical system:**
In the lab frame, the inner and outer magnetic rings produce superposed magnetic fields. The total field at position r is:

B_total(r,t) = B_inner(r,Ω_inner t) + B_outer(r,Ω_outer t)

With Ω_outer = −Ω_inner, the combined field contains both co-rotating (2Ω_inner t frequency) and static (0 frequency) components:

B_total(r,t) = B_static(r) + B_2ω(r,2Ω_inner t)

The static component B_static(r) has a spatially varying axial gradient ∂B_z/∂z that, via the Maxwell stress tensor, can produce sustained Z-axis force on any magnetic material or plasma in the interior. This is the physical mechanism proposed for UFO lift: the counter-rotating rings create a static axial field gradient that exerts continuous upward force.

**Outer coil count constraint:**
N_outer ∈ {6, 9, 12, 15, 18} — these are multiples of 3 (required for balanced 3-phase winding), specifically 3×{2, 3, 4, 5, 6}. The number of pole pairs for the outer ring p_outer = N_outer/3. Minimum viable: p_outer = 2 (6 coils), maximum practical: p_outer = 6 (18 coils). Higher pole count → lower synchronous speed for a given drive frequency, allowing finer control of Ω_outer relative to Ω_inner.

### 9-Coil / 12-Pole Drive — Fractional Slot Winding Physics

**Winding configuration: UVW-UVW-UVW**
9 coils, 12 magnets (6 pole pairs). This is a fractional-slot concentrated winding:
- Slots per pole per phase = Q/(2pm) = 9/(2×6×3) = 9/36 = 0.25

Standard for permanent magnet motors in UAV/robotics applications — minimizes cogging torque due to the fractional winding factor. The winding factor k_w for this configuration:

k_w = k_d × k_p

where k_d (distribution factor) and k_p (pitch factor) depend on the slot-pole combination. For 9-slot/12-pole: k_w ≈ 0.866 (tabulated value for concentrated windings).

**Effective air-gap field:**
B_gap(θ,t) = Σ_n B_n cos(n(pθ − ωt)) where fundamental n=1: B₁ = k_w × (4/π) × (NI/2g) × μ₀

For N turns per coil, I = 3 A, g = air gap = ~1–3 mm: B₁ ≈ several hundred mT to ~1 T for well-wound coils.

**Precession of large central magnet:**
With the outer ring of 12 magnets spinning at Ω_s = 2πf/6 ≈ 17.8 rad/s (at f = 17 Hz), the rotating ring's dipole field drives a central magnet into precession. The precession is then converted to upward Z-axis force by the constraint geometry — as confirmed experimentally in the 13_1912715675791159296 video.

### 3I/ATLAS Interstellar Object — Anomalous Spectroscopy

**JWST image data (August 6, 2025, released August 26, 2025):**
- Pixel scale: ~20 km/pixel (low resolution)
- Morphology: large cloud of CO₂ gas (orange channel) surrounding a large central rock (white/bright)

**Spectroscopic anomaly (September 7, 2025 data, per Avi Loeb medium.com article cited):**
- Coma previously: reddish color
- Coma now (September 2025): turning green
- Tail: also turning green
- Dominant species: nickel (Ni, Ni⁺) emission — green atomic/ionic emission lines

**Ni emission physics:**
Nickel atomic emission lines relevant to the observed green color: Ni I at 5475.43 Å (visible, green region) and Ni⁺ emission bands. The green color of the tail is anomalous because:
- Normal comets: coma turns green from C₂ Swan band emission (diatomic carbon, d³Πg → a³Πu electronic transition, peaks at 5165 Å)
- Normal comet tails: composed of ionized gas (CO⁺, N₂⁺, etc.) emitting blue/UV; NEVER green
- UNSW 2021 study (cited in tweet): confirmed 90-year observational rule that comet tails do not turn green; only comas can be green (C₂) but tails cannot
- 3I/ATLAS: both coma AND tail green + composed of nickel rather than carbon → cannot be a natural comet → anomalous source

**Ni/Fe abundance anomaly:**
Solar abundance ratio Ni/Fe ≈ 0.06 by number. If 3I/ATLAS were a rocky body of solar composition, Fe emission should dominate. The observation of "much nickel, but very little iron" deviates from expected solar/chondritic composition, suggesting:
1. The object is not of normal rocky composition
2. Nickel has been selectively mobilized by an active process (e.g., ablation of nickel-alloy structural material if the object is artificial)
3. The path change at perihelion (10% mass ejected as "small spaceships" per 1987639439250882741) is consistent with selective nickel-alloy thruster exhaust

### 3I/ATLAS Path Change at Perihelion — Propulsion Claim

Drew's interpretation: 3I/ATLAS changed trajectory at perihelion because ~10% of its mass, in the form of small spacecraft, fired bright-blue thrusters against the main body to slow it. Result:
- Main rock: continues faster (less deceleration)
- Small spacecraft: decelerate more, dropping into Earth orbit (green spectrum signature consistent with nickel thruster exhaust)

**Physics of the claimed trajectory change:**
If a 10% mass fraction Δm fires thrusters imparting velocity change Δv_s to itself in the −r direction (against parent body), by conservation of momentum:

M_total × v_initial = (M_total − Δm) × v_main + Δm × v_small

(M_total − Δm)(v_main − v_initial) = −Δm(v_small − v_initial)

Δv_main = Δm/(M_total − Δm) × (v_initial − v_small)

For Δm/M_total = 0.1: Δv_main ≈ (0.1/0.9)(Δv_thruster) ≈ 0.111 Δv_thruster

A path change of several percent of orbital velocity at perihelion requires Δv_thruster on the order of several km/s from the small spacecraft — consistent with chemical or advanced propulsion systems.
