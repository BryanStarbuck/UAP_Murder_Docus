### James Webb Space Telescope Image of 3I/ATLAS — Observational Data

JWST image characteristics (August 6, 2025 data, released August 26, 2025):
- Pixel scale: ~20 km/pixel
- Orange channel: CO₂ gas cloud
- White/bright: large central rock
- Resolution limitation: insufficient to resolve surface details

Drew questions: "I don't understand how this image could be of such low quality, but maybe there is a reason?" — implying possible deliberate degradation of publicly released JWST imagery to conceal details of 3I/ATLAS. This is a suppression claim, not physics.

**Observational astronomy note:**
JWST primary mirror diameter: 6.5 m
Angular resolution: θ ≈ 1.22 λ/D
At λ = 2 μm (NIRCam), D = 6.5 m: θ ≈ 0.38 arcsec
At a distance of ~0.5–1 AU from JWST, 0.38 arcsec corresponds to:
0.38 arcsec × (1 AU / 206265 arcsec) × 1.5 × 10¹¹ m = 2.8 × 10⁵ m = 280 km

A pixel scale of ~20 km/pixel would require the object to be at ~0.071 AU (10.6 million km) and the image to be sampled at the diffraction limit. At ~0.5 AU distance, the pixel scale of ~20 km/pixel is NOT at the JWST diffraction limit (which would give ~280 km resolution at 0.5 AU) — the stated 20 km/pixel implies the object was much closer to JWST than 0.5 AU, OR the image was made with a smaller aperture / longer wavelength. This inconsistency supports Drew's questioning of the image quality, though his explanation (deliberate degradation) is speculative.
