### Spin as the Source of Gravitational Potential Increase

Core claim: "Spin leads to increased gravitational potential."

For Z-axis lift, the craft must increase its gravitational potential energy: ΔU_grav = mgh, where h is altitude gained. In Newtonian mechanics, this requires either an external upward force (thrust) or a transfer of energy from another form. The claim here is that spin (kinetic rotational energy E_rot = ½Iω²) couples directly to gravitational potential via a non-Newtonian mechanism.

If valid, the energy budget for lift:
**ΔU_grav = mgh ≤ ΔE_rot = Δ(½Iω²)**

This would mean spinning faster (increasing ω) directly raises the craft. The efficiency of conversion (ΔU_grav/ΔE_rot) is unknown; it may be much less than unity. The claim requires ΔE_rot to manifest as vertical potential energy gain rather than as kinetic energy, heat, or radiation.

### Focal-Point Magnetic Levitation: Z-Axis Lift from Spinning Top + Lower Magnet

Hypothesis for vertical lift:
1. Spinning top magnet placed at focal point of large lower magnet
2. Spinning top magnet rises at focal point
3. Large lower magnet (and entire craft assembly) carried upward by the rising top

Mechanistic interpretation of "focal point rise":
The focal point of a large (annular or disk) magnet's field is a region of field gradient that traps the spinning magnetic dipole. When the spinning magnet gains kinetic energy (spin-up), the gyroscopic stabilization prevents dipole flip, and the interaction with the field gradient converts rotational energy to translational kinetic energy (Z-axis).

Quantitative: force on spinning magnet dipole m along z-axis:
**F_z = m_z ∂B_z/∂z**

For lift, need F_z > mg_craft (weight of entire assembly). This requires:
**m_z ∂B_z/∂z > m_total g**

For a 1 kg assembly, m_total g ≈ 10 N. For a strong permanent magnet with m_z ≈ 10 A·m² in a gradient ∂B_z/∂z ≈ 10 T/m: F_z ≈ 100 N → lifts ~10 kg. This scale suggests the focal-point mechanism could in principle provide significant lift for laboratory-scale prototypes.

The coupling mechanism (spinning top rise carries lower magnet) is mechanical — the spinning top must be physically coupled to the lower assembly via a ball joint or similar mechanism.

### Gyroscope Rise to Vertical Under Spin Increase: Lift in the Vertical Direction

Experimental observation:
- Gyroscope with mass offset (blue tack) precessing at some angle θ from vertical
- Additional spin added (resistance touching): disc rises to vertical (θ → 0°)

In standard gyroscope mechanics, increasing spin speed:
- Reduces precession rate: Ω_p ∝ 1/ω_spin
- In the fast-rotation limit, the gyroscope behaves as a "sleeping top" with axis vertical

The "rise to vertical" is: the disc's axis aligns with the vertical (gravity axis). The mass offset (blue tack at one side) no longer droops to the bottom. The disc's angular momentum vector L is now vertical.

Energetics: as θ → 0° (vertical), the mass offset m_b at radius r_b is lifted from its lowest position (θ = θ_0 from vertical) to θ = 0° (horizontal offset from a vertical axis, meaning the blue tack is at the same height as the center). Actually:

Height of mass offset above pivot = r_b cos θ (taking θ as angle from vertical)
At θ = θ_0: height = r_b cos θ_0
At θ = 0°: height = r_b (maximum)

So the blue tack rises as θ → 0°. The energy for this rise comes from the increase in spin kinetic energy (ΔE_rot = Δ(½Iω²)).

The "pushing against the vacuum" interpretation: the mechanism by which additional spin energy is converted to potential energy (raising the blue tack) involves the gyroscope's interaction with the vacuum rather than with a classical external force. This is the non-Newtonian component of the claim.

### Z-Axis Lift from XY-Plane Spin + Three-Phase Upward Drive

Two-stage lift mechanism:

Stage 1 (XY dynamics → spin creation):
The DC Lorentz force creates azimuthal spin u_θ in the liquid metal. This builds angular momentum in the horizontal plane:
**L_xy = I_fluid Ω_fluid ẑ**

where I_fluid = ∫ r² dm (moment of inertia of spinning fluid) and Ω_fluid is the angular velocity.

Stage 2 (3-phase field → Z-axis force):
The three-phase rotating field **B_3ph** with frequency f is applied upward from coils below. The interaction of the three-phase field with the spinning conducting fluid:

If the three-phase coil geometry is arranged to produce a net axial (Z-axis) electromagnetic body force on the fluid, the result is:

**F_z = ∫∫∫ (J × B_3ph)_z dV**

For the "trident" geometry (three spokes at 120° spacing): the Lorentz force from the interaction of radial current components with the azimuthal component of B_3ph has a net vertical component when the coils are below the fluid and the field has an axial gradient:

**F_z ∝ σ u_θ B_z,3ph(z) B_r,3ph(r)**

where the product of the rotating-field components generates the vertical force. This is a second-order effect, proportional to the product of the fluid spin velocity and the three-phase field components.

Dimensional estimate: for σ(galinstan) ≈ 3.46 × 10⁶ S/m, u_θ ~ 0.1 m/s, B_3ph ~ 0.1 T, geometry factor ~ 0.01 m³:
**F_z ~ 3.46 × 10⁶ × 0.1 × 0.1 × 0.01 ≈ 3460 N**

This is a rough upper bound. The actual force depends heavily on coil geometry and the specific spatial configuration.

### Spin / Counter-Spin: Torque Cancellation with Net Z-Lift Preserved

The spin/counter-spin architecture:
- Rotor A: spin ω_A (clockwise viewed from above), moment of inertia I_A
- Rotor B: spin ω_B (anti-clockwise viewed from above), moment of inertia I_B, with I_A ω_A = I_B ω_B

Torque reaction: L_total = 0 → no net torque on craft → craft does not counter-rotate ✓

Net lift from both rotors: the MHD Lorentz body force in the Z-direction is determined by:
**F_z = ∫ (J_θ B_r − J_r B_θ) dV** (from J × B in cylindrical coordinates)

For rotor A (clockwise, J_θ > 0) with field B_r from coil above:
**F_z,A = ∫ J_θ,A B_r dV > 0** (upward)

For rotor B (anti-clockwise, J_θ < 0) with same field B_r:
**F_z,B = ∫ J_θ,B B_r dV < 0?**

The critical question is whether counter-spinning rotor B generates upward or downward force. If both rotors are energized by the same coil assembly with the same J_r direction (DC current sense unchanged), and the B field is unchanged, then:
- **f_θ(A)** from DC+MHD: clockwise (from J_r × B_z with Magnet 1)
- **f_θ(B)**: anti-clockwise (requires reversed DC polarity for rotor B)

With reversed DC for rotor B: J_r → −J_r → F_z,B = −F_z,A (downward) — this would cancel lift.

Resolution: the Brazil crop pictures apparently show a geometry where both rotors generate upward force despite counter-spinning. This may require the two rotors to have different orientations relative to the field source, or a more complex magnetic circuit. The precise mechanism is not fully resolved in the source material but is presented as the key finding from the Brazil 2024-2025 formations.
