The radial current density J_r (directed radially outward in the liquid metal) interacts with the axial magnetic field B_z (from the permanent magnet ring):

**F_θ = J_r × B_z**

where F_θ is the azimuthal (tangential) body force per unit volume. This is the Lorentz body force in an electrically conducting fluid, also known as the magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) body force:

**f = J × B**

where **J** is the current density vector (A/m²) and **B** is the magnetic flux density (T). The resulting **F_θ** drives azimuthal fluid flow → the liquid metal spins in the XY-plane.

- DC polarity reversed (red/outside, black/center) → spin in opposite direction (clockwise)
This is consistent with the reversal of **J**, which reverses **J × B**.

### Spinning Magnetic Top at Focal Point of Large Magnet Below

The hypothesis:
- A spinning magnetic top is positioned at the "focal point" of a large magnet below
- The spinning top magnet rises at this focal point
- The rising spinning magnet carries the large lower magnet upward

"Focal point" in the context of a magnetic field refers to the point of maximum field gradient (∂B/∂z), or alternatively the field null point or saddle point depending on magnet geometry. For an annular (ring) magnet with axis vertical, there is a region along the axis where the axial field gradient ∂B_z/∂z reverses sign — this is a natural trap point for paramagnetic or ferromagnetic objects under certain conditions.

The force on a magnetic dipole in a non-uniform field:
**F = ∇(m · B)**

For a magnetic dipole **m** aligned with a field **B(z)** along the z-axis:
**F_z = m ∂B_z/∂z**

At the focal point (field gradient maximum), F_z is maximized. A spinning magnetic top at this point experiences a gyroscopic stabilization of its dipole orientation, preventing the dipole from flipping — which would reverse F_z from attractive to repulsive.

The coupling to the large lower magnet is mechanical: the spinning top is physically part of the same assembly or mechanically connected to the large magnet, so its upward displacement translates directly to lift of the lower assembly.
