### MHD Body Force: XY-Plane Spin Generation

The fundamental driving equation for the liquid-metal vortex spin in the XY-plane:

**f = J × B**

Components in cylindrical coordinates (r, θ, z):
- **J** = J_r(r) r̂ (radial DC current density, A/m²)
- **B** = B_z(r,z) ẑ (axial field from permanent magnet ring, T)
- **f_θ** = J_r B_z (azimuthal body force density, N/m³)

The negative sign convention: **r̂ × ẑ = −θ̂**, so:
**f = J_r r̂ × B_z ẑ = J_r B_z (r̂ × ẑ) = −J_r B_z θ̂**

Direction: if J_r > 0 (current outward) and B_z > 0 (field upward), then f_θ < 0 (clockwise when viewed from above). Reversing J_r (flipping DC polarity) reverses the spin direction.

Steady-state azimuthal velocity (simplified, laminar flow, cylindrical annulus):
From Navier-Stokes in θ-direction with no-slip boundary conditions at r = r_inner and r = r_outer:

**η [d²u_θ/dr² + (1/r) du_θ/dr − u_θ/r²] = −J_r B_z**

This is a second-order ODE for u_θ(r). General solution (Couette-type with body force):
**u_θ(r) = A r + B/r + (J_r B_z / 4η) r (...)** (depending on radial variation of J_r and B_z)

The current density in the liquid metal (radially from center electrode at r = 0 to outer at r = R):
**J_r(r) = I / (2π r h)**

where I is the total current (A), h is the depth of the liquid metal layer (m), r is the radial position. This gives a 1/r fall-off of current density with radius.

Experimental values:
- I ≈ 50 A (limited by 0.14 Ω Nichrome resistance at 12 V)
- Without resistance: I = V/R_galinstan ≈ 12 V / (near zero Ω) → 200–400 A
- Power at 50 A, 12 V: P = VI = 600 W total
- Power in NiCr: P_NiCr = I²R = (50)²(0.14) = 350 W (dissipated in resistance wire)
- Power available for MHD: P_MHD ≈ 250 W (rough estimate)

### Three-Phase Rotating Magnetic Field: Mathematical Form

A balanced three-phase winding with phase currents:
**I_a(t) = I_0 cos(ωt)**
**I_b(t) = I_0 cos(ωt − 2π/3)**
**I_c(t) = I_0 cos(ωt − 4π/3)**

Produces a spatially rotating magnetic field in the plane of the coils:
**B_3ph(t) = B_0 [cos(ωt) x̂ + sin(ωt) ŷ]**

where B_0 = (3/2) μ₀ N I_0 / (2g) (proportional to the number of turns N, current amplitude I_0, and inversely proportional to the air gap g).

The angular frequency: **ω = 2πf** where f is the applied AC frequency (Hz).

At f = 30 Hz: ω = 60π rad/s ≈ 188.5 rad/s
At f = 40 Hz: ω = 80π rad/s ≈ 251.3 rad/s
At f = 20 Hz: ω = 40π rad/s ≈ 125.7 rad/s

Slip frequency relative to DC-driven fluid rotation (angular velocity Ω_fluid):
**ω_slip = ω_3ph − Ω_fluid**

Induction-motor analogy: the three-phase field induces eddy currents in the spinning conducting fluid at slip frequency ω_slip. The induced current density:
**J_induced = σ ω_slip B_0 r / 2** (approximate, for r << winding radius)

The resulting electromagnetic torque on the fluid:
**T_em = ∫∫∫ r (J_induced × B_0) dV ∝ σ B_0² ω_slip V_fluid**

where V_fluid is the volume of the conducting fluid.

### Standard Gyroscope Precession Equations

Gyroscope torque-precession relationship:
**τ = dL/dt = Ω_p × L**

where:
- **τ** = applied torque (N·m), here gravitational torque τ = m_b g r_b sin θ
- **L** = angular momentum = I_disc ω_spin ŝ (ŝ = spin axis unit vector)
- **Ω_p** = precession angular velocity (rad/s)

Precession rate (steady precession, θ = constant):
**|Ω_p| = τ / (|L| sin θ) = m_b g r_b / (I_disc ω_spin)**

Note: |Ω_p| is independent of θ in this approximation. As ω_spin → ∞, Ω_p → 0 (disc becomes "stiffer").

Direction rule: **Ω_p = (L × τ) / |L|² sin θ** — the precession angular velocity vector is perpendicular to both L and τ, consistent with right-hand rule.

For disc spinning toward observer (+x direction in standard orientation): L = +L x̂. Gravitational torque (from mass offset below pivot): τ = τ ŷ (horizontal). Then:
**Ω_p = (L x̂ × τ ŷ) / |L|² = τ/|L| ẑ** → precession about +z axis → anti-clockwise when viewed from above (consistent with Drew's observation).

For disc spinning away from observer: L = −L x̂. Then:
**Ω_p = (−L x̂ × τ ŷ) / L² = −τ/L ẑ** → precession about −z axis → clockwise when viewed from above (consistent with Drew's observation).

The directional dependence on spin sense is therefore derivable from standard Newtonian mechanics via the right-hand rule. However, the "rising to vertical" behavior when speed increases is the non-trivial claim requiring quantitative test.

Rise to vertical mathematics (energy analysis):
The effective potential for a gyroscope with one end fixed at the pivot, in the fast-spinning (precession-dominated) limit:

**V_eff(θ) = −m_b g r_b cos θ + L² / (2 I_disc (1 − cos θ) sin²θ) × ...** (approximation; exact form involves elliptic integrals for the full Euler-angle treatment)

As ω_spin → ∞ (|L| → ∞): the second term (centrifugal barrier) dominates for θ near 0, driving θ → 0 (vertical). The gyroscope rises toward vertical as spin increases, recovering the sleeping-top limit.

### Rotating Magnetic Dipole: Field Equations

A spinning magnetic dipole with moment:
**m(t) = m_0 [cos(ω_s t) x̂ + sin(ω_s t) ŷ]**

(ω_s = spin angular frequency of the magnet)

Near-field (quasi-static, r << c/ω_s):
**B(r,t) = (μ₀/4π) [3(m(t)·r̂)r̂ − m(t)] / r³**

This is the standard magnetic dipole field, rotating at angular frequency ω_s. The time-varying component radiates as a magnetic dipole:

Radiation power:
**P_rad = (μ₀ ω_s⁴ m_0²) / (12π c³)**

This is the classical rotating-dipole radiation formula. For a 40 mm permanent magnet with remnant magnetization B_r ≈ 1.2 T, volume V_mag:
**m_0 = B_r V_mag / μ₀**

For V_mag ≈ π(0.02)²(0.01) ≈ 1.26 × 10⁻⁵ m³:
**m_0 ≈ (1.2)(1.26 × 10⁻⁵) / (4π × 10⁻⁷) ≈ 12 A·m²**

At ω_s = 2π × 40 Hz = 251 rad/s:
**P_rad = (4π × 10⁻⁷)(251)⁴(12)²/(12π × 3 × 10⁸)³ ≈ negligible (classical radiation)**

The classical radiation power at 40 Hz is vanishingly small. The vacuum-interaction levitation claim requires a non-classical coupling mechanism.

### Focal Point Interaction: Force on Spinning Magnetic Dipole in Gradient Field

Force on a magnetic dipole in non-uniform field:
**F = ∇(m · B) = (m · ∇)B + m × (∇ × B)**

In free space (∇ × B = 0 outside source currents):
**F = (m · ∇)B**

For a dipole **m = m ẑ** in an axially symmetric field B_z(r=0, z) along the symmetry axis:
**F_z = m ∂B_z/∂z**

For attraction (F_z > 0 upward, m > 0): need ∂B_z/∂z > 0 (field increasing upward).
For repulsion (F_z < 0 downward): need ∂B_z/∂z < 0 (field decreasing upward).

At a focal point (field gradient maximum), ∂²B_z/∂z² = 0 (inflection point of gradient). The force is maximized here. Stability condition for levitation at this point:

**∂F_z/∂z = m ∂²B_z/∂z² = 0 (neutral at focal point)**

Earnshaw's theorem: static magnetic levitation of a permanent magnet by another magnet is unstable. The spinning gyroscopic stabilization (angular momentum preventing dipole flip) is the mechanism that circumvents Earnshaw's theorem, allowing a meta-stable or dynamically stable levitation.

Angular momentum stabilization condition: for the spin to prevent dipole flip, the precession frequency Ω_p must be less than the nutation frequency ω_n:

**ω_n = ω_s ≫ Ω_p = τ_magnetic / |L|**

where τ_magnetic is the torque that would try to flip the dipole (from Earnshaw instability), and |L| = I_mag ω_s.

### Newton's Third Law Torque Equation for Single Rotor

For a rotor with moment of inertia I_rotor spinning at angular velocity ω_rotor:
**L_rotor = I_rotor ω_rotor ẑ**

Conservation of angular momentum (isolated system):
**L_rotor + L_craft = L_total = constant**

If L_total = 0 (system starts at rest):
**L_craft = −L_rotor = −I_rotor ω_rotor ẑ**

The craft body rotates anti-clockwise with angular velocity:
**ω_craft = −(I_rotor/I_craft) ω_rotor**

This is the torque-reaction problem. To prevent craft counter-rotation:

Solution (counter-spin):
Rotor 1: L₁ = I₁ω₁ ẑ
Rotor 2: L₂ = −I₂ω₂ ẑ (counter-spin)
Condition: I₁ω₁ = I₂ω₂ → L_total = 0 → craft does not rotate

The net upward force from both rotors (in the Drew model):
**F_z(total) = F_z(rotor 1) + F_z(rotor 2)**

Both F_z contributions are in the same (+ẑ) direction if both rotors have the same orientation relative to the applied 3-phase field (the upward force is independent of the rotation sense — it depends on the current direction and field orientation, not on which way the rotor spins). This is the key claim: spin direction determines torque reaction, but the upward force direction is set by the external 3-phase field geometry.
