**Standard gyroscope physics** (Newtonian mechanics):

For a symmetric gyroscope of moment of inertia I_spin about its spin axis, spinning at angular velocity ω_s:

- Precession angular velocity: Ω_p = τ/L = Mgr/(I_spin · ω_s)

The gyroscope precesses (orbits the support point) at Ω_p. At any instant, the centripetal acceleration required to maintain the circular orbit is:
**a_c = Ω_p² · R** (where R is the orbital radius of the CM)
The centripetal force **F_c = M · Ω_p² · R** is provided by the pivot's normal force component. Standard Newtonian mechanics fully accounts for orbit stability.

Classical mechanics predicts this is entirely an artifact of the precession dynamics and the distribution of normal forces over the contact points — no true weight reduction occurs.
