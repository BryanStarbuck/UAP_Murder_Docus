### Newton's 3rd Law Cancellation — Fundamental Violation Claim

**Standard Newton's 3rd Law:**
For every action force F_AB (body A exerts on body B), there is an equal and opposite reaction force F_BA (body B exerts on body A).

For a closed system: Σ F_internal = 0. Net momentum of isolated system = constant.

**Drew's claim:**
The spinning liquid metal "cancels any downward force due to Newton's 3rd Law" when the central magnet rises.

**What this means physically:**
If the magnet exerts upward force F_z on itself (rises), and the liquid metal absorbs the downward reaction force:
- The magnet gains upward momentum
- The liquid metal gains downward momentum
- Net system momentum: 0 (conserved)

**This is actually consistent with Newton's 3rd Law!** The momentum is conserved — the reaction goes into the liquid metal, not the frame. This is analogous to a rocket: the rocket goes up, exhaust goes down, total momentum conserved.

**The question is whether the mechanism works as claimed:**
In a rocket, the exhaust is physically ejected (leaves the system). In Drew's device, the liquid metal stays in the container — it cannot indefinitely absorb downward linear momentum as angular momentum (these are conserved separately in standard mechanics).

For sustained upward motion (not just a one-time impulse), the liquid metal would need to continuously absorb downward linear momentum → its downward linear momentum would grow without bound → it would eventually crash into the container bottom.

**Genuinely beyond Newtonian mechanics:** If the device produces sustained net upward force on the entire system (magnet + frame + liquid metal together), then the total momentum of the system is changing → an external force must be acting (the ground/atmosphere/field) OR momentum conservation is violated at some level.

Drew's statement "not in any physics textbook on Earth (of this era)" acknowledges that this represents a departure from known physics.

### Non-Equivalence of Rotation Reference Frames in Electromagnetic Systems

**Drew's claim:** The lift effect is NOT relative — spinning the outer magnets produces an effect that cannot be replicated by adjusting the spin of the central magnet alone (even to achieve the same relative angular velocity).

**Implication for classical physics:**
In classical electrodynamics, two systems with the same relative motion should produce the same electromagnetic effects (within the reference frame). If observer A sees central magnet spinning at +50 Hz with stationary outer magnets, and observer B (rotating at +30 Hz) sees central magnet spinning at +20 Hz with outer magnets spinning at -30 Hz, the relative motion is different but within a single reference frame transformation.

However: if the ABSOLUTE spin (relative to the lab frame / inertial frame / gravitational field) matters, then reference frame equivalence is broken for this effect.

**Connection to Mach's Principle:**
Mach's Principle: the inertia of an object depends on its motion relative to the entire distribution of mass in the universe. Local experiments can detect absolute rotation relative to "distant stars" — this is why a gyroscope maintains its orientation in space (Foucault pendulum). Drew's claim that absolute rotation (not relative rotation) determines the effect is consistent with a Mach-principle-type coupling where the rotating magnet interacts with the gravitational field of the Earth (or cosmos) rather than just with the other magnets.

**Mathematical statement:**
If the effect depends on Ω_absolute (not Ω_relative = Ω_c - Ω_o), then:
F_z = f(Ω_c, Ω_o) where f(Ω_c, 0) = 0 but f(Ω_c, -Ω_o) ≠ 0 for Ω_o ≠ 0

This is a functional dependence that cannot be written as f(Ω_c - Ω_o) — i.e., it is not a function of relative velocity alone. This directly violates the Galilean relativity principle for rotational motion and is beyond classical mechanics.
