### Geodesic Equation and Spacetime Curvature Modification by Spin

**Standard GR geodesic:**
d²xᵘ/dτ² + Γᵘ_αβ (dxᵅ/dτ)(dx^β/dτ) = 0

where Γᵘ_αβ = (1/2) gᵘᵛ (∂_α g_βν + ∂_β g_αν - ∂_ν g_αβ) are the Christoffel symbols.

For a weak gravitational field (Newtonian limit), the dominant term gives:
Γⁱ_00 = -(1/2c²) ∂ᵢΦ_Newtonian, so d²xⁱ/dt² ≈ -∂ᵢΦ = g (downward acceleration)

**Lense-Thirring frame dragging (Kerr metric weak-field limit):**
Off-diagonal metric components for a rotating body with angular momentum J:
g_0i ≈ -(2G/c² r³)(J × r)_i

The gravitomagnetic field (analogue of B in electromagnetism):
B_grav = -(G/c²) [J/r³ - 3(J·r̂)r̂/r³]

**Precession rate of a gyroscope in this field:**
Ω_LT = (G/c²r³)[3(J·r̂)r̂ - J]   (Lense-Thirring precession)

For a lab-scale gyroscope (J_lab = I ω, I = (1/2)MR²):
|Ω_LT| = G J_lab / (c² r³) ≈ (6.67×10⁻¹¹ × I × ω) / (c² r³)

For M = 0.1 kg, R = 0.05 m, ω = 2π × 50 Hz:
I = 0.5 × 0.1 × 0.0025 = 1.25×10⁻⁴ kg·m²
J_lab = 1.25×10⁻⁴ × 314 = 0.039 kg·m²/s
|Ω_LT| ≈ (6.67×10⁻¹¹ × 0.039) / (9×10¹⁶ × r³) ≈ 2.9×10⁻²⁶ / r³

At r = 0.1 m: Ω_LT ≈ 2.9×10⁻²³ rad/s

This is 24 orders of magnitude too small to cancel gravitational acceleration (g ≈ 9.81 m/s²).

**Implication:** Drew's claim (fast-spinning disc cancels local gravity) is NOT explained by standard GR Lense-Thirring frame dragging for lab-scale devices. An enhancement by a factor of ~10²⁴ would be required — which would require new physics beyond GR.

Drew explicitly acknowledges: "Our 'physics' is so primitive compared with theirs" — suggesting the mechanism is not in the standard GR Lense-Thirring calculation but in an unknown extension of it.

### Gyroscopic Precession + 3-Phase Torque → Z-Axis Force

**Angular momentum of spinning disc:**
L = I ω_z ẑ  where I = (1/2)MR² (solid disc), ω_z = spin angular velocity

**Torque from 3-phase wobble field:**
τ_⊥ = μ × B_3phase = m B₀ sin(Ω_w t) (oscillating in XY-plane at wobble frequency Ω_w)

**Precession:**
dL/dt = τ_⊥
Ω_precession = τ_⊥ / |L| = m B₀ sin(Ω_w t) / (I ω_z)

The precession traces the disc axis through a cone angle θ_p = m B₀ / (I ω_z Ω_w) (for slow wobble relative to spin).

**Helical path of disc face normal during wobble:**
If the disc normal precesses at Ω_p while the disc itself spins at ω_z, the face normal traces a cone. The tangential velocity of the disc face has components both in XY (from spin) and in Z (from precession cone).

**Effective "propeller pitch" angle:**
α_pitch = arctan(v_z_precession / v_xy_spin) = arctan(R Ω_p / (R ω_z)) = arctan(Ω_p / ω_z)

For the disc to act as a propeller generating net Z force, the pitch angle must be nonzero:
F_z ~ (1/2) ρ v_tip² A C_T sin(2α_pitch)

where ρ is medium density, v_tip = R ω_z, A = π R² disc area, C_T is thrust coefficient.

### Newton's 3rd Law Cancellation via Counter-Rotating Liquid Metal

**The Newton's 3rd Law problem in levitation:**
If a device generates an upward force F_z on itself, by Newton's 3rd Law it must exert an equal downward force on something else. For a rocket, the "something else" is exhaust mass. For a closed system, there is no net momentum change — the center of mass cannot accelerate.

Drew's proposed solution:
- Primary system: 3-phase field + spinning magnet → upward force F_z on magnet
- Reaction: normally this would push down on the coil/frame by F_z (Newton 3rd)
- Liquid metal spinning via Lorentz force: this spinning conducting liquid has angular momentum L_liq = I_liq ω_liq
- Drew claims: the Lorentz-force-driven spin of the liquid metal provides a "reaction mass" that absorbs the Newton 3rd downward force

**Mathematical statement of the problem:**
For momentum conservation in a closed system:
d/dt(p_magnet + p_frame + p_liquidmetal) = 0

If F_z acts upward on the magnet: dp_magnet/dt = F_z ẑ
Then: dp_frame/dt + dp_liquidmetal/dt = -F_z ẑ

Drew's claim: dp_liquidmetal/dt = -F_z ẑ (liquid metal absorbs the reaction), so dp_frame/dt ≈ 0.

**How could liquid metal absorb a downward linear momentum?**
The liquid metal spins (angular momentum), but acquiring downward linear momentum while spinning would require a force with a vertical component on the liquid metal itself. This is not obviously provided by the Lorentz force in the XY-plane. The mechanism by which circular XY spin absorbs Z-axis linear momentum is the unresolved step in Drew's model.

Drew states this is "not in any physics textbook on Earth (of this era)" — acknowledging that the coupling between the liquid metal's XY angular momentum and the Z linear momentum of the frame is the non-standard claim.

### 9:12 Winding Ratio Analysis — Fractional Winding Factor

**Standard winding analysis:**
For Q slots and P poles in a 3-phase motor:
- Slots per pole per phase: q = Q/(3P) = 9/(3×12) = 9/36 = 0.25
- For integer q: standard concentrated winding → smooth rotating field
- For fractional q = 1/4: fractional-pitch winding → harmonic-rich MMF distribution

**Magnetomotive force (MMF) spatial harmonics:**
The stator MMF of a fractional winding produces space harmonics at:
ν = 6q_k ± 1 for k = 0, ±1, ±2, ...

For q = 1/4:
ν = ±1, ±5, ±7, ±11, ±13, ... AND sub-harmonics at ν = 1/4, 3/4, 5/4 ...

Sub-harmonic MMF components (ν < 1) produce pole-changing effects and non-synchronous torques that can drive the rotor at frequencies different from the fundamental drive frequency — including potentially in the reverse direction.

**Counter-rotation explanation attempt:**
The dominant sub-harmonic at ν = -1/4 (negative order = backward traveling wave) could drive the rotor counter to the main field. If the magnitude of this backward-traveling sub-harmonic is larger than the forward fundamental (due to the specific 9:12 geometry), the rotor would preferentially align with and follow the backward wave → counter-rotation.

### Beat Frequency Analysis of Counter-Spinning Magnets

Central magnet angular velocity: Ω_c = 2π × 50 rad/s (upward direction, +Z axis definition)
Outer magnets angular velocity: Ω_o = 2π × (30 to 40) rad/s (opposite direction, -Z)

**Interaction field:**
B_central(t) ~ cos(Ω_c t) (rotating dipole)
B_outer_k(t) ~ cos(-Ω_o t + 2πk/4) for k = 0,1,2,3

**Beat frequencies produced by central-outer interaction:**
ω_beat+ = Ω_c + Ω_o = 2π(50 + 35) = 2π × 85 Hz (sum frequency)
ω_beat- = Ω_c - Ω_o = 2π(50 - 35) = 2π × 15 Hz (difference frequency)

**Resonance condition:**
If either beat frequency matches the natural mechanical oscillation frequency of the device, amplified oscillatory forces would result. For vertical resonance (bounce mode) of the device mass m on a magnetic spring constant k_eff:
ω_resonance = √(k_eff/m)

If ω_beat- = ω_resonance → parametric resonance → exponentially growing amplitude of vertical oscillation.

**Significance of non-relative effect:**
Drew's claim that spinning the central magnet at 50 Hz with stationary outer magnets produces "nothing unusual" means:
B_interaction(Ω_c, 0) produces no anomalous vertical force.
But B_interaction(Ω_c, -Ω_o) DOES produce an anomalous force (if Drew's hypothesis is confirmed).

This mathematical difference: the beat frequency ω_beat = Ω_c + Ω_o only exists when BOTH systems spin (counter to each other). With stationary outer magnets, Ω_o = 0, and the beat frequency = Ω_c alone — which produces no vertical effect. This is consistent with the effect being mediated by the beat frequency (Ω_c + Ω_o), not by Ω_c alone.

**Specific prediction:** The anomalous vertical force should appear at the beat frequency ω_beat = Ω_c + Ω_o, and its magnitude should vary with both Ω_c and Ω_o, maximizing when ω_beat matches some resonance condition.
