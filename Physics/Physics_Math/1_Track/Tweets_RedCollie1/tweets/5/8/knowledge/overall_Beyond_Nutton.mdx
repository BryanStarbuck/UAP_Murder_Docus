```

```
For every force exerted by body 1 on body 2, there is an equal and opposite reaction force on body 1.


A closed electromagnetic system cannot change its center-of-mass momentum without interaction with external fields or objects (conservation of momentum in a closed system).

**Classical assumption**: A magnetic field exerts force on charged particles and conductors according to F = qv × B (Lorentz) and F = I L × B (on conductors). For a uniform field, forces on a closed loop cancel (net force = 0 in uniform field).

**Beyond-uniform-field claim**: Drew's reference to a "focal point" implies a non-uniform rotating magnetic field with a gradient. In a non-uniform field, a conducting loop (or eddy-current-carrying disk) experiences a net force along the field gradient:
```
F = ∇(m · B) = ∇((magnetic moment of eddy currents) · B)
```
For a conducting loop of magnetic moment m = I A (I = eddy current, A = loop area) in a field with gradient ∂B/∂z:
```
F_z = m ∂B/∂z
```
If the rotating magnetic field is arranged so that its amplitude has a strong gradient along ẑ near the "focal point" below the conducting disc (stronger near the center/source, weaker farther away), then the disc is pulled toward the focal point if m·B > 0, or repelled if m·B < 0 (Lenz's law: induced m opposes B → repulsion).

**This gives Z-axis lift without requiring rotation in the Z-axis direction** — the field gradient along ẑ does the work. The rotating field in the XY-plane creates the eddy currents; the field gradient along ẑ creates the lift force on those currents.
