### Liquid-Metal Vortex, Corbino Disc, and Homopolar Motor as Magnetic Drive Mechanisms

Three 2012 England crop pictures are interpreted as describing a "liquid-metal vortex," also described as a Corbino disc or homopolar motor. Drew explicitly identifies these as crop-circle-encoded presentations of a "puzzle in magnetism."

Corbino disc physics:
A Corbino disc is a circular conducting disc with a central axial magnetic field B (parallel to the disc axis, i.e., in the Z-direction) and radial current flow J_r. The Lorentz force on the radial current is:

F = J × B

With J in the r-direction (radial, XY-plane) and B in the z-direction:
F_phi = J_r * B_z

This azimuthal (tangential) force drives the disc to spin in the XY-plane. In the liquid-metal implementation, the conducting "disc" is a liquid metal (e.g., mercury, gallium, or another liquid conductor) contained in a disc-shaped vessel. The spinning liquid metal generates its own secondary magnetic field through magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) self-induction:

nabla × B = mu_0 * J + mu_0 * epsilon_0 * (partial E / partial t)

In the MHD steady-flow limit, the induced secondary field B_induced ~ mu_0 * sigma * v × B_applied, where sigma is the electrical conductivity of the liquid metal and v is the bulk flow velocity.

Homopolar motor classical formula: For a disc of radius R, axial field B_z, and radial current I, the torque is:
tau = (1/2) * I * B_z * R^2

Drew's interpretation: The liquid-metal vortex in a Corbino/homopolar configuration is a component (or the primary drive mechanism) of UFO propulsion. The crop pictures from 2012 that show spinning cube motifs are claimed to represent this same device geometry.

### Spin and Counter-Spin in Magnetic Levitation — From Hoverboards to UFOs

Hoverboard levitation mechanism (classical, for context):
A hoverboard uses counter-rotating permanent magnet arrays (Halbach arrays) spinning over a conducting track. The rotating Halbach array produces a time-varying magnetic flux through the conducting track, inducing eddy currents J_eddy in the track. By Lenz's law, these induced currents create a magnetic field opposing the change that caused them — producing a repulsive force that levitates the board.

The levitation force per unit area scales as:
F_lev ~ (B^2 * sigma * v * delta) / 2

where B is the peak magnetic field of the Halbach array, sigma is the electrical conductivity of the track, v is the relative velocity of the spinning array, and delta is the skin depth (delta = sqrt(2 / (omega * mu * sigma))).

Drew explicitly invokes this as a reference system for understanding UFO spin/counter-spin levitation, but extends the principle: in the UFO case, no conducting track is required — the interaction is with the quantum vacuum rather than with an external conductor.

### Spark-Gap Tesla Coils — Evaluated and Rejected for UFO Propulsion

Drew states: "spark-gap Tesla coils are pretty cool, but I doubt whether they can be used for UFO propulsion."

Tesla coil physics: A spark-gap Tesla coil is a resonant transformer operating at high Q, producing high-voltage AC at radio frequencies (typically 50 kHz–500 kHz). The primary circuit is: V_primary / V_secondary = N_primary / N_secondary, with resonance condition L * C = 1/omega^2. The demonstration shown (wireless power at ~50 kHz lighting a fluorescent tube nearby) illustrates near-field inductive energy transfer, not propulsive force.

Drew's rejection of Tesla coils as UFO propulsion: the near-field inductive coupling produces no net mechanical force in the far-field and cannot produce Z-axis displacement. This is a pedagogical elimination — Tesla coil EM is interesting (wireless power at ~50 kHz is demonstrable and real), but it is not the magnetic mechanism responsible for UFO lift.

The video demonstrates: "wireless power around 50 kHz lights up a long, fluorescent bulb nearby" — this is conventional near-field inductive coupling, well-described by classical electrodynamics.
