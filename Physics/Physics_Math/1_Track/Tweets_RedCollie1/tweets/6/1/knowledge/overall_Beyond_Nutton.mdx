### Non-Newtonian Forces in Spinning Lenz Levitation — Violation of Classical Electromagnetic Force Laws

**The claim:** "AC Lenz levitation shows non-Newtonian forces, when its disc conductor is given rapid spin." (Drew, confirmed experimentally)

**What classical theory predicts:**
AC Lenz levitation of a disc: the levitation force depends on disc conductivity, thickness, AC frequency, and field amplitude. It does NOT depend on disc rotation in classical electrodynamics, because the eddy current generation is determined by the time-derivative of the magnetic flux through the disc (Faraday's Law), and the disc rotation does not alter the rate of change of flux from a uniform external AC field (since a uniformly conducting, homogeneous spinning disc has the same average conductance in all directions).

**The anomaly:** Adding disc spin changes the levitation force — a result classical EM theory does not predict. The force is "non-Newtonian," implying it exceeds, supplements, or transforms the classical Lenz force in a direction or magnitude that violates classical predictions.

**Possible beyond-classical mechanisms:**
1. **Spin-orbit coupling analogy:** In quantum mechanics, spin-orbit coupling produces forces (fine structure, precession effects) that have no classical analogue. If conduction electrons in the spinning disc experience an analogous coupling between their spin and the disc's orbital angular momentum, this could produce anomalous electromagnetic forces.
2. **Frame-dragging at low ω (Einstein-Lense-Thirring):** A rapidly rotating conducting mass creates gravitomagnetic effects (Lense-Thirring effect). The gravitomagnetic field B_gm of the rotating disc:
   B_gm ~ (2G × J) / (c² × r³)
   where J = I × ω is the disc's angular momentum. For a 10-kg disc, r = 0.1 m, ω = 100π rad/s:
   B_gm ~ (2 × 6.67×10⁻¹¹ × 10 × π × 100 × 10⁻³) / (9×10¹⁶ × 10⁻³) ~ 10⁻²¹ T
   This is completely negligible. Frame-dragging cannot explain the observed non-Newtonian force at laboratory scales.
3. **Magneto-rotational instability (MRI):** In rotating conducting fluids in magnetic fields, the Balbus-Hawley MRI provides anomalous angular momentum transport. For solid conductors, no analogous instability is known, but this could be a direction for investigation.
4. **Non-classical inertial effect:** If the spinning disc's inertial frame is locally modified by its rotation (Mach's Principle), the eddy currents generated in the non-inertial frame differ from those predicted in the inertial lab frame — producing an anomalous force.
