### Spinning Lenz Levitation — AC Lenz Force Modified by Disc Rotation

**Classical AC Lenz levitation:**
A conducting disc placed above an AC electromagnet experiences a repulsive levitation force due to Lenz's Law eddy current interaction. The time-averaged levitation force for a disc of:
- Conductivity σ
- Thickness δ  
- Radius r
- AC field frequency f
- Field amplitude B₀

is approximately:
  F_lev ~ σ × δ × f² × B₀² × r⁴ × (geometric factor)

This is a Z-axis (vertical) force: the disc is pushed upward (away from the electromagnet).

**Anomalous result with spinning disc:**
When the disc is given rapid rotation ω about the Z-axis simultaneously with AC Lenz levitation, the levitation force acquires non-Newtonian character. Drew's experimental finding: "AC Lenz levitation shows non-Newtonian forces, when its disc conductor is given rapid spin."

**Proposed sources of non-Newtonian modification:**

1. **Coriolis force on eddy currents:**
In the rotating disc frame, each eddy current element J_eddy experiences:
  F_Cor = -2m × (Ω × v_eddy)
where Ω = ω ẑ (disc spin angular velocity) and v_eddy is the eddy current drift velocity. This Coriolis acceleration curves the eddy current paths, changing their geometry and thus their coupling to the applied AC field. The modified eddy current distribution produces a modified levitation force pattern.

2. **Change in effective resistance of eddy current paths:**
The Coriolis-curved eddy current paths have longer effective path lengths, increasing their resistance. Higher resistance → lower eddy current amplitude → lower (normal) Lenz repulsion force. But the spatial redistribution of the force may create new force components.

3. **Gyromagnetic interaction:**
Spinning conducting loops carrying current (eddy currents) have both mechanical angular momentum and magnetic moment. The interaction energy between the magnetic moment and the applied field, modified by the gyromagnetic ratio of the conduction electrons, could produce additional force terms not present in the non-rotating case.

**5-Phase + Single-Phase Configuration for Spinning Lenz Levitation:**
- 5-phase rotating field (XY-plane): drives disc spin at ω
- Single-phase AC field (Z-axis): provides the Lenz levitation force
- Interaction: the Z-axis oscillating field induces eddy currents that are then subject to Coriolis modification from the disc spin driven by the XY-plane 5-phase field

The 5-phase geometry (72° coil spacing) is chosen because it provides a more uniform rotating field than 3-phase (better approximation of a pure circular rotating field), which may be necessary for stable spinning Lenz levitation without wobble.

However, the subjective sensation of lightness would be felt in the body while the scale would show reduced reading — not "no change."
