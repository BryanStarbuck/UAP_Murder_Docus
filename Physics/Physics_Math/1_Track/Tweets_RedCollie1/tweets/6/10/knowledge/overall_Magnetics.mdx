### Physical Content
Governing equation: Lorentz body force density in a conducting fluid is
f = J x B
where:
J = current density (A/m^2), directed radially from inner wire (anode) to outer wire (cathode), or vice versa
B = magnetic flux density (T) from the permanent magnet below, directed upward (+z)

For J in the radial (+r) direction and B in the axial (+z) direction:
f = J x B is in the azimuthal (phi) direction

Specifically: J_r * B_z hat_phi — giving a circumferential (swirling) body force, creating the observed vortex rotation.

The direction (clockwise vs. anticlockwise) is determined by:
(a) Sign of J (which wire is + and which is -): reversing DC polarity reverses J_r, reversing the swirl direction
(b) Sign of B (N vs. S pole upward): reversing the magnet polarity reverses B_z, reversing the swirl direction

## 7. Homopolar Motor Reference (Faraday Disc)

From preview description (13_1922666128960356352_preview.txt):
"Homopolar motor/generator (Faraday disc) demonstration. Roobert33 device: inverted stainless steel bowl on a wooden board with two copper bus bar connections."

The Faraday disc (homopolar motor) is the classical example of continuous DC rotation from a static magnetic field without commutation. In a homopolar motor:
- Current flows radially through a conducting disc
- Static axial magnetic field B provides the force: F = q(v x B) acting on charge carriers moving radially, producing an azimuthal (tangential) force
- The disc spins continuously without the polarity switching required in conventional DC motors

This is the classical antecedent to the galinstan liquid-metal vortex experiment — replacing the solid conducting disc with a liquid metal allows the same J x B azimuthal body force to drive fluid rotation rather than solid rotation.
