## 1. Lorentz Force Body Density — Governing Equation of Liquid Metal Vortex

f = J x B  [N/m^3]

Components in cylindrical coordinates (r, phi, z) for the galinstan vortex experiment:
- J = J_r * r_hat  (radial current from inner to outer electrode)
- B = B_z * z_hat  (axial field from permanent magnet below)
- f = J_r * r_hat x B_z * z_hat = J_r * B_z * (r_hat x z_hat) = -J_r * B_z * phi_hat

Result: pure azimuthal (swirling) body force. Sign determines CW vs CCW rotation.

Reversals:
- J_r -> -J_r (swap DC polarity): f_phi reverses
- B_z -> -B_z (flip magnet): f_phi reverses
- Both reversed: f_phi unchanged (double negation)

Confirmed experimentally (transcription 13_1966038196808200193).

## 3. Eddy Current Braking (Lenz's Law) — Spinning Disc Above Magnet

For a conducting disc (radius R, thickness t, conductivity sigma_disc) rotating at angular velocity omega above a permanent magnet of field B_z:

Induced EMF in an annular ring at radius r, width dr:
d(epsilon) = B_z * omega * r * dr  [V/m path]

Eddy current density in thin disc approximation:
j_eddy(r) ~ sigma_disc * B_z * omega * r / 2  [A/m^2]

Ohmic dissipation per unit volume:
p = j_eddy^2 / sigma_disc = (sigma_disc * B_z^2 * omega^2 * r^2) / 4  [W/m^3]

Total braking torque (integrated over disc area, approximate):
tau_brake ~ -pi * sigma_disc * t * B_z^2 * omega * R^4 / 8  [N*m]

This is the standard Lenz's law braking result: torque proportional to omega, B^2, and disc conductivity/geometry.

## 4. Faraday Disc (Homopolar Motor) — Reference Equations

Torque on homopolar motor:
tau = B * I * (R_outer^2 - R_inner^2) / 2  [N*m]

where B = axial magnetic field (T), I = radial current (A), R_outer and R_inner = disc outer and inner radii (m)

EMF generated (generator mode):
EMF = B * omega * (R_outer^2 - R_inner^2) / 2  [V]

Power:
P_mechanical = tau * omega = EMF * I  [W]

## 5. Counter-Rotating Flywheel Angular Momentum Balance

For a system with outer flywheel (moment of inertia I_outer, angular velocity omega_outer, direction -z_hat) and inner rotor (moment of inertia I_inner, angular velocity omega_inner, direction +z_hat):

L_outer = -I_outer * omega_outer * z_hat
L_inner = +I_inner * omega_inner * z_hat
L_total = (I_inner * omega_inner - I_outer * omega_outer) * z_hat

For angular momentum cancellation: I_inner * omega_inner = I_outer * omega_outer

For a disc: I = (1/2) * m * R^2

From transcription (13_2020784923054112768_video.mdx):
Observed spin frequency: f = 40-50 Hz
omega = 2*pi*f = 251-314 rad/s

If the outer flywheel has mass m_o, radius R_o and the inner has mass m_i, radius R_i:
Balance condition: (1/2)*m_i*R_i^2*omega_i = (1/2)*m_o*R_o^2*omega_o
-> m_i*R_i^2*omega_i = m_o*R_o^2*omega_o

The net gyroscopic torque on the apparatus from an external rotation Omega:
tau_gyro = L_total x Omega = (L_net) * Omega * sin(theta)
If L_total = 0, tau_gyro = 0 — no gyroscopic precession coupling, regardless of external rotation.

## 6. Stator Field Spatial Harmonics — Classical Explanation Candidate for Counter-Rotation

A 3-phase, 6-coil stator wound for p=1 pole pair produces a fundamental rotating field at frequency f rotating in the +phi direction. The field also contains spatial harmonics of order n = 6k +/- 1 for k = 1, 2, 3, ...

For k=1: 5th harmonic (n=5) rotates at -f/5 (backward, CW for CCW fundamental), 7th harmonic (n=7) rotates at +f/7 (forward)

The 5th harmonic rotating field can drive a conducting rotor (outer ring) in the CW direction while the fundamental drives the inner rotor CCW. The slip of each rotor element relative to its driving harmonic determines the operating speed.

Slip for fundamental drive of inner rotor:
s_1 = (omega_sync_1 - omega_rotor) / omega_sync_1
where omega_sync_1 = 2*pi*f (for p=1)

Slip for 5th harmonic drive of outer element:
s_5 = (omega_sync_5 - omega_outer) / omega_sync_5
where omega_sync_5 = -2*pi*f/5 (backward, so magnitude -omega/5)

This is the standard harmonic analysis taught in electrical machine theory. The experimenter claims this analysis does not explain the observed counter-rotation, stating it "violates EVERYTHING they teach in school."

## 8. Water Free Surface Magnetic Pressure

For a diamagnetic fluid (water, chi_m ~ -9 x 10^-6) in a non-uniform magnetic field:

Magnetic pressure on free surface:
Delta_P = (mu_0/2) * (H_out^2 - H_in^2)  [Pa]

Where H_out and H_in are the field strengths just outside and inside the fluid at the surface.

For a downward-pointing field gradient (field stronger at surface than in bulk):
Delta_P < 0 (for diamagnetic fluid) → surface depresses inward (downward inverted arch), consistent with observed image (preview 13_1913946853445619712_preview.txt).

Surface depression depth (approximate):
h = Delta_P / (rho*g) ~ (mu_0 * chi_m * H^2) / (2 * rho * g)

For H ~ 10^4 A/m (0.01 T, weak field) near the surface:
h ~ (4*pi*10^-7 * 9*10^-6 * 10^8) / (2 * 1000 * 9.8)
  ~ (4*pi*10^-7 * 9*10^2) / (1.96*10^4)
  ~ (1.13*10^-3) / (1.96*10^4)
  ~ 5.8 x 10^-8 m

This would be invisible — the observed clear depression implies either a much stronger field near the surface or a ferrofluid-like enhancement. The submerged metallic object may be creating local field enhancement.

## 10. Galinstan Composition and Material Parameters

- Composition by mass: Ga ~68.5%, In ~21.5%, Sn ~10%
- Density: rho = 6440 kg/m^3 (at 20 C)
- Electrical conductivity: sigma = 3.46 x 10^6 S/m
- Dynamic viscosity: eta ~ 1.37 x 10^-3 Pa*s (water-like, low viscosity)
- Surface tension: gamma ~ 718 mN/m (much higher than water at 72 mN/m)
- Toxicity: non-toxic (safe substitute for mercury)

Magnetic Reynolds number (relevant for MHD induction effects):
Rm = mu_0 * sigma * L * v
where L = characteristic length scale, v = characteristic velocity

For L ~ 0.05 m, v ~ 0.1 m/s (estimated vortex rim speed):
Rm = (4*pi*10^-7) * (3.46*10^6) * 0.05 * 0.1 = (4*pi*10^-7) * 1.73*10^4 = 0.022

Rm << 1: inductive effects are negligible; the Lorentz force is dominated by the externally applied DC field, not self-generated MHD fields. This is the low-Rm MHD regime (standard for liquid metals in most laboratory experiments).
