## 1. Lorentz Body Force in Liquid Metal — Classical Foundation

### Physical Law Applied
f_body = J x B  [N/m^3]

where J = current density (A/m^2), B = magnetic flux density (T).

In the galinstan vortex experiment (transcription 13_1966038196808200193_video.mdx):
- Geometry: current flows radially (J = J_r r_hat) between inner and outer electrodes immersed in the liquid metal
- Static field: permanent magnet below provides B = B_z z_hat (upward)
- Resulting force: f = J_r r_hat x B_z z_hat = J_r B_z (r_hat x z_hat) = -J_r B_z phi_hat

The negative phi_hat (azimuthal) component drives the fluid in the clockwise or anticlockwise direction depending on sign of J_r and B_z.

When J_r > 0 and B_z > 0: f_phi < 0 → clockwise rotation (viewed from above)
When J_r reversed (J_r < 0): f_phi > 0 → anticlockwise
When B_z reversed: same reversal

## 2. Eddy Currents and Lenz's Law — Spinning Conductor Above Permanent Magnet

### Setup (from preview 13_1913946810210746368_preview.txt)
- Spinning copper-colored disc above a stationary neodymium cube magnet (North pole upward)
- Tool: rotary Dremel with spinning disc element
- Configuration: rotating conductor above static field source

### Physical Laws Applied
When a conducting disc rotates above a stationary permanent magnet:
- The time-varying flux through any area element dA in the conductor: Phi(t) = B_z * dA * cos(theta(t))
- Induced EMF in a conducting annular element: epsilon = -d(Phi)/dt = B_z * dA * omega * sin(omega t)
- Induced eddy current density: j_eddy ~ sigma * epsilon / (2*pi*r)
- Braking torque from Lenz's law: tau = -k * omega * B_z^2 (proportional to angular speed and field squared)

### Claimed Non-Classical Effect
The experimenter claims that under specific geometrical conditions, the spinning element above the permanent magnet is "pulled in" (net inward force) rather than the expected braking behavior. This would imply:
- A net radial (centripetal, -r_hat) force in addition to the tangential braking force
- Possibly arising from the spatial gradient of B: F_z = m * (dB_z/dz) for a magnetic dipole in a field gradient (Stern-Gerlach type), but this applies to a magnetic object being attracted to or repelled from the magnet, not a spinning conductor
- For an induced-dipole (paramagnetic or eddy-current induced): F_r depends on the radial gradient of B in the horizontal plane, which is non-zero at the edge of the magnet pole

### Classical Prediction
In standard induction motor theory:
- A conducting element inside the rotating field rotates in the direction of the field (motor action, positive slip)
- A conducting element outside the stator field experiences mainly fringe/leakage flux, which is weaker and has reversed spatial harmonic content relative to the interior field
- The outer conductor could rotate in the opposite direction if driven by a spatial harmonic of the field that is reverse-rotating relative to the fundamental

Classical explanation possibility: In a 6-coil arrangement with 3-phase excitation, the field contains spatial harmonics at 5th and 7th order. The 5th harmonic rotates in the reverse direction (backward-rotating component). An outer conducting ring might be driven by the 5th harmonic (CW for a CCW fundamental field), while the inner rotor is driven by the fundamental (CCW).

The experimenter asserts this result "violates EVERYTHING they teach in school and peer-reviewed journals" — i.e., he treats it as a result not explainable by harmonic analysis. ## 4. Gravity-Parallel Spin — Claimed Coupling Between Rotation and Gravity

### Source
Video transcription (13_2014075285935890433_video.mdx):
"This is spinning the magnet parallel to Earth's gravity field... you can see some sort of distortion whose face takes place... This is parallel to the downward force of gravity."

### Physical Context
Spinning a permanent magnet with its rotation axis parallel to g (z-axis) produces a time-dependent field in the horizontal (xy) plane. The "distortion" observed could be:

(a) Mechanical precession: if the spinning magnet is also subject to a torque (from an adjacent field), the rotation axis precesses — this is purely Newtonian gyroscopic mechanics:
    tau = dL/dt, where L = I * omega (angular momentum), and tau = r x F

(b) Gravitomagnetic analogy: in general relativity, a spinning mass generates a gravitomagnetic field B_g (analogous to magnetic field from charge rotation):
    B_g = G * J / (c^2 * r^3) (far field approximation)
    where J = angular momentum of spinning mass
    This is the Lense-Thirring effect (frame dragging). For laboratory masses spinning at achievable RPM, B_g is unmeasurably small.

(c) Claimed new coupling: the experimenter appears to suggest that the alignment of spin axis with g creates a coupling between the magnetic rotation and gravity — analogous to how spinning a charged body creates a magnetic moment, but for gravity (i.e., a gravitomagnetic moment from mass current). This is speculative and unconfirmed.

## 5. Astronomical Jets as Analogue

Tweet (2025-04-20, id 1913947949807378671):
"How do astronomical 'jets' work?"

Physical context: astrophysical jets (e.g., from AGN, quasars, and young stellar objects) are collimated bipolar outflows extending over kiloparsec scales. The standard model involves:
- A rotating accretion disc with a magnetic field B threading it
- The Blandford-Znajek mechanism (for black hole jets): extraction of rotational energy via frame-dragging of the ergosphere field, producing a Poynting flux along the rotation axis
- The Blandford-Payne mechanism (for disc jets): centrifugal acceleration of plasma along inclined field lines above the disc

The proposed connection: the "spinning magnetic vortex" in the lab that pulls in ionized air is proposed as a small-scale analogue of the accretion disc magnetic field structure that produces and collimates astrophysical jets. The inward pull in the lab (centripetal force on ionized gas) is proposed as analogous to the accretion flow, and the axial escape (jet) corresponds to the Z-axis thrust.

## 9. Spin Angular Momentum — Gyroscopic Stability and Propulsion

From multiple tweets and video content, the consistent physical concept is:
L = I * omega
where L = angular momentum (kg*m^2/s), I = moment of inertia (kg*m^2), omega = angular velocity (rad/s)

For counter-rotating elements:
L_total = L_outer + L_inner = I_outer * omega_outer * (-z_hat) + I_inner * omega_inner * (z_hat)

If I_outer * omega_outer = I_inner * omega_inner, then L_total = 0 — no net angular momentum, no gyroscopic coupling to external frame, but claimed net Z-force from the field interaction.

The 40-50 Hz rotation rate cited for the flywheel gives:
omega = 2*pi*f = 2*pi*(45 Hz) ~ 283 rad/s
L = I * 283 (units: kg*m^2*s^-1)

For a disc flywheel of mass m and radius R: I = (1/2)m*R^2

The ratio of outer to inner moment of inertia determines whether angular momentum cancels, and this is presented as a tunable design parameter for "anti-gravity" effect optimization.
