- Superconductors (Meissner effect — perfect diamagnetism)
- Mechanical constraint

```

This is the same physics as optical trapping (laser tweezers) and magneto-optic traps (used in ultracold atom physics), extended to macroscopic permanent magnets.

**Beyond Newtonian in what sense:**
The result (levitation) is predicted by Maxwell's electrodynamics + Newtonian mechanics (no departure from standard physics). However, it lies beyond the regime where standard Newtonian intuition applies, because the stabilization is dynamic (requires rotation) rather than static (requires fixed force balance). An observer analyzing the system with Newtonian intuition and Earnshaw's theorem (applicable to static fields) would incorrectly predict that the levitation is impossible.

This stiffness is absent in the static analysis and appears only through the time-dependent rotating field.

### Laithwaite Gyroscope / Drew Experiment: Claimed Weight Reduction

**Claim (empirical):** A spinning gyroscope balanced about its center of mass at a pivot point appears lighter than its measured weight, or "transfers its mass center to the pivot" in a way that changes the apparent gravitational loading.

**Newtonian prediction:**
For a gyroscope of mass M in gravitational field g, the total weight W = Mg, regardless of spin rate. Gyroscopic spin distributes the gravitational reaction forces differently across the support structure (precession changes the time-averaged force at the pivot), but the total vertical force must still equal Mg in the static limit (Newton's third law).

**Laithwaite's empirical claim (1974 Royal Institution Lecture):**
Eric Laithwaite, inventor of the linear induction motor, demonstrated that large, rapidly spinning gyroscopes appeared to violate the expected weight when he lifted them overhead and allowed them to precess — claiming they "felt" much lighter than their gravitational weight and that a small child could lift a large gyroscope that would otherwise be too heavy. Mainstream physicists attributed this to the dynamic redistribution of forces during precession (the lever arm effect, effective reduction of required lift force due to the gyroscopic moment assisting the lift), not to actual weight reduction.

**Drew's experimental chain of reasoning (from tweet + video):**

Step 1: Balance the gyroscope about its center of mass at a pivot point. With no spin, the gyroscope tips (as demonstrated in 13_1956329124328611840_video.mdx).

Step 2: With spin activated, the gyroscope remains balanced at the pivot — the precession dynamics "transfer the center of mass to the pivot" in the sense that the gyroscopic precession forces keep the object horizontal without tipping.

Step 3: Drew claims this constitutes "making the body appear lighter" — because the object no longer falls even when supported only at a single point that is not typically the center of mass.

**Beyond-Newtonian interpretation (Drew's):**
If the gyroscope's spin generates a genuine reduction in effective gravitational coupling (not just dynamic force redistribution), this would imply a modification of the equivalence principle: the spin angular momentum L creates a coupling to spacetime curvature that partially shields the gyroscope from the gravitational field. This has been proposed in the framework of gravitomagnetic effects (Lense-Thirring effect for spinning masses in GR) but at macroscopic spin rates, the Lense-Thirring correction is negligibly small:
```

The claimed magnitude of the effect (perceptible lifting force reduction) is orders of magnitude larger than what GR predicts. If genuine, it would require new physics beyond GR.

**Drake's actual claim (conservative version):**

The subjective sensation of apparent lightness (reduced lifting force required due to dynamic gyroscopic assistance) may be the entire effect, consistent with standard mechanics.
