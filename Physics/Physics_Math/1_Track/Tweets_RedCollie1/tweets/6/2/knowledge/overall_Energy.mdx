### 500 kHz Tesla Coil as Novel Energy Coupling Mechanism

Drew's "ion rocket" experiment demonstrates an energy input modality not recognized by mainstream propulsion physics: high-frequency (500 kHz) RF energy from a solid-state Tesla coil is coupled directly into a noble gas (argon), ionizing it and then transferring mechanical impulse via Lenz repulsion.

**Energy conversion chain:**
```
Electrical energy (60 W at 500 kHz) → Ionization energy (argon plasma) → Lenz force × displacement → Kinetic energy of expelled plasma
```

Each step has an efficiency:
1. Electrical → RF radiation: η_RF (Tesla coil efficiency, typically 70–90%)
2. RF → Ionization: η_ion (dependent on argon pressure, coil geometry, frequency)
3. RF → Lenz force: η_Lenz = work done against Lenz force / total RF power

The total specific impulse (I_sp) and thrust depend on the product of these efficiencies.

**Key energy anomaly reported:**
The frequency-dependent nature of the energy coupling (works at 500 kHz, fails at 50 Hz) is not an energy anomaly per se (no violation of energy conservation), but the specific mechanism — Lenz repulsion of ionized noble gas at RF frequencies — is Drew's claimed novel discovery. The energy source is conventional electrical power; the novelty is the conversion pathway.

**Comparison to conventional ion thrusters:**
Standard Hall effect ion thrusters achieve specific impulse I_sp ≈ 1000–3000 s with thrust efficiencies of 50–70%. Drew's system operates at atmospheric pressure (vs. vacuum for conventional ion thrusters) and uses inductive ionization rather than electrostatic acceleration. No quantitative I_sp measurements are reported for Drew's device.

The combined Lorentz spin + 3-phase uplift system requires continuous power input to maintain:
1. Rotor rotation (losses from bearing friction, eddy currents in rotor, air resistance)

**Power balance for Lorentz motor:**
Mechanical power output of homopolar motor:
```
P_mech = τ_motor × ω = (ω² B_z² R⁴) / (4 R_ext)
```
Power drawn from electrical source:
```
P_electric = I² R_ext = (ω B_z R²)² / (4 R_ext)
```
Homopolar motor efficiency: η = P_mech / P_electric = 1 (ideal case, no losses). In practice, losses reduce efficiency.

**Energy storage in spinning rotor:**
```
KE_rotor = (1/2) I_rotor ω²
```
For a 20 cm diameter, 1 kg neodymium disk spinning at 10,000 RPM (ω ≈ 1047 rad/s):
```
I_rotor = (1/2)(1)(0.1)² = 0.005 kg·m²
KE = (1/2)(0.005)(1047)² ≈ 2742 J ≈ 2.7 kJ
```
This stored rotational kinetic energy is available as a flywheel reservoir — the spinning rotor stores energy that can be drawn upon during transient thrust demands.
