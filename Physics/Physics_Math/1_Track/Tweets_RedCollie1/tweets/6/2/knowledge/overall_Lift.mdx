### 3-Phase Stator Field → Z-Axis Lift Mechanism

Drew's phrase "3-phase uplift" designates the generation of net upward Z-axis force by the rotating magnetic field of a 3-phase electromagnetic system.

**Mechanism derivation:**

For a 3-phase stator coil system with coils in the XY-plane at z = 0, the axial field B_z above the stator (z > 0) has the form:
```
B_z(r, z, t) = B_z0(r, z) cos(ωt − n φ)
```
where n is the number of pole pairs and φ is the azimuthal angle. For a spinning rotor (magnetic dipole m_rotor at height z = h) with angular velocity ω (locked to the stator field):
```
<F_z> = m_rotor_z (∂B_z/∂z)|_{z=h}
```
The sign of ∂B_z/∂z above a finite-height stator coil assembly is negative (field decreasing with height above the coils). For a rotor magnetic moment aligned with the stator field (m_rotor_z > 0):
```
<F_z> = m_rotor_z × (negative value) < 0   → downward force
```
For levitation, need ∂B_z/∂z > 0 (field increasing with height) or m_rotor_z < 0 (anti-aligned rotor). An anti-aligned rotor in a below-axis (attractive) trap geometry produces upward levitation. This is the Paschen-Back regime of magnetic trapping scaled to macroscopic objects.

Drew's configuration likely exploits a specific coil geometry where the stator creates a "magnetic bottle" or "mirror" geometry above the coil plane, where B_z has a local maximum at some height z_eq — a magnetic high-field seeking trap for diamagnetic rotors, or a low-field seeking trap for appropriately configured paramagnetic/ferromagnetic rotors that orient anti-parallel to the local field.

**3-phase coil advantage for lift:**
A 3-phase system provides a rotating field of constant amplitude |B_rot| = (3/2)B_0, unlike a single-phase field which oscillates between +B_0 and −B_0 with zero time-averaged Z-force from a symmetric geometry. The constant-amplitude rotating field maintains a constant gradient force:
```
<F_z> = (3/2) m_rotor_z (∂B_z0/∂z)
```
versus a single-phase field that gives:
```
<F_z>_1phase = (1/2) m_rotor_z (∂B_z0/∂z)
```
3-phase provides 3× the time-averaged lift force compared to single-phase excitation at the same peak field amplitude.

### Can Gyroscopic Precession Generate Z-Axis Lift?

Drew poses: "Could a UFO fly due to gyroscopic inertia, with an applied torque from a 3-phase magnetic field?"

**Standard mechanics answer:**
Gyroscopic precession (as analyzed by Euler's equations) does not generate net Z-axis linear force. The precession is a rotational phenomenon — it changes the direction of the angular momentum vector without involving linear forces. A freely precessing gyroscope does not rise, fall, or translate in standard Newtonian mechanics.

**Non-standard mechanisms:**

Mechanism A: **Gyroscopic + gradient field interaction**
If the precessing gyroscope's magnetic moment sweeps a non-uniform magnetic field (the gradient field of the 3-phase stator), the time-averaged force on the oscillating magnetic moment can have a net component. The instantaneous force:
```
F_z(t) = m_z(t) (∂B_z/∂z)
```
If m_z(t) oscillates in phase with ∂B_z/∂z oscillations, the time-averaged <F_z> ≠ 0. Specifically, if the nutation of the gyroscope (driven by the magnetic torque) causes m_z to be largest when ∂B_z/∂z is largest, a net upward force results.

Mechanism B: **Mathieu-equation parametric resonance**
The coupled gyroscope + rotating magnetic field system can exhibit parametric resonance when the nutation frequency matches the driving frequency. This can produce exponentially growing oscillations that transfer energy from the rotating field to the gyroscope's Z-axis linear motion — a form of parametric amplification leading to lift.

Mechanism C: **Eric Laithwaite effect (empirically claimed)**
Laithwaite's 1974 Royal Institution Christmas Lecture demonstrations (the probable source of Drew's 1974 reference) showed that large gyroscopes appear to weigh less when precessing rapidly. While mainstream physics maintains this is an observational artifact or measurement error, Drew is actively investigating whether this weight reduction is real. The video (13_1956329124328611840_video.mdx) shows an experiment testing whether "a gyro transfers its mass centre to the pivot" — precisely the configuration Laithwaite demonstrated, where a precessing gyroscope supported at a single pivot point appeared to balance without falling.
