### Rapidly Rotating Magnet Levitation — New Modality Distinct from Classical Maglev

Drew references a recently discovered levitation mechanism (as of ~2023) that is categorically distinct from conventional magnetic levitation used in maglev trains. The classical maglev regime operates via static or quasi-static field exclusion (diamagnetic levitation, superconducting Meissner effect) or active electrodynamic suspension (repulsive lift via induced eddy currents in a moving conductor). The newly identified regime operates via a **rapidly rotating permanent magnet** suspending a second magnet in free air without a conducting track substrate.

**Experimental apparatus (from preview image analysis, 13_1913486746995871744_preview.txt):**

* Metallic disc rotor, apparent diameter ~20 cm, material consistent with aluminum or steel substrate
* Three disc-shaped permanent magnets embedded at 120-degree angular intervals on the rotor disc surface — this is a canonical 3-pole rotor configuration for 3-phase brushless DC (BLDC) motor operation
* Central thin shaft/pin, diameter ~3 mm, defining the rotation axis (z-axis)
* Upper-left magnet shows bicolor red/blue face markings — indicating mixed polarity presentation (N and S poles facing upward simultaneously, consistent with an obliquely magnetized disk or a biaxially magnetized tile)
* Two remaining magnets (upper-right, lower-center) show uniform red top faces — single-pole-up orientation
* Handwritten annotations on surrounding white board: "30" (degrees or mm), "N3" (third north pole or magnet-3 designation), angular measurement lines at 45-degree and 90-degree intervals
* Red alligator clip visible at left edge — external electrical connection present, suggesting possible powered coil excitation concurrent with mechanical rotation

**Physical mechanism (theory):**

The 3-pole rotor at 120-degree intervals is the rotor topology for a 3-phase BLDC motor stator excitation. When driven by a 3-phase AC source with 120-degree phase offset, the stator coils generate a rotating magnetic field vector **B_rot(t)** that sweeps the azimuthal plane (XY-plane) at angular frequency ω:

```
B_x(t) = B_0 cos(ωt)
B_y(t) = B_0 cos(ωt - 2π/3)
B_z(t) = B_0 cos(ωt - 4π/3)
```

The resultant rotating field vector has constant magnitude |B_rot| = (3/2)B_0 for a balanced 3-phase system, rotating at ω in the XY-plane. The rotor permanent magnets attempt to align with this rotating field, producing torque and synchronous rotation.

The **new levitation phenomenon** observed: a suspended second magnet above the rotating primary magnet disc experiences a time-averaged force with a net upward (Z-axis) component under specific conditions of rotation rate, magnet geometry, and inter-magnet spacing. This is not Meissner-effect levitation (requires superconductor) and not conventional eddy-current lift (requires conducting track). The mechanism is proposed to involve:

1. **Gyromagnetic coupling**: the rotating magnetic dipole of the primary magnet generates a time-varying magnetic flux through the secondary magnet that produces a net repulsive force in the Z-axis averaged over one rotation period
2. **Spin-stabilized magnetic suspension**: the secondary magnet's own magnetic moment precesses in the rotating field of the primary, analogous to a spinning gyroscope precessing in a gravitational field, with the equilibrium suspension point at a fixed height above the primary rotor

### Disk Magnet Geometry Optimization for Rapid Spin

Drew reports iterative experimental optimization of disk magnet geometry for the spinning-magnet levitation apparatus. Key constraints:

* Primary design intent: **rapid spin** — geometry must minimize moment of inertia I = (1/2)mr² for a disk to allow high angular velocities ω
* Thin disks minimize axial extent, reducing I_axial but the relevant moment for spin about the z-axis is I_z = (1/2)mr²
* Trade-off: increasing disk thickness increases magnetic flux (through larger magnetic volume and remanence B_r × Volume) but increases rotational inertia

For a uniform cylindrical disk magnet of radius r and thickness h:
```
I_z = (1/2) m r²
m = ρ π r² h   (ρ = density of Nd₂Fe₁₄B ≈ 7400 kg/m³)
I_z = (1/2)(ρ π r² h)(r²) = (π ρ h r⁴)/2
```

For fixed mass budget, a thinner disk (small h) at fixed radius r has lower I_z than a thicker disk at smaller r — confirming the design preference for thin disks for rapid spin applications.

**Force scaling with magnet size:**
The magnetic dipole moment m_d of a uniformly magnetized disk:
```
m_d = M_s × V = M_s × π r² h
```
where M_s is the saturation magnetization (~1.28 MA/m for Nd₂Fe₁₄B). The far-field dipole force between two aligned magnetic dipoles separated by distance d along the common axis:
```
F_z = (3μ_0 m_d1 m_d2) / (2π d⁴)
```
Scaling: F_z ∝ m_d ∝ r²h ∝ r³ (for constant aspect ratio). Thus a 60 mm magnet has (60/40)³ = 3.375 times the force of a 40 mm magnet at the same separation d, consistent with Drew's experimental observation that "the most powerful force is with a 60 mm magnet."

### Lorentz Spin Combined with 3-Phase Uplift

Drew states the UAP propulsion mechanism involves **"Lorentz spin with 3-phase uplift in combination."** This is a compressed technical claim integrating two distinct physical effects:

**Lorentz Spin:**
The Lorentz force on a charge q moving with velocity **v** in magnetic field **B** and electric field **E**:
```
F = q(E + v × B)
```
For a charged rotating body (or a conducting disk rotating in a magnetic field), the Lorentz force on conduction electrons generates radial charge separation (homopolar induction, Faraday disk effect). In a spinning disk of conductivity σ, radius R, rotating at angular velocity ω in an axial magnetic field B_z:
```
EMF = (1/2) ω B_z R²
```
This radial EMF drives a current that interacts with B_z to produce an azimuthal (torque) force. "Lorentz spin" likely refers to the torque coupling between the rotating magnetic field and induced currents in a conducting rotor — a self-reinforcing spin mechanism.

**3-Phase Uplift:**
In a 3-phase rotating magnetic field system (stator coils at 120-degree offsets, driven by 3-phase AC), the rotating field vector B_rot sweeps the XY-plane. Drew claims that this rotating field configuration produces an upward Z-axis force component — "uplift." The physical mechanism proposed: the precessing magnetic moments of a suspended object in the rotating field experience a gradient force analogous to the magnetic gradient force:
```
F_z = ∇(m · B) = m_z (∂B_z/∂z)
```
If the rotating field geometry creates a divergent or convergent flux pattern in the Z-axis above the stator plane, a magnetic dipole placed in this region experiences net upward or downward force. In a 3-phase coil geometry, the field above the stator plane can be shaped to have ∂B_z/∂z < 0 (flux decreasing upward), producing a restoring (levitation) force on a paramagnetic or ferromagnetic object with m_z aligned with B_z.

The **combination** (Lorentz spin + 3-phase uplift) implies the rotating conductor disk simultaneously:
1. Experiences Lorentz-driven torque that sustains or amplifies its rotation
2. Generates or responds to the 3-phase field in a manner that produces net Z-axis lift

This is Drew's claimed first-order mechanism for UAP hovering and vertical propulsion.

* Inner ring: spins in direction D1
* Outer ring (or counter-flywheel): spins in direction D2 = −D1 (counter-rotation)

**Angular momentum cancellation:**
For two counter-rotating flywheels with angular momentum vectors:
```
L_1 = I_1 ω_1 ẑ
L_2 = I_2 ω_2 (−ẑ)
```
If I_1 ω_1 = I_2 ω_2, then L_total = 0. A system with zero net angular momentum does not exhibit gyroscopic precession and has no preferred orientation — it is isotropically "neutral" in angular momentum space. ### Ion Rocket — Lenz Repulsion at Very High Frequency (500 kHz Tesla Coil)

Drew claims an original physics discovery: **at 500 kHz from a solid-state Tesla coil, argon gas can be repelled outward in a "Lenz" sense**, whereas excitation at standard 50 Hz does not produce this repulsion effect.

**Lenz's Law (standard formulation):**
Lenz's Law states that the direction of an induced current opposes the change in flux that caused it:
```
ε = −dΦ_B/dt
```
For a conductor in a time-varying magnetic field, the induced EMF drives currents that produce a magnetic field opposing the applied field — effectively a repulsive magnetomotive response. For conducting media (metals, plasmas, ionized gases), this appears as apparent repulsion of the conductor from the time-varying field source.

**Frequency dependence — skin depth and plasma dynamics:**
The skin depth δ in a conducting medium:
```
δ = √(2ρ / (ω μ))
```
where ρ is resistivity, ω = 2πf, μ is permeability. At high frequencies, δ decreases as ω^(−1/2), concentrating induced currents near the surface and increasing the surface current density — enhancing the repulsive Lenz effect.

For argon gas at 50 Hz: argon is a noble gas, non-ionizing under low-intensity 50 Hz excitation. No plasma channel forms, no Lenz effect.

At 500 kHz from a Tesla coil: The high-frequency, high-voltage output ionizes argon gas, creating a weakly ionized plasma. The plasma has finite electrical conductivity σ_plasma. The Tesla coil's rapidly oscillating field then induces currents in the plasma that produce Lenz repulsion — effectively propelling the ionized argon outward from the coil.

**Video transcription (13_1963008525036339201_video.mdx):** "When we turn up the power we get very impressive results. At 60 watts, one out going through the coil. The iron rocket." — the "60 watts" figure gives the coil power level. "Iron rocket" is likely a verbal transcription error for "ion rocket."

**Drew's claim:** mainstream physics does not know or understand this frequency-dependent plasma-repulsion effect. He asserts it could in principle drive a space rocket by Lenz repulsion, though his homemade Tesla coil lacked sufficient power for significant thrust.

**Force scaling:**
Lenz repulsion force on a plasma element of volume dV with current density J in field B:
```
dF = J × B dV
```
For plasma ionized by the Tesla coil field, J = σ_plasma E_induced. At 500 kHz, the induced E-field is proportional to dB/dt ∝ ω B, so J ∝ ω, and the force density |dF/dV| ∝ ω B². The super-linear frequency dependence means that going from 50 Hz to 500 kHz (a factor of 10,000 in frequency) produces a factor of 10,000× increase in force density (holding B constant), which explains why the effect is unobservable at 50 Hz but visible at 500 kHz.
