## Section: Gyroscopic Precession Equations — 1941729579741348241.yaml

### Precession Rate Formula

For a symmetric top (gyroscope) under gravitational torque:

ω_p = τ / L = (Mgr) / (Iω)

where:
- ω_p = precession angular velocity (rad/s) in the horizontal (XY) plane
- τ = gravitational torque magnitude = Mgr (N·m)
- L = spin angular momentum magnitude = Iω (kg·m²/s)
- M = total mass of gyroscope (kg)
- g = gravitational acceleration = 9.81 m/s²
- r = distance from pivot point to center of mass (m)
- I = moment of inertia of rotor about spin axis (kg·m²)
- ω = spin angular velocity of rotor (rad/s)

**Key relationship:** ω_p ∝ 1/ω — as spin rate ω increases, precession rate ω_p decreases. This is the basis for Drew's claim that increasing spin causes the gyroscope to transition from horizontal precession to vertical rise: as ω_p decreases, the precessional orbit cannot be maintained at constant height, and the gyroscope must rise.

### Angular Momentum Vector

**L** = I · **ω_spin**

where **ω_spin** is directed along the spin axis (z-axis for vertical spin). The torque from gravity is:

**τ** = **r** × M**g** = r·M·g · (horizontal unit vector)

The precession equation:
d**L**/dt = **τ**

gives **L** rotating in the XY-plane at rate ω_p.

### Two-Step Transition: Low Spin to High Spin

**Low spin regime (small ω):**
- ω_p is large (fast precession in XY-plane).
- Precessional orbit is horizontal.
- Downward gravity torque is fully converted to horizontal precession.
- Z-component of net force on gyroscope = 0 (horizontal orbit, no net z-motion).

**High spin regime (large ω):**
- ω_p decreases (as ω_p ∝ 1/ω).
- Gyroscope must rise to maintain gyroscopic stability (invariance of spin plane orientation in inertial space).
- Drew's claim: z-force > 0 (net upward force) during this rise.
- The rise force is not directly derivable from the standard precession formula — it is the *anomalous* claim that goes beyond standard mechanics.
