### 1 kg Upward Force on Aluminum Rod — Energy Efficiency Not Stated

The measurement of ~9.81 N upward force on an aluminum rod in the rotating-field "space propeller" configuration raises the question of energy efficiency: what electrical power input is required to sustain this force?

In a standard linear induction motor operating at efficiency η:
```
P_input = F_thrust · v_synchronous / η
```
For a stationary rod (v = 0 relative to the field), the force-velocity relationship changes: the rod is at "zero slip" condition (slip = 1 if the rod is stationary), which corresponds to maximum force but also maximum resistive losses (all rotor power dissipated as heat).

The 9.81 N force on the aluminum rod in this context is suggestive of a high-force operating point, but without knowing the input power, the energy balance cannot be assessed. The "rockets are so 20th century" commentary implies Drew sees this force generation as energetically superior to chemical rockets, though no quantitative energy comparison is provided.
