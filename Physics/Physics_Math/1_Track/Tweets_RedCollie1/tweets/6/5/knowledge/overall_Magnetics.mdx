### Three-Phase Rotating Magnetic Field: Triangle Coil Geometry

Configuration: Three coils arranged at the vertices (or edges) of an equilateral triangle,
driven with three-phase AC power. Three-phase power creates a rotating magnetic field vector
whose angular velocity in the XY-plane equals the electrical drive frequency. The three coil
phases are offset by 120 degrees electrically, producing a field that rotates smoothly rather
than pulsing.

Interaction with permanent disc magnet:
* An 80 mm diameter permanent disc magnet (10 mm central hole) positioned coaxially above
  the coil plane couples to the rotating field.
* The rotating field exerts a torque on the magnet (motor effect) causing it to spin at or
  near the electrical drive frequency (synchronous/near-synchronous coupling).
* Additionally, the interaction produces a net Z-axis (axial) force that displaces the magnet
  upward.

Magnet geometry: Disc magnet with central bore. Magnetization direction likely axial (pole
faces on flat disc surfaces). The central hole allows the magnet to ride on a central pole
shaft.

Apparatus constraint: Upper mechanical stop limits Z-axis displacement to approximately 20 mm.

Physical question: Whether the Z-axis force represents a true net force exceeding gravity (i.e.,
effective weight reduction of the system when measured on a scale), or merely a redistribution
of magnetic forces within the apparatus.

### Vortex Field vs. Dipole Field: Fundamental Distinction

Author's critical distinction: The observed anti-gravity field from a spinning magnet is
explicitly identified as different from and unrelated to conventional North-South dipole
magnetism:

Standard dipole field: B field of a magnetic dipole falls off as r^{-3} (far field approximation),
with B_r = (μ_0 / 4π)(2m cos θ / r^3) and B_θ = (μ_0 / 4π)(m sin θ / r^3). The force on
another magnetic dipole scales accordingly. This field has a characteristic bipolar geometry
with field lines entering at south and exiting at north.

Claimed "vortex field": Does not follow the dipole spatial falloff law. The field maintains
constant effect magnitude from 5 mm through at least 200 mm, then terminates abruptly. This
is inconsistent with any r^{-3} or r^{-2} decay. The spatial profile described is more
consistent with a field that has approximately uniform strength within a finite volume (a
"top hat" spatial profile) rather than a smooth decay.

The author claims: "There are no other possible explanations" — ruling out explanations based
on standard dipole-dipole interactions, standard eddy current forces, and standard Lenz law
repulsion.

Secondary observation: Non-magnetic spinning gyroscopes produce the same anti-gravity effect
on nearby masses while spinning. This is significant: if non-magnetic rotating masses produce
the same vortex anti-gravity field, then the mechanism cannot be purely electromagnetic in
origin. The rotating mass itself (independent of magnetic properties) may be the generator
of the vortex field. This connects to Woodward's Mach Effect, gravitomagnetism, or frame-
dragging proposals (see also: Tajmar rotating superconductor anomalous gravitomagnetic fields).

### Rotating Electromagnetic Device: Force vs. Frequency Relationship

Device: Axial flux or brushless motor topology with Hall effect position sensing (Hall effect
sensors at 90/180-degree intervals on rotor disc).

Force measurements as function of rotation frequency:
* f < threshold (low spin): ΔF = 0 (no measurable force change)
* Critical onset: When pole reaches vertical (top) orientation → ΔF initiates downward
* f = 15 Hz: ΔF = 700 g downward
* f = 17 Hz: ΔF = 900 g downward
* f → 17+ Hz: ΔF → 1000 g (1 kg) downward (projected)

Force direction: Downward in this experiment (weight increase). The companion experiment
showed 1 kg UPWARD force for aluminum rod.

Note on directionality: The downward force in this experiment vs. upward force in companion
experiment suggests the direction of the anomalous force depends on the material properties
(magnetic material vs. non-magnetic aluminum) or the geometry of coupling to the rotating
field. Aluminum is a non-magnetic conductor; permanent magnets are ferromagnetic. The
mechanisms generating force on each may differ in sign.

### Magnet Polarity Patterns in Counter-Spinning Ring

Thin outer ring magnet configurations:

Configuration A (preferred): 16 small magnets in NN-SS-NN-SS-NN-SS-NN-SS polarity pattern,
each magnet 20 mm in length.
* Total ring circumference: 16 × 20 mm = 320 mm
* Ring circumference: 2π × radius → radius ≈ 50.9 mm (≈ 51 mm)
* Number of full magnetic periods: 8 (each period = NN-SS, 40 mm)
* Spatial frequency of magnetization: 8 periods / 320 mm = 0.025 periods/mm
* This multi-pole configuration produces a higher-order multipole field that rotates
  with the ring

Configuration B: N-S-N-S-N-S-N-S alternating polarity, each magnet 30 mm.
* Total ring circumference: 16 × 30 mm = 480 mm (larger ring)
* 8 full magnetic periods (each period = N-S, 60 mm)
* Larger magnets create stronger individual pole fields

Functional difference: Configuration A (NN-SS) preferred because it starts better and
spins better with less mass-weight. This suggests the NN-SS configuration couples more
efficiently to the three-phase drive field, possibly because the NN-SS arrangement produces
a dominant 8th-order multipole that better matches the spatial harmonics of the stator's
rotating field.

Visual appearance: At high rotational speeds, the ring of 16 small magnets appears as a
featureless uniform ring (consistent with visual blur averaging over the alternating poles).

### Edge-to-Edge Magnetic Attraction Between Inner Disc and Outer Flywheel

Inner magnet (blue) interactions:
* Magnetic edge-to-edge attractive coupling to the 4-magnet flywheel (red) surrounds it
  on all sides.
* This edge attraction prevents the inner magnet from tilting or wobbling; it constrains
  spin to a flat plane.
* When the inner magnet naturally "wants to lift up" (Z-axis force from spin), the edge
  attraction provides a restoring radial force maintaining flat-plane rotation.

Outer 4-magnet flywheel (red) interactions:
* Spinning at 40 Hz in the direction OPPOSITE to inner magnet
* Edge-to-edge attraction to inner magnet exerts a tangential drag force attempting to
  couple inner magnet rotation to outer flywheel rotation (i.e., to reverse-spin the inner
  magnet).
* The counter-spinning of the flywheel generates a net downward force on the inner magnet
  (Z-axis: −Z direction).

Proposed electromagnetic explanation of downward force: Counter-rotation of the outer
4-magnet flywheel relative to the inner magnet creates a rapidly time-varying magnetic field
environment at the inner magnet's surface. This is functionally equivalent to a high-rate
alternating field being applied to the inner magnet from below and the sides. Lenz's law
repulsion from this changing field would push the inner magnet in the direction opposing
the field change source — if the flywheel is below/around the inner magnet, the repulsion
pushes inward and downward. This is the proposed mechanism, but it is noted as not fully
explainable by standard textbook equations.
