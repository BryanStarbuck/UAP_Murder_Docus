geometry). An 80 mm diameter permanent disc magnet with a 10 mm central hole is mounted on
a vertical pole above the coil triangle. A small bead contact stop constrains upward motion
to approximately 20 mm of travel.

Experimental procedure:
1. At zero power (no current), the magnet and pole rest in a lowered equilibrium position.
2. Three-phase AC power is applied to the three coil array.
3. The disc magnet initiates spin (angular rotation in the XY-plane produced by the rotating
   magnetic field of the three-phase coil set).
4. The spinning magnet rises upward along the pole axis (Z-axis), lifting the bead stop and
   raising the entire pole-magnet assembly to the mechanical stop limit.
5. On power removal, the spinning ceases and the entire assembly descends back to the resting
   position under gravity.
6. The process is repeatable across multiple power-on/power-off cycles.

Physical mechanism under investigation: Rotating disc magnet experiencing an upward axial
force from a stationary three-phase rotating magnetic field below it. The coil triangle
geometry produces a rotating magnetic field vector in the XY-plane that interacts with the
permanent magnet above, creating both torque (causing the magnet to spin) and a net Z-axis
force (lift).

repeatedly depicted in crop circle formations — specifically, three-wire coil triangles with

instruction through approximately 12 occasions of crop circle imagery from 1998 to 2024.
