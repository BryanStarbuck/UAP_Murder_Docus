### Magnetic Dipole Field — Exact Equation

    B(r) = (μ₀/4π) · [3(m·r̂)r̂ - m] / r³

**Pieces:**
- μ₀ = 4π × 10⁻⁷ T·m/A: permeability of free space
- m: magnetic dipole moment vector [A·m²]; for a solenoid of N turns, area A, current I: m = NIA ẑ
- r: radial distance from dipole center [m]
- r̂: unit vector in radial direction
- 3(m·r̂)r̂: radial component of field (stronger along axis)
- -m: subtracts the dipole moment direction (gives the field the correct angular structure)

**Limiting cases:**
- On-axis (r̂ ∥ m): B_axial = (μ₀/4π)(2m/r³) [pointing same direction as m]
- Equatorial (r̂ ⊥ m): B_equatorial = -(μ₀/4π)(m/r³) [antiparallel to m]

**Relationship to XY-plane dynamics:**
The solenoid axis (m direction) can be taken as ẑ. The equatorial plane (z=0) is the XY-plane. In the XY-plane:
    B_XY = -(μ₀/4π)(m/r³) ẑ
This anti-parallel field in the equatorial plane is what interacts with a second magnet or coil placed in that plane.

### Three-Phase Rotating Magnetic Field — Full Mathematical Derivation

**Input currents (balanced 3-phase at angular frequency ω):**
    I_U(t) = I₀ cos(ωt)
    I_V(t) = I₀ cos(ωt - 2π/3)
    I_W(t) = I₀ cos(ωt - 4π/3)

**Fields produced by each coil (coils at angular positions 0°, 120°, 240° in XY-plane):**
Each coil k at position φ_k = 2π(k-1)/3 produces field:
    B_k(t) = B₀ cos(ωt - φ_k) · (cos(φ_k) x̂ + sin(φ_k) ŷ)

**Superposition:**
    B_total(t) = Σ_k B_k(t)
             = B₀ Σ_k cos(ωt - φ_k)(cos(φ_k) x̂ + sin(φ_k) ŷ)

Using the identity Σ_k cos(ωt - φ_k)cos(φ_k) = (3/2)cos(ωt) and Σ_k cos(ωt - φ_k)sin(φ_k) = (3/2)sin(ωt):

    B_total(t) = (3/2)B₀ [cos(ωt) x̂ + sin(ωt) ŷ]

**Result:** Uniformly rotating field of constant magnitude (3/2)B₀ rotating at angular frequency ω in XY-plane. This is exact for balanced 3-phase; unbalanced phases produce elliptically polarized rotating field.

**Five-phase generalization:**
For n-phase system with coils at φ_k = 2π(k-1)/n:
    B_total(t) = (n/2)B₀ [cos(ωt) x̂ + sin(ωt) ŷ]

For n=5: amplitude = (5/2)B₀; for n=6: amplitude = 3B₀.

**Relationship between XY-plane rotation and Z-axis force (unsolved):**
Standard EM: a rotating B-field in XY produces no net Z-force on a free dipole (the force F = ∇(m·B) averages to zero over one rotation). The claim that XY rotating B → +ẑ force requires a non-standard coupling term.

One candidate: if the object has an anisotropic magnetic susceptibility tensor χ_ij with χ_zz ≠ χ_xx = χ_yy:
    F_z = (1/2μ₀) · (χ_zz - χ_xx) · (∂B²/∂z)
This is the magneto-gravitational force used in diamagnetic levitation, but requires a B-field gradient in ẑ, which is not obviously present in a flat rotating configuration.

Another candidate: gyroscopic coupling. If the spinning object has L ≠ 0 (angular momentum from rotation), and the rotating B-field applies a torque τ = m × B, the precessional response:
    dL/dt = τ → Ω_p = τ/L in a direction perpendicular to both L and τ
If L is in XY-plane and τ is also in XY-plane, then dL/dt ∝ ẑ → the precession is a ẑ rotation. But this still does not directly produce a net translational F_z.

The mathematical pathway from rotating B-field to net +ẑ translational force remains the open problem in Drew's research.

### Gyroscopic Precession Equations

**Angular momentum:**
    L = Iω

where I = (1/2)mr² (solid disk, axis through center), ω = rotational angular velocity [rad/s].

**Gravitational torque on tilted gyroscope:**
    τ = r_pivot × F_gravity = r · Mg · sin(θ) [at angle θ to vertical]

For θ = 90° (horizontal spin axis, pivot at end):
    τ = r · Mg

**Natural precession rate:**
    Ω_p = τ/L = (Mgr)/(Iω)

**Relationship:** Ω_p ∝ 1/ω. As ω → ∞, Ω_p → 0 (slower precession for faster spin). As ω → 0, Ω_p → ∞ (uncontrolled tumbling).

**Super-precession condition (Drew's Law 3):**
    Ω_applied > Ω_natural = Mgr/(Iω)

**Net upward force from super-precession:**
    F_up = I·ω·(Ω_applied - Ω_natural)/r = I·ω·Ω_applied/r - Mg

For F_up > 0: I·ω·Ω_applied > Mg·r → Ω_applied > Mg·r/(I·ω) = Ω_natural [self-consistent]

**Power to sustain super-precession:**
The power required to drive precession at Ω_applied:
    P = τ · Ω_applied = Mg·r·Ω_applied

For anti-gravity (F_up = Mg): P = Mg·r·Ω_natural = Mg·r·(Mgr/(Iω)) = (Mgr)²/(Iω)

This shows power decreases as ω increases — higher spin speed requires less power to achieve gravity cancellation.

**Spin decay:**
If the gyroscope spins with friction torque τ_friction = bω (viscous friction, b = damping coefficient):
    I·dω/dt = -bω → ω(t) = ω₀ · exp(-bt/I)

Gravity cancellation time constant: t_cancel = I/(b) — after several time constants, ω has decayed sufficiently that the weight returns. This is why the cancellation effect diminishes over time in lab demonstrations.

### 16-Pole Magnet Ring — Field Harmonic Analysis

For a ring of N = 16 equally spaced alternating N-S magnets of dipole moment m_0 each, at radius R:

**Azimuthal field component at ring radius r (r < R):**
The field is dominated by the N-th spatial harmonic (N = 16):

    B_φ(r, φ) ≈ B_16 · (r/R)^(N-1) · cos(N·φ) = B_16 · (r/R)^15 · cos(16φ)

The field falls rapidly inward from the ring (as (r/R)^15), concentrating near the ring.

**At r = R (the ring itself):**
    B_φ = B_16 · cos(16φ)   [16 maxima around the ring]
    B_16 ≈ (N · m_0) / (2π R³)  [approximate peak field]

**Rotation at angular velocity ω_rot:**
When the ring spins, the field pattern rotates: φ → φ - ω_rot·t:
    B_φ(r, φ, t) = B_16 · (r/R)^15 · cos(16(φ - ω_rot·t)) = B_16·(r/R)^15·cos(16φ - 16ω_rot·t)

The rotating field has temporal frequency:
    f_field = 16 · f_rot   (16th harmonic of mechanical rotation)

**Stroboscopic appearance at high ω:**
At high ω_rot: the 16-magnet ring appears optically to have 15 poles due to aliasing/stroboscopic effect:
    Apparent count = N - 1 = 15   (one period aliased at the frame rate of human vision or camera)

This is the mathematical explanation for Drew's observation: "16 small magnets in rapid spin look like 15 small magnets overlapping."

### Tesla Unipolar Motor EMF Equation

For a conducting disk of radius r rotating at angular velocity ω = 2πf in a static axial magnetic field B_z:

    EMF = ∫₀ʳ (v × B) · dr = ∫₀ʳ (ω·ρ·B_z) dρ = (1/2)·B_z·ω·r²

**Pieces:**
- v = ω × ρ ρ̂ = ω·ρ φ̂ (tangential velocity at radius ρ)
- B = B_z ẑ (static axial field)
- v × B = (ω·ρ φ̂) × (B_z ẑ) = ω·ρ·B_z (φ̂ × ẑ) = ω·ρ·B_z (-ρ̂) [negative radial direction]
- Wait — for disk rotating in B_z field, motional EMF drives current radially:
  EMF = -(1/2)·B_z·ω·r²  (polarity depends on convention)

**Power:**
    P = EMF² / R_external   (if used as generator)
    P = EMF · I             (if used as motor)

**Scaling:**
EMF ∝ f² (for fixed r, B_z): doubling the rotation speed quadruples EMF and thus power (P ∝ f²). This favorable scaling is why the unipolar motor is efficient at high RPM.

**Drew's "opposite of textbook" interpretation:**
Standard textbook motor: rotating B (produced by AC coils) drags a stationary conductor → torque.
Unipolar: stationary B + rotating conductor → EMF → current → force. The roles of "rotating" and "stationary" are swapped between field and conductor. Drew implies his UAP propulsion system makes use of this inverted topology.

### Inner-Outer Magnet Counter-Rotation — Angular Momentum Balance

**System parameters:**
- Inner magnet (blue): moment of inertia I_in, angular velocity ω_in (CW, positive ẑ)
- Outer 4-magnet flywheel (red): moment of inertia I_out, angular velocity ω_out (CCW, negative ẑ)

**Angular momenta:**
    L_in = +I_in · ω_in   (positive ẑ)
    L_out = -I_out · ω_out   (negative ẑ)

**Net system angular momentum:**
    L_net = L_in - L_out = I_in·ω_in - I_out·ω_out

**Balance condition:**
    L_net = 0  ⟺  I_in·ω_in = I_out·ω_out
    ⟺  ω_out/ω_in = I_in/I_out

**Given:** ω_in ≈ 2π(15–30) = 94–188 rad/s, ω_out = 2π(40) = 251 rad/s
For balance: I_in/I_out = ω_out/ω_in = 251/94 – 251/188 = 1.34 – 2.67

The inner magnet must have a moment of inertia between 1.34× and 2.67× that of the outer flywheel to achieve L_net = 0.

**Energy in the system:**
    KE_total = (1/2)I_in·ω_in² + (1/2)I_out·ω_out²

Even when L_net = 0, KE_total can be large. The system carries significant kinetic energy while having zero net angular momentum. Drew's claim: this zero-L high-energy state enables a non-classical force output.
