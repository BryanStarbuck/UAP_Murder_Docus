## Lorentz Body Force Density (MHD)

Source: YAML 1988193077077700816 (referenced tweet — liquid metal channel mechanism)

  f = J × B   [N/m³]

Components:
  f = body force density vector (N/m³) on the conducting fluid
  J = current density vector (A/m²) — imposed by DC power supply
  B = magnetic flux density vector (T) — provided by central disc magnet

Geometry in the device:
  J directed radially: J = J_r r̂   (outward radial direction in cylindrical coords)
  B directed axially: B = B_z ẑ   (along rotation axis, from disc magnet)

Cross product:
  f = J_r B_z (r̂ × ẑ) = −J_r B_z φ̂   (azimuthal direction, drives rotation)

Or equivalently, with inward current:
  f = J_r B_z φ̂   (positive azimuthal if J inward and B upward)

This is the standard MHD body force in a cylindrical geometry — the operating principle of the electromagnetic pump, the homopolar motor (Faraday wheel), and MHD drives in liquid metal systems.

## Radiation Pressure on Cometary Dust — Sunward Tail Anomaly

Source: YAML 2005083489771946473, TXT 3_2005082745282985984.txt

Standard cometary dust tail direction:

  F_rad = (S · A_eff) / c   [N]

Components:
  F_rad = radiation pressure force (N), directed anti-solar
  S = solar irradiance at comet's heliocentric distance r: S = L_sun / (4π r²)   [W/m²]
  L_sun = solar luminosity = 3.828 × 10²⁶ W
  A_eff = effective cross-sectional area of grain (m²)
  c = speed of light = 2.998 × 10⁸ m/s

β parameter (ratio of radiation pressure force to gravitational force on a dust grain):
  β = F_rad / F_grav = (3 L_sun Q_pr) / (16π G M_sun c ρ a)

where:
  Q_pr = radiation pressure efficiency coefficient (dimensionless)
  G = gravitational constant = 6.674 × 10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg²
  M_sun = solar mass = 1.989 × 10³⁰ kg
  ρ = grain bulk density (kg/m³)
  a = grain radius (m)

Grains with β > 1: radiation pressure exceeds gravity — driven strongly anti-solar
Grains with β < 1: gravity dominates — remain in bound orbit; can appear sunward if orbital geometry produces synchrone/syndyne projection

Drew's anomaly claim: 3I/ATLAS tail is pointing TOWARD the Sun. If this is not a projection artifact (anti-tail/synchrone from Earth crossing the orbital plane), it implies either:
(a) The tail is not radiation-pressure-driven dust (i.e., not cometary in origin), or
(b) The ejected material has β << 0 (requires negative effective radiation pressure — not physical for normal dust), or
(c) It is propulsive exhaust directed anti-motion (i.e., the craft decelerates/accelerates toward the Sun by ejecting mass sunward)

## Angular Resolution and Linear Scale Calculation

Source: YAML 1960580782977900911 (note_tweet)

Relationship between angular resolution (arcseconds), distance (km), and linear pixel scale (km/pixel):

  Δx = D · tan(θ) ≈ D · θ   (small angle approximation)

where:
  Δx = linear scale per pixel (km/pixel)
  D = distance to target (km)
  θ = angular resolution per pixel (radians)
  θ [radians] = θ [arcsec] × (π / 648000) = θ [arcsec] × 4.848 × 10⁻⁶ rad/arcsec

Drew's calculation for JWST at 3I/ATLAS:
  D ≈ 350 million miles = 350 × 10⁶ × 1.609 km ≈ 5.63 × 10⁸ km
  θ_JWST_expected = 0.07 arcsec/pixel = 0.07 × 4.848 × 10⁻⁶ rad/pixel = 3.394 × 10⁻⁷ rad/pixel
  Δx_expected = 5.63 × 10⁸ km × 3.394 × 10⁻⁷ rad/pixel ≈ 191 km/pixel

Drew states expected ≈ 120 km/pixel (using slightly different distance assumption).

Observed coma width in JWST image:
  Width_coma = 350,000 km (from physical size estimate)
  N_pixels ≈ 25-30 pixels spanning coma
  Δx_observed = 350,000 / 27.5 ≈ 12,727 km/pixel (Drew states ~12,000)

Ratio:
  Resolution_deficit = Δx_observed / Δx_expected = 12,000 / 120 = 100

Effective angular resolution of JWST as observed:
  θ_effective = Δx_observed / D = 12,000 / (5.63 × 10⁸) = 2.131 × 10⁻⁵ rad
  θ_effective [arcsec] = 2.131 × 10⁻⁵ / 4.848 × 10⁻⁶ ≈ 4.4 arcsec/pixel

Drew states the effective resolution seen in slide 3 is about 7 arcsec/pixel (this difference from the above is likely due to different pixel count assumptions). He also states SPHERE-X resolution is 6.2 arcsec/pixel and notes the JWST effective and SPHERE-X values match, used as a cross-check.

Conclusion stated: JWST observed resolution ≈ 100× worse than its nominal specification for 3I/ATLAS.

## 3-Phase Rotating Magnetic Field — Vector Sum Mathematics

Source: YAML 1988193077077700816 (referenced tweet — "3-phase magnetism" added to system)

Three-phase AC produces three sinusoidal magnetic fields 120° apart in both space and time:

  B_1(t) = B_m cos(ωt) x̂
  B_2(t) = B_m cos(ωt - 2π/3) · (−(1/2)x̂ + (√3/2)ŷ)
  B_3(t) = B_m cos(ωt - 4π/3) · (−(1/2)x̂ − (√3/2)ŷ)

Vector sum:
  B_total(t) = B_m [cos(ωt) x̂ + cos(ωt−2π/3)(−(1/2)x̂+(√3/2)ŷ) + cos(ωt−4π/3)(−(1/2)x̂−(√3/2)ŷ)]

Expanding (using trig identities):
  x-component: B_m [cos(ωt) − (1/2)cos(ωt−2π/3) − (1/2)cos(ωt−4π/3)]
  = B_m · (3/2) cos(ωt)   ... after applying sum-to-product identities

  y-component: B_m [(√3/2)cos(ωt−2π/3) − (√3/2)cos(ωt−4π/3)]
  = B_m · (3/2) sin(ωt)   ... after applying sum-to-product identities

Result:
  B_total(t) = (3/2) B_m [cos(ωt) x̂ + sin(ωt) ŷ]

Magnitude: |B_total| = (3/2) B_m = constant
Direction: rotates in the XY plane at angular frequency ω

This is the standard result for a balanced three-phase winding: the resultant magnetic field has constant amplitude (3/2)B_m and rotates uniformly at ω rad/s. This rotating field is superimposed on the self-excited dynamo field from the spinning liquid metal in Drew's device.

## Babylonian Star Path Declination — Astronomical Angular Coordinate

Source: YAML 1937716500582007245 (note_tweet)

Drew references Babylonian astronomical classification:
* "Path of Enlil": stars with declination > +17° North
* "Path of Anu": stars with declination between −17° S and +17° N
* "Path of Ea": stars with declination < −17° South

These are angular boundaries in the equatorial coordinate system:
  δ_Enlil > +17°
  −17° ≤ δ_Anu ≤ +17°
  δ_Ea < −17°

Drew proposes using these as a key to decode crop pictures drawn in Italy (2011 and 2025), by overlaying the crop patterns onto star maps of Earth's northern or southern hemispheres at the appropriate declination ranges.

Mathematical method proposed:
* "Program each kind of crop-drawn star-pattern (or their overlap) into a computer"
* Perform a "rotation search" — systematically rotate the pattern to find a match to real star maps
* Drew explicitly names the analogous crystallographic technique: "In x-ray crystallography, they call this a 'rotation-translation search'"

The rotation-translation search in x-ray crystallography: used in molecular replacement to find the orientation and position of a known protein structure within an unknown crystal unit cell, by searching over SO(3) (rotation group in 3D) and R³ (translation space) for maximal correlation of Patterson function peaks. Drew proposes applying the equivalent computational search to match crop patterns to sky survey data.
