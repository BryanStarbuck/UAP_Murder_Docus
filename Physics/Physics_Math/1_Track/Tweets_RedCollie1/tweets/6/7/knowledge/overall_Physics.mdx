## Lorentz Force — J × B Drive for Liquid Metal Rotation

Source: YAML 1988193077077700816 (referenced tweet)

The Lorentz body force density in a conducting fluid:

  f = J × B   [N/m³]

where:
  J = current density vector [A/m²]
  B = magnetic flux density [T]

Application: DC current J is passed radially (r-direction in cylindrical coordinates) through a liquid metal (mercury or galinstan) in an annular channel. The central disc magnet provides B in the z-direction. The cross product J × B acts in the azimuthal (φ) direction, driving the liquid metal in circular rotation about the z-axis.

This is the magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) body force — the same mechanism used in electromagnetic pumps and the homopolar motor.

Relationship: Once the liquid metal is set in rotation, it constitutes a moving conducting fluid, which by Ampere's law and Faraday's law generates its own magnetic field. Drew claims this induced field adds constructively to the central disc magnet's field (self-excited dynamo), rather than opposing it (Lenz's law behavior for solid coil + moving magnet).

The distinction Drew draws:
* Solid wire coil + moving permanent magnet: induced current → opposing B field (Lenz's law, standard Faraday induction)
* Liquid metal outer channel + central disc magnet + DC Lorentz drive: induced field → constructive addition (self-excited dynamo regime)

Whether this represents a genuine departure from classical MHD depends on the geometry — in a homopolar generator (Faraday disk) configuration, the induced EMF can indeed reinforce the primary field under specific conditions. Drew frames this as a non-classical result; from the MHD perspective it is the self-exciting homopolar generator configuration.

## 3-Phase Rotating Magnetic Field — Addition to Dynamo System

Source: YAML 1988193077077700816 (referenced tweet)

After establishing the DC-driven liquid metal Lorentz rotation + disc magnet base, Drew adds 3-phase magnetism:

A 3-phase rotating magnetic field is produced by three stator coils arranged at 120° spatial intervals, driven by three-phase AC current with 120° phase separation:
  B_total(t) = B_1·cos(ωt) + B_2·cos(ωt - 2π/3) + B_3·cos(ωt - 4π/3)

The vector sum of the three phase contributions produces a resultant B vector of constant magnitude rotating at angular frequency ω (synchronous with the AC supply frequency). This is standard induction motor theory.

In Drew's proposed device:

Mechanically: The 3-phase rotating field applies a torque to any magnetized object or conducting element that can respond to the field rotation — analogous to the rotating field acting on the squirrel cage rotor in an induction motor. If the rotor (or the craft as a whole) is the responding element, the rotating field induces currents and forces.

## Self-Excited Dynamo — Field Addition vs. Lenz Opposition

Source: YAML 1988208405702705389

Key physics distinction stated by Drew:

  → Total field is stronger than disc magnet alone
  → This is the "self-excited dynamo" named by Drew

CASE 2 — Lenz opposition (conventional induction):
  A moving permanent magnet induces current in a nearby wire coil

Drew's framing: These two cases represent different physical regimes. The self-excited dynamo regime is claimed to be the basis for the levitation device and for UAP propulsion. The Lenz opposition regime is classical electrodynamics as taught in textbooks.

## Propeller Wake Vortex Physics — Classical Contrast Case

Source: TXT 13_1913570616609026048_preview.txt

The CGI visualization of a propeller vortex wake shows:

1. Helical tip vortex formation: As each blade sweeps through the fluid, it sheds a tip vortex. These tip vortices organize into a helical (screw-like) vortex wake. In potential flow theory, the vortex sheet shed from the trailing edge of each blade rolls up into discrete vortex filaments.

2. Vortex ring structure: In vertical orientation (propeller at bottom, wake extending upward), the helical tip vortices appear as a series of approximately 8-10 toroidal vortex rings — the helical vortex wound around the slipstream cylinder.

3. Slipstream (central jet): The actuator disk model predicts:
   V_slipstream = V_∞ + v_induced
   where the induced velocity v_induced equals half the far-wake induced velocity.

4. Wake contraction: The slipstream contracts downstream (closer spacing of vortex rings at top of frame vs. bottom) — the rotating propeller accelerates and contracts the wake as the fluid passes through the disk.

Physics relevance in Drew's framing:
* Propeller propulsion requires momentum exchange with a fluid medium — Newton's 3rd law applied to fluid mechanics
* The helical wake is the physical record of this momentum exchange
* UAPs are claimed to show NO comparable wake vortex structure
* Conclusion drawn: UAP propulsion does NOT operate via fluid momentum exchange — it couples directly to field/gravitational/inertial mechanisms

## Cometary Physics — Dust Tail, Ion Tail, Anti-tail

Source: TXT 3_2005082745282985984.txt (comet photograph description)

Observed features of the photographed comet:
* Narrow, highly collimated bright white/yellow tail extending at approximately 45 degrees from nucleus toward lower-left
* Green coma (C₂/CN emission — Swan bands at 516 nm from C₂, CN violet system at 388 nm) extending broadly around nucleus
* No broad ion tail visible in the exposure

Physical interpretation:
* Narrow dust tail: fine particulate matter (μm-scale grains) driven by radiation pressure; points anti-solar
* Green coma: photodissociation of parent molecules by solar UV produces C₂ and CN radicals; their fluorescence gives the characteristic green color
* Ion tail (plasma tail, Type I): solar wind interaction with cometary ions; points nearly exactly anti-solar due to high solar wind speed (~400 km/s); absent in this exposure (may be faint or at unfavorable angle)
* Anti-tail/synchrone: dust particles of very small β parameter (radiation pressure to gravity ratio) can appear to form a spike pointing sunward when Earth crosses the comet's orbital plane
