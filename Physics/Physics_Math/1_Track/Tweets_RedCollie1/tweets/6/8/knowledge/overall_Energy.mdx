### 1. Magnetic Flywheel as Self-Sustaining Power Source

Source: tweet 2013830404101546217 (note_tweet).

Claim: UFOs "generate electricity from their fast-spinning magnetic flywheels."

The architectural claim is that the magnetic flywheel simultaneously:
1. Generates electrical power (acting as a generator / alternator)
2. Provides propulsion

If the flywheel generates enough electrical power to drive the 3-phase stator coils (which in turn drive the flywheel's rotor), this could approach or constitute a closed-loop energy arrangement. In conventional electromagnetic machines, this would violate energy conservation: a motor-generator pair cannot run indefinitely with unity efficiency because all real systems have resistive losses (I²R in coils), eddy current losses (σ B² ω² in core), and mechanical friction losses (bearing drag). However, the source material does not claim strict over-unity — it claims that "electricity is generated from the fast-spinning flywheels" as a feature of the architecture, implying the primary energy input (whatever spins the flywheel initially) is supplemented or partially recovered by the generator function, reducing net external power consumption. The specific energy source for initial spin-up is not identified in this dataset.

The Leedskalnin architecture at Coral Castle (1930s) is cited as the historical precedent. Leedskalnin's documented device used permanent magnets on a hand-cranked flywheel to generate electricity — a conventional permanent-magnet generator, not an over-unity device. The claimed "anomalous" aspect is the additional propulsive output that conventional analysis would not predict.
