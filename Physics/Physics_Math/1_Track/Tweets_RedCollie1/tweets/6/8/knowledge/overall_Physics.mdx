### 1. Gyroscopic Inertia and Spacetime Curvature Decoupling

Source: tweet 1956882401071866004 (text and note_tweet), MDX 13_1956878594044809216_video.mdx.

Eric Laithwaite's 1974 experiment (Imperial College): A fast-spinning gyroscope held at the end of a rod does not descend at the same rate as a non-spinning mass on the opposite end. The source interprets this as the spinning gyroscope being effectively decoupled from spacetime curvature.

Chain of reasoning given:

Step 1: "Weight is just a downward acceleration, due to Earth's normal field of planetary gravity." This is the equivalence principle: a = g, where g = -GM/r² r̂ in the Newtonian limit. In GR, a freely falling body follows a geodesic, and weight arises only in accelerated (non-inertial) frames.

Step 2: "The spacetime curvature associated with normal gravity seems to have almost NO EFFECT on a fast-spinning gyroscope." In GR, the motion of a spinning body deviates from a geodesic via the Mathisson-Papapetrou-Dixon (MPD) equations:

   Dp^μ/dτ = -(1/2) R^μ_νρσ u^ν S^ρσ   [spin-curvature force]
   DS^μν/dτ = p^μ u^ν - p^ν u^μ          [spin evolution]

where:
   p^μ = 4-momentum of the body
   u^μ = 4-velocity along the worldline
   S^μν = spin angular momentum tensor (antisymmetric)
   R^μ_νρσ = Riemann curvature tensor

The spin-curvature coupling term -(1/2) R^μ_νρσ u^ν S^ρσ is a force that shifts the body away from the geodesic. For Earth's gravitational field and a macroscopic gyroscope, this term is typically extremely small compared to the gravitational (geodesic) acceleration — it is not large enough to explain Laithwaite's observed effect through standard GR.

Step 3 (speculative interpretation): "Perhaps because its fast-spinning disc creates large changes of inertia, which insulate that gyroscope from normal spacetime curvature."

This invokes a non-standard mechanism: the claim that the moment of inertia tensor I_{ij} of the spinning body becomes dynamically significant in its coupling to the curvature tensor R_{μνρσ}. In classical GR this is not a standard result, but extensions involving spinning-body quadrupole moments (the Dixon extension of MPD) include:

   Dp^μ/dτ = -(1/2) R^μ_νρσ u^ν S^ρσ - (1/6) ∇^μ R_νρστ J^νρστ + ...

where J^νρστ is the quadrupole moment of the spinning body. At high angular velocity ω, the dynamical quadrupole (Iω terms) could in principle contribute to gravitational coupling, though this remains speculative at the claimed magnitude.

Physical consequence claimed: Fast-spinning body experiences reduced effective gravitational acceleration g_eff < g. The device exploits this to achieve lift without consuming propellant.

* Vertical (Z-axis): Reduced gravitational coupling via spin-curvature interaction

### 3. Rotating Field and Rotor Synchronization: Torque Chain

Source: tweets 1922640989367439397 (note_tweet), 1926951087585579163.

3-phase rotating magnetic field applied to a magnetic rotor:

The synchronous torque on a rotor with magnetic moment m in a rotating field B_rot(t) = B_0[cos(ωt)x̂ + sin(ωt)ŷ]:

   τ = m × B_rot

At synchronous speed (rotor turning at ω in phase with field), τ = 0 in the steady state (no lag). Under load (rotor lagging by angle δ):

   |τ| = |m| |B_0| sin(δ)

This is the pull-out torque condition. Maximum torque occurs at δ = π/2. This is directly analogous to the synchronous motor torque equation:

   T = (3/2) (P/2) (λ_f I_s sin(δ)) / ω_s

where P = pole pairs, λ_f = flux linkage of the permanent magnet, I_s = stator current magnitude.

* 6 coils → 3-phase stator, 2 coils per phase → this produces the rotating B_rot
* 3 coils → possibly a second set producing a secondary field at different frequency or phase
* Phase difference between the two power supplies introduces a phase offset φ between B_rot,1 and B_rot,2

The resultant torque on the rotor from two overlapping rotating fields at the same frequency ω but different phases φ:

   B_total(t) = B₁[cos(ωt)x̂ + sin(ωt)ŷ] + B₂[cos(ωt + φ)x̂ + sin(ωt + φ)ŷ]

         = [B₁ + B₂ cos(φ)]cos(ωt) x̂ + [B₁ + B₂ cos(φ)]sin(ωt) ŷ

For φ = 0: constructive interference, B_eff = B₁ + B₂ (maximum torque).
