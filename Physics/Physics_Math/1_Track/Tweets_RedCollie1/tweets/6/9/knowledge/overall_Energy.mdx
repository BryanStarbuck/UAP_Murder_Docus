1. POSITIVE-FEEDBACK MAGNETIC AMPLIFICATION — POTENTIAL ENERGY GAIN LOOP
   Source: tweet 1965941848457003483 (2025-09-11), note_tweet
   Claim: "Lorentz spin creates an induced magnetic field which strengthens the applied magnetic field!"
   Contrast stated: "Completely unlike the Lenz force used in our electric generators, where an induced magnetic field weakens and opposes the applied magnetic field."
   Energy implications:
   -- In standard generators: the Lenz-opposition of the induced field to the driving field is the mechanism by which electrical energy is extracted from mechanical energy (work is done against the opposing Lenz force). Net energy balance: work_in = electrical_out + losses (1st Law satisfied).
   -- In Drew's claimed Corbino/plasma geometry: if the induced field strengthens the applied field, the system would be adding to the stored field energy without equivalent mechanical input — an apparent violation of energy conservation.
   -- Alternatively, if an external ionization power source (the 6.6 kW AC source) supplies the energy to create the plasma current that generates the reinforcing field, then no conservation violation need occur. The "amplification" could simply be a more efficient use of input power to generate a stronger net B field than would be achievable by the same power applied to a standard solenoid.
   -- Drew does not explicitly claim over-unity energy generation in these sources. The claim is specifically about the direction of the induced field (co-aligned rather than opposing) not necessarily about the energy balance. However, the implications for a self-sustaining loop are evident: if B_total > B_applied and the system is closed, the field would grow without bound — which would require external energy input to sustain or would be limited by nonlinear saturation.
   Status: Experimental claim (observed in lab), not yet quantified in terms of power balance.

Experimental apparatus power level: 6.6 kW (AC) — laboratory scale
Claimed phenomenon: ionized-air plasma vortex around spinning horizontal N-S magnet
Ionization voltage: 6.6 kV AC
Operating medium: Air (approximate composition N2 78%, O2 21%, Ar 1%; ionization energies: N2 first ionization ~15.6 eV, O2 ~12.1 eV, Ar ~15.8 eV)
Ionization mechanism: Corona discharge or dielectric barrier discharge at 6.6 kV — sufficient for Paschen-curve breakdown in air at atmospheric pressure for gaps of appropriate geometry (Paschen minimum for air: ~330 V at pd ≈ 0.57 Torr·cm; at 1 atm, 6.6 kV supports breakdown across gaps of order 1-2 cm)

Energy required to sustain plasma:
-- Ionization energy per ion-pair in air: ~34 eV (empirical W-value)
-- At 6.6 kW input and ~34 eV/pair: ion-pair production rate ≈ 6600 / (34 × 1.6×10⁻¹⁹) ≈ 1.2 × 10²¹ pairs/second
-- This is a very large ion production rate at laboratory scale, but the recombination rate is also high in air at atmospheric pressure (τ_recombination ~ microseconds to milliseconds)
-- Net plasma density depends on balance between production and recombination

Relevance to propulsion: At 6.6 kW producing a plasma vortex, the achievable thrust would be small (fraction of 1 Newton for the described apparatus) unless the electromagnetic coupling efficiency to ambient air momentum is very high. Scaling to craft-level propulsion would require orders-of-magnitude larger power or a fundamentally different efficiency regime.
