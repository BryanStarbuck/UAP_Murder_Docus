1. MAGNETIC DIPOLE FIELD — SPINNING MAGNET GEOMETRY
====================================================================

For a magnetic dipole of moment m̄ = m ẑ (before rotation), located at the origin:

B̄(r, θ) = (μ_0 / 4π r³) [2m cos θ r̂ + m sin θ θ̂]

For a dipole spinning in the horizontal (XY) plane at angular velocity ω_spin about Z:
m̄(t) = m [cos(ω_spin t) x̂ + sin(ω_spin t) ŷ]

The field at position r in the equatorial plane (θ = π/2) oscillates at ω_spin:
B_x(t) = (μ_0 m / 4π r³) cos(ω_spin t)
B_y(t) = (μ_0 m / 4π r³) sin(ω_spin t)

This creates a rotating transverse field in the equatorial region — the same geometry as a rotating magnetic field in the stator plane of an AC motor. The ionized air surrounding this magnet experiences a time-varying B that drives carrier drift via Lorentz force.

2. LORENTZ FORCE ON IONIZED MEDIUM
====================================================================

f̄ = q(Ē + v̄ × B̄)

F̄ = ρ_c Ē + J̄ × B̄

-- ρ_c = charge density (C/m³)
-- J̄ = current density (A/m²)
-- B̄ = applied magnetic field (T)

7. GYROSCOPE PRECESSION MATHEMATICS
====================================================================

Equation of motion for a symmetric top (gyroscope):
dL̄/dt = τ̄ = r̄_cm × F̄_gravity = r r̂ × (-Mg ẑ) = Mgr (r̂ × -ẑ) = Mgr φ̂

Precessional solution (steady precession, ω >> Ω_p):
Ω_p = Mgr / L = Mgr / (Iω)

as ω → ∞: Ω_p → 0 (gyroscope stands still; precession vanishes)

Energy state of horizontal vs. vertical spin:
E_horizontal (spin axis horizontal) vs. E_vertical (spin axis vertical)

For a gyroscope with spin axis horizontal:
-- The rotor plane is vertical
-- The spin angular momentum L is horizontal
-- Gravity creates torque about the horizontal axis perpendicular to L
-- This torque causes precession (horizontal orbital rotation)
-- The spinning rotor strongly resists tilt out of its spin plane (gyroscopic inertia)

For a gyroscope with spin axis vertical:
-- The rotor plane is horizontal
-- L is vertical (parallel to g)
-- No gravitational torque about the spin axis (τ = r × F = 0 for F parallel to L)
-- No precession required — rotor can spin freely in horizontal plane without gravity-induced torque

Drew's energy argument: "By rising-up vertically, that fast-spinning rotor can reach a low-energy state, where no out-of-plane motions take place." The low-energy state is vertical spin because it eliminates the precession work. The transition itself is claimed to produce net upward force.

The force during transition:
During the horizontal-to-vertical axis change, dL/dt = torque applied.
If the transition is self-generated (no external torque), then Newton's 3rd law dictates a reaction on the support structure. Drew claims this reaction is absent — the key non-classical assertion.

9. SCREW ANALOGY — CHIRAL ROTATIONAL INTERACTION
====================================================================

Two co-axial rotations in opposite senses:
Ω_1 = +Ω ẑ (3-phase clockwise field, in Drew's convention: CW from above = -Ω ẑ in standard RH convention)
Ω_2 = -Ω ẑ (Lorentz anti-clockwise)

Net angular velocity: Ω_net = Ω_1 + Ω_2 = 0 (cancel if equal magnitude)

But the interaction between two co-existing counter-rotating fields is not simply the sum:
-- The cross-coupling terms produce second-order effects
-- In plasma: J × B force depends on the combined instantaneous field, not the time-averaged field
-- The product of two counter-rotating field components produces a static (DC) component: cos(ωt) × cos(ωt + π) = -cos²(ωt) = -(1 + cos(2ωt))/2 — with a DC offset of -1/2
-- A DC Z-axis force would arise from this DC offset if it couples to a Z-directed field gradient

Mathematical form: If B_1 = B cos(ωt) φ̂ and B_2 = B cos(ωt + π) φ̂ = -B cos(ωt) φ̂, net B_total = 0 (exact cancellation). But if B_1 and B_2 are not identical (different geometries, frequencies, or spatial distributions), non-zero cross-products are possible.

Drew does not provide the specific mathematical coupling mechanism. The screw analogy is qualitative. The formal coupling between counter-rotating azimuthal fields and a Z-axis force requires a non-zero term of the form (Ω_1 × Ω_2) · ẑ or equivalent, which vanishes for exact counter-rotation and requires asymmetry.
