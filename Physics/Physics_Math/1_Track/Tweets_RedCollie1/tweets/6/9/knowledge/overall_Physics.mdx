1. LORENTZ FORCE (foundational to all Drew experiments)
   F = q(E + v × B)
   In plasma / ionized gas context:
   -- q = charge of ion or electron
   -- v = drift velocity of charged carrier
   -- B = applied magnetic field (from spinning permanent magnet or stator coils)
   -- E = electric field (from ionization voltage source or self-field)
   The azimuthal component of F = q(v_r × B_z) drives carriers in the φ-direction (Corbino geometry), producing a bulk rotation of the ionized medium.
   Source: tweet 1965941848457003483, 1963122578001994134

2. FARADAY'S LAW / LENZ'S LAW (classical, then contrasted)
   ε = -dΦ_B/dt
   Φ_B = ∫∫ B · dA
   Lenz's law: the sign of ε is such that the induced current opposes the change causing it → B_induced opposes ΔB_applied.
   Drew's claim: In the Corbino/homopolar geometry with ionized gas, B_induced aligns with B_applied (positive feedback), in contrast to standard Lenz behavior.
   Source: tweet 1965941848457003483

3. MAGNETIC DIPOLE FIELD (spinning permanent magnet)
   B_r(r,θ) = (μ_0 m / 4π) · (2 cos θ / r³)
   B_θ(r,θ) = (μ_0 m / 4π) · (sin θ / r³)
   where m = magnetic dipole moment, r = radial distance, θ = polar angle from dipole axis, μ_0 = 4π × 10⁻⁷ H/m
   For a spinning dipole in the horizontal plane at angular velocity ω, the field at any fixed point r oscillates sinusoidally at frequency ω (the rotation frequency), generating an alternating near-field that couples to the surrounding ionized medium.
   Source: Implicit in tweet 1913912117994086451 apparatus description

4. GYROSCOPE PRECESSION EQUATION
   Ω_p = τ / L = Mgr / (Iω)
   where:
   -- Ω_p = precessional angular velocity
   -- τ = gravitational torque = Mgr (M = rotor mass, g = 9.81 m/s², r = distance from pivot to center of mass)
   -- L = angular momentum of spinning rotor = Iω (I = moment of inertia, ω = spin angular velocity)
   High spin limit (ω → ∞): Ω_p → 0, gyroscope resists precession more strongly; transition to vertical orientation possible.
   Drew's claim: At sufficiently high ω, the energy state of horizontal spin exceeds that of vertical spin, and the rotor transitions upward, producing a net Z-axis impulse without Newton's 3rd Law back-reaction.
   Source: tweet 1941823558147342518; referenced tweet 1941729579741348241

5. ANGULAR MOMENTUM OF SPINNING ROTOR
   L = Iω (vector, along spin axis)
   For horizontal spin: L is horizontal (in XY plane), pointing along the spin axis
   For vertical spin: L is vertical (along Z axis)
   Transition from horizontal to vertical: ΔL = L_vertical - L_horizontal = Iω (ẑ - x̂)
   A change in L requires torque τ = dL/dt. Drew's claim is that this change in L can be self-generated by the spin energy state transition without external reaction force.
   Source: tweet 1941823558147342518

6. ROTATING MAGNETIC FIELD (3-PHASE SYSTEM)
   B_rotating(t) = B_0 · [cos(ωt) x̂ + cos(ωt - 2π/3) ŷ]
   (For a 2-pole 3-phase system; the third phase is dependent. A balanced 3-phase system with symmetric winding arrangement produces a field of constant magnitude B_0 rotating at angular frequency ω in the stator plane.)
   Stator field rotates at synchronous speed: n_s = 120f/p (rpm), where f = supply frequency (Hz), p = number of poles.
   Slip: s = (n_s - n_r) / n_s, where n_r = rotor speed. Standard theory: 0 < s < 1 for motoring; s < 0 for generating. Counter-rotation (n_r opposite to n_s): s > 1, requires torque input. Drew claims spontaneous counter-rotation under standard conditions — inconsistent with classical slip theory.
   Source: tweet 2017865458909680022

7. 5-PHASE ROTATING FIELD (BUGA ORB HYPOTHESIS)
   B_rotating(t) = B_0 · Σ_{k=0}^{4} cos(ωt - 2πk/5) · n̂_k
   where n̂_k = unit vectors at angles 72k° in the plane of the coil ring.
   Phase separation: 360°/5 = 72° (vs. 120° for 3-phase).
   Torque ripple: For n-phase system, ripple ∝ cos(2nωt)/n². For 5-phase: ripple at 10th harmonic, significantly smoother than 3-phase (6th harmonic). For plasma vortex generation: smoother rotating field → more uniform azimuthal Lorentz coupling → more coherent plasma vortex → potentially higher propulsive efficiency.
   Source: tweet 2043504674054218197

8. CORBINO DISC PHYSICS
   Configuration: disc of radius R, inner electrode at r = a, outer electrode at r = b, axial field B_z, radial current J_r.
   Lorentz force: F_φ = J_r × B_z (azimuthal torque on carriers)
   Azimuthal current contribution: J_φ(r) induced by Lorentz force on radially-moving carriers
   Classical Corbino result: J_φ / J_r = μ_H · B_z (Hall angle), where μ_H is Hall mobility
   Drew's extension: The azimuthal current circulation in the ionized medium (not the solid conductor) creates a net axial B that adds to B_z. This would require the plasma's Hall parameter Ω_e τ_e (electron cyclotron frequency × collision time) to be >> 1, so that electrons undergo many gyrations between collisions, creating a significant azimuthal current component.
   Source: tweets 1963122578001994134, 1965941848457003483

9. MAGNETOHYDRODYNAMICS (MHD) — WATER SURFACE EFFECT
   Lorentz body force on conducting fluid: f = J × B (force per unit volume, N/m³)
   For water (poor conductor, σ ≈ 10⁻⁴ S/m), MHD effects require strong fields.
   Magnetic pressure: P_magnetic = B²/(2μ_0)
   Rosensweig (ferrofluid surface) instability condition: B² / (2μ_0) > γ / λ (surface tension energy density), where γ = surface tension coefficient, λ = characteristic wavelength.
   For the observed water surface dimple above a submerged magnet: gradient force on diamagnetic water is repulsive (water is diamagnetic, χ_m ≈ -9 × 10⁻⁶), pulling the surface downward only if the object is ferromagnetic/paramagnetic and the field gradient is large enough to pull the water surface through the meniscus effect.
   Source: 13_1913911119221280769_preview.txt
