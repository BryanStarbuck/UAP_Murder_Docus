## Gyroscopic Rise as the Core Propulsion Hypothesis

Source tweet (1941998706066387266, note_tweet):
"My current hypothesis of UFO levitation is that the upward, anti-gravity force comes from the
rise of a spinning gyroscope, as we go from low to high spin? This hypothesis might also include
the Buga orb-sphere."

The operative claim is that a gyroscope undergoing angular acceleration (increasing spin rate)
exhibits a net upward force that is not fully accounted for by classical gyroscopic precession
equations. In standard Newtonian/Lagrangian mechanics, a gyroscope precesses but does not
spontaneously translate vertically. The claim asserts an additional vertical force term emerges
experimentally during spin-up, constituting deviation from the classical result.

The Lagrangian for a symmetric top (Euler angles theta, phi, psi) in a gravitational field g:

  L = (1/2) I_1 (theta_dot^2 + phi_dot^2 sin^2(theta))
      + (1/2) I_3 (psi_dot + phi_dot cos(theta))^2
      - M g l cos(theta)

where I_1 = I_2 are equatorial moments of inertia, I_3 is axial moment of inertia,
M = rotor mass, l = distance from pivot to center of mass.

Steady precession gives: phi_dot = M g l / (I_3 omega_3) for large omega_3.

The anomalous claim: during spin-up (d(omega_3)/dt > 0), a net upward impulsive force F_z > 0
appears beyond what the precession equations predict. This would require a new term in the
equations of motion — possibly a coupling between angular momentum L and the metric via a
torsional spacetime field.
