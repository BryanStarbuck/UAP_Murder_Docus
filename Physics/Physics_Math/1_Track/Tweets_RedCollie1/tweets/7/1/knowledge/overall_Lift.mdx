Physical model of 4-D spacetime vortex as lift mechanism:

In general relativity, spacetime is described by a metric tensor g_mu_nu. The gravitational
"force" on a test particle is the geodesic deviation caused by non-zero Riemann curvature
R^rho_sigma_mu_nu. In standard GR with no torsion, the geodesic equation:

  d^2 x^mu / d tau^2 + Gamma^mu_alpha_beta * (dx^alpha/d tau)(dx^beta/d tau) = 0

The Christoffel symbols Gamma encode the metric, and for the Earth's gravitational field
in the weak-field limit, they reduce to the Newtonian gravitational acceleration g.

In Einstein-Cartan theory (GR + torsion), the torsion tensor T^lambda_mu_nu is introduced.
For a spinning body (with intrinsic spin angular momentum S^mu_nu), the spin couples to
torsion:
  D S^mu_nu / d tau = S^mu_alpha * T^alpha_nu_beta (dx^beta / d tau)
                    - S^nu_alpha * T^alpha_mu_beta (dx^beta / d tau)

The claim that rapidly spinning objects create "spinning vortices in 4-D spacetime" maps
onto the creation of a non-zero torsion field T^lambda_mu_nu in the region surrounding
the spinning body — analogous to how a rotating mass in standard GR creates a non-zero
gravitomagnetic field (Lense-Thirring effect), but here through the torsion rather than
the Riemann curvature channel.

Within the torsion vortex region, the geodesic equation is modified:
  d^2 x^mu / d tau^2 + (Gamma^mu_alpha_beta + K^mu_alpha_beta) * ... = 0

where K^mu_alpha_beta is the contorsion tensor (derived from T^lambda_mu_nu). If the
contorsion has a component K^z_tt < 0 (negative z-component of time-time contorsion),
the effective gravitational acceleration g_eff < g, potentially reaching g_eff = 0 or
g_eff < 0 (net upward acceleration) within the vortex.

  * With 3-phase power: omega_rotor increases to produce 17 mm rise

The interaction of XY-plane rotation with Z-axis:
  The rotating magnetic field vector B(t) = B_0 (cos(omega_e t) x_hat + sin(omega_e t) y_hat)
  sweeps the XY-plane. Its time derivative: dB/dt = B_0 * omega_e (-sin x_hat + cos y_hat)
  is also in the XY-plane.

  The curl of B (related to current density via Ampere's law) has a Z-component:
  (curl B)_z = (dB_y/dx) - (dB_x/dy)

  For a uniform rotating field, (curl B)_z = 0 in the bulk. However, at the boundaries
  of the rotating field region (near the magnet surfaces), the field gradients are non-zero
  and (curl B)_z ≠ 0. This gradient force could have a Z-component depending on the
  geometry of the magnet arrangement.

The standard magnetic pressure force density: f = (1/mu_0) * (B . grad) B - grad(B^2/(2*mu_0))
This has components in all directions depending on the field geometry. For the specific
4-magnet surround geometry with a central spinning magnet, the Z-component of this force
needs numerical field analysis (FEM) to evaluate — Drew has not provided such analysis,

## Doubling Magnet Strength and Its Effect on Lift

Source: 13_2043606527630786560_video.mdx (experimenter states effect of doubling):
"I've doubled the strength of that magnet, made it 6 millimetres rather than 3 millimetres thick."

The magnetic moment of a permanent magnet (approximately):
  m = M_r * V = M_r * A * t

where M_r = remnant magnetization, A = pole face area, t = thickness.
Doubling t → doubles m → doubles both attractive force to surrounding magnets AND
doubles the field gradient in the vicinity → doubles the magnitude of any field-gradient-based
Z-force. This explains why the "double strength magnet" produces a qualitatively stronger
rising force compared to the single-strength baseline.

The field from a bar magnet at distance r along the axis:
  B_z(r) = (mu_0 / 4*pi) * 2*m / r^3

Doubling m → B_z doubles → dB_z/dz also doubles (linear scaling for dipole) →
magnetic force on an adjacent dipole (F ~ m * dB/dz) scales as m^2:

  F_z(double) / F_z(single) = (2m * 2 * dB/dz) / (m * dB/dz) = 4

So doubling magnet strength produces 4× increase in the magnetic gradient force —
but the experimenter reports qualitative, not quantitative, comparison between single
and double strength configurations in this video.
