## Rotating Magnetic Field (RMF) Architecture: 3-Phase and 5-Phase Systems

### 3-Phase System (Standard Polyphase Motor Architecture)

Source tweet (2017866159291568301), referenced_tweets[0].text:
"A ring of 4 permanent magnets will spin either WITH a rotating magnetic field (made by
6 wire coils wired as U-V-W-U-V-W), or AGAINST a rotating magnetic field, depending on
whether those 4 rotor magnets are arranged as N-S-N-S or N-N-N-N. The same principle
holds true for 9 [coils/magnets — text truncated]."

The standard 3-phase rotating magnetic field is generated by 3 sets of stator windings
(U, V, W phases) displaced 120° spatially, driven by currents:

  I_U(t) = I_0 cos(omega_e * t)
  I_V(t) = I_0 cos(omega_e * t - 2*pi/3)
  I_W(t) = I_0 cos(omega_e * t - 4*pi/3)

where omega_e = 2*pi*f_e, f_e = electrical supply frequency.

The resultant air-gap flux vector B(t) rotates at angular velocity omega_e in the XY-plane:

  B_x(t) = B_peak * cos(omega_e * t)
  B_y(t) = B_peak * sin(omega_e * t)
  |B(t)| = B_peak (constant magnitude, rotating direction)

For the 6-coil configuration (U-V-W-U-V-W), each phase occupies 2 coil slots 180° apart,
creating a 2-pole field with one complete rotation per electrical cycle.

Drew's notation "U-V-W-U-V-W" confirms standard 3-phase, 6-slot, 2-pole stator winding
topology — the same fundamental architecture as Tesla Model 3 induction motor stators.

### Rotor Polarity Reversal Effect (Key Non-Classical Claim)

The critical observation: rotor magnet arrangement determines whether the rotor spins
WITH or AGAINST the rotating field direction.

* N-S-N-S arrangement (alternating poles): rotor aligns with field, spins WITH field
  (synchronous following, standard reluctance/PM motor behavior)
* N-N-N-N arrangement (same poles): rotor spins AGAINST field direction

Standard electromagnetic theory predicts N-N-N-N rotors would simply experience net
zero torque in a uniform rotating field due to symmetric repulsion, or would exhibit
unstable equilibria. The claimed counter-field rotation for the N-N-N-N configuration is
presented as anomalous — exceeding what standard Maxwell + Lorentz force analysis predicts
for a symmetric same-polarity rotor in a rotating external field.

Drew describes testing this same principle with rings of 9 magnets (text truncated in
tweet), suggesting he is investigating scaling behavior of this reversal effect across
different numbers of pole pairs.

## XY-Plane Rotation and Its Relationship to Z-Axis Rise

The geometric relationship stated throughout the experimental results:
* XY-plane: plane of magnetic field rotation (horizontal, perpendicular to gravity vector g)
* Z-axis: vertical direction, parallel to g, direction of claimed lift force

The proposed coupling: XY rotation of field at sufficient angular velocity omega → creation
of a torsion field T_z (or equivalent) aligned with Z-axis → net force F_z = -m * T_z
opposing gravitational attraction F_g = -m * g, yielding F_net = m(T_z - g).

When |T_z| > |g| = 9.81 m/s^2, net upward acceleration results.

Source (1941998706066387266, note_tweet): "the upward, anti-gravity force comes from the
rise of a spinning gyroscope, as we go from low to high spin" — the spin-up phase (d*omega/dt > 0)
is identified as the critical regime, not steady-state rotation.

## Counter-Rotation (Spin and Counter-Spin)

Source tweets (2014078283214950480 and its referenced_tweet):
Tweet text: "Spin and counter-spin?"
Referenced tweet: "Spin and counter-spin: new physics?"

The video (13_2014078193758777344_video.mdx) yielded only "You" in transcription — no
substantive audio content. However, the framing "new physics?" for counter-rotation
implies the experimenter is testing configurations where two components rotate in
opposite directions (counter-rotating).

In classical electromagnetism, two counter-rotating charged rings of equal magnitude
produce a net zero magnetic dipole moment along the symmetry axis (the fields cancel).
However, they produce a non-zero magnetic quadrupole moment. The interaction of a
high-order multipole field with the spin angular momentum of a gyroscopic rotor could
produce forces not captured in the dipole approximation — particularly if the field
gradients (dB/dz) are significant in the inter-component gap.

The "new physics" framing suggests Drew believes counter-rotation produces Z-axis
forces not accountable by the dipole-level field analysis.
