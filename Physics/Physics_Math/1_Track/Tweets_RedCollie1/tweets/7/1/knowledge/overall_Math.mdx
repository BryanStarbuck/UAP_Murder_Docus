## Polyphase AC Motor Mathematics

### 3-Phase Rotating Magnetic Field

Stator winding currents (3-phase, balanced, positive-sequence):
  I_U(t) = I_0 cos(omega_e * t)
  I_V(t) = I_0 cos(omega_e * t - 2*pi/3)
  I_W(t) = I_0 cos(omega_e * t - 4*pi/3)

Resultant air-gap flux density (Fourier synthesis of fundamental spatial harmonic):
  B_x(t) = (3/2) * B_1 * cos(omega_e * t)
  B_y(t) = (3/2) * B_1 * sin(omega_e * t)

where B_1 is the peak of the fundamental flux density from a single phase.
Magnitude: |B(t)| = (3/2) * B_1 = constant (rotating at omega_e, constant amplitude)

Synchronous angular velocity of field rotation:
  omega_s = omega_e / p_pairs   (rad/s mechanical, for p_pairs pole pairs)

For U-V-W-U-V-W 6-slot winding: p_pairs = 1, so omega_s = omega_e (1 mechanical
revolution per electrical cycle).

Electromagnetic torque on rotor (synchronous machine):
  T_em = (3 * V_s * E_f / omega_s) * sin(delta) / X_s

where V_s = stator terminal voltage, E_f = rotor back-EMF, X_s = synchronous reactance,
delta = torque angle (angle between V_s and E_f phasors).

### 5-Phase Rotating Magnetic Field

Phase displacement angle: 72° = 2*pi/5 between adjacent stator phases.
  I_k(t) = I_0 cos(omega_e * t - (k-1) * 2*pi/5),   k = 1, 2, 3, 4, 5

Resultant rotating field amplitude:
  |B_5-phase(t)| = (5/2) * B_1   (for equal-amplitude 5-phase excitation)

Torque ripple frequency: fundamental at 10 * f_e (vs. 6 * f_e for 3-phase)
Smoother torque ripple (lower peak-to-peak variation): approximately 4% for 5-phase vs.
approximately 5% for 3-phase (values depend on harmonic content of windings).

## Gyroscope and Rigid Body Rotation Mathematics

### Euler Angles and Symmetric Top Lagrangian

Generalized coordinates: theta (nutation), phi (precession), psi (spin)

Lagrangian for symmetric top (I_1 = I_2 ≠ I_3) in gravitational field:
  L = (1/2) I_1 (theta_dot^2 + phi_dot^2 sin^2(theta))
      + (1/2) I_3 (psi_dot + phi_dot cos(theta))^2
      - M g l cos(theta)

Euler-Lagrange equations (equations of motion):
  d/dt (partial L / partial theta_dot) - partial L / partial theta = 0
  d/dt (partial L / partial phi_dot)   - partial L / partial phi   = 0
  d/dt (partial L / partial psi_dot)   - partial L / partial psi   = 0

Conservation laws:
  p_phi = partial L / partial phi_dot
        = (I_1 sin^2(theta) + I_3 cos^2(theta)) phi_dot + I_3 psi_dot cos(theta) = const.
  p_psi = partial L / partial psi_dot
        = I_3 (psi_dot + phi_dot cos(theta)) = I_3 * omega_3 = const.
  E     = (1/2) I_1 (theta_dot^2 + phi_dot^2 sin^2(theta))
          + (1/2) I_3 (psi_dot + phi_dot cos(theta))^2
          + M g l cos(theta) = const.

Steady precession (theta = const., theta_dot = 0):
  phi_dot = M g l / (p_psi - I_1 phi_dot cos(theta))
           ≈ M g l / (I_3 omega_3)   for large omega_3

Nutation frequency for the sleeping top (theta ~ 0):
  omega_nut = (p_psi / I_1) = I_3 * omega_3 / I_1

### Anomalous Vertical Force: Proposed Mathematical Extension

The claimed new term (not in standard Euler equations):
  F_z = k * d|L|/dt = k * I_3 * d(omega_3)/dt

Dimensional analysis of k:
  [F_z] = N = kg * m / s^2
  [d|L|/dt] = kg * m^2 / s^3
  Therefore [k] = s / m

This coupling constant k is the key experimental observable. Drew's experimental program
amounts to measuring the vertical displacement z(t) during spin-up, extracting:
  k = F_z / (I_3 * d(omega_3)/dt) = m * z_ddot / (I_3 * d(omega_3)/dt)

If k is measured to be non-zero and reproducible, it quantifies the anomalous coupling.
If k scales with omega_3 (rather than d(omega_3)/dt), this points to a steady-state
rather than transient mechanism.

## Astronomical / Orbital Mathematics for 3I/ATLAS

Orbital inclination: i = 5° above ecliptic
Periapsis distance: r_peri = 1.3 AU = 1.944 × 10^11 m

For a hyperbolic trajectory with periapsis at 1.3 AU, the vis-viva equation:
  v^2 = mu * (2/r - 1/a)

For a hyperbola: a < 0 (semi-major axis negative convention), e > 1.
Velocity at periapsis for v_inf given:
  v_peri = sqrt(mu * (2/r_peri) + v_inf^2)

where mu = G * M_sun = 1.327 × 10^20 m^3/s^2, v_inf = hyperbolic excess speed.

Eccentricity-inclination relationship: these are independent orbital elements for
a hyperbolic orbit. The 5° inclination is consistent with an origin in the ecliptic
plane of the source star system, or with interstellar travel approximately in the
ecliptic of the solar system.

Structural breakup spin rate (Roche limit analog for self-gravitating body):
  omega_crit = sqrt(4*pi*G*rho/3)

For rho = 500 kg/m^3 (low-density cometary material):
  omega_crit = sqrt(4 * pi * 6.674e-11 * 500 / 3)
             = sqrt(4 * 3.1416 * 6.674e-11 * 166.7)
             = sqrt(1.396e-7)
             = 3.74 × 10^-4 rad/s
  T_crit = 2*pi / omega_crit = 16,800 s ~ 4.7 hours

For rotation periods of "minutes" (T ~ 1-10 minutes = 60-600 s):
  omega_observed = 2*pi/60 ~ 0.105 rad/s   to   2*pi/600 ~ 0.0105 rad/s

Ratio: omega_observed / omega_crit ~ 0.105 / 3.74e-4 ~ 280   to   28

This means the claimed rotation is 28-280× faster than the structural breakup limit for
cometary density material — supporting the claim of anomalous strength or artificial construction.

Mass estimate (Avi Loeb): M = 33 × 10^9 tonnes = 3.3 × 10^13 kg

For reference:
  Comet 67P/Churyumov-Gerasimenko: M ~ 10^13 kg  (comparable order of magnitude)
  Density 67P: rho ~ 538 kg/m^3 (confirming low-density estimate is appropriate)

Source tweet (1922671663642292625, note_tweet):
"That crop picture was date-coded for the solar eclipse of 2017, as Year 5 of the new
Mayan calendar."

The Mayan Long Count Calendar:
  1 baktun  = 144,000 days
  1 katun   = 7,200 days
  1 tun     = 360 days
  1 uinal   = 20 days
  1 kin     = 1 day

"Year 5 of the new Mayan calendar" refers to 5 years after the end of the 13th baktun
cycle (December 21, 2012 in the Gregorian calendar). Year 5 of the new cycle:
  2012 + 5 = 2017   (consistent with the solar eclipse of August 21, 2017)

The mathematical claim is that crop formations encode not just symbolic messages but
precise date calculations using calendar arithmetic — implying the makers have knowledge
of both Gregorian and Mayan calendrical systems and can encode dates in physical geometry.
