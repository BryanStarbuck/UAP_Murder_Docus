## F. Einstein-de Haas / Barnett Effects — Classical Gyromagnetic Effects as Baseline

Relevant gyromagnetic effects that ARE within standard physics:

Einstein-de Haas (1915): mechanical rotation induced in a ferromagnet by changing magnetization
  ΔL = (g × e) / (2m_e) × ΔM × V
  where g is the g-factor (≈2 for electron spin), e is electron charge, m_e is electron mass,
  M is magnetization, V is volume, L is mechanical angular momentum

Barnett effect (1915): a ferromagnet rotating at angular velocity ω develops magnetization
  M = χ × ω / γ
  where χ is magnetic susceptibility, γ = g × e / (2m_e) is the gyromagnetic ratio

These effects are real (measured) but extremely small at laboratory scales. For a permanent
magnet rotating at ω = 1000 rad/s (≈9550 rpm), the Barnett-induced magnetization:
  M_Barnett = χ × ω / γ ≈ (10⁻³) × (10³) / (1.76 × 10¹¹ s⁻¹T⁻¹) ≈ 5.7 × 10⁻¹² T
  (for χ ≈ 10⁻³, γ ≈ 1.76 × 10¹¹ s⁻¹T⁻¹)

This is 12 orders of magnitude below the permanent magnet's intrinsic magnetization (typically
0.1-1 T). The gyromagnetic effects alone cannot explain the claimed lift or gravity generation.
The author's claim, if verified, would require a mechanism many orders of magnitude more powerful
than these known gyromagnetic effects.
