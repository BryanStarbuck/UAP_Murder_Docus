## A. Core Lift Mechanism: Spinning Magnet Rising in Three-Phase Field

### Experimental Observation
When a disc permanent magnet (70 mm diameter, 14 mm bore) mounted on a stainless steel rod
(8 mm diameter, 150 mm tall) is excited by three-phase rotating magnetic field from three lower
wire coils (UVW, tight triangular arrangement), the magnet:
  1. Spins clockwise (synchronous with the rotating field)
  2. Rises along the Z-axis (up the stainless steel rod)

This Z-axis rise is the primary lift claim. In standard electromagnetism, a spinning axially-
symmetric permanent magnet in a rotating field in the horizontal plane should not experience a
net axial (Z-direction) translational force, because:
  F_z = ∇(m · B)|_z = m_z × (∂B_z/∂z)
For a uniform horizontally-rotating field (no Z-gradient), F_z = 0.
Source: MDX 13_2032260628157714433_video; YAML 2032263041216365012.

## C. Precession and Wobble as Precursor to Rise

From MDX 13_1945035075034189824_video (three-phase magnet-on-pole demonstration):
"to get the proper motion with three-phase power, a magnet sitting on a pole, spins like this,
three-phase, the [magnet] spins around and off some exit wobble, [the magnet rises] faster at
a rise like that."

Observed sequence:
  1. Three-phase power applied to coils
  2. Magnet begins spinning (CW or CCW depending on phase sequence)
  3. Magnet begins precessing / wobbling around the vertical pole axis
  4. At higher rotational speed, precession amplitude increases
  5. Magnet rises along pole axis

The transition from pure spinning to rising is the physically interesting regime. In classical
gyroscope physics, a spinning top under gravity precesses; if the precession is driven by an
external rotating field rather than gravity, forced nutation could cause the top's center of
mass to translate along the rotation axis — but only if there is an axial force available to
drive that translation.
