## A. Three-Phase Rotating Magnetic Fields

### Three-Wire Coil Configuration (UVW)
Three wire coils powered by three-phase AC (phases U, V, W) produce a rotating magnetic field.
When a permanent magnet is placed in this field on a pole mount, the magnet spins. This is the
fundamental operating principle of the AC induction motor and three-phase synchronous motor.

In the UFO-physics context, the rotation of the magnet under three-phase excitation also produces
a precession / wobble ("gyroscopic exit wobble") which the author observes increases as rotation
speed increases. At sufficient speed, the magnet "rises."
Source: MDX 13_1945035075034189824_video.

### Six-Wire Coil Configuration (UUVVWW)
Six wire coils, powered by three-phase supply with two coils per phase (U, U, V, V, W, W), are
arranged symmetrically around a circular aperture. This creates a central void / aperture through
which a test magnet can be inserted to measure the field.

Experimental observation (MDX 13_2014594201829220352_video):
* A test magnet placed at the center of the six-coil ring spins in one direction (e.g., clockwise)
* Identical test magnets placed in the gaps between coils on the outside of the ring spin in the
  opposite direction (anti-clockwise)
* Interpretation: the rotating magnetic field produced by six-phase excitation has an inner region
  rotating clockwise and an annular outer region rotating anti-clockwise
* This dual-rotation topology — inner clockwise, outer anti-clockwise — is the claimed mechanism
  for UFO spin/counter-spin

### Two-Tier Coil Configuration (Device Construction)

Upper tier: 6 coils, UVW × 2 wiring, three-phase power → both field regions go clockwise

## E. XY-Plane Dynamics

The three-phase coils are arranged symmetrically about the vertical (Z) axis, producing a
field vector that rotates at the AC supply frequency in the horizontal plane.

The test magnet placed vertically in this field (pole axis vertical, i.e., the dipole moment
aligned with Z) does NOT experience a simple torque from the horizontal rotating field if it were
a static dipole — standard electromagnetism would predict no net torque on a dipole aligned with
the rotation axis of the field. However, the author observes the magnet does spin, which he
attributes to the "helical" nature of the field rather than classical induction or torque.

The outer annular region of the six-coil arrangement has a field rotating in the opposite
direction to the inner region — this counter-rotation in the XY-plane is observed experimentally
and is the core magnetic phenomenon of interest.
