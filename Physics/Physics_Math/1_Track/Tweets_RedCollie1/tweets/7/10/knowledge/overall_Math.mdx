## A. Vortex Physics Formula (Claimed)

### Formula
  ω = h / a

### Components
  ω: angular frequency of the spinning vortex (rad/s)
  h: height of the spinning vortex (m)
  a: cross-sectional area of the vortex = π × R² (m²)
  R: radius of the vortex (m)

### Derived Forms
  ω = h / (π × R²)
  h = ω × π × R²
  R = sqrt(h / (ω × π))

### Relationships Described
* Increasing ω (faster spin) at fixed R → larger h (taller vortex)
* Increasing R (wider vortex) at fixed ω → smaller h (shorter vortex)
* Increasing h (taller desired vortex) at fixed R → requires larger ω

### Dimensional Analysis
Standard SI:
  [ω] = s⁻¹
  [h/a] = m / m² = m⁻¹

These dimensions do not match. The formula as stated requires either:
  (a) a proportionality constant k with units [k] = m·s⁻¹: ω = k × h / a
  (b) h is not in meters but in some angular or frequency unit
  (c) The formula is an empirical scaling relation with unstated units convention

The formula is presented as received (claimed dream / psychic message) and described as
"transitional magnetism" governing UFO vortex physics. It is not derived from first principles
in the source material.

## B. Homopolar Generator EMF

### Standard Formula (Faraday Disc)
  EMF = (1/2) × B × ω × (R_outer² - R_inner²)

### Components
  B: axial magnetic field through the disc (T)
  ω: angular velocity of disc (rad/s)
  R_outer: outer radius of disc (m)
  R_inner: inner (hub) radius of disc (m)

### Derivation sketch
The EMF is derived by integrating the motional EMF (Lorentz force on charges) along a radial path:
  EMF = ∫[R_inner to R_outer] (v × B) · dr = ∫[R_inner to R_outer] (ω × r × B) dr
       = ω × B × ∫[R_inner to R_outer] r dr = ω × B × (R_outer² - R_inner²) / 2

### Applied to Paulsen Disc (12-Magnet Periphery)
For the Paulsen disc, B is not uniform — it is provided by 12 peripheral permanent magnets
(6N + 6S at 30° spacing). The effective B for EMF calculation is:

  B_eff = (1/πR²) × ∫∫ B_z(r,φ) dA

the area-averaged axial field over the disc face. For alternating polarity peripheral magnets,
the net flux through the disc may be significantly less than the peak pole field.

### Two Counter-Rotating Discs
For two discs rotating at ±ω:
  EMF₁ = (1/2) × B_eff × ω × (R₂² - R₁²)  [disc 1, CW]
  EMF₂ = (1/2) × B_eff × ω × (R₂² - R₁²)  [disc 2, CCW — same sign due to both ω and
                                               effective B_eff sign reversing together]
Total output if hubs connected in series:
  EMF_total = EMF₁ + EMF₂ = B_eff × ω × (R₂² - R₁²)

## C. Gyroscopic Precession Rate

### Formula
  Ω = τ / L = τ / (I × ω_spin)

### Components
  Ω: precession angular velocity (rad/s)
  τ: applied torque (N·m)
  L: angular momentum = I × ω_spin (kg·m²/s)
  I: moment of inertia of spinning magnet about spin axis (kg·m²)
  ω_spin: spin angular velocity (rad/s)

For a disc magnet (solid cylinder approximation):
  I = (1/2) × m × r²
where m is disc mass and r is disc radius.

For the 70 mm diameter disc magnet (r = 35 mm = 0.035 m):
  I = (1/2) × m × (0.035)² = (1/2) × m × 1.225 × 10⁻³ m²
  I ≈ 6.1 × 10⁻⁴ × m [kg·m²] for mass m in kg

Applied torque from three-phase rotating field on the precessing magnet:
  τ = m_magnetic × B × sin(α)
where m_magnetic is the magnetic dipole moment of the disc (A·m²), B is the field amplitude,
and α is the angle between the magnetic moment and the field.

At resonance (spin frequency = field frequency), α → 0, τ → 0, and precession rate decreases.
The rise behavior at high rpm is argued to occur near or above this resonance condition.

## D. Euler's Equations for Symmetric Top Under Three-Phase Torque

### Euler's Equations
  I_⊥ × (dω₁/dt) + (I_∥ - I_⊥) × ω₂ × ω₃ = τ₁
  I_⊥ × (dω₂/dt) + (I_⊥ - I_∥) × ω₁ × ω₃ = τ₂
  I_∥ × (dω₃/dt) = τ₃

### Under Three-Phase Rotating Torque
  τ₁(t) = τ₀ × cos(ω_s × t)
  τ₂(t) = τ₀ × sin(ω_s × t)
  τ₃(t) = 0

This drives forced nutation. At resonance ω_s = Ω_nutation (natural nutation frequency):
  Ω_nutation = I_∥ × ω_spin / I_⊥

the response amplitude grows, leading to large-angle precession and potentially the rising
behavior observed.

## E. Rotating Magnetic Field Torque on Axial Dipole

### Setup
Permanent magnet with dipole moment m = m_z ẑ placed in rotating field:
  B(t) = B₀ × [cos(ω_s t) x̂ + sin(ω_s t) ŷ]

### Torque
  τ = m × B = m_z ẑ × B₀ [cos(ω_s t) x̂ + sin(ω_s t) ŷ]
  τ = m_z × B₀ [cos(ω_s t) (ẑ × x̂) + sin(ω_s t) (ẑ × ŷ)]
  τ = m_z × B₀ [cos(ω_s t) ŷ - sin(ω_s t) x̂]

Z-component of torque — consistent with the author's observation that North/South pole orientation

a different mechanism (helical field, as claimed).

## F. Three-Phase Rotating Field Mathematics

### Field Components
Three coils (U, V, W) at 120° angular spacing (φ = 0°, 120°, 240°) with sinusoidal currents:
  I_U(t) = I₀ × cos(ω_s t)
  I_V(t) = I₀ × cos(ω_s t - 2π/3)
  I_W(t) = I₀ × cos(ω_s t - 4π/3)

Each coil i produces a field contribution along its axis:
  B_i(t) = B₀ × I_i(t) × [cos(φ_i) x̂ + sin(φ_i) ŷ]

Summing all three:
  B_total(t) = (3/2) × B₀ × I₀ × [cos(ω_s t) x̂ + sin(ω_s t) ŷ]

This is a field of constant amplitude (3/2)B₀I₀ rotating at angular frequency ω_s.

For the six-coil configuration (UU, VV, WW — two coils per phase):
Each phase excites two coils (at different angular positions), modifying the field amplitude
and spatial harmonics but maintaining the fundamental rotating field. The key geometric result
is that the configuration creates a central "hole" (null or reversed-field region) where the
field rotates opposite to the outer annular region.

### Homopolar Generator Power Output

Power delivered to external load resistance R_load from homopolar generator:
  P_output = EMF² / (R_internal + R_load)

For maximum power transfer: R_load = R_internal
  P_max = EMF² / (4 × R_internal)

where R_internal = ρ × (path length) / (cross-sectional area of current path in disc).

For a solid disc of conductivity σ and thickness t:
  R_internal = (1/σ) × ∫[R_inner to R_outer] dr / (2πrt) = ln(R_outer/R_inner) / (2π × σ × t)

For the Paulsen disc (R_inner ≈ hub radius, R_outer ≈ disc radius):
  R_internal ≈ ln(R_outer/R_inner) / (2π × σ × t)

With counter-rotating disc 2 in series:

  EMF_total = 2 × EMF_single

  (Power doubles for two discs in series, as expected.)

### Corbino Disc Hall Angle

The Hall angle θ_H for current in the Corbino disc in magnetic field B_z:
  tan(θ_H) = μ × B_z
where μ is the charge carrier mobility (m²/V·s).

For a conducting disc material (e.g., copper: μ ≈ 4.4 × 10⁻³ m²/V·s) at B_z = 1 T:
  tan(θ_H) = 4.4 × 10⁻³ — Hall angle ≈ 0.25°

For a semiconductor (e.g., InSb: μ ≈ 7.8 m²/V·s) at B_z = 1 T:
  tan(θ_H) = 7.8 — Hall angle ≈ 83°

The Corbino effect is most pronounced at large Hall angles (semiconducting or high-field regime).
For maximum rotational coupling, a high-mobility Corbino disc material would be preferred.
This is not discussed in the source material but follows directly from Corbino disc theory.
