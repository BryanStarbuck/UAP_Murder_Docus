### Homopolar Motor Physics

The Faraday disk (homopolar) motor/generator (1831) converts between rotational kinetic energy and electrical energy:

Generator mode (mechanical → electrical):
EMF = (1/2) ω B R²
Power output: P_gen = EMF × I = (1/2) ω B R² × I

Motor mode (electrical → mechanical):
Torque: τ = (1/2) I B R²
Power input: P_motor = τ × ω = (1/2) I B R² × ω

Efficiency: η = P_out / P_in ≤ 1 (conservation of energy applies in standard operation)

Liquid metal variant advantages:
* Zero contact resistance at current input/output (liquid-solid interface eliminates brush friction losses in conventional homopolar machines).
* Potential MHD self-organization: liquid metal in rotating magnetic field develops toroidal circulation that may reduce internal ohmic losses.
* Gallium alloys (Galinstan, melting point −19°C) or mercury have conductivity σ ≈ 3-4 × 10⁶ S/m — comparable to metals, enabling efficient current conduction.

No over-unity energy claim is explicitly made for the homopolar motor per se — it is positioned as an efficient conventional energy converter for powering the propulsion system.

## 6. The Leedskalnin Energy Model

Edward Leedskalnin's published work ("Magnetic Current," 1945) proposed:
* Electricity consists of individual north and south magnetic pole particles streaming in opposite directions simultaneously.
* Both DC and AC currents are composed of these "magnetic currents."
* The wheel-shaped flywheel with embedded magnets at Coral Castle demonstrates the generation and control of these magnetic currents.

Modern interpretation in Drew's framework:
* Leedskalnin's "magnetic current" concept — individual N and S pole particles — may correspond to the topological defects (magnetic monopoles, or vortex lines in a superconductor, or domain wall excitations) that carry net magnetic charge in condensed matter systems.
* If macroscopic magnetic "monopole currents" can be sustained, they would transform the energy landscape: a monopole current through a toroidal conductor could sustain flux indefinitely (analogous to a persistent current in a superconducting loop) without resistive dissipation.

No experimental evidence for macroscopic magnetic monopole currents is established in mainstream physics. Drew's framework treats Leedskalnin's device as an empirical demonstration rather than a theoretically-derived system.
