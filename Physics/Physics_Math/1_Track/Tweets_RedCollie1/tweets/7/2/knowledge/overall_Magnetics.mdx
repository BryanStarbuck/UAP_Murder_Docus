A rotating magnetic field introduces a time-varying B(r, t) which can produce non-trivial trajectories in the surrounding gas — particularly vortical motion if the field rotation imparts angular momentum to the plasma via the J × B body force (magnetohydrodynamic force density f = J × B, where J is current density and B is magnetic flux density).

* Applied voltage: 6.6 kV (sufficient for Paschen-curve breakdown in air at appropriate gap geometries, producing glow discharge / weakly ionized plasma).
* Geometry claimed: spinning N-S magnet in horizontal orientation.
* Result: ionized gas vortex directed downward toward the magnet.

This downward-spinning ionized gas implies by Newton's third law a reaction upward force on the magnet-apparatus system — consistent with the claimed "upward Lorentz force" on the stationary magnet frame, providing a possible Z-axis propulsion mechanism.

## 3. Rotor Magnet Polarity Determines Rotation Direction Relative to Stator Field

This is the central non-textbook magnetic finding documented in this dataset.

### Finding A: N-S-N-S Alternating Rotor — Contra-Rotation

Configuration:
* Stator: 6 coils, UVW-UVW, field rotating ACW.
* Rotor: 8 magnets arranged N-S-N-S-N-S-N-S (alternating polarity around the rotor circumference).
* Observed result: rotor spins CLOCKWISE — opposite to the stator field direction.

Source (video transcription 13_2017866917693034499): "NSNSNS, eight magnets, six wire coils spinning anti-clockwise and you turn it on the rotor spins clockwise the other direction. This is not in any textbook."

Source (2017867745082462217.yaml note_tweet): "The rotating magnetic field goes anti-clockwise, while the rotor magnets spin clockwise! Completely 'impossible' by all textbook theories of academia."

### Finding B: N-N-N-N Same-Polarity Rotor — Co-Rotation

* Rotor magnets arranged N-N-N-N (all same polarity).
* Result: rotor spins WITH the rotating magnetic field (co-rotation, expected synchronous behavior).

Source (referenced_tweet 2017453696750334151): "A ring of 4 permanent magnets will spin either WITH a rotating magnetic field (made by 6 wire coils wired as U-V-W-U-V-W), or AGAINST a rotating magnetic field, depending on whether those 4 rotor magnets are arranged as N-S-N-S or N-N-N-N. The same principle holds true for 9..."

### Textbook Context and the Anomaly

In standard induction motor theory, a squirrel-cage or wound rotor experiences torque due to induced eddy currents or reluctance effects that drive it to follow the rotating stator field. A permanent magnet rotor in a synchronous motor is attracted to the rotating field and co-rotates with it.

The claim here is that with alternating N-S polarity around the rotor circumference, the interaction geometry reverses, producing sustained contra-rotation. This is not described in standard motor design literature for permanent magnet synchronous machines (PMSM) or switched reluctance machines (SRM).

A possible classical mechanism: if the alternating N-S arrangement creates a spatial flux harmonic that interacts with a sub-harmonic of the stator rotating field, producing a backward-traveling wave component in the air-gap flux, the rotor might lock to that backward wave. However, Drew states this mechanism is unknown in academic literature.

* One spins CW, one spins CCW — counter-rotation is the critical condition.

Source (1927022713874366668.yaml text): "They also drew this crop picture of how they fly using a 'wobbling magnet' (2005)."

### Physical Mechanism Hypothesized

A spinning magnetic dipole (magnetic gyroscope) precesses about the applied field axis (analogous to Larmor precession for magnetic moments: dM/dt = γ M × B_eff, where γ is the gyromagnetic ratio). If the precession of a large-scale magnetic flywheel creates a periodic torque imbalance, the resultant force on the center of mass can have a net Z-axis component.

For a gyroscope of moment of inertia I spinning at angular velocity ω in an effective gravitational field g with torque τ = r × F_gravity, the precession rate Ω_p = τ/(I ω) = Mgr/(Iω). As ω increases from low to high, Ω_p decreases but the gyroscopic stabilization force increases — Drew claims this increasing gyroscopic force manifests as an upward anti-gravity force.

## 6. Homopolar Motor (Faraday Disk) — Liquid Metal Variant

Source (2005202731997552647.yaml): "the crop circles show a homopolar motor based apparently on liquid conductive metal."

The homopolar motor (Faraday disk, 1831) produces torque via: F = q(v × B), where a radial current I flows outward from the central shaft through a conducting disk rotating in an axial magnetic field B. Torque τ = (1/2) I B R² where R is disk radius.

The liquid conductive metal variant:
* Replaces solid conducting disk with a liquid metal (e.g., mercury Hg, gallium Ga, or gallium alloys such as Galinstan).
* The liquid metal provides: zero mechanical friction at sliding contacts, continuous current conduction without commutator wear, potential for variable geometry and self-organizing flow patterns.
* Potential connection to magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) propulsion: J × B forces on the liquid metal could produce directed fluid flow, creating thrust without moving mechanical parts.

The crop circle encoding of this configuration (as claimed by Drew) implies the UAP propulsion system may use a homopolar-type interaction in a liquid conducting medium rather than a solid rotor.
