## 1. Lorentz Force Law (Classical Electrodynamics)

The fundamental equation underlying all magnetic force claims in this dataset:

F = q(E + v × B)

Components:
* F: electromagnetic force on charge carrier [N]
* q: electric charge [C] (q = −e for electron, q = +Ze for ion of atomic number Z)
* E: electric field vector [V/m]
* v: velocity vector of charge carrier [m/s]
* B: magnetic flux density vector [T]
* ×: cross product (produces force perpendicular to both v and B)

Magnetic-only Lorentz force (E = 0 case):
F_mag = q(v × B)

This is the basis for:
* Circular/helical trajectories of charged particles in magnetic fields.
* Motor torque on current-carrying conductors.
* The claimed upward reaction force on the spinning magnet from deflected ionized air.

## 2. Rotating Magnetic Field — Three-Phase Stator

For a 3-phase, p-pole-pair stator with coils at angular positions θ_k = 2π(k-1)/n_coils, k = 1,...,n_coils, carrying currents:

I_k(t) = I_0 cos(ωt − 2π(k-1)/3) [for n_coils = 6, UVW-UVW pattern, alternating phases]

The resultant magnetic flux density at azimuthal angle θ in the air gap:

B(θ, t) = (3/2) B_0 cos(ωt − pθ) [forward-traveling wave, fundamental]

plus harmonic terms:
B_harmonic(θ, t) = Σ_n B_n cos(ωt ± npθ) [backward and forward harmonics]

where:
* ω = 2πf [rad/s], f = supply frequency [Hz]
* p = pole pair number (= 3 for 6-coil UVW-UVW winding)
* B_0 = peak flux density per phase [T]

For the observed UVW-UVW, 6-coil geometry: p = 3 → three full flux cycles around the stator circumference.

## 3. Rotor Synchronous Speed

For synchronous operation (rotor locking to stator rotating field):

n_s = f / p [rev/s]   or   ω_s = ω / p [rad/s]

For contra-rotation to the fundamental (backward wave locking):

n_contra = −f / p = −n_s [rev/s] (opposite sign convention)

Drew's 8-magnet N-S-N-S rotor has p_rotor = 4 pole pairs. The mismatch:
Δp = p_rotor − p_stator = 4 − 3 = 1

The rotor may interact with the (p_stator ± p_rotor) = (3 ± 4) spatial harmonics of the stator field:
* p = 7 harmonic: forward-traveling, slow.
* p = −1 harmonic (i.e., p = 1 backward): backward-traveling component.

If the backward p = 1 component is significant, the rotor could lock to it and contra-rotate at:
n_contra = −f/1 = −f [rev/s]

This is a much higher speed than the fundamental synchronous speed n_s = f/3, which may explain the energetic nature of the observed contra-rotation.

## 4. Torque in a Magnetic Motor

General expression for electromagnetic torque on a rotor:

τ = (p/2) × (3/2) × (L_d − L_q) I_d I_q + (p/2) × (3/2) × λ_f I_q

For a surface permanent magnet motor (SPMSM, where L_d ≈ L_q):
τ ≈ (3p/4) λ_f I_q

where:
* p = pole pair count
* L_d, L_q = d-axis and q-axis inductances [H]
* I_d, I_q = d-axis and q-axis current components [A]
* λ_f = flux linkage from permanent magnets [Wb]

For the contra-rotating configuration, this formula applies with a negative effective Ω_r (rotor angular velocity), meaning the torque direction is reversed relative to standard operation.

## 5. Homopolar Motor / Faraday Disk EMF

For a conducting disk of radius R rotating at angular velocity ω_disk in uniform axial magnetic field B_axial:

EMF = (1/2) ω_disk B_axial R²

Torque for drive current I through the disk:
τ_homo = (1/2) I B_axial R²

Power:
P = τ_homo × ω_disk = (1/2) I B_axial R² ω_disk = EMF × I

For the liquid metal variant (replacing solid disk with conducting liquid of conductivity σ_liquid):
* Current density: J = σ_liquid (E + v × B) [A/m²]
* Body force density: f = J × B [N/m³]
* Net torque from integration over liquid volume V:
τ_MHD = ∫_V (r × (J × B)) dV

where r is the position vector from the rotation axis.

## 6. Gyroscope Precession Rate

For a gyroscope of:
* Mass M
* Spin angular velocity ω (about spin axis, aligned with symmetry axis)
* Moment of inertia about spin axis: I_spin
* Center of mass offset from pivot: r [m]

Precession angular velocity:
Ω_p = Mgr / (I_spin ω) = τ_gravity / L

where L = I_spin ω = angular momentum magnitude, τ_gravity = Mgr = gravitational torque magnitude.

As ω → ∞: Ω_p → 0 (gyroscope stabilizes vertically).
As ω → 0: Ω_p → ∞ (gyroscope falls chaotically).

Drew's anti-gravity hypothesis requires identifying a term F_z(ω) such that:
F_z = dL_z/dt > 0 for increasing ω

In standard mechanics, for a gyroscope precessing about a vertical axis:
L_z = I_spin ω cos(θ) + I_transverse Ω_p sin(θ) cos(θ)

where θ = nutation angle. For constant θ and ω increasing, L_z increases, but the required torque τ_z = dL_z/dt must come from somewhere — the pivot reaction force. If the pivot is replaced by the ground reaction on a craft, an increasing L_z would manifest as an increasing reaction force at the ground contact — consistent with apparent weight reduction (anti-gravity).

## 7. Periodic Lift Force from Oscillating Coupled Flywheels

For two magnetically coupled counter-spinning flywheels, each of moment of inertia I and magnetic moment m, spinning at ±ω:

Magnetic interaction energy between two aligned dipoles at separation d:
U(d, φ) = (μ₀/4π) [m₁·m₂/d³ − 3(m₁·r̂)(m₂·r̂)/d³]

where φ = relative azimuthal angle between the dipoles.

For counter-spinning flywheels, φ(t) = 2ωt (relative angle advances at twice the spin rate). The interaction energy oscillates at 2ω:
U(t) = U_0 cos(2ωt + φ_0)

The vertical force between the two flywheels:
F_z = −∂U/∂d = F_0(d) cos(2ωt + φ_0)

The time-averaged vertical force is zero for symmetric counter-spinning. However, if the oscillation is asymmetric (different pole strengths above vs. below, or offset geometry), a net bias emerges:

<F_z> = (1/T) ∫₀ᵀ F_z(t) dt ≠ 0

This net time-averaged vertical force is the proposed lift mechanism. The "periodic upward motion" described by Drew corresponds to the positive half-cycles of this oscillating F_z.

## 8. Comet Nucleus Rotation — Angular Momentum Constraints

Source: claimed rotation period of 3I/ATLAS core = minutes.

For a spherical comet nucleus of radius R and density ρ rotating at angular velocity ω_comet:
* Moment of inertia: I = (2/5) M R² = (2/5)(4πρR³/3)R² = (8π/15)ρR⁵
* Angular momentum: L = Iω_comet = (8π/15)ρR⁵ω_comet
* Centrifugal surface acceleration: a_c = ω_comet² R
* Gravitational surface acceleration: g_s = GM/R² = G(4πρR³/3)/R² = (4π/3)GρR

For structural integrity (centrifugal breakup limit):
a_c < g_s → ω_comet² R < (4π/3)GρR → ω_comet < √[(4π/3)Gρ]

For ρ = 500 kg/m³ (typical comet density):
ω_max = √[(4π/3)(6.674×10⁻¹¹)(500)] ≈ √(1.4×10⁻⁷) ≈ 3.75×10⁻⁴ rad/s
T_min = 2π/ω_max ≈ 16,750 s ≈ 4.6 hours

For a rotation period of minutes (say T = 5 min = 300 s):
ω_required = 2π/300 ≈ 0.021 rad/s

Ratio: ω_required/ω_max ≈ 56 — requiring centrifugal acceleration 56² ≈ 3,136 times stronger than gravitational cohesion.

This means: a natural cometary body CANNOT rotate with a period of minutes without flying apart, unless it is either:
1. Made of anomalously high-density, high-tensile material (non-ice composition), or
2. An artificial rigid structure with sufficient internal binding strength.

This is the mathematical basis for Drew's claim that 3I/ATLAS's fast-spinning core implies artificiality.

## 9. Ionization Voltage and Plasma Formation

Applied voltage for ionization: 6.6 kV (stated in referenced tweet).

Paschen's Law for air breakdown:
V_breakdown = B(pd) / [ln(pd) + ln(A) − ln(ln(1 + 1/γ_se))]

where:
* p = gas pressure [Pa]
* d = electrode gap [m]
* A ≈ 12 (cm·Pa)⁻¹, B ≈ 365 V/(cm·Pa) for air
* γ_se = secondary electron emission coefficient

At atmospheric pressure (p = 101,325 Pa = 760 Torr), the Paschen minimum for air:
V_min ≈ 330 V at pd ≈ 0.75 Torr·cm

For V = 6,600 V at atmospheric pressure:
pd = V/B × f(pd) → gap d ≈ several mm to cm (depending on electrode geometry)

The 6.6 kV excitation produces glow discharge or streamer discharge in air, creating a weakly ionized plasma suitable for MHD interaction with the magnet's rotating field.

## 10. Motional EMF for Rotating Conducting Sphere

For a conducting sphere of radius a rotating at angular velocity ω about its symmetry axis (z-axis) in an external magnetic field B = B_0 ẑ:

The motional EMF induced along any radius from center to surface:
ε = ∫₀ᵃ (v × B) · dr = ∫₀ᵃ (ωr × B_0 ẑ) · r̂ dr

For v = ωr sin(θ) φ̂ (azimuthal velocity at polar angle θ) and B = B_0 ẑ:
(v × B) = ωr sin(θ) B_0 (φ̂ × ẑ) = ωr sin(θ) B_0 (−r̂ sin(θ) + θ̂ cos(θ)) ... [spherical geometry]

Integrating along the equatorial radius (θ = π/2):
ε = ∫₀ᵃ ωr B_0 dr = (1/2) ω B_0 a²

This is the same result as the Faraday disk (homopolar generator), confirming the spherical conducting ball in the LED demonstration is operating on homopolar generator principles.

For a = 0.025 m (2.5 cm radius), B_0 = 0.1 T, ω = 100 rad/s:
ε = (1/2)(100)(0.1)(0.025²) = 3.125 × 10⁻³ V = 3.1 mV

To light an LED requires V_LED ≈ 1.8−3.5 V, so either:
* ω must be much higher (ω ≈ 57,600 rad/s for a 1.8 V LED), or
* Multiple turns / amplification stage used, or
* The demonstration uses a different source geometry with stronger B or more favorable geometry.
