## 1. 3-Phase Rotating Magnetic Field — Mathematical Derivation

Source: YAML 2017877032907247916, YAML 2021005921309385050, MDX 13_2017872609355116544_video.mdx

### Phase Currents

Three coils displaced 120° apart in physical space carry currents:

    I_A(t) = I₀ sin(ωt)
    I_B(t) = I₀ sin(ωt + 2π/3)
    I_C(t) = I₀ sin(ωt + 4π/3)

Each coil i produces a magnetic field B_i(t) = B₀ sin(ωt + φᵢ) along its own axis n̂ᵢ,
where φ_A = 0, φ_B = 2π/3, φ_C = 4π/3.

Defining coil axes in the XY-plane:
    n̂_A = x̂,  n̂_B = -x̂/2 + √3ŷ/2,  n̂_C = -x̂/2 - √3ŷ/2

Total field (superposition):
    B(t) = B₀ [ sin(ωt) x̂
              + sin(ωt + 2π/3)(-x̂/2 + √3ŷ/2)
              + sin(ωt + 4π/3)(-x̂/2 - √3ŷ/2) ]

Expanding with trigonometric identities sin(A+B) = sinA cosB + cosA sinB:

    B_x(t) = B₀ [sin(ωt) - (1/2)sin(ωt + 2π/3) - (1/2)sin(ωt + 4π/3)]
            = B₀ sin(ωt) [1 - (1/2)cos(2π/3) - (1/2)cos(4π/3)]
            - B₀ cos(ωt) [(1/2)sin(2π/3) + (1/2)sin(4π/3)]
            = B₀ sin(ωt) [1 + 1/4 + 1/4] - B₀ cos(ωt) [√3/4 - √3/4]
            = (3/2) B₀ sin(ωt)

    B_y(t) = B₀ [(√3/2)sin(ωt + 2π/3) - (√3/2)sin(ωt + 4π/3)]
            = (√3 B₀/2) [sin(ωt + 2π/3) - sin(ωt + 4π/3)]
            Using sum-to-product: sin(A) - sin(B) = 2cos((A+B)/2)sin((A-B)/2)
            A+B = 2ωt + 2π, A-B = 2π/3... etc.
            Result: B_y(t) = -(3/2) B₀ cos(ωt)

    Therefore:
    B(t) = (3/2) B₀ [sin(ωt) x̂ - cos(ωt) ŷ]
          = (3/2) B₀ [cos(ωt - π/2) x̂ + cos(ωt + π) ŷ] ... (rotates clockwise)
    or equivalently (choosing phase convention):
    B(t) = (3/2) B₀ [cos(ωt) x̂ + sin(ωt) ŷ]  (counter-clockwise)

Magnitude: |B| = (3/2) B₀ = constant.

Conclusion: The 3-phase coil arrangement produces a rotating field of CONSTANT magnitude
(3/2)B₀ rotating at angular frequency ω in the XY-plane. This is the key mathematical
property distinguishing 3-phase from single-phase (which produces oscillating, not rotating,
field of varying magnitude).

## 2. Torque on Magnetic Dipole in Rotating Field

Source: YAML 2017877032907247916, YAML 1978591957686661487

### Synchronous Torque

A magnetic dipole moment m in field B experiences:
    τ = m × B

For a ring magnet spinning at angular velocity ω_m about Z-axis (synchronized to field):
    m = m (sin θ cos(ω_m t) x̂ + sin θ sin(ω_m t) ŷ + cos θ ẑ)

where θ is the angle between m and the Z-axis.

If ω_m = ω (synchronous): the torque τ = m × B(t) has a constant component in the ẑ direction
driving continuous rotation. The synchronous torque (pull-out torque) is:
    τ_max = m × (3/2) B₀

For the magnet to spin at ω, the applied field must supply enough torque to overcome friction
and any magnetic braking (homopolar effect).

## 3. Gyroscopic Precession — Force Redirection

Source: YAML 2017877032907247916 (note_tweet)

### Angular Momentum of Spinning Flywheel

    L = I Ω ẑ   (spinning flywheel, moment of inertia I, spin rate Ω)

where ẑ is the flywheel spin axis (vertical, for a horizontal flywheel).

### Applied Torque from Newton 3rd Law Reaction

If a downward force F_down = -F ẑ is applied at radius r from the flywheel center:
    τ_applied = r × F_down = r x̂ × (-F ẑ) = rF ŷ   (or similar, depending on direction)

### Resulting Precession

    dL/dt = τ_applied
    dΩ_precession / dt = τ_applied / |L| = rF / (I Ω)

Precession occurs in the horizontal (XY) plane, at angular velocity:
    Ω_p = τ_applied / |L| = rF / (I Ω)

Physical consequence: the downward force F_down causes the flywheel to precess horizontally,
NOT to move downward. The Z-component of motion is eliminated; the force is "redirected" into
the XY-plane as precession. This is the mechanism Drew proposes for cancelling the Newton
reaction force.

### Quantitative Condition for Full Redirection

For the precession to fully absorb the reaction without significant Z-axis displacement:
    Ω_p << Ω   (gyroscopic approximation valid)
    i.e., rF << I Ω²

This requires fast spin (large Ω) and/or large moment of inertia (large I) of the counter-
spinning flywheel. Drew states the flywheel must be "fast-spinning" for this to work.

## 4. Homopolar Generator / Faraday Disk — Induced EMF and Force

Source: YAML 1978591957686661487 (note_tweet), txt file 3_1978591003490848768.txt

### Induced EMF in Rotating Conducting Disk

For a conducting disk of radius R rotating at angular velocity ω_d about its axis (Z-axis)
in an axial magnetic field B_z:

The motional EMF for a radial element dr at radius r:
    dV = v × B · dr̂ = (ω_d r) × B_z · dr = ω_d B_z r dr

Total EMF from center to rim:
    V = ∫₀ᴿ ω_d B_z r dr = (1/2) ω_d B_z R²

### Current and Force

If the circuit resistance is R_c:
    I_rad = V / R_c = ω_d B_z R² / (2 R_c)

The axial Lorentz force on the radial current in the field B_z:
    F_z = ∫ I_rad × B_z × 2πr dr / R  (integrated over disk)

Simplified: the electromagnetic braking torque on the disk is:
    τ_brake = B_z² R⁴ ω_d / (4 R_c)

This opposes rotation (Lenz's law). The corresponding axial force component (the "Lorentz
homopolar force" cited by Drew as the counter-spin / downward reaction in the UFO model)
depends on the coupling geometry.

### Railgun Force (Linear)

For a linear railgun with rail separation d, rail radius r_rail, current I:
    F_Lorentz = (μ₀ I² / π) ln(d / r_rail)   [inductance gradient formula]

This is the force on the projectile. In the circular railgun variant (trefoil crop circle
encoding), the force is tangential, producing torque rather than linear thrust.
