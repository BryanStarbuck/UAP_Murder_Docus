## 1. N-S-N-S-N-S Coil Configuration Produces Levitation — Claimed Break from Classical EM Theory

This is the primary claimed non-classical result in the source files.

The claim:
"Nobody has ever seen magnetic rotation/levitation due to alternating N-S-N-S-N-S wire coils,
given 3-phase power, before today. All textbooks would suggest that those wire coils have to be
N-N-N-N-N-N. The new result thus breaks all known 'laws of physics'."
(YAML 1927341040354169205, note_tweet)

What classical electromagnetic theory predicts:

In a standard 3-phase induction motor (synchronous or asynchronous), the stator windings are
wound such that all poles in the same phase group produce field in the same direction. The
spatial arrangement of phases produces a rotating field at the center. The key classical result
is that all poles of the rotating stator field point the same "inward" direction per phase, so
the field amplitudes add coherently.

For an N-S-N-S-N-S arrangement: adjacent coils have opposing polarity. In the simplest
analysis, the fields of adjacent N and S coils partially cancel in the interior, reducing
the net rotating field amplitude. The expectation is that a disc magnet in the center would
experience a weaker rotating field and therefore weaker torque and less tendency to rotate,
let alone levitate.

The experimental result: rotation AND levitation still occur. Specifically:
  * A 70mm disc magnet placed at the center of 6 N-S-N-S-N-S coils energized with UVW 3-phase
    power spins and rises.
  * An 80mm disc magnet (6mm rod, 10mm hole) also rises under the same configuration.

Why this might occur — possible (not proven) explanations:
  (a) The interior field is not zero despite N-S alternation. For finite coil geometry,
      the fields do not perfectly cancel at the center of the ring. The residual field
      may be sufficient to drive rotation.
  (b) The alternating polarity creates a higher spatial harmonic content of the field.
      The rotor may couple to a spatial harmonic (2nd, 3rd order) that still rotates at
      omega_s and provides torque and lift.
  (c) The field cancellation at large distances (external field = 0) is compatible with
      non-zero field at the interior of the ring — the interior field depends on the
      geometry of the ring radius vs. distance to center.

None of these alternative explanations are provided in the source materials; they are
analytical possibilities consistent with known physics. The author claims the result
"breaks all known laws of physics" — this is likely an overstatement. A more precise
statement: it breaks the EXPECTATION derived from simplified induction motor theory,
but a full calculation of the N-S-N-S-N-S field configuration is needed to determine
whether it is truly anomalous.

Experimental status: CONFIRMED (laboratory demonstration). Theoretical status: UNEXPLAINED
within the source materials.

## 2. Disc Magnet Rise Without Scale Weight Change — Separation of Thrust from Gravitational Mass

Classical Newtonian mechanics and classical electrodynamics are entirely consistent with
this result — it is NOT strictly beyond Newtonian mechanics. However, it is beyond common
expectation and relevant to claims about "anti-gravity":

Result: when a disc magnet levitates against a stop inside a 3-coil rotating field device,
the total weight of the device on a laboratory scale does NOT change.

Classical explanation: the upward force on the rotor is an internal force of the device
system (coil-array + rotor). By Newton's 3rd law, the upward force on the rotor equals the
downward reaction force on the coil array frame. The scale measures the total weight of
the device (frame + rotor) which is unaffected by internal forces. Total system weight =
sum of all gravitational forces = (m_frame + m_rotor) * g = constant.

Why this is relevant to "beyond Newtonian" claims:
  If the lift force were due to gravitational mass reduction (true anti-gravity),
  the scale WOULD read less. The scale does NOT read less. This rules out gravitational
  mass modification as the mechanism. The effect is purely electromagnetic thrust, consistent
  with Newton's 3rd law.

The author says: "It's like a repeller, but there's no change of anti-gravity weight of
the whole device on the scale." This correctly identifies the limitation: it's a repulsion,
not a gravitational modification. For practical UAP propulsion, the thrust would need to
be reacted against something external to the craft (the atmosphere, Earth's magnetic field,
or the quantum vacuum — the last of which would cross into "beyond Newtonian" territory).

## 3. Gyroscope on Pole Generating Upward Force — Claimed Non-Classical Hypothesis

Standard Newtonian mechanics prediction for gyroscope on a pole in a gravity field:
  The gyroscope precesses at Omega_p = (m*g*l) / (I*omega) about the vertical axis.
  There is NO net upward force generated. The gyroscope's weight is fully borne by the pole.

The author's hypothesis: a magnetic gyroscope rotor, spun at high omega by a 5-phase rotating
field, may generate an upward force opposing gravity.

"Gyroscopes usually DO sit on a stable pole! Upward force to oppose downward gravity?
Nobody knows, we have to test this new but plausible hypothesis in the lab."
(YAML 1942032833985560854, note_tweet)

If confirmed in the laboratory, this would be a genuine non-classical result. Known
related research:
  * Hayasaka and Takeuchi (1989): claimed measurement of weight reduction in rapidly spinning
    gyroscope; not consistently replicated.
  * Eric Laithwaite's gyroscope demonstrations at Royal Institution (1974, 1994): claimed
    gyroscopes feel lighter under specific precession conditions; mainstream physics rejects
    the claim as failure to correctly account for centripetal forces.
  * Woodward Mach Effect: involves oscillating masses, not rotating gyroscopes.

The Buga sphere 5-phase gyroscope hypothesis has NOT been experimentally tested as of
the source file dates (author states "we have to test this").

## 4. Alternating Polarity Coil Producing Net Rotating Field — Theoretical Tension

The classical field superposition calculation for N-S-N-S-N-S coils:

6 coils at angles phi_k = (k-1)*60 degrees:
  Even k (k=2,4,6): south-up, contribute -B_0 in z-direction at their positions
  Odd k (k=1,3,5): north-up, contribute +B_0 in z-direction

In 3-phase wiring: coil pairs {1,4}, {2,5}, {3,6} are on phases U, V, W respectively,
with coils in each pair having opposite orientation (since the coils at 180 degrees apart
in the ring have opposite N-S designation).

The resulting field at the ring center:
  A coil of moment +m at angle phi contributes field component proportional to cos(phi)*x + sin(phi)*y
  A coil of moment -m at angle phi+pi (its partner 180 degrees away) contributes:
    -m * (cos(phi+pi)*x + sin(phi+pi)*y) = -m * (-cos(phi)*x - sin(phi)*y) = +m*(cos(phi)*x + sin(phi)*y)

This means the coil and its opposite partner BOTH contribute in the SAME direction at the center
despite having opposite z-orientations! The "cancellation" occurs in the external field (at large r),
not at the center. At the center of the ring, the alternating N-S arrangement may actually produce
the SAME or comparable rotating field as the all-same N-N-N arrangement, when the coils are
connected to opposing phases such that the current directions compensate for the polarity reversal.

This analysis suggests the "break from classical physics" claim may be a misinterpretation of
the classical result. The experiment may be entirely consistent with Maxwell's equations when
properly analyzed with the full coil geometry. However, this is an analysis the author has not
performed — and it does not diminish the experimental result itself.

## 6. Magnetic Inversion Symmetry of Lift Force

The experimental result that the disc magnet rises regardless of whether north or south pole
faces up (after inversion test) is a non-trivial result that deserves theoretical examination:

In standard magnetic levitation (Earnshaw's theorem context): a permanent magnet cannot be
stably levitated by a static magnetic field in 3D — at least one axis is always unstable.
Active or dynamic field control is required for stable levitation.

In the rotating field case: the dynamic rotating field provides a time-averaged stable
coupling that violates the assumptions of Earnshaw's theorem (which applies to static fields).
The time-averaged lift force F_z can be non-zero and upward for a rotating dipole in a
rotating field.

The inversion result (both orientations lift) suggests F_z is independent of the sign of
the rotor's axial dipole moment — or that the coupling mechanism that produces F_z does not
depend on whether the axial component m_z is positive or negative.

One possible mechanism: the torque that drives XY-plane rotation may be the same regardless
of axial polarity (since the rotating field couples to the equatorial components of the rotor
dipole, not its axial component, for a disc magnet spinning about the Z-axis). And if the
Z-axis force depends on the XY-plane rotation (rather than on m_z), then F_z would be
independent of m_z sign.

This is not derived in the source materials but is consistent with the observation.
