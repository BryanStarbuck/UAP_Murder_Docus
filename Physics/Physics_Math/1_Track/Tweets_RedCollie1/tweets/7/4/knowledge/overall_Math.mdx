## 1. Weight Transfer in Dipole Magnetic Suspension

From image description in 3_1914619703752056837:

Variables:
  m_man = 100 kg (man's body mass)
  m_ball = 1 kg (iron ball mass)
  g = 9.81 m/s^2 (standard gravitational acceleration)

Equations:
  W_ball = m_ball * g = 1 * 9.81 = 9.81 N         [weight of ball, downward]
  F_mag = W_ball = 9.81 N                           [magnetic lift force, upward, condition for levitation]
  F_reaction = -F_mag = -9.81 N                     [Newton's 3rd law: reaction on man's magnet, downward]
  W_man_effective = m_man * g + |F_reaction|
                  = 100 * 9.81 + 1 * 9.81
                  = 9.81 * (100 + 1)
                  = 9.81 * 101
                  = 990.81 N
                  = 101 kg_f

Result: Ball effective weight = 0; Man effective weight = 101 kg_f; System total = 101 kg_f = unchanged.

Conservation check:
  delta_W_system = W_ball + W_man_effective - (m_man + m_ball) * g
                = 0 + 990.81 - 101 * 9.81
                = 990.81 - 990.81 = 0  [Confirmed: system weight conserved]

## 2. 3-Phase Rotating Magnetic Field — Superposition

Standard electromagnetic result (not explicitly derived in sources but is the physical basis):

Three coils at positions phi_k = (k-1) * 2*pi/3 for k = 1, 2, 3, each carrying current:
  I_k(t) = I_0 * cos(omega_s * t - (k-1) * 2*pi/3)

Magnetic field at center from coil k (small-coil approximation, on-axis dipole field):
  B_k(t) = B_0 * cos(omega_s * t - (k-1) * 2*pi/3) * (cos(phi_k) x_hat + sin(phi_k) y_hat)

Superposition:
  B_x(t) = B_0 * sum_{k=1}^{3} cos(omega_s*t - (k-1)*2pi/3) * cos((k-1)*2pi/3)
          = (3/2) * B_0 * cos(omega_s * t)

  B_y(t) = B_0 * sum_{k=1}^{3} cos(omega_s*t - (k-1)*2pi/3) * sin((k-1)*2pi/3)
          = (3/2) * B_0 * sin(omega_s * t)

  |B(t)| = sqrt(B_x^2 + B_y^2) = (3/2) * B_0    [constant amplitude]
  theta(t) = arctan(B_y/B_x) = omega_s * t        [field angle rotates linearly with time]

For N-phase (N=5), same calculation with 5 coils at phi_k = (k-1)*2*pi/5:
  |B(t)| = (5/2) * B_0   [5-phase gives 25% stronger rotating field than 3-phase]
  theta(t) = omega_s * t  [same rotation rate]

## 3. External Field Cancellation — 6-Coil N-S-N-S-N-S Configuration

For 6 coils at phi_k = (k-1) * pi/3, alternating north (+z) and south (-z) orientation:
  Coil moments: m_k = +m_0 * z_hat for k = 1, 3, 5 (north-up, R, G, B phases)
                m_k = -m_0 * z_hat for k = 2, 4, 6 (south-up, interleaved)

Net dipole moment:
  M_total = sum_{k=1}^{6} m_k = 3 * m_0 * z_hat + 3 * (-m_0 * z_hat) = 0

External field at large distances r >> R (ring radius):
  B_dipole(r) ~ mu_0 * M_total / (4*pi*r^3) = 0   [dipole term vanishes]
  Leading term: quadrupole, decays as r^-5

Practical result: near-zero external field at distances r >> R. Device appears magnetically
inert to external ferromagnetic objects. The net magnetic moment is zero.

## 4. Critical Height for Levitation Equilibrium

Let F_z(h) = net upward electromagnetic force on rotor at height h above coil plane.

Equilibrium condition:
  F_z(h_c) = m_rotor * g

where h_c is the critical height.

For small perturbations about h_c:
  If dF_z/dh < 0 (force decreases with height, as expected for decaying coil field):
    The equilibrium is UNSTABLE: displacement upward gives F_z < m*g, rotor falls;
    displacement downward gives F_z > m*g, rotor rises back.
    This produces oscillation through h_c rather than stable hover.

Force-height dependence approximation (on-axis field of a coil ring of radius R at height h):
  B_z(h) = (mu_0 * n * I * R^2) / (2 * (R^2 + h^2)^(3/2))

  dB_z/dh = -3 * mu_0 * n * I * R^2 * h / (2 * (R^2 + h^2)^(5/2))  [negative for h > 0]

  The field gradient dB_z/dh is negative and its magnitude increases from h=0, peaks at h = R/2,
  then decreases. The lift force on a magnetic dipole m_z in a gradient field:
  F_z = m_z * dB_z/dh

  This reaches maximum near h ~ R/sqrt(2) and falls off for h >> R. So h_c satisfies:
    m_z * dB_z/dh |_{h=h_c} = m_rotor * g

  For standard parameters (R ~ 30-40 mm coil radius, m_z ~ few mJ/T for a 70mm disc magnet),
  h_c would be on the order of a few mm to ~2 cm above the coil plane.

## 5-Phase AC Rotating Field (Buga Sphere) — Extended Math

5-coil arrangement at angles phi_k = (k-1) * 72 degrees for k=1..5
5-phase currents at time offsets Delta_t_k = (k-1) * T/5 where T = 1/f_supply:
  I_k(t) = I_0 * cos(2*pi*f*t - (k-1)*2*pi/5)

Resultant B at center:
  B_total = (5/2) * B_0 * [cos(omega_s*t) x_hat + sin(omega_s*t) y_hat]

The rotating field amplitude is (5/2)*B_0 vs (3/2)*B_0 for 3-phase.
Ratio of field strengths: (5/2)/(3/2) = 5/3 = 1.667
A 5-phase system provides 67% stronger rotating field than 3-phase with the same individual
coil field strength B_0. This would drive faster rotor spin at the same power level.

## Gyroscope Angular Momentum

For Buga sphere rotor:
  Moment of inertia of disc about symmetry axis: I_disc = (1/2) * m * r^2
  Angular momentum: L = I_disc * omega = (1/2) * m * r^2 * omega

  At angular velocity omega, gyroscopic precession rate:
    Omega_prec = tau_gravity / L = (m * g * l) / ((1/2) * m * r^2 * omega)
               = (2 * g * l) / (r^2 * omega)

  where l is the distance from pivot to center of mass of the rotor.

  For increasing omega: Omega_prec decreases as 1/omega.
  For standard mechanics: no net upward force is generated.
  The hypothesis that Omega_prec couples to gravity to produce F_z_upward requires
  additional physics beyond standard mechanics — not derived in source materials.
