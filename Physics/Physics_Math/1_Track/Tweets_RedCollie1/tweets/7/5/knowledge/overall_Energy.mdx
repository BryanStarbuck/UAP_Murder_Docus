Where R_load = load resistance.

The "from the flywheel" language implies the device generates electricity in addition to providing lift — suggesting a single rotating assembly that simultaneously produces mechanical lift force and electrical output. From tweet 1969904219017900157:
"rotating AC magnetic field — 5-phase is more powerful than 3-phase"

Energy efficiency analysis (conventional motors):
* Input power: P_in = V_line × I_line × PF (power factor), summed over all phases.
* For 3-phase: P_in = 3 × V_phase × I_phase × cos(φ), where φ = phase angle between V and I.
* For 5-phase: P_in = 5 × V_phase × I_phase × cos(φ).

For the same coil dimensions and peak B field:
* 5-phase requires 5/3 times more total input power than 3-phase (more coils, same power per coil).
* But the rotating field amplitude is 2.5/1.5 = 5/3 times larger.
* The torque on the rotor scales as T ∝ B × I_rotor, so more field → more torque at the same rotor current.

The net efficiency (torque per watt of input) may favor 5-phase IF the additional field uniformity reduces harmonic losses. This is a plausible engineering claim within conventional AC motor physics.

The anomalous energy claim within the 5-phase context: a more perfect rotating field more efficiently "pulls on" the zero-point field, extracting more ZPF energy per unit of applied AC power. This is Drew's extension beyond classical motor engineering, though no quantitative basis is provided.

## IV. "Delta" as an Energy Gate

From video 13_2043978897302401026_video.mdx:

The "delta" transition (attraction → repulsion under counter-rotation) is described as qualitative — the system transitions between two regimes. This transition can be interpreted as an energy gate:

* Below critical angular velocity ω_c: system is in the attractive regime. Energy stored in the magnetic field U = −μ_0 m_1 m_2 / (4π r³) is negative (bound state). Moving the components apart COSTS energy.

* Above ω_c: system is in the repulsive regime. The effective interaction energy is positive (unbound state). Moving the components apart RELEASES energy.

If the angular velocity is maintained above ω_c by the AC input, the system continuously releases energy as the components repel (or as the repulsion does work against gravity in the case of a constrained geometry producing lift). The AC input maintains the rotation; the repulsion energy does the lifting work.

The net energy balance:
P_in (AC) = P_lift + P_losses (heat in coils, bearing friction, etc.)

For overunity: P_lift > P_in. This would require the "delta" interaction to draw energy from a source other than the AC input — identified as the ZPF.

No experimental measurements of P_in vs. P_lift are provided in the source material. The claim remains at the qualitative level: the phenomenon (transition from attraction to repulsion) was observed; the quantitative energy balance was not reported.
