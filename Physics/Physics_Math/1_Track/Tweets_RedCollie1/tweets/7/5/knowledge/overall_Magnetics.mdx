### A. Dipole Magnetism (Static Field, Attraction Mode)

From slide series in tweet 1914620318435385436 and referenced tweet:

Scenario: A permanent magnet held by a man (100 kg) levitates an iron ball (1 kg).

Force analysis (Newtonian):
* Magnetic force F_mag acts upward on the iron ball, magnitude = m_ball × g = 1 kg × 9.81 m/s² = 9.81 N.
* By Newton's third law, an equal and opposite force acts downward on the magnet (and therefore the man).
* Man's apparent weight = 100 kg × g + 1 kg × g = 101 kg equivalent.
* Iron ball's apparent weight = 0 kg.
* Total system weight conserved: 101 + 0 = 101 kg equivalent.

This is classical dipole-dipole interaction: the force between two magnetic dipoles with moments m_1 and m_2 separated by distance r along the axis scales as F ~ (μ_0 / 4π) × 6 m_1 m_2 / r^4 along the axial direction.

The net effect: weight is transferred from the iron ball to the man. No energy is gained or lost; lifting the ball requires work W = F × d against gravity, paid by whatever holds the magnet against the downward reaction force.

The rotating magnetic field produced by this arrangement rotates at the synchronous speed n_s = f / p where f = AC frequency (Hz) and p = number of pole pairs. ## IV. XY-Plane and Z-Axis Dynamics

The rotating magnetic field from the AC coil ring operates in the XY-plane (the horizontal plane). The field vector rotates azimuthally:
B(t) = B_0 [cos(ωt) x̂ + sin(ωt) ŷ]

The magnetic flywheel spinning about the vertical Z-axis has its magnetic moment oriented in the XY-plane (for a diametrically magnetized disc) or along Z (for an axially magnetized disc).

In the proposed device:
* If the flywheel is diametrically magnetized, its moment m rotates in the XY-plane at angular frequency ω_rotor.
* The applied rotating field B rotates at ω_field.
* The slip frequency Δω = ω_field − ω_rotor determines the torque on the rotor.

For the counter-rotation case, ω_rotor and ω_field are of opposite sign, so Δω = ω_field + |ω_rotor|, which is larger than in the co-rotation case. The proposed "delta" repulsion effect is associated with this large Δω regime.

The Z-axis (vertical) force would require a component of the interaction to be directed axially. In the proposed geometry, if the inner magnet is axially magnetized and the outer flywheel is diametrically magnetized (or vice versa), the rotating interaction between them generates a screw-like motion with a net axial component — the "rising like a top" described in the slide text.

The pinwheel crop circle (3_1978594037679759360.txt: 5-6 blade rotational symmetry within concentric rings) is interpreted as a diagram of this device geometry: the spiral blades represent the rotating magnetic field, the concentric rings represent the field boundaries, and the rotation direction of the blades relative to the ring represents the counter-rotation geometry.

## V. Beyond Classical EM: The Proposed New Interaction

Drew summarizes the key claim (tweet 2043979750209925538):
"The careful study of alien crop pictures has led me to discover a new kind of physics, where a non-spinning magnetic attraction turns into a spinning magnetic repulsion."

This transition — attraction in the static case, repulsion in the spinning case, at the same spatial configuration — is not predicted by classical magnetostatics. It requires either:

1. A velocity-dependent correction to the magnetic force law: F = F_dipole + α × (dω/dt) × ẑ, where α is an empirically determined coupling constant.
2. A spin-spin coupling at macroscopic scale analogous to the Heisenberg exchange interaction: E_exchange = −J S_1 · S_2, where for parallel macroscopic spin (co-rotation), J > 0 (ferromagnetic, attractive) and for antiparallel macroscopic spin (counter-rotation), J < 0 (antiferromagnetic, repulsive). If J can be macroscopically large, the repulsion could be significant.
3. A relativistic correction: at high ω (peripheral velocity v → c), the rotating magnetic moment has a large time-varying electric dipole component (since a moving magnetic dipole generates an electric dipole p = v × m / c²). The interaction of this induced electric dipole with the electric field of the counter-rotating assembly could produce a net repulsive force along Z.

No equation is provided by Drew for the magnitude of "delta"; the claim is stated as an experimental observation pending quantitative characterization.
