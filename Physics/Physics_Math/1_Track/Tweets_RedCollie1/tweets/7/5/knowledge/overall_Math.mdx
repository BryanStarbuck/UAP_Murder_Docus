## II. Magnetic Dipole Force Equations

### Static Dipole-Dipole Force (Axial Configuration)

The force on a magnetic dipole m_2 in the field of a dipole m_1 at axial separation r:

F_axial = (μ_0 / 4π) × (6 m_1 m_2) / r^4

Components:
* μ_0 = 4π × 10^−7 T·m/A (magnetic permeability of free space)
* m_1, m_2 = magnetic dipole moments (A·m²)
* r = center-to-center separation (m)
* F_axial = force along the axis connecting the dipoles (N); positive = repulsive, negative = attractive

The factor r^−4 (vs. r^−2 for monopoles) means dipole forces fall off rapidly with distance.

For antiparallel moments (attractive alignment, as in the static iron ball + magnet case):
F_axial = −(μ_0 / 4π) × (6 m_1 m_2) / r^4 (attractive)

The energy:
U_dipole = −(μ_0 / 4π) × (2 m_1 · m_2) / r^3

### Dynamic (Counter-Rotating) Interaction: "Delta" Effect

No explicit equation provided by Drew. The transition from attraction to repulsion under counter-rotation implies:

F_total = F_dipole + F_delta(ω_rel)

Where F_delta is the spin-dependent correction term:
* F_delta = 0 when ω_rel = 0 (static case → standard dipole attraction dominates)
* F_delta > |F_dipole| when ω_rel > ω_c (critical relative angular velocity → repulsion dominates)

The magnitude and functional form of F_delta are not specified in the source material. Drew describes it as experimental ("new to physics on Earth") without providing a governing equation. The critical angular velocity ω_c is also not specified quantitatively.

## III. Rotating Magnetic Field (n-Phase AC)

### General Formula

For n-phase AC coils at angular positions φ_k = 2π k/n, k = 0, 1, ..., n−1, each carrying current I_k = I_0 cos(ωt − 2πk/n), the combined magnetic field at the center is:

B(t) = B_0 × Σ_k [cos(ωt − 2πk/n)] × [cos(2πk/n) x̂ + sin(2πk/n) ŷ]

For n ≥ 3, the sum simplifies (using trigonometric identities):
B(t) = (n/2) × B_0 × [cos(ωt) x̂ + sin(ωt) ŷ]

Components:
* B_0 = peak field per phase (T)
* n/2 = amplitude factor (increases with n)
* ω = 2πf = angular frequency of AC supply (rad/s)
* The field magnitude |B| = (n/2) B_0 = constant, rotating at ω

### 3-Phase vs. 5-Phase Comparison (from tweet 1969904219017900157)

Claim: "5-phase is more powerful than 3-phase"

Mathematically:
* n=3: |B| = (3/2) B_0 = 1.5 B_0
* n=5: |B| = (5/2) B_0 = 2.5 B_0

The 5-phase system produces 2.5/1.5 = 1.667× larger field amplitude for the same peak current per phase. Additionally, the harmonic content differs:

For n=3: the field contains 5th and 7th harmonic components (from winding imperfections).
For n=5: the field contains 9th and 11th harmonic components — smaller in magnitude and higher in frequency — resulting in a more nearly perfect circular rotating field.

The motor torque from a rotating field of amplitude B on a rotor with moment m:
τ = m × B × sin(δ)

where δ is the torque angle (load angle). For a given δ, τ ∝ B, so the 5-phase system produces 1.667× more torque at the same rotor moment.

From slide context (3_1914620064239902722.txt) and spinning top image:

Precession under applied torque τ:

Components:
