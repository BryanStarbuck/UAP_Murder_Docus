## II. Gyroscopic Physics Applied to the "Rising Like a Top" Mechanism

From slide 3_1914620064239902722.txt:

The "pulling on a string to make it rise like a top" metaphor invokes gyroscopic precession:

For a spinning top with angular momentum L = I × ω (where I = moment of inertia about the spin axis, ω = spin angular frequency), a torque τ applied perpendicular to L produces precession:
τ = dL/dt

The precession angular velocity Ω_precession = τ / L = τ / (I × ω).

For a top that "rises" (nutation to vertical): a spinning top that is initially tilted precesses, and with sufficient spin, the gyroscopic effect resists toppling. The metaphor of "pulling on a string" suggests applying a force that converts precession into axial (Z) translation.

The electromagnetic analog: a spinning magnetized rotor (magnetic moment m, spin angular frequency ω) in an external rotating magnetic field B experiences a torque:
τ = m × B (cross product)

When m and B precess about the Z-axis, the torque produces a net axial force if the geometry is asymmetric (i.e., if the field has a Z-gradient or the rotor moment has an axial component).

This is connected to the observation of the spinning top/gyroscope in image 13_1914620216472166401_preview.txt: a red and black bicolor spherical spinning top/gyroscope, approximately 60-70px diameter, tilted at 20-30 degrees from vertical, with a small black axle stub protruding from the equatorial side. The tilt and the axle position are consistent with a gyroscope experiencing precession about the vertical axis.

Step 4: The induced current in the flywheel creates its own magnetic field opposing the source magnet — this is the Meissner-like repulsion effect at high angular velocity.
Step 5: Above a critical relative velocity ω_c, the repulsive Lenz-law force exceeds the static attractive dipole force. This is the "delta" transition.
