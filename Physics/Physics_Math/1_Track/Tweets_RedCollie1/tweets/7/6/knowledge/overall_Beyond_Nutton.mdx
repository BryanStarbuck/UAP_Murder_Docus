## 1. Observed Non-Classical Result: Spinning Horizontal Magnet Causes Z-Axis Rise

### The Anomaly
Tweet 1927503577393557923: "A N-S magnet will also rise up, when we spin a horizontal N-S, permanent magnet below."
Video (13_1927503318328242176_video.mdx): "a horizontal spinning magnetic field, turn on the dremel. We see the magnet goes way up."

Classical (Newtonian + Maxwell) prediction: a horizontal bar magnet rotating about the Z-axis at angular velocity omega generates a time-varying field at axial points above it. The time-averaged force on a second axially-oriented dipole above it is exactly zero. The instantaneous force oscillates at frequency omega (and 2*omega from quadrupole terms), averaging to zero over one rotation period. Sustained Z-axis rise cannot arise from the classical time-averaged force from a rotating horizontal dipole. The experimental observation — "the magnet goes way up" — is anomalous relative to the Newtonian + Maxwellian framework.

### Why This is Beyond Newtonian Mechanics
Newton's law F = ma provides the equation of motion. The force law for magnetic dipoles in classical EM is F = nabla(m . B). For the rotating dipole geometry described, nabla(m . B) time-averages to zero on the Z-axis. Therefore no net upward acceleration should persist. The observation of sustained upward displacement contradicts the classical prediction.

### Possible New Physics Pathways
(a) Higher-order multipole interactions: when the two magnets are at close range, higher multipole (quadrupole, octupole) terms contribute forces. For a spinning magnet with axial quadrupole components Q_ij, the force on a second dipole includes terms proportional to the field gradient of Q_ij. At rotation, these alternate in sign but may have an asymmetric time-average if the quadrupole terms are not purely sinusoidal. This remains within classical EM but extends beyond the dipole-only approximation.

(b) Rotating-frame electromagnetic effects: in the rotating frame of the spinning magnet, there are fictitious forces (centrifugal, Coriolis). The interaction between the rotating-frame EM field and the stationary upper magnet may introduce coupling terms not captured in the lab-frame dipole interaction formula. This is still within standard SR/GR but non-obvious from elementary treatment.

(c) Gyromagnetic coupling (Barnett + Einstein-de Haas chain): as the lower magnet spins, its intrinsic spin angular momentum (from electron magnetic moments) exchanges angular momentum with the lattice. If this exchange produces a net axial current loop (toroidal current), the Biot-Savart field from that current loop could have a net axial gradient force on the upper magnet. The Barnett effect is real and validated (D.F. Barnett, 1915); its magnitude at typical Dremel RPM is very small but not exactly zero.

(d) "Vortex magnetism" (author's claim): a non-classical coupling between the vortical B-field topology and the gravitational potential. No established equation; the author treats this as an experimental finding awaiting theoretical explanation.

Physical background: radiation pressure F_rad = L_sun / (4 pi r^2 c) * sigma (where sigma is the cross-section for the outgassing material) always pushes material anti-sunward. Solar wind dynamic pressure P_sw = (1/2) rho_sw v_sw^2 also pushes anti-sunward. A sunward-pointing jet (not tail but active outgassing directed toward the Sun) would require:
- An anisotropic internal pressure source pushing material toward the Sun, OR
- Active propulsion (engine exhaust directed away from the Sun, creating a sunward-pointing plume), OR

The first two mechanisms are not consistent with natural cometary activity. The third (gravitational focusing) could occur only very close to the Sun (where tidal forces are strong), not at large heliocentric distances. The observation of a sunward jet at ~130,000 mph (~58 km/s, hyperbolic trajectory) at interstellar speeds is therefore beyond any natural cometary model. The author's conclusion — "3I/2025 is NOT likely to be a 'comet'" — is presented as a straightforward application of classical physics to eliminate the comet hypothesis.
