1. A rotating (spinning) magnetic field generated by multi-phase AC electromagnet arrays.

## Specific Physics Advances Cited as Enabling This

### 1. Rotating Magnetic Field (RMF) as Propulsive Element
Conventional rotating magnetic field theory (as in 3-phase or 5-phase AC induction motors) describes a field that rotates in the XY plane. The author's claim extends this: when a rotating magnetic field is coupled to a counter-rotating secondary field (produced by a separate set of coils or permanent magnet ring), the composite field configuration rises along the Z axis, taking the craft with it.

Conventional benchmark: in a standard 3-phase induction motor, a rotating magnetic field of angular frequency omega drives a rotor. The Lorentz force law F = q(v x B) governs particle trajectory. No net Z-force exists in a symmetric RMF.

The author's departure: introducing broken axial symmetry via counter-spin produces a helical or vortical composite B-field with a non-zero divergence component along Z. This is the "vortex magnetism" claim.

### 2. Liquid Homopolar Motor (Corbino Disc) as the Starting Mechanism
Tweet 1978763662576984572 explicitly identifies the first stage of "UFO physics as drawn in crops 2012" as "a liquid homopolar motor (or 'Corbino disc')."

The Corbino disc is a conducting disk with inner radius R1 and outer radius R2. Current flows radially (I_in at inner radius, I_out at outer, or vice versa). In the presence of an axial magnetic field B_z, the radial current j_r interacts with B_z to produce an azimuthal (toroidal) body force:

    F_phi = j_r x B_z

This drives circular fluid flow in a conducting liquid (mercury, gallium, galinstan). The diagram in 3_1978761676246917120.txt shows exactly this: inner radius R1, outer radius R2, spiral current path with labeled I_in, I_out, and voltage taps at the inner radius — a schematic of a Faraday/Corbino disk device.

The advance claimed: the Corbino disc, when operated with a conducting liquid (mercury-vortex, "vimana" reference; or galinstan per tweet 1923030175962267693), drives a spinning liquid conductor. The spinning charged fluid then generates its own magnetic field (Biot-Savart contribution from the rotating current distribution), which in turn interacts with the external applied AC field to produce the secondary levitation force.

### 3. Multi-Phase AC (3-phase and 5-phase) as the Enabling Electrical Architecture
Tweets 1942035902269989159 and 2031920907003015459 explicitly state:

    "They fly using a 5-phase, rotating magnetic field."
    "5-phase AC power is a specialized multiphase system used for high-power, high-reliability applications... It offers increased power density, reduced torque ripple in motors, and better fault tolerance compared to 3-phase systems."

The author uses 3-phase in his lab (U-V-W, 120-degree phase-separated coils) as an approximation because commercial 5-phase power supplies are not economically available. 5-phase (72-degree phase separation) produces a smoother rotating field with less torque ripple — relevant because pulsed fields would produce oscillatory rather than sustained levitation.

Simultaneously, a separate inner coil set (triangle arrangement, 3 wire coils U-V-W, 120-degree spacing) drives the central disc magnet anti-clockwise.

### 5. The N-S Magnet Rise Effect (Experimental Observation)
Tweet 1927503577393557923 states:

    "A N-S magnet will also rise up, when we spin a horizontal N-S, permanent magnet below."

And the video (13_1927503318328242176_video.mdx transcription):

    "We're going to use a different magnet in the rotor, which is north or one side, blue on the other side, a horizontal spinning magnetic field, turn on the dremel. We see the magnet goes way up."

This is the core experimental result: a stationary North-South permanent magnet placed above a horizontally-spinning North-South permanent magnet (driven by a Dremel tool) rises upward (Z-direction). This is not explained by classical dipole-dipole interaction, which predicts oscillatory forces averaged to zero by the rotation, not a sustained levitation force.
