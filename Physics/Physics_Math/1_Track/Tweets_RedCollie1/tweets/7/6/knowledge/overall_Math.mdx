## 1. Corbino Disc Geometry and Equations

### Notation
- R1: inner radius of conducting annular disk (labeled in diagram 3_1978761676246917120.txt)
- R2: outer radius of conducting annular disk (labeled in same diagram)
- I_in: input current (labeled with downward arrow)
- I_out: output current (labeled with downward arrow)
- J_r(r): radial current density = I_in / (2 pi r t), where t is disk thickness
- B_z: externally applied axial magnetic field

### Azimuthal Body Force Density
    f_phi(r) = J_r(r) x B_z = (I_in / (2 pi r t)) * B_z   [N/m^3]

### Net Azimuthal Torque on Disk Annulus
    tau = Integral_{R1}^{R2} f_phi(r) * (2 pi r t) * r dr
        = I_in * B_z * Integral_{R1}^{R2} r dr
        = I_in * B_z * (R2^2 - R1^2) / 2

### Homopolar Generator EMF (Faraday Disc)
    EMF = Integral_{R1}^{R2} (v x B) . dr
        = Integral_{R1}^{R2} (omega * r * B_z) dr
        = (1/2) * omega * B_z * (R2^2 - R1^2)

where omega is the angular velocity of the spinning disk.

### Energy Conversion
Input electrical power: P_in = I_in * EMF = I_in * (1/2) * omega * B_z * (R2^2 - R1^2)
Mechanical output power: P_mech = tau * omega = I_in * B_z * (R2^2 - R1^2)/2 * omega
These are equal (ideal case), confirming the homopolar motor as a transducer.

## 2. Rotating Magnetic Field Mathematics

### 3-Phase (N=3, 120° spacing)
Coil phase currents:
    I_k(t) = I_0 cos(omega t - 2pi(k-1)/3),  k = 1, 2, 3

Composite field vector in the coil plane (2D, x-y):
    B_x(t) = B_0 [cos(omega t) + cos(omega t - 2pi/3) * cos(120°) + cos(omega t - 4pi/3) * cos(240°)]

Using phasor addition:
    B_total(t) = (3/2) B_0 [cos(omega t) e_x + sin(omega t) e_y]

Magnitude: |B_total| = (3/2) B_0 = constant
Angular velocity of field vector: omega (same as supply frequency)

### 5-Phase (N=5, 72° spacing)
    I_k(t) = I_0 cos(omega t - 2pi(k-1)/5),  k = 1..5

Composite field:
    B_total(t) = (5/2) B_0 [cos(omega t) e_x + sin(omega t) e_y]

Magnitude: (5/2) B_0 = constant; same rotation as supply frequency.
Dominant harmonic: 10th harmonic torque ripple (vs. 6th for 3-phase); smoother rotation.

### 6-Pole 3-Phase Winding (U-V-W-U-V-W)
6 coils at 60° intervals, phase sequence repeating twice (U-V-W-U-V-W):

Electrical pole pairs: p/2 = 3 (6-pole machine)
Mechanical synchronous speed: omega_mech = omega_elec / (p/2) = omega_elec / 3

For 50 Hz supply: omega_elec = 2pi * 50 = 314.16 rad/s, omega_mech = 104.7 rad/s = 1000 RPM.

The field pattern produced by 6 poles in the outer ring space drives the 15-16 element outer magnet ring. The number of poles in the rotating field (6) must match or be a harmonic of the number of permanent magnet poles in the outer ring (15-16 poles ~ 7-8 pole pairs). This mismatch may produce asynchronous torque pulsations rather than synchronous drive; the author does not address this explicitly.

## 3. Barnett Effect Quantification

### Classical Barnett Magnetization
    M_z = chi_m * omega_z / gamma_e

where:
    chi_m = magnetic susceptibility (dimensionless, ~10^3 - 10^5 for ferromagnets, relative to mu_0)
    omega_z = angular velocity about Z-axis [rad/s]
    gamma_e = gyromagnetic ratio = e / (2 m_e) = 8.794 x 10^10 rad/(s*T)

For a ferromagnetic disc (e.g., NdFeB permanent magnet) spinning at omega_z = 10^4 rad/s (approximately 95,000 RPM):
    M_z ~ chi_m * 10^4 / (8.794 x 10^10)

The contribution is very small unless chi_m is enormous (not the case for a hard permanent magnet, which has small susceptibility tensor). The Barnett effect in hard magnets is therefore negligible compared to the intrinsic remanent magnetization.

### Spin-Angular Momentum Coupling
For a single electron: mu_s = -g_s * mu_B * S / hbar, where g_s ~ 2, mu_B = 9.274 x 10^-24 J/T.

For a macroscopic ferromagnet with net spin angular momentum L_s:
    M = gamma_e * L_s

Conservation of total angular momentum (mechanical + spin):
    d/dt (L_mech + L_spin) = tau_external

When L_spin changes due to demagnetization or re-orientation, L_mech changes to compensate.

## 4. Magnetic Dipole Force and Gradient

The Kelvin force on a magnetic dipole m in an inhomogeneous field:
    F = nabla(m . B)

For a fixed dipole m_z in a field with axial gradient dB_z/dz:
    F_z = m_z * (dB_z/dz)

This is the mechanism of magnetic levitation in standard magnetostatics (e.g., Earnshaw's theorem applies: stable levitation of a purely diamagnetic or paramagnetic object requires active feedback or time-varying fields).

Earnshaw's theorem: no configuration of static magnetic fields can stably levitate a static magnetic dipole. Therefore:
- Passive levitation of a permanent magnet above another permanent magnet is impossible in static configuration (unstable in at least one DOF).
- Time-varying fields (rotating fields, AC fields) can circumvent Earnshaw's theorem and produce stable levitation (e.g., as in the Levitron, or rotating field levitators).

The author's counter-rotating field device is a time-varying field system and therefore is not a priori ruled out by Earnshaw's theorem from producing a net levitation effect.

## 5. Phase-Space Trajectory of Inner/Outer Spinning Elements

Let:
    theta_inner(t) = -omega_inner * t  (anti-clockwise, negative sign convention)
    theta_outer(t) = +omega_outer * t  (clockwise, positive sign convention)

The relative angle between inner and outer elements:
    Delta_theta(t) = theta_inner - theta_outer = -(omega_inner + omega_outer) * t

This relative angle increases linearly in time — the inner and outer elements are never phase-locked unless omega_inner = omega_outer = 0. The composite magnetic field seen at an intermediate radius r (R1 < r < R2) samples both contributions with different phase relationships at each instant.

The composite B_phi at radius r:
    B_phi(r, t) = B_inner(r, t, theta_inner) + B_outer(r, t, theta_outer)

This superposition is in general a quasi-periodic or beat-frequency field if omega_inner ≠ omega_outer, producing time-varying Lorentz forces on any conducting material at radius r.

## 7. 3-Phase Induction Motor Slip and Torque

Tweet 1960833497612062911: identification of a "lag" slide for a 3-phase induction motor.

Slip definition: s = (omega_s - omega_r) / omega_s, dimensionless, 0 ≤ s ≤ 1

Rotor-induced EMF: E_2s = s * E_2  (proportional to slip)
Rotor current: I_2 = s * E_2 / sqrt(R_2^2 + (s * X_2)^2)
Torque: T = (3 * |I_2|^2 * R_2/s) / omega_s = (3 E_2^2 * R_2 * s) / (omega_s * (R_2^2 + s^2 * X_2^2))

Maximum torque (pull-out torque) occurs at s_max = R_2 / X_2.

For the UAP disc device: the disc magnet acts as the "rotor" and the coil assembly as the "stator." The disc magnet's mechanical load determines the operating slip, which determines the torque applied to the disc by the rotating field.
