## 1. Classical Electromagnetic Foundations Invoked

### Lorentz Force Law
F = q(E + v x B)

For a current-carrying conductor in a magnetic field: F = I L x B (vector form), where L is the vector length element along the conductor.

Applied in Corbino disc: radial current I_r in axial field B_z produces azimuthal force F_phi = I_r x B_z, driving rotation.

### Biot-Savart Law
B(r) = (mu_0 / 4pi) * Integral( I (dl x hat_r) / r^2 )

Applied: the spinning liquid conductor in the Corbino disc generates its own magnetic field contribution via Biot-Savart. The rotating current loop of radius a at height z produces an axial field:

    B_z = (mu_0 * I * a^2) / (2 * (a^2 + z^2)^(3/2))

### Faraday's Law (Applied to Homopolar Generator)
For a conducting disk of radius R rotating at angular velocity omega in an axial field B:

    EMF = (1/2) * omega * B * R^2

For the Corbino disc with inner radius R1 and outer radius R2:

    EMF = (1/2) * omega * B_z * (R2^2 - R1^2)

This is the back-EMF generated by the spinning disk, relevant to the energy balance of the device.

### Magnetic Dipole Field
For a magnetic dipole m located at origin:

    B(r) = (mu_0 / 4pi) * [ (3(m . hat_r)hat_r - m) / r^3 ]

For a horizontal dipole m = m_0 (cos(omega_r*t) e_x + sin(omega_r*t) e_y) rotating in the XY plane, the field at a point (0, 0, z) on the symmetry axis is:

    B_x(t) = -(mu_0 m_0 / 4pi z^3) cos(omega_r*t)
    B_y(t) = -(mu_0 m_0 / 4pi z^3) sin(omega_r*t)
    B_z(t) = 0 (by symmetry for horizontal dipole on-axis)

Time average of the field at the axial point: <B_x> = 0, <B_y> = 0, <B_z> = 0. Therefore the time-averaged force on a second fixed dipole at (0,0,z) is zero. This is the classical null result that the experimental rise observation contradicts.

## 2. Rotating Magnetic Field Phase Analysis

### 3-Phase System (120° phase separation)
Three coils with axes e_1, e_2, e_3 at 120° intervals, driven by:

    I_1(t) = I_0 cos(omega t)
    I_2(t) = I_0 cos(omega t - 2pi/3)
    I_3(t) = I_0 cos(omega t - 4pi/3)

The composite field at the center:

    B(t) = B_0 sum_{k=1}^{3} cos(omega t - 2pi(k-1)/3) e_k

This resolves to a rotating vector of constant magnitude (3/2) B_0 in the plane of the three coils, rotating at angular frequency omega. There is no Z-component in a symmetric planar arrangement.

### 5-Phase System (72° phase separation)
Five coils at 72° intervals:

    I_k(t) = I_0 cos(omega t - 2pi(k-1)/5),  k = 1..5

The rotating field has the same constant-magnitude property but with the fifth-harmonic torque ripple suppressed (the dominant torque ripple in a 3-phase system is at 6f; in 5-phase it is at 10f, much smaller amplitude).

Cited advantage (tweet 2031920907003015459): "increased power density, reduced torque ripple in motors, and better fault tolerance compared to 3-phase systems." For the UAP application, reduced torque ripple means smoother sustained rotation of the inner disc magnet and outer magnet ring.

### 6-Pole Winding (U-V-W-U-V-W)
Six coils at 60° intervals driven as U-V-W-U-V-W:

    Coil positions: 0°, 60°, 120°, 180°, 240°, 300°
    Phases:         U,   V,    W,    U,    V,    W

This is a standard 6-pole, 3-phase winding producing a field that rotates at omega/3 (one third of the electrical frequency for a 6-pole machine, or equivalently, the mechanical synchronous speed is omega_s / (p/2) = omega_s / 3 for p=6 poles). The 6-pole field interacts with the outer ring of 15-16 N-N-S-S magnets (even number of alternating poles) to drive clockwise rotation of the outer ring.

## 3. Gyromagnetic Relationships

### Barnett Effect
A ferromagnetic body rotating at angular velocity omega acquires a magnetization:

    M = chi_m * omega / gamma_e

where:
    chi_m = dimensionless magnetic susceptibility
    gamma_e = -e / (2 m_e) = gyromagnetic ratio of electron (approximately -1.76 × 10^11 rad s^-1 T^-1)

For a ferromagnetic spinning disc (disc magnet in the device), the Barnett effect contributes a magnetization aligned with the rotation axis (Z-axis) proportional to the angular velocity. This provides an additional axial magnetic moment M_z = chi_m * omega_z / gamma_e as the disc spins, which may contribute to a net axial (Z-direction) interaction with the applied field. This is a legitimate quantum-mechanical effect (validated experimentally) that is non-classical in origin but well within the standard model.

### Einstein-de Haas Effect (Inverse Barnett)
A change in magnetization delta_M in a ferromagnet causes a mechanical angular momentum change:

    delta_L = (2 m_e / e) * V * delta_M

where V is the volume. This couples the magnetic and mechanical degrees of freedom. In the context of the counter-rotating device: if the inner disc magnet's magnetization changes as it spins, it exchanges angular momentum with the coil assembly, potentially producing a net Z-axis impulse.

## 4. 3-Phase Induction Motor Lag (Phase Lag / Slip)

Tweet 1960833497612062911: "That slide at bottom right shows 'lag' for a 3-phase induction motor."

In an induction motor, the rotor lags the stator rotating field by the slip frequency:

    slip s = (omega_s - omega_r) / omega_s

where omega_s is the synchronous speed and omega_r is the rotor angular velocity. The torque-speed characteristic is:

    T = (3 V_s^2 R_r/s) / (omega_s * ((R_s + R_r/s)^2 + (X_s + X_r)^2))

For the UAP device, the "lag" between the rotating field and the disc magnet's rotation determines the torque applied to the disc. At synchronous speed (s=0) there is no torque; at high slip there is maximum torque. The operating point (slip value) determines how much electromagnetic torque is available to overcome friction and produce net rotation.

## 5. Counter-Rotation and Vortex Field Geometry

### Claim: Counter-Rotating Fields Produce Z-Axis Net Force
The physical argument (from slides and tweets) is:

    Inner disc magnet rotates anti-clockwise at omega_1 (Z-axis positive sense)
    Outer ring of magnets rotates clockwise at omega_2 (Z-axis negative sense)
    Net angular momentum of the magnetic system: L_net = I_inner * omega_1 - I_outer * omega_2

where I_inner, I_outer are the effective moments of inertia of the respective magnetic subsystems.

If L_net ≠ 0, the composite field has net angular momentum. By conservation of angular momentum, if the field couples to the spacecraft structure (or ambient field), a net torque or force results. The author claims this manifests as a Z-axis force rather than a torque.

### Helical B-Field Argument
When inner and outer fields counter-rotate, their superposition is not a simple rotating dipole but a more complex topology. At intermediate radii, the field from the anti-clockwise inner disc and the clockwise outer ring partially cancel in the azimuthal direction but add in the axial direction, creating a region of enhanced B_z. This helical or vortical field structure is what the author refers to as "vortex magnetism."

Mathematical sketch: if the inner disc generates B_inner = B1 (cos(omega1*t) e_x + sin(omega1*t) e_y) and the outer ring generates B_outer = B2 (cos(-omega2*t) e_x + sin(-omega2*t) e_y) (opposite sense), their superposition is:

    B_total = (B1 cos(omega1*t) + B2 cos(omega2*t)) e_x + (B1 sin(omega1*t) - B2 sin(omega2*t)) e_y

This is an elliptically polarized rotating field when omega1 ≠ omega2, or a linearly oscillating field when omega1 = omega2. Neither case trivially produces a Z-axis force in classical EM without additional coupling to Z-axis field gradients.

## 6. Astronomy / Observational Physics

### Cometary Tail Anomaly (3I/2025)
Tweet 1957218126904660432 (note_tweet):
    "ASTRONOMY 101: A comet's tail always points AWAY from the Sun, regardless of the comet's direction of travel. 3I/2025 has a 'tail' which points TOWARDS the Sun, while it is travelling left to right at 130,000 mph.
    Therefore, according to all current knowledge of astronomy, 3I/2025 is NOT likely to be a 'comet'."

Physical basis: cometary tails form due to solar radiation pressure and solar wind pressure on volatile outgassing and dust. Both the dust tail (pushed by radiation pressure) and plasma tail (pushed by solar wind) always point anti-sunward. An object with a sunward-pointing tail violates this — radiation pressure and solar wind cannot push material toward the Sun. A sunward jet would require active propulsion or a very different morphology (e.g., a focused exhaust plume from a propulsion system pointing away from the Sun, making the apparent "tail" actually a forward-pointing exhaust). The tweet argues this is evidence that 3I/ATLAS is a spacecraft rather than a natural comet.

Tweet 1988714009969979647 (retweeted text): "Weird new 3I/ATLAS picture from the Nordic Optical Telescope on UT 2025 November 11 by astronomers David Jewitt and Jane Luu. The big jet is pointing towards the Sun."

Velocity data: 130,000 mph (~58 km/s) — hyperbolic (unbound) trajectory; consistent with interstellar origin. The combination of hyperbolic trajectory and anti-solar jet is presented as the anomaly warranting explanation beyond natural cometary physics.
