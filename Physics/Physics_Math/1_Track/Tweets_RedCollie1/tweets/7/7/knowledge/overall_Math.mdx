where p = number of magnetic poles.

## 6. Magnetic Dipole Interaction — Time-Averaged Counter-Rotating Case

Two magnetic dipoles:
  m_1(t) = m_0 [cos(ω_1 t) x̂ + sin(ω_1 t) ŷ]  (inner magnet, spinning at ω_1)
  m_2(t) = m_0 [cos(-ω_2 t) x̂ + sin(-ω_2 t) ŷ]  (outer flywheel, counter-spinning at -ω_2)

Counter-rotation: ω_1 > 0, ω_2 > 0, opposite senses.

Instantaneous mutual potential energy:
  U(t) = (μ_0 / 4π r³) [m_1(t) · m_2(t) - 3(m_1(t) · r̂)(m_2(t) · r̂)]

For dipoles in the XY-plane separated by distance r along ẑ (axial separation):
  r̂ = ẑ (so m_i · r̂ = 0 for XY-plane dipoles)
  U(t) = (μ_0 / 4π r³) m_1(t) · m_2(t)
        = (μ_0 m_0² / 4π r³) cos((ω_1 + ω_2)t)  [since counter-rotating]

Time-average over one beat period T = 2π/(ω_1 + ω_2):
  ⟨U⟩ = (μ_0 m_0² / 4π r³) × (1/T) ∫_0^T cos((ω_1 + ω_2)t) dt = 0

The classical time-averaged interaction vanishes for counter-rotating dipoles at different frequencies. However, near resonance (ω_1 ≈ ω_2 = ω_r):
  U(t) = (μ_0 m_0² / 4π r³) cos(2ω_r t)

Still averages to zero over a full period. This is the mathematical tension: the author's claimed δ force (non-zero time-averaged axial repulsion) is NOT predicted by classical dipole-dipole magnetostatics.

If valid, the δ force would require a non-linear or non-equilibrium magnetodynamic mechanism — possibly related to the rotating frame dynamics or a gyroscopic coupling term not present in static field theory.

## Appendix: Coil Winding Count Scaling — Torque Cancellation Analysis

From tweet 2027427280973402279 (note_tweet), the torque cancellation at the axis for N outer coils wired as k repetitions of U-V-W (where N = 3k) can be characterized as follows.

For N = 6 (k=2, pattern U-V-W-U-V-W):
Each phase U appears at positions 1 and 4 (separated by 180°). The dipole field from position-1 U-coil and position-4 U-coil at the axis:
  B_axis_U(t) ∝ cos(ωt)[B(r_1) + B(r_4)]
For symmetric placement (positions 1 and 4 diametrically opposite, equal distances from axis):
  B(r_1) = B(r_4) with opposite sign contribution → B_axis_net = 0 (cancellation by symmetry)

For N = 9 (k=3, U-V-W-U-V-W-U-V-W): each phase appears 3 times at 0°, 120°, 240° positions (equally spaced) → 3-fold symmetry → vector sum at axis = 0 (as for a balanced 3-phase system).

For N = 12 (k=4, U-V-W repeated 4×): each phase appears at 4 positions, 90° apart → 4-fold symmetry → sum at axis = 0.

General result: for N = 3k outer coils with k repetitions of U-V-W, the net field at the axis from the outer coils is exactly zero for any k ≥ 2 (provided the coils are equally spaced). This is why increasing coil count (9 or 12 vs. 6) improves cancellation in the non-ideal (finite-size, non-point-dipole) case: while the idealized result is zero in all cases, the higher-order multipole contributions (which do not cancel perfectly for small k) decrease with increasing k due to better angular averaging.
