## 3. Three-Phase AC Induction — Rotating Field Physics

Standard 3-phase rotating magnetic field (synchronous angular velocity ω_s):
  B_x(t) = B_0 cos(ωt)
  B_y(t) = B_0 cos(ωt - 2π/3)
  (third phase: B_0 cos(ωt - 4π/3))

Resultant field vector magnitude:
  |B_total| = (3/2) B_0 = constant

The field rotates at angular frequency ω (= 2πf, where f is supply frequency in Hz).

Synchronous speed: n_s = 120f / p (rpm), where p = number of poles.

The author uses this standard relationship in the experimental context:
- f = 20 Hz → n_s = 1200 rpm (2-pole) or 600 rpm (4-pole) etc.
- f = 34 Hz → n_s = 2040 rpm (2-pole)
- Resonant collapse observed at ~34 Hz in the spinning device

Slip (s): in a standard induction motor, s = (n_s - n_r)/n_s, where n_r = rotor speed. At synchronous speed (s=0), no torque is produced. Normal operation: s > 0. The author's device may operate near a specific non-zero slip condition that maximizes axial coupling.

## 5. Magnetic Repulsion Between Counter-Rotating Elements

From video 13_2044024931256852480 transcription (the author's "delta" force):
> "When we get spin motions of the magnet and the flywheel in opposite directions, then we get a repulsion between the magnet and the flywheel which I call delta (δ) which starts spinning and they have to move apart."

In classical magnetostatics, two magnetic dipoles m_1 and m_2 interact via:
  U = (μ_0 / 4π) [m_1 · m_2 / r³ - 3(m_1 · r̂)(m_2 · r̂) / r³]

The force F = -∇U. For aligned dipoles (same orientation), force is attractive. For anti-aligned (opposite), force is repulsive.

When the inner magnet spins clockwise and the outer flywheel spins counterclockwise, the instantaneous relative orientation of their dipoles oscillates. However, time-averaged over the spin cycle, the symmetry of the counter-rotation may produce a net repulsive coupling in the axial direction.

The author designates this time-averaged axial repulsion as δ. This is NOT a standard result of classical magnetostatics or induction motor theory — it is the non-standard physics claim at the core of this research.

## 9. Axial Force from Rotating Systems — Classical Constraints

To summarize the classical physics constraints that the author's thesis must exceed:

b) Magnetic levitation (Meissner effect): Requires superconductors. No superconductors used in the author's bench-top apparatus.
