## 2. Spinning Magnet Z-Axis Ascent on Three-Phase Drive (Anomalous Directional Force)

Source: tweet 1914662157633167571, video series.

Classical EM prediction: a permanent magnet in a three-phase rotating magnetic field
experiences a synchronizing torque in the XY-plane (causing it to spin at synchronous
speed). There is no XY-to-Z force conversion mechanism in standard electrodynamics.
The axial (Z-axis) force on a magnetic dipole m in an external field B is:
  F_z = grad_z (m . B) = m_z * (dB_z/dz) + m_x * (dB_x/dz) + m_y * (dB_y/dz)

For a spatially uniform rotating field (constant B magnitude, no gradient), this force
is zero. Only if there is a spatial gradient in B along Z would a force exist.

Drew's observation: a spinning magnet rises up a vertical pole when given three-phase
power. The three-phase coils do not rise. This directional asymmetry (magnet rises,
coils do not) is beyond classical prediction if the field is spatially uniform. If the
pole geometry creates a spatial gradient (the pole may be ferromagnetic, creating an
axial B_z gradient), standard gradient force could explain it. Drew's implicit claim is
that the rotating spin state of the magnet itself contributes to the axial force beyond
what gradient force alone would predict.

## 6. Corbino Disc as Beyond-Classical Propulsion Element

The Corbino disc geometry (annular conductor with radial current flow in axial field) was
described by Italian physicist O. M. Corbino in 1911 in the context of magnetoresistance
measurements. Its use as a propulsion element (rather than a measurement device) is a
non-standard application. The Corbino effect describes:
  When a current flows radially in a conducting disc in axial field B_z, a Hall field
  E_H = mu_H * J_r x B_z develops in the azimuthal direction, where mu_H is the Hall
  mobility of the carrier.
  
In standard Corbino geometry:
  J_r = sigma_r * E_r  (radial current)
  E_phi_Hall = (sigma_H / sigma) * J_r * B_z  (azimuthal Hall field)
  The Hall field drives an azimuthal current J_phi, which interacts with B_z to produce
  a radial body force F_r = J_phi x B_z

Drew's proposed propulsion application: this closed-loop Lorentz force (radial current ->
Hall azimuthal field -> azimuthal current -> radial Lorentz force -> modifies radial
current distribution) in combination with the externally applied three-phase rotating
field constitutes a self-reinforcing electromagnetic drive that produces torque without
conventional brush commutation. The coupling of this system to an axial force (Z-direction)
is the unresolved step.
