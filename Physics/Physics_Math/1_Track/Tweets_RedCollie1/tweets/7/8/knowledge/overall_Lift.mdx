## Primary Z-Axis Lift Hypotheses

### Hypothesis 1: Spinning Magnet Rising on Vertical Pole (Three-Phase Powered)

Empirical observation (tweet 1914662157633167571, video 13_1914659684906328064):
A permanent magnet, given three-phase power, rises up a vertical pole (Z-axis ascent).
The three-phase wire coils encasing the magnet do NOT rise. The magnet physically travels
upward along the pole axis when the three-phase rotating field is applied.

Key observation from video transcription (13_1914659684906328064): "The magnet spinning
now, no change in weight. No magnet rising. Practically no change in weight on the scale
look at that. And the pole doesn't go up." — This appears to describe a FAILED attempt
where the coil+scale system did not change weight. The contrast is: in some configuration,
the spinning magnet did rise (Drew's reference to this as motivation); in this specific
video the effect was not reproduced or was tested differently.

Drew's stated goal (note_tweet on 1914662157633167571): "I can't seem to make those 3
wire coils rise up with the magnet!" — confirming the asymmetry: magnet rises but coils do not.

Proposed mechanism: The rotating magnetic field imparts angular momentum (spin) to the
permanent magnet. The spinning magnet then interacts with the vertical pole's magnetic
field or geometry in a way that produces net axial (Z-axis) force. Drew does not specify
whether this is gyroscopic precession, diamagnetic levitation, or a novel electromagnetic
Lorentz-force mechanism.

## XY-Plane to Z-Axis Coupling: Chain of Reasoning

The full chain Drew is building across all experiments:

Step 1: Three-phase XY-plane rotating field drives Corbino disc inner rotor (XY rotation).
Step 2: At high frequency, inner disc counter-spins (three-phase field overcomes static
  magnetic attraction to outer flywheel).
Step 3: When inner disc spin flips direction, outer flywheel counter-spins faster
  (angular momentum conservation: L_total = const).
Step 4 (proposed but undemonstrated): The angular momentum exchange between inner and outer
  disc, in the context of the gravity-as-pressure-gradient model, produces a net Z-axis
  force — either because (a) the spinning vortex field locally reduces vacuum pressure
  gradient, or (b) the rapid counter-spin creates a reaction momentum along Z.
  The missing physical law connecting XY angular momentum exchange to Z linear momentum
  is what Drew cannot yet solve.
