## Devices and Configurations Described or Tested

### Homopolar Motor / Faraday Disc (video 13_1914659640278913024)
Device: Inverted stainless steel bowl (dome, approximately 20-25 cm diameter) mounted on
wooden board, serving as the rotating conducting disc (Faraday disc geometry). Two wide
copper bus bars / straps (approximately 2 cm wide, 15 cm long) provide radial current
contacts from center hub to rim (or rim to center). Blue and black insulated cables
connect to the current source.

Physics: In the homopolar (Faraday disc) geometry, an axial magnetic field B_z is produced
by a permanent magnet or electromagnet below/inside the disc. Radial current I_r flows
from center to rim (or vice versa). The Lorentz force on each current element is:
  dF = I (dl x B)
where dl is the radial current element and B = B_z hat{z}. This produces an azimuthal
(tangential) force F_phi = I_r * B_z * r, integrated from r=0 to r=R, giving a net torque
tau = I_r * B_z * R^2 / 2 (for uniform B and uniform current density). This is the
standard homopolar motor torque expression. The bowl serves as the rotating conducting disc;
the copper bus bars serve as the sliding contacts.

### Spinning Magnet Rising on a Pole (key anomaly)
Experimental observation described by Drew: a spinning permanent magnet, when given
three-phase power, rises up a vertical pole. The three-phase wire coils encasing the magnet
do NOT rise. Drew reports: "No magnet rising. Practically no change in weight on the scale.
The pole doesn't go up." (Video 13_1914659684906328064 transcription.) This asymmetry is
cited as the motivating empirical anomaly for the anti-gravity search.

Three-phase drive for magnet levitation: The three-phase rotating magnetic field (angular
frequency omega_s) imparts spin to the permanent magnet via synchronous or near-synchronous
coupling. As the magnet spins, Drew observes altitude gain along the pole axis. The
coil assembly remains stationary. This implies that the spinning magnet experiences an
upward axial force not present in the stationary configuration.

### Lenz Levitation Experiment (tweet 1938141188844097978, video 13_1938140068872609792)
Setup: Large AC wire coil (Lenz levitation coil), aluminum plate, DC motor, 24V DC
power supply. The AC coil drives at 150 V AC. The DC motor spins the aluminum disc.

Mechanism of standard Lenz levitation: an alternating axial magnetic field B_AC(t)
induces eddy currents in a conducting plate placed above the coil. The induced eddy
currents create an opposing magnetic field (Lenz's law: the induced EMF opposes the
change in flux, epsilon = -d(Phi_B)/dt). The interaction between the driving B_AC and
the eddy-current-induced field produces a repulsive (upward) force on the plate.
Quantitatively, the repulsive force scales as F ~ sigma * omega^2 * B^2 * delta * A,
where sigma is electrical conductivity, omega is AC angular frequency, B is peak field,
delta is skin depth, and A is area. At low omega (skin depth >> plate thickness), force
increases with omega^2. At high omega (skin depth << plate thickness), the repulsion
saturates and becomes approximately independent of omega.

Key experimental result (claimed novel, "NOT IN TEXTBOOKS"):
When the aluminum disc is set spinning rapidly (gyroscopic regime), the downward repulsive
strength of Lenz levitation drops to approximately 20% of the stationary-disc value.
Equivalently: fast gyroscopic spin reduces Lenz-effect magnetic repulsion by 80%.
  F_spin = 0.20 * F_stationary
Drew claims this coupling between disc angular momentum L = I * omega_spin and the
electromagnetic induction efficiency is not found in standard textbooks. No anti-gravity
effect (Z-axis rise) was produced; only the reduction in downward repulsive strength
was confirmed experimentally.

Interpretation: As the disc acquires large angular momentum L (gyroscopic regime), its
ability to support eddy currents in the plane perpendicular to its spin axis is altered.
The gyroscopic rigidity of the disc may suppress the in-plane eddy current circulation
that normally produces Lenz repulsion. This is a non-trivial coupling between classical
mechanics (rigid body rotation, Euler equations) and electromagnetic induction. No
standard textbook derivation of this effect was cited by Drew.

### Outer Flywheel with 16 Magnets + Inner Single Magnet System (tweet 2038912402595693015, video 13_2038909613236744192)
Device: Outer flywheel with 16 permanent magnets arranged in an S-S-N-N pattern
(4 groups of alternating polarity, or SSN-SS or similar as described: "SSN and SS that
goes anti-clockwise"). Inner single disc magnet: 70 mm diameter, 14 mm center hole,
arranged to spin clockwise relative to outer flywheel.

XY-plane dynamics (magnetic interaction):
At LOW frequencies of outer flywheel rotation:
  The inner disc magnet spins ANTI-CLOCKWISE (same direction as outer flywheel)
  because it is attracted weakly to the series of NN-SS poles in the outer flywheel.
  The attractive force dominates at low relative velocity (small induced EMF).

At HIGH frequencies of outer flywheel rotation:
  The inner disc magnet spins CLOCKWISE (opposite to outer flywheel)
  because the clockwise three-phase U-V-W field becomes more powerful than the
  weak magnetic attraction to the outer flywheel.
  The rotating field EMF dominates over static attraction.

Angular momentum conservation coupling (Z-axis dynamics):
When the inner magnet's spin FLIPS from anti-clockwise to clockwise (the transition
between low-frequency and high-frequency regimes), the outer flywheel counter-spins
even faster, to conserve total angular momentum:
  L_total = L_outer + L_inner = constant
  When delta_L_inner reverses sign (anti-clockwise -> clockwise), delta_L_outer
  must compensate: L_outer increases (spins faster).
This is the same angular momentum conservation principle demonstrated in the Action Lab
"spinning blade on floating boat" demonstration (video 13_2038909635663654913):
  L_total = 0 initially. When blade spin reverses, boat spins opposite to compensate.

### Corbino Disc + Three-Phase Outer Field (UFO Propulsion Configuration, tweet 2007939663563231736)
The Corbino disc differs from the Faraday disc in that current flows radially in a flat
annular conductor without a strong radial arm (no brushes required at intermediate radii).
The geometry: inner radius r1, outer radius r2, axial field B_z. Radial current flows
between inner and outer contacts. Lorentz force produces azimuthal torque continuously
around the full annular area.

In Drew's proposed UFO configuration:
  Outer stator: spinning three-phase magnetic field (U-V-W-U-V-W sequence, phase angle
  120 degrees between each phase), producing a rotating B-field at angular frequency omega_s

  radial current in the rotating external field

The three-phase outer field induces EMF in the Corbino disc via Faraday induction, driving
additional current and torque. The interaction between the Lorentz-driven spin (omega_r)
and the three-phase counter-spin (omega_s) is what Drew labels "Lorentz spin, then a
3-phase counter-spin."

The device with four sets of three-phase coils (outer) and four-magnet N-S-N-S rotor (inner)
is described explicitly in tweet 2000488534151737643.

### Axial Flux Motor / Generator Configuration (video 13_1914659684906328064 preview)
Device: Flat rotor disc (dark resin, approximately 15-20 cm diameter) between stator coil
assemblies on each side (axial flux geometry). Central shaft with chrome hub. Copper wire
stator coils (solenoid-wound enameled wire, approximately 8 cm diameter coils visible on
each side). Red electrical tape marker on rotor disc at approximately 3 o'clock position
(for optical speed measurement or Hall sensor reference).

Physics: Axial flux configuration. The stator coils produce B_z along the axis. If the
rotor contains embedded permanent magnets, it rotates (motor operation). If the rotor is
a conductor in an alternating axial field, it experiences eddy-current braking (induction
operation). The red tape marker enables rotational speed measurement via optical or
stroboscopic methods.
