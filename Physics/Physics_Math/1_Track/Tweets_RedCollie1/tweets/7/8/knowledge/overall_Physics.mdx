## 1. Lorentz Force in Radial-Current / Axial-Field Geometry (Homopolar / Corbino)

The fundamental force law applied throughout:
  F = q(E + v x B)

For a current-carrying conductor element dl carrying current I in external field B:
  dF = I (dl x B)

In the Corbino / Faraday-disc geometry:
  dl = dr * hat{r}  (radial current element)
  B = B_z * hat{z}  (axial magnetic field)
  dF = I * dr * (hat{r} x hat{z}) = I * dr * (-hat{phi})

The azimuthal force component drives disc rotation. Integrating from inner radius r1
to outer radius r2 (Corbino geometry):
  tau = integral_{r1}^{r2} I * r * B_z * dr = I * B_z * (r2^2 - r1^2) / 2

For uniform current density J_r = I / (2 * pi * r * d) (d = disc thickness):
  tau = J_r * B_z * integral r dA = B_z * I * (r2^2 - r1^2) / 2

This is the Lorentz torque expression for homopolar/Corbino disc motors. Spin direction
is determined by the sign of I and the direction of B_z (right-hand rule).

## 2. Three-Phase Rotating Magnetic Field

A three-phase (U-V-W) set of stator coils wound 120 degrees apart in space, driven
by three-phase currents offset 120 degrees in time, produces a rotating magnetic field:
  B_x(t) = B_m * cos(omega_s * t)
  B_y(t) = B_m * cos(omega_s * t - 2*pi/3)
  B_total rotates at angular frequency omega_s (synchronous speed)

In Drew's outer-stator configuration, four sets of three-phase coils produce a four-pole
rotating field (p=4 poles), so synchronous mechanical speed:
  omega_s_mech = omega_e / (p/2) = omega_e / 2

where omega_e is electrical angular frequency of the supply.

The inner Corbino disc (rotor) spins at omega_r. The slip:
  s = (omega_s - omega_r) / omega_s

At s = 1 (rotor stationary), maximum induction. At s = 0 (synchronous), zero slip torque.
The behavior observed (low frequency: disc spins with outer flywheel; high frequency: disc
spins counter to outer flywheel) can be modeled as a transition from attraction-dominated
to induction-dominated regime as frequency increases.

## 3. Angular Momentum Conservation in Counter-Spin Experiment

For an isolated system (frictionless floating boat or low-friction flywheel assembly):
  L_total = L_outer + L_inner = const

If L_inner changes by delta_L (reversal of spin direction):
  delta_L_inner = -2 * I_inner * omega_inner  (sign reversal)
  delta_L_outer = +2 * I_inner * omega_inner  (opposite change to conserve L_total)
  omega_outer_new = omega_outer_old + 2 * I_inner * omega_inner / I_outer

This is the classical angular momentum conservation law. Drew's experiment (and the Action
Lab boat demonstration) confirms: when the inner magnet's spin direction flips from
anti-clockwise to clockwise, the outer flywheel counter-spins faster by exactly this amount.

The key claim: if this angular-momentum-coupled counter-spin can be harnessed as a
net axial (Z-axis) force component, it would constitute a propulsion mechanism beyond
classical reaction-mass propulsion. The coupling between rotational angular momentum
transfer and linear momentum (axial force) is the non-trivial step Drew has not yet
experimentally demonstrated.

## 7. Axial Flux Motor / Induction Motor Physics

In the axial flux configuration (video 13_1914659684906328064):
  Stator coils produce B_z along rotation axis
  If rotor contains permanent magnets: synchronous motor operation
    Torque T = p * psi_m * I_q  (standard PMSM torque equation, p = pole pairs,
    psi_m = magnet flux linkage, I_q = quadrature current component)
  If rotor is conducting plate in alternating B_z: induction motor operation
    Torque T ~ s * V_1^2 * R_2 / (R_2^2 + (s*X_2)^2)  (standard induction motor
    torque-slip equation, V_1 = applied voltage, R_2 = rotor resistance, X_2 = rotor
    reactance, s = slip)

  L_total = I_blade * omega_blade + I_boat * 0 = I_blade * omega_blade (not zero,
