## 1. Capacitor Energy Storage and Discharge

Source: 13_1914664247965171712_preview.txt, 13_1914664288897392642_preview.txt

Capacitor stored energy:
    E = (1/2) * C * V^2

where:
* E = energy in joules (J)
* C = capacitance in farads (F)
* V = voltage at which capacitor is charged (V)

For N capacitors in parallel:
    C_parallel = N * C_individual
    E_total = (1/2) * N * C_individual * V^2

The three-capacitor bank observed (N = 3) triples the total available energy and capacitance.

Instantaneous discharge current (into load resistance R):
    I(t) = (V_0 / R) * exp(-t / (R * C))

Peak current at t=0:
    I_peak = V_0 / R

The time constant:
    tau_RC = R * C

For supercapacitors with C = 10 F, R = 0.1 ohm: tau = 1 s (slow discharge).
For electrolytic capacitors with C = 10^-2 F, R = 0.1 ohm: tau = 10^-3 s (pulse discharge).

## 2. Three-Phase Rotating Magnetic Field

Source: 13_2044596310473580544_video.mdx

Three-phase supply with phases separated by 2*pi/3 radians (120 degrees):
    I_1(t) = I_0 * cos(omega * t)
    I_2(t) = I_0 * cos(omega * t - 2*pi/3)
    I_3(t) = I_0 * cos(omega * t - 4*pi/3)

Magnetic field contributions from three coils at angles 0, 2*pi/3, 4*pi/3 in the XY-plane:
    B_x(t) = B_0 * [cos(0) * I_1(t) + cos(2*pi/3) * I_2(t) + cos(4*pi/3) * I_3(t)]
    B_y(t) = B_0 * [sin(0) * I_1(t) + sin(2*pi/3) * I_2(t) + sin(4*pi/3) * I_3(t)]

Evaluating using trigonometric identities:
    B_x(t) = (3/2) * B_0 * I_0 * cos(omega * t)
    B_y(t) = (3/2) * B_0 * I_0 * sin(omega * t)

The resultant field vector:
    B_rot(t) = (3/2) * B_0 * I_0 * [cos(omega * t) x-hat + sin(omega * t) y-hat]

Magnitude: |B_rot| = (3/2) * B_0 * I_0 = constant.
The field vector rotates uniformly in the XY-plane at angular frequency omega = 2*pi*f.

For f = 12 Hz (observed oscillation rate of disk magnet):
    omega = 2 * pi * 12 = 75.4 rad/s

The rotating field period: T = 1/f = 1/12 s ≈ 83 ms.

## 3. Magnetic Torque on Disk Magnet

Source: 13_2044596310473580544_video.mdx

Torque on a magnetic dipole m in external field B:
    tau = m x B

where m = M * V_magnet * n-hat (M = remanent magnetization of magnet, V_magnet = volume, n-hat = dipole axis unit vector).

For a disk magnet (diameter d = 50 mm, central bore diameter d_bore = 20 mm, thickness t):
    V_magnet = pi * t * [(d/2)^2 - (d_bore/2)^2] = pi * t * [(0.025)^2 - (0.010)^2] = pi * t * (6.25 - 1.00) x 10^-4 = pi * t * 5.25 x 10^-4 m^3

For NdFeB magnet with M ≈ 10^6 A/m:
    |m| = 10^6 * pi * t * 5.25 x 10^-4 = 1.65 * t (A*m^2) [where t is in meters]

Maximum torque when m is perpendicular to B:
    |tau_max| = |m| * |B|

This torque drives the magnet to align with the rotating field vector. If the moment of inertia of the disk magnet about its central axis is I_disk:
    I_disk = (1/2) * M_disk * [(r_outer)^2 - (r_inner)^2]

the angular acceleration at maximum torque:
    alpha = tau_max / I_disk

For large enough torque relative to I_disk, the magnet synchronizes with the rotating field (synchronous motor operation). At 12 Hz this is readily achievable for small disk magnets.

## 4. Counter-Rotating Frequency and Relative Angular Velocity

Source: 13_2044596310473580544_video.mdx

Inner magnet angular frequency: omega_c (clockwise, so -omega_c in standard convention)
Outer flywheel angular frequency: omega_f (anticlockwise, +omega_f)

Relative angular velocity between inner magnet and outer flywheel:
    omega_rel = omega_f + omega_c = omega_f - (-omega_c)

For omega_f = 2*pi*40 = 251.3 rad/s and omega_c = 2*pi*40 = 251.3 rad/s:
    omega_rel = 251.3 + 251.3 = 502.6 rad/s = 2*pi*80 rad/s → 80 Hz

This is the rate at which the relative phase between the two spinning magnetic assemblies changes. Any test object or measurement point in the inter-ring gap experiences a magnetic field pattern that cycles at 80 Hz.

## 5. Angular Momentum of Counter-Rotating Assembly

Source: 13_2044596310473580544_video.mdx, tweet 1957293340841709744

Angular momentum of flywheel:
    L_f = I_f * omega_f (anticlockwise)

Angular momentum of central magnet:
    L_c = I_c * omega_c (clockwise) = -I_c * omega_c (in same sign convention)

Net angular momentum:
    L_net = I_f * omega_f - I_c * omega_c

In the limit I_f * omega_f = I_c * omega_c:
    L_net = 0

A system with L_net = 0 has no gyroscopic resistance to precession (it behaves as if it were not spinning, from the perspective of external torques). This cancellation of angular momenta in the counter-rotating assembly may be a key design feature of the proposed drive — it would allow the spin axis to reorient without the gyroscopic resistance typical of a single fast-spinning rotor.

## 6. Dipole-Dipole Magnetic Interaction Force

Source: 13_2044596310473580544_video.mdx, overall magnetic framework

Force between two aligned magnetic dipoles m_1 and m_2 separated by distance r along the axis:
    F_axial = (3 * mu_0 / (2 * pi)) * m_1 * m_2 / r^4

(attractive for aligned dipoles, repulsive for antiparallel)

Force on central magnet (south pole up) from four surrounding ring magnets (north pole up), at a common height (r = horizontal ring radius):
The central magnet is attracted in all four horizontal directions, but these cancel by symmetry. The net force is axial.

If the central magnet is displaced upward by delta_z from the plane of the ring magnets, the force from each ring magnet has a downward component (restoring toward the ring plane). This creates a stable equilibrium in the axial direction at the ring plane — which is consistent with the observed "hovering right at the same level as those ring magnets because it's attracted to it at all four sides."

When three-phase drive causes the central magnet to jump upward above the ring plane, it encounters a repulsive region (if above the north-pole-up ring magnets, the south-pole-up central magnet is now approaching the south poles of the ring magnets from above — net repulsion). This creates the "barrier of attraction" described: "upward jumping force from below the plane to the plane where it hits a barrier of attraction."

## 7. Gyroscope Precession Rate

Source: 13_1957293251477811200_video.mdx

Standard gyroscope precession angular velocity:
    Omega_p = tau / L = (M * g * r_cm) / (I * omega)

where:
* Omega_p = precession rate (rad/s)
* tau = gravitational torque = M * g * r_cm (N*m)
* M = gyroscope mass (kg)
* g = gravitational acceleration = 9.81 m/s^2
* r_cm = distance from pivot to center of mass (m)
* I = moment of inertia about spin axis (kg*m^2)
* omega = spin angular velocity (rad/s)

For a lopsided gyroscope with additional mass m_tack at distance r_tack from spin axis:
    tau_additional = m_tack * g * r_tack * sin(theta)

where theta is the angle between the spin axis and vertical. The precession goes clockwise (viewed from above) for a gyroscope with its angular momentum pointing upward and the gravitational torque pointing in the horizontal plane away from the center-of-mass displacement.

Drew's "vortex theory" is a phenomenological re-description: the precession is said to occur because "there's a vortex in space created by the spinning gyroscope" and "it's pressing against normal space and rolling with it like that just sideways." This is not a mathematically distinct prediction from the standard formula above — it is a different physical picture that leads to the same observed precession direction and rate.

## 8. Topological Mathematical Content — Celtic Knot as Torus Knot

Source: 3_1923179966268841984.txt (image description of crop circle)

From the image description of the crop circle at The Gallops, Wiltshire, 15 May 2025:
"Celtic quaternary knots represent continuous looping with no beginning or end — a topological motif. In the context of UAP physics and crop circle research, the Celtic knot is sometimes cited as an ancient representation of: (1) topological field structures (field lines that continuously loop), (2) torus knots (mathematical knots that live on the surface of a torus), or (3) the Hopf fibration."

Mathematical content cited:

Torus knot T(p,q): A torus knot lives on the surface of a torus T^2 = S^1 x S^1 in R^3. It winds p times around the longitudinal direction and q times around the meridional direction. The quaternary (4-fold symmetry) Celtic knot in this formation is consistent with T(4,2) or T(2,4) topology (an unknot) or T(4,3) (a proper knot).

Hopf fibration: The Hopf fibration is a continuous surjection from S^3 to S^2 with fiber S^1:
    h: S^3 → S^2

Every point on S^2 has a preimage that is a circle (S^1) in S^3. The fibers are linked Hopf circles — each pair of fibers is linked exactly once. The Hopf fibration appears in quantum mechanics (spinor fibration), magnetic monopole theory (fiber bundle over S^2 with U(1) gauge structure), and topological field theory.

In the context of rotating magnetic fields: A rotating magnetic dipole field has a topological structure that can be related to the Hopf fibration through the hairy ball theorem and the classification of vector fields on S^2.

## 9. Rail Gun Acceleration Math

Source: 13_1914664288897392642_preview.txt

For a rail gun driven by capacitor bank, the equation of motion of the armature (mass m_armature) along the rails:
    m_armature * (d^2 x / dt^2) = F(t) = (1/2) * (dL/dx) * I(t)^2

where L(x) is the inductance as a function of armature position x, and I(t) is the discharge current.

The per-unit-length inductance gradient for parallel rails of separation w:
    dL/dx ≈ mu_0 / pi * [ln(w/r) + 0.25]

where r is the rail conductor radius.

For a simple LC discharge circuit (capacitor C discharging through rail inductance L and resistance R):
    L * (d^2 q / dt^2) + R * (dq / dt) + q/C = 0

where q is the charge remaining on the capacitor. This gives oscillatory (underdamped) or critically/overdamped discharge depending on the damping ratio:
    zeta = R / (2 * sqrt(L/C))

The muzzle energy is:
    E_muzzle = (1/2) * m_armature * v_final^2 = (1/2) * C * V_0^2 * eta

where eta is the efficiency (typically 1-40% for simple lab rail guns).

## 3. Gyroscopic Precession Rate (Standard Classical Derivation)

Standard result for precession angular velocity under gravitational torque:
  Omega_p = (M * g * r) / L = (M * g * r) / (I * omega)
where:
  M = mass of gyroscope
  g = 9.81 m/s^2 (gravitational acceleration)
  r = distance from pivot to center of mass
  I = principal moment of inertia about spin axis
  omega = spin angular velocity (rad/s)

Direction: Omega_p is perpendicular to L (angular momentum), causing L to precess around
the vertical (gravity direction). For standard spin orientation (upward angular momentum
vector, gravitational torque pointing horizontally), precession is clockwise viewed from above.

The experiment in video 13_1957293251477811200 adds an asymmetric mass ("blue tackle") to
one side, increasing the gravitational torque tau = r x (M_added * g) on the loaded side,
which increases Omega_p locally and causes the gyroscope to drift in one direction.

## 4. Lorentz Force in Homopolar / Liquid-Metal Motor

Standard expression:
  F = q * (v x B) (for a charge carrier)
  f = J x B (force per unit volume, for current density J in field B)
  tau = integral(r x (J x B)) dV (net torque on conducting body)

For radial current flow (J = J_r * r_hat) in axial field (B = B_z * z_hat):
  J x B = (J_r * B_z) * (r_hat x z_hat) = J_r * B_z * (-theta_hat)
(The azimuthal force is in the -theta direction for outward radial current in upward B.)
Net torque drives the conductor in the azimuthal direction (spin).

Applied to galinstan vortex:
  tau_azimuthal = (J_r * B_z) * Volume_active
  Power_input = tau * omega = I * V (energy conservation in ideal motor)

At 12V DC: P = V * I. Wire heating P_heat = I^2 * R_wire.
Fraction wasted as heat: eta = 1 - R_wire / R_total. For low-resistance galinstan:
R_galinstan << R_wire => most power is dissipated in the wire, not the motor.
Engineering solution: add series resistance R_s or use thicker/lower-resistance wire.

## 6. Three-Phase Rotating Field Vector (Mathematical Description)

For a three-phase AC supply with angular frequency omega and phase separation 2*pi/3:
  B_1(t) = B_0 * cos(omega * t) * n_hat_1
  B_2(t) = B_0 * cos(omega * t - 2*pi/3) * n_hat_2
  B_3(t) = B_0 * cos(omega * t - 4*pi/3) * n_hat_3
where n_hat_1, n_hat_2, n_hat_3 are unit vectors in the stator plane at 120-degree intervals.

The vector sum:
  B_total(t) = (3/2) * B_0 * [cos(omega*t) * x_hat + sin(omega*t) * y_hat]
This is a rotating field of constant magnitude (3/2)*B_0 at angular frequency omega.
Source: six-wire coil assembly ("six wire coils" mentioned in video 13_2044596310473580544).

Ring magnet counter-spin drive: "Actually they're using clockwise three phase power to do
so for these six wire coils." This means the three-phase supply phase sequence drives the
ring magnets in the clockwise direction, while the crop-picture prescription calls for
counter-clockwise ring rotation — achieved by reversing two of the three phase connections
(phase reversal in a three-phase system reverses the rotation direction of the rotating field).

## 7. Scale Weight Measurement Equation for Anomalous Lift Detection

Proposed experiment from video 13_2044596310473580544:
Total system weight measurement on scale:
  W_measured = W_true - F_lift_anomalous
where F_lift_anomalous is any non-Newtonian upward force generated by the counter-rotating
magnetic system.

Standard prediction: F_lift_anomalous = 0 (Newton's Third Law holds, internal forces cancel).
Claimed possibility: F_lift_anomalous > 0 at omega_rel = 80 Hz.

If W_measured < W_true (static weight), this constitutes evidence for anomalous lift.
The measurement requires accurate baseline (device off) vs. active (device spinning at
omega_1 + omega_2 ~ 80 Hz relative) comparison.
Thermal drift and vibration are confounds requiring careful experimental design.
