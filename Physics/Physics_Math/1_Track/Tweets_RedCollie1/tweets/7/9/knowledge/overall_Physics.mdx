## 1. Lorentz Force in the Liquid-Metal Homopolar Motor

Source: tweet 1966452992540017086, tweet 1988803652103127145

The fundamental drive mechanism of the galinstan homopolar motor is the Lorentz body force on a current-carrying conductor in a magnetic field:

    f = J x B

where:
* f = body force density (N/m^3) on the liquid metal
* J = current density vector (A/m^2) in the liquid metal
* B = applied static magnetic field vector (T), produced by the outer ring of electromagnets

In the homopolar motor geometry with an axial B field (B = B_z z-hat) and a radial current density (J = J_r r-hat), the cross product gives:

    f = J_r B_z (r-hat x z-hat) = -J_r B_z phi-hat

This produces an azimuthal (circumferential) body force on the liquid metal, driving it to rotate. The sign determines the direction of rotation (clockwise or anticlockwise, depending on the sign of J_r and the direction of B_z).

The liquid metal (galinstan) has electrical conductivity sigma ≈ 3.46 x 10^6 S/m. The current density in the motor is set by the applied voltage and the geometry. At 12 V DC with wire resistance R and liquid metal resistance R_lm:

    I = V / (R + R_lm)

The power dissipated:
    P = I^2 * R_total

This explains the wire heating — at 12 V without a ballast resistor, the current is limited only by the wire resistance, which is low, causing high I and high P = I^2 * R in the wire, leading to thermal failure within ~10 seconds.

## 2. Three-Phase Rotating Magnetic Field — Interaction with Disk Magnet

Source: 13_2044596310473580544_video.mdx

A balanced three-phase AC supply with phases:
    phi_1(t) = I_0 sin(omega t)
    phi_2(t) = I_0 sin(omega t - 2*pi/3)
    phi_3(t) = I_0 sin(omega t - 4*pi/3)

applied to three coils arranged symmetrically (120 degrees apart in space) produces a rotating magnetic field vector B_rot(t) in the plane of the coils:

    B_rot(t) = (3/2) * B_peak * [cos(omega*t) x-hat + sin(omega*t) y-hat]

where omega = 2*pi*f, f ≈ 12 Hz in the experimental results.

The disk magnet, placed on the axis of this rotating field, experiences a torque:

    tau = m x B_rot(t)

where m is the magnetic dipole moment of the disk magnet. If the magnet is free to rotate, it follows the rotating field (synchronous operation) at the drive frequency. The off-axis central bore (20 mm hole in a 50 mm disk) introduces asymmetry that causes the magnet to wobble in a precession-like motion off-axis, exactly as described: "wobbling in plane slightly off axis because of the hole in the center."

The axial (Z-direction) oscillation at ~12 Hz is not trivially explained by the rotating field alone. Drew's claim is that the three-phase drive causes the disk magnet to oscillate axially. A possible mechanism: the wobble couples to the axis through the gradient of the magnetic field from the surrounding ring of magnets. If the magnet precesses off-axis, its distance from the surrounding ring magnets varies periodically, and the net axial force from those ring magnets oscillates at the precession frequency, driving axial oscillation.

Force from surrounding ring magnets on central magnet (simplified dipole-dipole approximation):
    F_z ~ (3/2) * mu_0 / (4*pi) * m_central * m_ring / r^4

where r is the separation between dipoles. As the central magnet precesses and changes r periodically, F_z oscillates.

## 3. Counter-Rotating Disc Dynamics — Gyroscopic Effects

Source: 13_2044596310473580544_video.mdx, tweet 1957293340841709744

The experimental setup involves a flywheel (outer ring) rotating at omega_f (anticlockwise) and a central magnet rotating at omega_c (clockwise). Gyroscopic rigidity of the flywheel:

    L_flywheel = I * omega_f

where I is the moment of inertia of the flywheel about its symmetry axis. At 20 Hz (omega_f = 2*pi*20 = 125.7 rad/s), the flywheel has substantial angular momentum. Gyroscopic force becomes significant: "a little gyroscopic force."

When the central magnet is introduced spinning clockwise at omega_c, the net angular momentum of the system is:

    L_total = I_f * omega_f - I_c * omega_c

(opposite signs because counter-rotating). In the limit where |L_f| ≈ |L_c|, the net angular momentum approaches zero, which may reduce gyroscopic resistance to reorientation of the spin axis.

## 4. Gyroscope Precession and Vortex Theory

Source: 13_1957293251477811200_video.mdx, tweet 1957293340841709744

From video transcription: "A spinning gyroscope can get slightly lopsided if we place some blue tack on one side that gets pulled down more strongly by gravity on this side than that side. And also we see it goes clockwise in the circle."

Standard gyroscope precession: When an external torque tau is applied to a spinning gyroscope with angular momentum L = I*omega, the precession angular velocity is:

    Omega_precession = tau / L = tau / (I * omega)

For a gyroscope with a slight mass imbalance (blue tack on one side), the gravitational torque:
    tau = m_tack * g * r_tack

where r_tack is the distance of the extra mass from the spin axis. This causes clockwise precession when viewed from above (for a downward-pointing weight on a gyroscope spinning such that L points upward).

Drew's proposed "vortex theory" reinterpretation: "We can understand where the gyroscope precesses clockwise because there's a vortex in space created by the spinning gyroscope. When it pulls down on one side, it's pressing against normal space and rolling with it like that — just sideways — and that makes it go round and round at the boundary layer." This is a non-standard descriptive framework invoking a "vortex in space" as the cause of precession, rather than the torque-angular-momentum relation above. This is a phenomenological re-description, not a derivation.

## 5. Non-Newtonian Force Hypothesis at High Spin Rates

Source: 13_2044596310473580544_video.mdx

Stated claim: "We may see non-Newtonian forces which change the interactions quite a lot due to the fast spin rates." Context: central magnet at ~40 Hz + flywheel at ~40 Hz in opposite direction = relative velocity ~80 Hz.

Standard analysis: In Newtonian mechanics, internal forces (forces between parts of the same system) cancel by Newton's Third Law, and cannot change the center-of-mass momentum of the system. A magnet pushing upward on a flywheel within the same device will be exactly counteracted by the flywheel pushing downward on the magnet. The total weight of the system measured on a scale is unchanged.

The anomalous claim: at high relative spin rates, this cancellation may not hold exactly. Possible physical mechanisms that could produce an apparent violation (all speculative / not in mainstream physics):
* Relativistic corrections to the magnetostatic interaction at high speeds (special relativistic effects on moving magnetic dipoles — Biot-Savart corrections for moving source): these would be of order (v/c)^2 ≈ (omega * r / c)^2. For omega = 80 Hz * 2*pi ≈ 503 rad/s and r ≈ 0.05 m: v ≈ 25 m/s, (v/c)^2 ≈ 7 x 10^-15 — entirely negligible.
* Woodward-Mach transient mass fluctuation effect: Woodward's theory predicts transient mass fluctuations in accelerating energy-storage objects: delta_m = -(1/c^2) * (P / (phi_0)) where P is the power flow rate and phi_0 is the Newtonian gravitational potential. At high rotational accelerations, the magnets (as energy storage objects) might have oscillating effective mass, but this effect is measured in the pico-gram range for typical laboratory power levels.
* Spin-spin gravitomagnetic coupling (Lense-Thirring extension to laboratory scale): in general relativity, rotating masses drag spacetime (Lense-Thirring effect). At laboratory spin rates and masses, the effect is ~10^-30 m/s^2 — not measurable.

Drew's stated experimental approach: "We have to put the whole device on a weighing scale and see how these motions affect its actual total gravity weight... just like for a real UFO." This is the correct experimental approach — if the effect exists, a precision scale would show it. He cites "lens levitation or spin" as a prior observation.

## 1. Gyroscopic Precession — Standard Framework vs. Proposed Vortex Theory

Standard classical mechanics (Eulerian):
The precession of a gyroscope under an applied torque tau is governed by:
  tau = dL/dt
where L = I * omega is the angular momentum (I = moment of inertia, omega = spin angular
velocity). Under gravitational torque tau = r x (mg) acting on a gyroscope with spin
axis tilted from vertical, the precession angular velocity is:
  Omega_p = tau / L = (mgr) / (I * omega)
Direction: Omega_p is perpendicular to both tau and L, giving circular horizontal
precession. For the standard top: clockwise precession when viewed from above under
standard downward gravity with standard spin orientation.

Proposed alternative (vortex theory, video 13_1957293251477811200):
"There's a vortex in space created by the spinning gyroscope."
"It's when it pulls down on one side, it's pressing against normal space and rolling
with it like that — sideways — and that makes it go round and round at the boundary layer."

Claim: the spinning body generates a spatial vortex structure; asymmetric loading causes
the spinning body to "press" against this vortex at a boundary layer, producing the
observed circular precession. This is not mathematically formalised in the source
material — it is a qualitative physical model.

Observation in experiment: "A spinning gyroscope can get slightly loft-sided if we place
some blue tackle [a weight] on one side that gets pulled down more strongly by gravity
on this side than that side. And also we see it goes clockwise in the circle."
— Consistent with standard precession prediction (Omega_p clockwise from above with the
appropriate spin/load configuration).

## 3. Three-Phase AC Rotating Magnetic Field and Axial Oscillation of Disk Magnet

Three-phase AC generates a rotating magnetic field vector B_rot(t) in the plane of the
stator coil assembly:
  B_x(t) = B_0 * cos(omega*t)
  B_y(t) = B_0 * cos(omega*t - 2*pi/3)
  B_z(t) = B_0 * cos(omega*t - 4*pi/3)
(for a three-phase system with 120-degree phase separation)
The resultant field vector rotates at angular frequency omega in the stator plane.

For a disk magnet placed on a vertical axis within this rotating field, the magnetic
torque drives the disk to spin at the field rotation frequency (synchronous motor
behavior if friction is low enough, or asynchronous with slip if not).

Axial oscillation mechanism:
