PHYSICS CONTENT — FARADAY'S PARADOX AND HOMOPOLAR MOTOR/GENERATOR:
Faraday's paradox (also called "the unipolar induction paradox" or "Faraday disc paradox") is a classic problem in electromagnetism. The paradox concerns the homopolar generator (Faraday disc):

- A conducting disc rotates in a magnetic field aligned with the disc's rotation axis (z-axis)
- A voltage (EMF) is generated between the center and the rim of the disc
- The paradox: Which part of the system is actually moving? In some configurations, the magnetic field rotates with the disc (co-rotating magnets). In others, the magnets are stationary.
- The question: Does the magnet need to move relative to the conductor to generate EMF? Or does only the conductor's motion relative to the magnetic field lines matter?
- Resolution involves understanding that the magnetic field lines in a homopolar generator are not "attached" to the magnet — they are stationary even when the magnet rotates, because B-field lines of a symmetric dipole are rotationally symmetric
- This was historically one of the first paradoxes of electromagnetic induction and led Einstein to develop special relativity partly in response to similar questions about relative motion and electromagnetic fields

SIGNIFICANCE IN UAP/EXOTIC PHYSICS CONTEXT:
The homopolar motor/generator is significant in exotic propulsion research because:
1. Thomas Townsend Brown allegedly used homopolar principles in his "electrogravitic" experiments
2. Some theorists claim homopolar generators operating in specific configurations can tap zero-point energy
3. The Faraday paradox, if misunderstood, leads to claims of "free energy" from spinning magnets
4. The resolution of Faraday's paradox involves careful analysis of reference frames — relevant to discussions of Lorentz invariance and exotic field theories
