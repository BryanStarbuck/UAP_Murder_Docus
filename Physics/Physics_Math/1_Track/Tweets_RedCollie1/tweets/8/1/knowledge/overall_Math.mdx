## 1. Homopolar Generator EMF — Exact Derivation

EMF = ∫₀ᴿ (v × B) · dr

Vector components:
* v = ωr φ̂  (velocity of disc element at radius r, angular velocity ω)
* B = B_z ẑ  (uniform axial magnetic field)

Cross product: v × B = ωr(φ̂ × ẑ) = ωr(-r̂) = -ωr r̂

Dot with dr = dr r̂:
(v × B) · dr r̂ = -ωr B_z dr

Integrating outward (center to rim):
EMF = -∫₀ᴿ ωr B_z dr = -½ ω B_z R²

Magnitude: |EMF| = ½ ω B_z R²

Where:
* ω [rad/s] = angular velocity of conducting disc
* B_z [T] = axial component of magnetic flux density
* R [m] = disc radius
* EMF [V] = open-circuit voltage between center and rim

Key relationship: EMF ∝ ω × B_z × R² — quadratic in disc radius, linear in field strength
and rotation rate. Doubling R quadruples EMF; doubling ω or B_z doubles EMF.

## 3. Three-Phase Rotating Magnetic Field — Phasor Analysis

Three coils (a, b, c) at spatial positions 0°, 120°, 240° in the XY-plane.
Coil a produces field: B_a(t) = B_max cos(ωt) x̂
Coil b produces field: B_b(t) = B_max cos(ωt - 2π/3)(−½x̂ + (√3/2)ŷ)
Coil c produces field: B_c(t) = B_max cos(ωt + 2π/3)(−½x̂ − (√3/2)ŷ)

Total field:
B_x(t) = B_max[cos(ωt) - ½cos(ωt-2π/3) - ½cos(ωt+2π/3)]
       = B_max[cos(ωt) - ½·2cos(ωt)cos(2π/3)]
       = B_max[cos(ωt) + ½cos(ωt)]  [since cos(2π/3) = -½]
       = (3/2)B_max cos(ωt)

B_y(t) = B_max[(√3/2)cos(ωt-2π/3) - (√3/2)cos(ωt+2π/3)]
       = B_max(√3/2)[2sin(ωt)sin(2π/3)]
       = B_max(√3/2)(√3)sin(ωt)
       = (3/2)B_max sin(ωt)

Result: B_total(t) = (3/2)B_max [cos(ωt) x̂ + sin(ωt) ŷ]

This is a vector of constant magnitude (3/2)B_max rotating at angular frequency ω in the
XY-plane. The factor 3/2 is the three-phase RMF amplitude gain (larger than any single
coil's B_max by 50%).

### 11. Lorentz Force on Spinning Magnetic Dipole in Rotating Magnetic Field

For a magnetic dipole m = m_0 ẑ (north-up) spinning at angular velocity ω_m in a rotating field B_RMF rotating at ω_stator:

The interaction energy: U(t) = -m · B_RMF(t)

For m and B_RMF coaxial and co-rotating at the same speed (synchronous): U is constant, producing sustained torque rather than oscillating torque.

The synchronous torque (per classical induction theory):
τ = m × B_RMF = |m||B_RMF| sin(δ) φ̂

where δ is the phase lag between the rotor dipole axis and the RMF vector. At δ = 90°, torque is maximum. At δ = 0°, torque is zero but the rotor is in stable equilibrium (analogous to synchronous motor operation at rated load).

For the Z-axis force (not predicted by classical induction theory):
If the RMF has a Z-axis gradient component dB_z/dz ≠ 0, the axial force on the dipole is:
F_z = m_z (∂B_z/∂z) + m_x (∂B_x/∂z) + m_y (∂B_y/∂z)

In the time-average over one RMF rotation cycle, the m_x and m_y terms average to zero for a rotor spinning synchronously. Only the m_z term survives:
<F_z> = m_z <∂B_z/∂z>

This is classical magnetic gradient levitation. The stator's fringe field above the coil plane has a negative dB_z/dz (field decreases with height), giving: <F_z> = m_z × (negative gradient) — downward if m_z is positive (north-up). This cannot levitate a north-up rotor by this mechanism alone.

Drew's implied alternative: the spinning rotor in the counter-spin configuration generates an additional field that modifies dB_z/dz at the rotor location, changing the sign of the gradient force. This is the non-classical element and the one requiring experimental verification.
