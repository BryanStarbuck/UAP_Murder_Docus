## 1. Faraday's Law and Homopolar Induction

### 1a. EMF in Homopolar Generator (Faraday Disc)

For a conducting disc of radius R rotating at angular velocity ω in a uniform axial
magnetic field B_z (z-axis parallel to rotation axis):

EMF = ∫₀ᴿ (v × B) · dr̂

where v = ω r φ̂ (azimuthal velocity at radius r) and B = B_z ẑ.

v × B = (ω r φ̂) × (B_z ẑ) = ω r B_z (φ̂ × ẑ) = ω r B_z (-r̂)

Thus: (v × B) · r̂ = -ω r B_z   [Note: sign depends on field and rotation direction convention]

EMF = ∫₀ᴿ ω r B_z dr = ½ ω B_z R²

This is the fundamental Faraday disc EMF. The key physics point (resolution of "Faraday's
paradox"): B_z is the lab-frame field, which is independent of whether the magnet co-rotates
with the disc. The rotational symmetry of the dipole field means the B-field configuration
in the lab frame is invariant under rotation of the magnet about its symmetry axis.

Consequence: relative rotation between magnet and conductor is irrelevant; only conductor
motion relative to the lab-frame field matters. This invalidates intuitions derived from
motional EMF requiring "cutting" of field lines by relative motion between two objects.

### 1b. Lorentz Force in the Homopolar Motor

Force on a current-carrying conductor in a magnetic field:
dF = I (dL × B)

In the ring homopolar motor: current flows radially (r̂ direction) through the disc,
B = B_z ẑ (axial field from toroidal/ring permanent magnet).

dF = I (dr r̂) × (B_z ẑ) = I B_z dr (r̂ × ẑ) = I B_z dr (-φ̂)

Integrating from 0 to R: F_φ = -I B_z R (azimuthal force → rotation)

This produces pure torque τ = r × F about the z-axis, driving the disc/ring to spin.

## 3. Newton's Third Law and Proposed Bypass

### 3a. Classical Statement

For every action force F_12 exerted by object 1 on object 2, there is an equal and opposite
reaction force F_21 = -F_12 exerted by object 2 on object 1. In a propulsion context:
a craft accelerating upward by pushing down on the ground (or on exhaust mass) experiences
an equal upward reaction force balanced by an equal downward force on the ground/exhaust.

### 3c. Gyroscopic Dynamics as Related Mechanism

DC spin of the inner rotor creates a gyroscopic angular momentum vector L = Iω along the

The counter-rotating outer flywheel creates a net angular momentum of L_net = L_inner -

## 4. Corbino Disc Physics

### 4a. Standard Corbino Disc

The Corbino disc is a flat conducting disc with radial current flow in an axial magnetic
field. For a disc of conductivity σ, radial current density J_r in axial field B_z:

Azimuthal force per unit volume: f_φ = J_r B_z
(by Lorentz force law: f = J × B, with J = J_r r̂ and B = B_z ẑ → f = J_r B_z (r̂ × ẑ) = J_r B_z (-φ̂))

This drives azimuthal current flow (Hall effect geometry), which in turn produces additional
radial forces via the Hall effect:

Hall electric field: E_H = R_H (J × B) where R_H = 1/(ne) for electron carriers

In a conducting disc, the Hall effect creates an azimuthal electric field that drives
azimuthal current, which then contributes additional torque. The Corbino geometry (center
and rim contacts for radial current injection) eliminates the Hall EMF that would appear
in a bar geometry, making all the current radially uniform.

## 5. Magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) Coupling — Mercury/Galinstan

For a conducting fluid (mercury, σ_Hg ≈ 10^6 S/m; galinstan, σ_galinstan ≈ 3.46 × 10^6 S/m)
spinning at angular velocity Ω inside a spherical container of radius a in an external
magnetic field B:

### 5a. Induced EMF in Spinning Fluid

A conducting sphere spinning in an axial field B_z generates an EMF:
EMF_induced ~ Ω a² B_z (order-of-magnitude estimate)

This drives currents within the fluid that create a secondary magnetic field. For the
Buga orb configuration:
* DC current along z-axis creates toroidal field B_φ = μ_0 I_DC / (2πr).
* Spinning fluid in this toroidal field generates radial body force J_induced × B_φ.
* 5-phase RMF from outer coils drives fluid rotation and couples to the induced field.

### 5b. Hartmann Layer Physics

In MHD, when a conducting fluid flows in the presence of a magnetic field, a thin Hartmann
boundary layer forms at the fluid-wall interface with thickness:
δ_Ha = a / Ha
where Ha = B a (σ/μ)^(1/2) is the Hartmann number, σ is electrical conductivity, μ is
dynamic viscosity.

For mercury: μ = 1.526 × 10^-3 Pa·s, σ = 10^6 S/m.
For B = 0.1 T, a = 0.1 m: Ha = 0.1 × 0.1 × (10^6 / 1.526×10^-3)^(1/2) ≈ 0.1 × 0.1 × 2.56×10^4 ≈ 256

Hartmann number >> 1 indicates strong MHD effects dominate flow structure. These are not
simple rotary dynamics but full MHD turbulence regimes.
