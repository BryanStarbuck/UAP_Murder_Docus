## 2. Gyroscopic Lift: The Spinning Top Analogy and Extensions

Source: MDX 13_1916093512992653312_video.mdx; YAML 1916093709445194077

The behavior of a gyroscope under torque is described by:
tau = d(L)/dt = I * d(omega_spin * hat_n)/dt

For a spinning top with spin axis hat_n precessing about vertical hat_z under gravitational torque:
tau_grav = M * g * r_cm * (hat_n x hat_z) [magnitude = M*g*r_cm*sin(theta)]

Precession rate:
Omega_p = M*g*r_cm / (I_spin * omega_spin) [rad/s]

As omega_spin increases, Omega_p decreases. At very high omega_spin, the top's axis barely moves
(gyroscopic stiffness: S_gyro = I_spin * omega_spin).

In the three-phase magnet experiment: the "screw-advance" of the magnet (Newtonian regime) is
analogous to the torque-driven motion of a slow top. The "escape" into the field (non-Newtonian
regime) is analogous to the gyroscopic rigidity of a fast top that "pops up" rather than being
pushed down by an external force.

Extension proposed by experimenter:

DISPLACEMENT rather than mere precession, this would be a physically novel outcome. The specific
mechanism proposed: three-phase rotating field provides both the driving torque (maintaining spin)
and the vertical restoring force (levitation), while the gyroscopic term routes the mechanical
reaction into precession rather than ground contact.
