### Experimental Result: Z-Axis Force from Rotating Field

Regime 1 (Newtonian, f < 25 Hz):

- 10 Hz: +200 g upward force transferred to stainless steel plate above.
- 18 Hz: +250 g upward force.
- 20 Hz: +300 g upward force.
The upward force F_z increases with frequency (and therefore with angular velocity omega = 2*pi*f).
The force is transmitted mechanically through the rod to the plate, then reacts on the scale below
(Newton's Third Law, Newtonian regime).

Regime 2 (Non-Newtonian, f >= 25 Hz):
The magnet "escapes" the screw-advance regime and rises freely into the three-phase field region.
Scale reads zero. No force is transferred to the plate or scale.
Interpretation: the upward force is now generated WITHOUT a corresponding reaction on the support
structure. The magnet levitates freely in the three-phase field, driven upward by the electromagnetic
interaction between the rotating field and the spinning magnet.

### XY-Plane Rotation Causing Z-Axis Rise: The Key Relationship
The three-phase field rotates in the XY-plane. The magnet, aligned along Z, experiences:
tau_xy = m x B_xy(t)
This torque causes the magnet to spin about Z (if it tracks the field). As the magnet spins,
the interaction between the spinning magnetic moment and the rotating field can produce:
1. A torque in the XY-plane (driving spin).
2. An axial force along Z (if field gradients or asymmetric field configurations are present).

The axial force in the screw-advance regime arises because the magnet "screws" upward as it
rotates: the rotating field couples to the magnet geometry to convert rotational energy to
linear displacement. This is analogous to the working principle of a rotating magnetic motor
driving a linear actuator, but here the rotation IS the linear drive via the screw-advance mechanism.

At high frequency (Regime 2), the transition to free levitation suggests that the magnet's
angular momentum is sufficient to create a gyroscopic stabilization, and the three-phase field
provides enough restoring force (electromagnetic spring) to suspend the magnet axially without
mechanical contact. The weight of the magnet assembly is borne by the field, not by the rod.
