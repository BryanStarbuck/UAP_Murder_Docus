## 1. Three-Phase Rotating Magnetic Field: Fundamental Configuration

Source: YAML 1916093709445194077; MDX 13_1916093512992653312_video.mdx

### Three-Phase Field Geometry
A three-phase AC system drives three spatially separated coils, each displaced by 120 degrees
in phase angle (phi_k = k * 2*pi/3, k = 0, 1, 2).

If each coil produces a field component:
B_k(t) = B_0 * cos(omega*t - phi_k) * hat_n_k

where hat_n_k is the unit vector along coil k's axis, then for three coils arranged at 120-degree
spatial intervals in a plane, the superposition produces a spatially rotating field of constant
magnitude B_0, rotating at angular frequency omega:
B_total = B_0 * [cos(omega*t) * hat_x + sin(omega*t) * hat_y]

This is the standard symmetrical three-phase rotating field.

### XY-Plane vs. Z-Axis Field Orientation
The experiment explicitly distinguishes two configurations:
(a) Vertical wire coils: field vector rotates in the HORIZONTAL (XY) plane. The axis of rotation
is vertical (Z-axis). This is the standard configuration for a three-phase induction motor.
(b) Horizontal wire coils: field vector rotates about an axis in the horizontal plane. This places
the rotating field 90 degrees from configuration (a).

The critical experiment uses vertical wire coils (configuration a), producing a horizontal rotating
field, and drives a vertical-axis-of-symmetry permanent magnet into spin.

### Z-Axis Field Implications
When the rotating field is in the XY-plane, the torque on the permanent magnet dipole (moment
vector m aligned with the magnet's axis, initially vertical, i.e., along Z):
tau = m x B_total

B_total rotates in XY; m is along Z. The cross product m x B_total lies in the XY-plane and
acts to precess the magnet's spin axis. This is the mechanism underlying the Newtonian-to-non-
Newtonian spin transition (see overall_Physics.mdx).

## 2. Magnetic Repulsion Bearing

Source: MDX 13_1916093512992653312_video.mdx

Configuration: "below there's another magnet holding it up by repulsion of stability."
This is a passive magnetic bearing:
- A fixed lower magnet (same pole facing up) repels the lower end of the magnet/rod assembly.
- Provides passive Z-axis support force F_z = -dU_mag/dz, where U_mag is the potential energy
of the dipole-dipole interaction.
- For two co-axial magnetic dipoles with moments m_1 and m_2 aligned anti-parallel (repulsive):
  U(z) = (mu_0 / 4*pi) * (2*m_1*m_2 / z^3) [leading term, dipole approximation]
  F_z = -dU/dz = -3*(mu_0 / 2*pi) * (m_1*m_2 / z^4) [repulsive, upward on upper magnet]

This provides a non-contact, passive Z-axis support that allows free rotation without mechanical
bearing friction (neglecting air drag).

Note: passive magnetic bearings are unstable in all but the axial direction by Earnshaw's Theorem
(no stable equilibrium of a static charge/dipole in free space from static fields alone). The
vertical confinement is stabilized here by the rod constraint (the rod slides in a guide), providing
radial confinement mechanically while the magnetic repulsion provides axial support.

## 3. Permanent Magnet Dimensions and Spin Dynamics

Source: MDX 13_1916093512992653312_video.mdx

Magnet dimensions: 70 mm diameter, 14 mm central hole (annular magnet, ring magnet type).
Mounted on 8 mm aluminum rod.

For a ring magnet of outer radius R_out = 35 mm, inner radius R_in = 7 mm, height h (not
specified explicitly), the axial magnetic field on-axis and the dipole moment are determined by:
m = M * V_magnet = M * pi*(R_out^2 - R_in^2)*h

where M is magnetization (A/m). The moment of inertia for rotation about the Z-axis:
I_z = (1/2) * rho * V * (R_out^2 + R_in^2)

For the angular momentum at critical frequency f_c = 25 Hz:
L = I_z * omega_c = I_z * 2*pi*f_c

The gyroscopic precession rate Omega_p = tau / L = (m x B) / (I_z * omega_c), which decreases
as omega_c increases, explaining the transition from Newtonian screw-advance to gyroscopic levitation.

## 4. Five-Phase Power for Buga Orb-Sphere

Source: MDX 13_1934832086625710080_video.mdx; YAML 1934832303014072827

Five-phase system: five coils at 72-degree phase spacing (phi_k = k * 2*pi/5, k = 0..4).
The five-phase rotating field:
B_total(t) = (5*B_0/2) * [cos(omega*t) * hat_x + sin(omega*t) * hat_y]

The magnitude of the rotating field for an N-phase balanced system is (N/2)*B_0, so five-phase
gives 2.5*B_0 amplitude rotating field vs. (3/2)*B_0 for three-phase. Five-phase is smoother
(lower harmonic content, reduced torque ripple) and produces a higher effective field amplitude
per coil current than three-phase.

Laboratory three-phase analog: three coils at 120-degree spacing drive the experiment with
comparable geometry to what the Buga orb-sphere allegedly uses with five phases.

## 5. Lorentz Force on Conducting Fluids (Galinstan)

Source: YAML 1995974865439129760; YAML 2014995810874966207 (referenced_tweet)

Maxwell equation (Ampere's law with displacement current):
curl B = mu_0 * (J + epsilon_0 * dE/dt)

For a conductor in quasi-static approximation (low frequency, displacement current negligible):
curl B ~ mu_0 * J
J = sigma * (E + v x B) [Ohm's law in a moving conductor, sigma = conductivity]

The electromagnetic body force on the conducting fluid:
f_body = J x B [N/m^3]

For DC conduction through galinstan:
J applied by external electrodes; B applied by external permanent magnets or coils.
The J x B force drives azimuthal rotation.

For three-phase induction drive (no DC electrodes):
Traveling magnetic wave induces eddy currents J_eddy in galinstan.
J_eddy x B_applied produces distributed torque, driving rotation without ohmic electrode contact.
This is the homopolar induction drive principle.

Right-hand rule: J x B gives direction of force. The experimenter notes "interesting how they
describe the right-hand rule!" in the context of crop circle imagery showing this principle.

## 6. Counter-Rotating Magnetic Flywheels: Angular Momentum Cancellation

Source: YAML 2027846997080977894

Two magnetic flywheels spinning in opposite directions:
Flywheel 1: angular momentum L_1 = I_1 * omega_1 (e.g., +Z direction)
Flywheel 2: angular momentum L_2 = I_2 * (-omega_2) (-Z direction)
Net angular momentum: L_net = L_1 - L_2 = I_1*omega_1 - I_2*omega_2

For L_net = 0: I_1*omega_1 = I_2*omega_2 (balanced counter-rotation).

With zero net angular momentum, the system can generate forces in any direction without
gyroscopic reaction torque coupling. The magnetic coupling between the two spinning flywheels
produces rotating fields that can drive each other or interact with an external field.

The CARET program (claimed origin: classified reverse-engineering study) is cited as having
studied this architecture. Crop circle imagery is claimed to show this counter-rotating double
flywheel design "many places."

## 7. Three-Phase Inside, Linear Induction Outside Topology

Source: YAML 2014995810874966207

Proposed dual-drive topology for galinstan-based propulsion:
- Inner region: standard three-phase rotating field from coils surrounding the liquid metal.
  Produces rotating field B_inner(t) in XY-plane.
- Outer region: linear induction motor arrangement, producing a traveling magnetic wave along
  the Z-axis or the phi-direction.

The interaction:
- Inner field drives bulk rotation of galinstan (azimuthal current J_phi induced or directly driven).
- Outer linear induction field modifies the J distribution in galinstan to produce net Z-directed
  Lorentz force.

This separation of azimuthal spin drive (inner, 3-phase) from linear propulsion drive (outer,
linear induction) may allow independent control of spin rate and thrust vector.

## 8. Electromagnetic Device Hardware: Coil Configuration for Experiments

Source: TXT 13_1916093512992653312_preview.txt

Two large spool-wound coil assemblies, each approximately 10 cm diameter, wound with multiple
layers of copper wire. The coil inductance (for a multi-layer solenoid):
L = mu_0 * N^2 * A / l [single-layer approximation]
or for multi-layer, using Nagaoka's coefficient K_L:
L = K_L * mu_0 * N^2 * A / l

Uniform layer-by-layer winding ensures:
1. Predictable inductance (L determined by geometry).
2. Reduced self-resonance frequency (smaller parasitic capacitance between turns compared
   to random winding).
3. Reproducible field distribution: B(z) = mu_0 * n * I * K_L (uniform field for long solenoid,
   with end corrections for finite length).

Power supply type: ATX modified bench supply (12V DC rail, ~20A capability typical). This
suggests three-phase waveforms are generated by a downstream inverter/driver stage, not by
the ATX supply directly (ATX provides DC; inverter creates three-phase AC from DC).
