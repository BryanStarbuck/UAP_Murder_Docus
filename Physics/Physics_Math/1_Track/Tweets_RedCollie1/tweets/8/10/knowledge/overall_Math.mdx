## 1. Three-Phase Rotating Magnetic Field: Mathematical Structure

Source: YAML 1916093709445194077; MDX 13_1916093512992653312_video.mdx

### Field Components for Three-Phase System
Three coils at 120-degree spatial phase offset, driven by three-phase current:
I_k(t) = I_0 * cos(omega*t - k*2*pi/3), k = 0, 1, 2

Each coil produces a field along its axis hat_n_k:
B_k(t) = mu_0 * n * I_k(t) * hat_n_k = B_0 * cos(omega*t - k*2*pi/3) * hat_n_k

For coils arranged at 0, 120, 240 degrees in the XY-plane:
hat_n_0 = hat_x
hat_n_1 = cos(2*pi/3)*hat_x + sin(2*pi/3)*hat_y = (-1/2)*hat_x + (sqrt(3)/2)*hat_y
hat_n_2 = cos(4*pi/3)*hat_x + sin(4*pi/3)*hat_y = (-1/2)*hat_x - (sqrt(3)/2)*hat_y

Superposition:
B_total(t) = sum_{k=0}^{2} B_k(t) = (3*B_0/2)*[cos(omega*t)*hat_x + sin(omega*t)*hat_y]

Magnitude: |B_total| = 3*B_0/2 = constant (rotating field of constant amplitude).
Direction: rotates in XY-plane at angular frequency omega.

### Five-Phase Extension (Buga Orb-Sphere)
For five coils at 72-degree spacing (phi_k = k*2*pi/5):
B_total_5phase(t) = (5*B_0/2)*[cos(omega*t)*hat_x + sin(omega*t)*hat_y]

Amplitude ratio: 5-phase gives (5/2)*B_0 vs. (3/2)*B_0 for 3-phase.
Advantage: larger effective rotating field per coil current. Torque on internal magnet scales
as tau ~ m * |B_total|, so 5-phase produces (5/3) times the torque of 3-phase for the same coil
current.

## 2. Torque on Magnetic Dipole in Rotating Field

Source: MDX 13_1916093512992653312_video.mdx; YAML 1916093709445194077

Magnetic dipole moment of permanent magnet:
m = M * V_magnet [A*m^2]
where M is magnetization (A/m), V_magnet is volume.

Torque:
tau(t) = m x B_total(t)

For m = m_z * hat_z (magnet aligned along Z), B_total = B_rot * [cos(omega*t)*hat_x + sin(omega*t)*hat_y]:
tau(t) = m_z * B_rot * [cos(omega*t)*(hat_z x hat_x) + sin(omega*t)*(hat_z x hat_y)]
       = m_z * B_rot * [cos(omega*t)*hat_y - sin(omega*t)*hat_x]

This torque lies in the XY-plane and drives precession of the Z-axis spin about the XY rotating field.
If the magnet tilts into the XY-plane (spin axis = hat_x or hat_y), then:
m = m_0 * [cos(omega*t)*hat_x + sin(omega*t)*hat_y] (magnet tracks field, synchronous)
tau = m x B_total = 0 (parallel, no torque in synchronous mode)

In the synchronous regime, the magnet tracks the rotating field with zero net torque (stable
attraction state). All driving torque then goes to accelerating the magnet from zero to synchronous speed.

## 3. Angular Momentum and Gyroscopic Precession Rate

Source: MDX 13_1916093512992653312_video.mdx

Angular momentum of rotating magnet:
L = I_z * omega_spin [J*s = kg*m^2/s]

Moment of inertia for ring magnet (annular disk, rotation about Z-axis):
I_z = (1/2) * M_mag * (R_out^2 + R_in^2)
where M_mag is mass (kg), R_out = 35 mm, R_in = 7 mm.

Gyroscopic precession rate when torque tau is applied perpendicular to L:
Omega_precession = |tau| / |L| = |m x B| / (I_z * omega_spin)

At critical frequency f_c = 25 Hz:
omega_c = 2*pi*25 = 157.1 rad/s
L_c = I_z * omega_c

For Omega_precession << omega_c: gyroscopic regime (non-Newtonian spin). The magnet precesses
rather than following the torque direction, resulting in the rise into the field (analogous to
spinning-top rise under gravity).

The transition from Newtonian to non-Newtonian occurs when the precession rate falls below some
critical value related to the field rotation rate, approximately:
f_c is where omega_spin ~ omega_field [synchronism condition]
or where gyroscopic stiffness G = I_z * omega_spin^2 / torque_coupling exceeds a threshold.

## 4. Screw-Advance Force (Newtonian Regime)

Source: MDX 13_1916093512992653312_video.mdx

In the Newtonian regime, the rotating magnet acts as a mechanical screw. The effective axial
force generated per unit rotation:
F_z_Newton = tau_effective * k_screw
where k_screw is an effective screw constant (force per unit torque, determined by the helical
advance geometry of the field-magnet coupling).

Empirically:
At omega = 2*pi*10 rad/s: F_z = 200 g * g = 1.96 N
At omega = 2*pi*18 rad/s: F_z = 250 g * g = 2.45 N
At omega = 2*pi*20 rad/s: F_z = 300 g * g = 2.94 N

Slope: delta_F / delta_omega ~ (300 - 200)g / (20 - 10) Hz ~ 10 g/Hz
     = 0.098 N / (2*pi rad/s) = 0.0156 N*s/rad

This constitutes an experimental force-frequency relationship for this specific apparatus.
Note: units are N / (rad/s), which has units of kg*m/rad — consistent with a linear-to-angular
conversion constant of the screw mechanism.

## 5. Magnetic Repulsion Bearing: Force-Distance Relationship

Source: MDX 13_1916093512992653312_video.mdx

For two coaxial magnetic dipoles m_1, m_2 (opposing polarity, repulsive configuration) separated
by distance z along the Z-axis:

Potential energy (dipole-dipole approximation):
U(z) = (mu_0 / 4*pi) * (2*m_1*m_2 / z^3) [valid for z >> R_magnet]

Repulsive force:
F(z) = -dU/dz = 3*(mu_0 / 4*pi) * (2*m_1*m_2 / z^4)

Equilibrium condition (levitation height z_0 where magnetic force = weight W):
F(z_0) = W = M_mag * g
=> z_0 = [6*(mu_0 / 4*pi) * m_1*m_2 / W]^(1/4)

This is the equilibrium height at which the magnet assembly floats on the lower magnet.
Perturbation stability (axial): dF/dz = -12*(mu_0/4*pi)*m_1*m_2 / z^5 < 0 (restoring force in Z
for small perturbations), so axial stability is achieved. Radial instability is present by
Earnshaw's theorem (no stable equilibrium in free space) but is resolved mechanically by the rod guide.

## 6. Lorentz Force Equation and J x B Body Force

Source: YAML 1995974865439129760; YAML 2014995810874966207

Microscopic Lorentz force per charge:
F = q*(E + v x B)

Bulk (macroscopic) current density form:
f = J x B [N/m^3, body force per unit volume]

Total force on volume V:
F_total = integral_V (J x B) dV

For galinstan ring of inner radius R_1, outer radius R_2, height H, carrying azimuthal current
density J_phi (A/m^2) in axial field B_z:
F_z = 0 [azimuthal J, axial B produces radial force J_phi x B_z = -J_phi*B_z * hat_r]
F_r = -J_phi * B_z * r [outward radial force, no net Z lift]

For radial current density J_r in azimuthal field B_phi:
F_z = J_r x B_phi [axial force, enabling lift]

This is the configuration of an MHD axial thruster: radial current + azimuthal field => axial force.
For azimuthal thrust: axial current J_z in radial field B_r:
F_phi = J_z x B_r [azimuthal force, driving rotation]

The galinstan experiment uses either the azimuthal thrust (F_phi) or the linear induction traveling
wave to drive rotation, not directly axial lift. The connection to lift requires further analysis of
the rotating galinstan system's angular momentum interaction with external fields.

## 7. Counter-Rotation Angular Momentum Cancellation

Source: YAML 2027846997080977894

Total angular momentum of two counter-rotating flywheels:
L_net = I_1*omega_1*hat_z + I_2*(-omega_2)*hat_z = (I_1*omega_1 - I_2*omega_2)*hat_z

For cancellation: I_1*omega_1 = I_2*omega_2

If I_1 = I_2 (symmetric design): omega_1 = omega_2 (equal and opposite spin rates).

Net torque required to change L_net:
tau_net = dL_net/dt = I_1*(domega_1/dt) - I_2*(domega_2/dt)

For synchronized counter-rotation maintained by three-phase driving: tau_net is provided by
the electromagnetic drive, not by mechanical reaction to the environment.

Kinetic energy stored in counter-rotating flywheels:
KE = (1/2)*I_1*omega_1^2 + (1/2)*I_2*omega_2^2 = I*omega^2 [for I_1=I_2=I, omega_1=omega_2=omega]

This energy reservoir is available for pulse delivery of electromagnetic force, potentially
relevant to the pulsed-power aspects of UAP propulsion.

## 8. Vellone Counter-Rotating Halves: EMF Generation

Source: MDX 13_2014995745141895168_video.mdx

Two counter-rotating halves of a craft, each carrying permanent magnets or electromagnets,
with the other half carrying conductors (coils). The relative angular velocity:
Delta_omega = omega_1 + omega_2 [if spinning in opposite directions]

By Faraday's law:
EMF = -d*Phi_B/dt = -N * dB_eff/dt ~ N * Delta_omega * Phi_B0

where Phi_B0 is the peak flux per conductor loop. The EMF scales linearly with the sum of
rotation rates, giving more power at higher spin speeds.

Power generated:
P = EMF^2 / R_load = (N * Delta_omega * Phi_B0)^2 / R_load

For a given flux linkage design, power scales as (Delta_omega)^2. The counter-rotation doubles
the effective relative speed compared to single rotation, giving (2*omega)^2 = 4*omega^2
kinetic-to-electromagnetic conversion rate compared to rotating against a stationary stator.

## 9. Arecibo Reply Crop Circle: Elemental Composition Coding

Source: YAML 1950124459647455699 (note_tweet)

The 2001 Chilbolton "Arecibo Reply" crop circle encoded elemental composition using atomic number
representation. Original Sagan 1974 message encoded:
H-1, C-6, N-7, O-8, P-15 as elements of life.

The 2001 reply replaced P-15 with Si-14 (Silicon, atomic number 14) as an alternative element
of life. The mathematical fact: Si-14 is directly adjacent to P-15 in the periodic table. Silicon
can form four covalent bonds (like carbon), enabling a silicon-based organic chemistry analogue.

Atomic number representation in the crop circle used binary encoding (rows of binary digits
representing atomic number N). The substitution Si-14 for P-15 requires changing a single bit
in the binary representation:
P = 15 = 1111 (binary)
Si = 14 = 1110 (binary)
Difference: single bit flip in the least-significant position.

This is mathematically notable: the smallest possible change in the elemental composition
(one bit in binary atomic number representation), yet it points to the scientifically meaningful
alternative (silicon-based life) while maximally preserving the encoding structure of the
original Sagan message.
