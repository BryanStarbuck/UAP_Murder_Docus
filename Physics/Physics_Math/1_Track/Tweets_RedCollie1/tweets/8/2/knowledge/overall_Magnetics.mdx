## 1. Three-Phase AC Rotating Magnetic Field — Core Configuration

A rotating magnetic field (RMF) is produced by three spatially distributed wire coils (wound conductors), energized by three-phase AC power with 120° phase displacement:

* Phase U: coil 1
* Phase V: coil 2
* Phase W: coil 3

The instantaneous field vector B(t) rotates in the XY-plane at the AC frequency f (e.g., 24 Hz, 50 Hz). In the video experiments, three wire coils are arranged in a triangular pattern below a central vertical axis (the pole). The RMF drives a central permanent magnet to spin about the Z-axis (vertical axis).

### XY-Plane Dynamics

The rotating field exerts a continuous azimuthal torque on the permanent magnet. The magnet couples to the RMF and accelerates until it reaches synchronous speed or a slip-torque equilibrium. The direction of rotation (CW or CCW when viewed from above) is set by the phase sequence of the three-phase supply (UVW or UWV).

Observation from experiment: "The magnet spins clockwise and the coil spins anti-clockwise" — when the coil assembly is free to counter-rotate, it does so, demonstrating angular momentum conservation in the coupled magnet-coil system.

### Z-Axis Dynamics (Axial / Vertical)

As the magnet spins, it experiences an axial (Z-axis) force directed upward along the pole. Observed magnitudes:

* 70 mm permanent magnet (NdFeB, with 14 mm central hole), mounted on 8 mm stainless steel rod
* Spin rate: 20-30 Hz
* Upward force: approximately 500 grams (measured by resistance required to push rod downward: "If we push down on the rod, we get about 500 grams before necessary to push the rod downward")
* Net anti-gravity weight on scale: 30-50 grams (whole device)

The discrepancy between 500 g upward force and 30-50 g net anti-gravity is the central unsolved problem. The experimenter proposes the remaining ~450 g is cancelled by an unidentified downward reaction force.

From note_tweet 1970030336232026143:

Mechanism: The AC magnetic field from the coil induces eddy currents in the aluminum disc. By Lenz's law, these induced currents create a magnetic moment opposing the inducing field — the aluminum disc is repelled and rises (Lenz repulsion).

The aluminum disc has an induced magnetic field aligned to repel the coil field. This induced field is not permanent; it only exists while AC power is applied.

This matters because: if the rotating 3-phase field couples to the induced eddy-current magnetic moment in the aluminum disc, the disc gains angular momentum (spin) in addition to its existing linear (axial) momentum from Lenz repulsion. The coupling direction (co-rotation or counter-rotation) determines whether this adds to or subtracts from the levitation force. Results noted as "I will show the lab results soon."

Without AC power, the disc "hardly moves, because it has no induced magnetic field, to couple with a 3-phase field below." This confirms the coupling requires both the AC-induced eddy current field AND the 3-phase rotating field simultaneously.
