## 2. Lenz's Law — Faraday Induction

From note_tweet 1970030336232026143:

Faraday's law (differential form):

∇ × E = -∂B/∂t

For a conducting aluminum disc with conductivity σ in a time-varying applied field B_app(t):

J_eddy = σ E = -σ ∂A/∂t (in the Coulomb gauge, where A is the magnetic vector potential)

The induced eddy current density J_eddy creates a secondary magnetic field:

B_induced = ∇ × A_induced

By Lenz's law, B_induced opposes B_app:

B_induced · B_app < 0

The repulsive force between the induced field and the applied field:

F_repulsion = ∇(m_induced · B_app)

where m_induced is the effective magnetic dipole moment of the eddy current distribution:

m_induced = (1/2) ∫_V r × J_eddy dV

Since m_induced is anti-parallel to B_app, F_repulsion is directed away from the coil (upward, along -∇|B_app|).

## 5. Rotating Magnetic Field — Phase Vector Decomposition

For a symmetric n-phase AC supply with phases k = 0, 1, ..., n-1, each carrying current:

I_k(t) = I_0 cos(ωt - 2πk/n)

and coils arranged at azimuthal angles θ_k = 2πk/n:

The net magnetic field vector in the XY-plane:

B_xy(t) = B_0 Σ_k cos(ωt - 2πk/n) [cos(θ_k) x̂ + sin(θ_k) ŷ]

= B_0 (n/2) [cos(ωt) x̂ + sin(ωt) ŷ] (for n ≥ 3)

This is a uniformly rotating field of constant amplitude B_0 n/2, rotating at angular frequency ω. The amplitude does not pulsate (no torque ripple in the limit of continuous phases); for finite n, residual torque ripple decays rapidly with increasing n.

For n=3: B_xy(t) = (3/2)B_0 [cos(ωt) x̂ + sin(ωt) ŷ] — rotating field, amplitude = (3/2)B_0.
For n=5: B_xy(t) = (5/2)B_0 [cos(ωt) x̂ + sin(ωt) ŷ] — rotating field, amplitude = (5/2)B_0.

## 7. Angular Momentum Conservation — Coil-Magnet System

Observed: coil assembly rotates CCW when magnet rotates CW (free coil condition, video 13_1934191685191507968).

L_magnet + L_coil = L_external (from driving current)

## 9. Torque Ripple — Comparison 3-Phase vs 5-Phase

For n-phase rotating field drive, the torque ripple on the driven rotor (assuming linear coupling) contains harmonics at multiples of n:

T(t) = T_avg [1 + Σ_j a_j cos(j n ωt + φ_j)]

The dominant ripple term is at frequency nω. For 3-phase (n=3): dominant ripple at 3ω. For 5-phase (n=5): dominant ripple at 5ω, with smaller amplitude (a_1 for 5-phase < a_1 for 3-phase). The smoother torque from 5-phase reduces the periodic rise/fall oscillation that limits the 3-phase lab experiments to 50% time-averaged weight reduction — a continuous force (constant T_avg) would yield continuous levitation.
