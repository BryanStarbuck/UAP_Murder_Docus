## 1. Newton's Third Law Challenge — Spinning Magnet Rising Without Back-Reaction

### Claim (note_tweet 1927593867060093435)

"I have done MANY experiments which show that a 'spinning magnet' will rise without causing any downward back-reaction by Newton's 3rd Law!"

### Physical Setup

A permanent magnet (70 mm OD, 14 mm hole, NdFeB) is mounted on a vertical stainless steel pole. Three-phase AC wire coils below drive the magnet to spin about the Z-axis. As spin rate increases, the magnet rises axially.

### Newton's Third Law Expectation

If a force F_up acts on the magnet in the +Z direction, Newton's Third Law demands an equal and opposite reaction force F_down = -F_up on whatever exerts F_up on the magnet — namely, the coil assembly / base / Earth. This should be detectable as increased weight on a scale.

### Observed Result

The scale does NOT show a proportional increase in downward weight when the magnet rises. The experimenter reports: net anti-gravity effect of -20 to -50 grams (reduction of weight), not an increase.

### Proposed Resolution

The reaction force, if it exists, is "perpendicular to the pole on which it is rising" — i.e., horizontal (radial), not vertical. A radial reaction force would not register on a vertical scale. This is geometrically plausible in a rotating system: if the axial thrust is generated by helical or rotating field coupling, the reaction may indeed be in the azimuthal or radial direction rather than the axial direction.

However, the experimenter notes this does not fully explain UFO flight, because the whole device's weight only reduces by 30-50 grams while the upward force is 500 grams.

## 2. Lenz's Law — Quantitative Statement

### Setup (note_tweet 1970030336232026143)

Large wire coil (single-phase, 50 Hz AC), aluminum disc (60-80 mm diameter, 6 mm thick) on 5-6 mm pole, placed in center of coil.

### Physics

The time-varying flux Φ(t) from the energized coil through the aluminum disc (which is a conductor) induces an EMF by Faraday's law:

EMF = -dΦ/dt

This drives eddy currents J_eddy in the aluminum (azimuthal current loops). By Lenz's law, J_eddy creates a magnetic field B_eddy opposing the inducing field B_coil. The net result is a repulsive force:

F_repulsion = -∇(U_magnetic) where U = -(m_induced · B_coil)

Since m_induced is anti-parallel to B_coil (by Lenz), U > 0, and ∇U pushes the disc away from the coil — i.e., upward (axially out of the coil center).

### Coupling with 3-Phase Field

The additional question: when the aluminum disc has been lifted by Lenz repulsion (acquiring an induced magnetic moment m_induced), and a 3-phase rotating field B_3φ(t) is simultaneously applied from below — does B_3φ couple to m_induced and impart azimuthal torque (spin) to the disc?

Expected: yes, because m_induced is a real magnetic dipole moment (even if transient) that responds to any external rotating field. The sign of the coupling (CW or CCW spin) is the experimental unknown.

### Classical Jumping Ring

A conducting ring placed around an AC electromagnet core experiences repulsion when the coil is energized, because the induced current in the ring is 180° out of phase with the coil current (Lenz's law). The force on the ring:

F = -d/dz (m_ring · B_coil(z))

where m_ring is the induced magnetic moment of the ring (anti-parallel to B_coil), and B_coil(z) is the coil field gradient along the Z-axis. Since m_ring · B_coil < 0 and |B_coil| decreases with z, the gradient force is upward.

## 4. Faraday's Paradox / Homopolar Motor-Generator

### Source: tweet 1914936648145555818

"After 200 years. Go ahead, try to find this important result in your physics textbooks? 'The true solution of Faraday's Paradox for a homopolar motor/generator'"

### The Paradox

In a homopolar motor/generator (Faraday disc), a conducting disc rotates in an axial magnetic field. A radial EMF is generated:

EMF = (1/2) ω B r²

where ω is the angular velocity of the disc, B is the axial field, r is the disc radius. This EMF drives radial current if a circuit is completed via sliding contacts at the rim and the axle.

The paradox: if the magnet providing B also rotates with the disc (i.e., the magnet and disc are rigidly attached and co-rotate), is there still an EMF? Experiment says: yes, if the circuit (external leads) is stationary. But the question of what "rotates" the field lines has been debated since Faraday.

## 5. Corbino Disc — Lorentz Force on Radial Current in Axial Field

### Source: note_tweet 1992007523407466702

The Corbino disc is a flat conducting annulus (inner radius r₁, outer radius r₂) with current driven radially by electrodes at r₁ and r₂. An axial magnetic field B_z is applied.

### Force Density

Volume force density on the current-carrying conductor:

f = J × B

For radial current density J = J_r r̂ and axial field B = B_z ẑ:

f = J_r r̂ × B_z ẑ = J_r B_z (r̂ × ẑ) = -J_r B_z φ̂

This azimuthal force density drives the disc to rotate. The sign of rotation depends on the sign of J_r and B_z. Reversing either reverses the spin direction.

### Capacitive Phase Reversal (11 kV AC)

"A big capacitor can change the spin-direction of a Corbino disc with 11 kV AC power."

In an AC Corbino disc circuit: if a large capacitor is placed in series with the radial current path, the capacitive reactance X_C = 1/(ωC) shifts the phase of J_r by -90° relative to B_z. If B_z is also AC (from the same source), the phase relationship between J_r and B_z determines the time-averaged torque:

<τ> ∝ <J_r(t) × B_z(t)> = (1/2)|J_r||B_z| cos(φ_J - φ_B)

Reversing the sign of cos(φ_J - φ_B) by adding a 180° phase shift (achieved by capacitive tuning across the resonant frequency) reverses the time-averaged torque — hence reversing the disc's spin direction.

Application: galinstan (liquid metal alloy) or argon ion plasma as the conducting medium in a Corbino geometry provides the counter-rotating outer element of the spin/counter-spin propulsion system.

## 6. Time-Averaged Weight Reduction — Quantitative Model

### Source: note_tweet 1927593867060093435

Let W_0 be the static weight of the spinning magnet. Let f be the fraction of time the magnet spends in free fall (downward phase of its oscillatory rise/fall cycle).

During free fall: apparent weight = 0 (inertial reference: magnet and scale are not in contact during this phase, or the magnet decouples from the scale).

During rise: apparent weight ≈ W_0 (approximately, ignoring the contribution of the upward magnetic force to scale reading).

Time-averaged weight:

<W> = (1-f) · W_0 + f · 0 = (1-f) · W_0

For f = 0.5:

<W> = 0.5 · W_0

"So if we have say a spinning magnet of weight 100 grams, its TIME-AVERAGED weight will be just 50 grams, if it spends half of its time rising, and half of its time falling."

## 7. Rotating Field — Angular Momentum and Reaction Torque

### Observation

"The magnet spins clockwise and the coil spins anti-clockwise." — when the coil assembly is free to rotate, it counter-rotates.

Conservation of angular momentum in the isolated system (coil + magnet):

L_total = L_magnet + L_coil = constant

When the 3-phase field drives the magnet to angular momentum L_magnet = I_magnet · ω_magnet (clockwise), the reaction on the coil assembly is L_coil = -L_magnet (anti-clockwise).

The power supply provides the driving torque; the coil assembly recoils anti-clockwise if not restrained. The measured anti-clockwise displacement of the coil confirms angular momentum transfer from the field to the magnet and reaction back to the coil.

The counter-rotation of the coil assembly itself appears to add to the net anti-gravity measurement. This is a non-linear coupling that may involve the spin/counter-spin field configuration even within the single magnet + free coil system.
