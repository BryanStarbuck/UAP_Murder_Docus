## 1. Newton's 3rd Law Anomaly: DC Disk Experiment

### Experimental Claim
Source video transcription (13_2007971058230644737_video.mdx):
"Okay, I'm going to add juice to this circuit and the wires will rotate one direction and the
disc will rotate the other direction. But the wires are not enough mass to start the disc

### Newton's 3rd Law as Applied Here
Newton's 3rd Law: For every action force τ_A, there is an equal and opposite reaction τ_B.
Applied to the DC disk: the torque imparted to the disk by the electromagnetic interaction
with the current-carrying wires must equal in magnitude the back-torque exerted by the disk
on the wires (transmitted through the field).

Classical prediction: if the wires have insufficient mass/moment of inertia I_wire to produce
angular impulse J = ∫τ dt sufficient to spin the disk, the disk should not spin (or should
spin negligibly).

Observed: the disk spins at unexpected angular velocity — Drew argues the torque cannot
be accounted for by the wire mass alone as the sole reaction source.

### Physical Interpretation #1: EM Field Angular Momentum (Classical Extension)
Within classical electrodynamics, the electromagnetic field carries angular momentum:
  L_field = ε₀ ∫ (r × (E × B)) dV

For a current-carrying wire (generating B_wire) in an existing magnetic field or in the
field of the disk's own magnetization (generating E from charge redistribution):
  dL_field/dt ≠ 0 during current transients

The total angular momentum L_total = L_wire + L_disk + L_field is conserved. The apparent
imbalance between wire and disk torques is resolved if L_field is changing — i.e., the field
itself absorbs or releases angular momentum during the current pulse. This does NOT violate
Newton's 3rd Law; it shows that the "equal and opposite" constraint applies to the
wire + field + disk system collectively, not to wire and disk independently.

### Physical Interpretation #2: Non-Abelian Gauge Coupling (Non-Classical)
If the current-disk system involves sufficiently strong fields that non-linear electromagnetic
coupling (not captured by linear Maxwell equations) becomes significant, additional momentum
terms not present in standard electrodynamics could appear. Drew's characterization as
"not equal in opposite torques" is consistent with his suspicion of a genuinely new coupling
beyond classical Maxwell + Newton.

### Classical Baseline to Be Exceeded

expected upward force EXCEEDS the classical ∇(m·B) prediction — hence "non-Newtonian" (or
more precisely: non-classical EM).
