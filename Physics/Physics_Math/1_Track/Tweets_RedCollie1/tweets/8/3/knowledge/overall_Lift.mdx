## Primary Lift Mechanism Proposed: Spin/Counter-Spin in 3-Phase Magnetic Fields

### Core Claim
A spinning permanent magnet driven by 3-phase AC power exhibits anomalous upward (Z-axis)
force. This force is amplified or transitions to a non-Newtonian regime when the spinning
magnet passes through the center of a counter-spinning large magnetic flywheel.

Source tweet (2044768314229219586, note_tweet):
"Tomorrow I will show a new and simple video, that explains how and why a spinning magnet
'lifts up' under 3-phase power. But that is not enough to produce UFO flight! The spinning
magnet has to pass through the center of a large magnetic flywheel, which is spinning in the
opposite direction! ... I suspect that the upward force of that spinning magnet becomes
'non-Newtonian', when it pushes-up on the central magnetic field of a counter-spinning
flywheel, rather than just say a 'bead on a pole'."

### Stage 1: DC Excitation — Gyroscopic, No Lift
- DC current through an axial wire electrode produces gyroscopic spin (XY-plane rotation).
- This produces gyroscopic stabilization: strong rigidity against tilting.
- DC spin does NOT produce Z-axis displacement (lift).

Source tweet (1963381627910066488, referenced_tweet):
"DC spin will not lift it up, just produce a strong gyroscopic motion."

Physical reason: DC produces a static (non-rotating) magnetic field. A static field exerts
no net translational force on a symmetrical conductor or magnet in a uniform background field.
Only field gradients (∇B) can produce translational forces on magnetic dipoles:
  F = ∇(m · B)
If the background field is uniform, ∇B = 0 and F = 0 for a pure dipole.

### Stage 2: 3-Phase AC — Anomalous Lift
- 3-phase AC creates a rotating magnetic field.
- The rotating field interacts with the spinning permanent magnet to produce a net upward force.
- Described as: spinning magnet "lifts up" — empirically observed in workshop models.
- This is the primary claimed experimental result; the physical mechanism is not specified
  beyond "spin under 3-phase power."

### Stage 3: Counter-Rotating Flywheel — Non-Newtonian Lift Regime
- A large magnetic flywheel spins in the opposite direction to the inner spinning magnet.
- The spinning magnet passes through the CENTER of the counter-spinning flywheel.
- The upward push of the spinning magnet against the central field of the counter-spinning
  flywheel is hypothesized to produce "non-Newtonian" force — exceeding or operating
  differently from classical magnetic force predictions.
- Status: HYPOTHESIS. Stated as "I will try to test that hypothesis with more experiments soon."

## Gravity Manipulation vs. Reaction Force: Drew's Implicit Distinction

Drew consistently frames the lift mechanism as "non-Newtonian" — implying it is NOT:
- Aerodynamic lift (requires airflow)
- Rocket propulsion (requires propellant expulsion)
- Ion drive (requires propellant and high voltage)
- Buoyancy (requires fluid density differential)

The "non-Newtonian" characterization points to:
- A mechanism where the craft's internal field configuration generates net upward force
  without ejecting mass or pushing against the surrounding medium.
- This is functionally equivalent to gravity manipulation (creating a local field that
  interacts with the ambient gravitational field) or inertial mass reduction.
- Drew does not commit to a specific underlying physics — he describes the empirical
  behavior (anomalous torque in DC disk, anomalous lift under 3-phase) and leaves the
  theoretical explanation open as "non-Newtonian."
