## Special Relativity in 4-Dimensional Euclidean Space: sin(θ) = v/c

### Claim
Drew proposes that special relativity, when drawn in 4-dimensional Euclidean space using the
substitution sin(θ) = v/c, immediately reveals that special relativity and the de Broglie
equation predict exactly the same physical content.

Source tweet (2005526554861023598):
"Draw special relativity in 4-dimensional Euclidean space as sin(theta) = v/c. Then you will
see immediately, that special relativity and the deBroglie equation predict exactly the same
thing. High school math. Use your minds."

### Mathematical Elaboration

In standard Minkowski space, the spacetime interval is:
  ds² = -c²dt² + dx² + dy² + dz²

The Lorentz factor is:
  γ = 1/√(1 - v²/c²)

If instead one parameterizes velocity as:
  sin(θ) = v/c

then:
  cos(θ) = √(1 - sin²(θ)) = √(1 - v²/c²) = 1/γ

The relativistic energy-momentum relation is:
  E² = (pc)² + (m₀c²)²

Dividing through by E²:
  1 = (pc/E)² + (m₀c²/E)²

Using sin(θ) = pc/E and cos(θ) = m₀c²/E:
  sin²(θ) + cos²(θ) = 1  (trivially satisfied)

The de Broglie relation is:
  λ = h/p   →   p = h/λ = ħk

The phase velocity of a de Broglie wave:
  v_phase = E/p = (γm₀c²)/(γm₀v) = c²/v

Using sin(θ) = v/c:
  v_phase = c/sin(θ)

Drew's claim is that this geometric parameterization (placing all of SR in a 4D Euclidean
rotation by angle θ) makes the equivalence between SR kinematics and de Broglie wave
mechanics visually/algebraically obvious. In a 4D Euclidean space, the Lorentz boost becomes
a rotation by imaginary angle, which in the Euclidean version corresponds to rotation by real
angle θ where sin(θ) = v/c.

### Relation to Wick Rotation / Euclidean Signature

This is related to the Wick rotation t → iτ, which converts Minkowski metric
(-,+,+,+) to Euclidean metric (+,+,+,+). In Euclidean signature, hyperbolic boost rapidity φ
(where tanh(φ) = v/c) becomes an ordinary angle θ via:
  φ = iθ  and  sin(θ) = v/c  (exactly Drew's substitution)

The key geometric insight: in Euclidean 4-space, all four dimensions are equivalent and the
"boost" is an ordinary rotation. The sin(θ) = v/c parameterization directly gives the
geometric angle of rotation in the (x, ct) plane.

### Connection to de Broglie

The de Broglie wavelength λ = h/p, combined with the relativistic energy-momentum hyperbola
E² - (pc)² = (m₀c²)², maps directly onto the unit circle sin²(θ) + cos²(θ) = 1 when
sin(θ) = v/c. Drew's claim is that once drawn geometrically, the identical structure of
SR and de Broglie wave mechanics becomes apparent — suggesting they are not two separate
theories but two descriptions of the same 4D Euclidean rotation geometry.

## DC Electromagnetic Disk: Torque Non-Conservation

### Experiment (Empirical)
See overall_Magnetics.mdx for full description.

### Physical Chain of Reasoning

Step 1: Apply DC current to a circuit consisting of wire elements mounted above a conducting
disk in a magnetic field background.

Step 2 (predicted by classical EM): By Newton's 3rd Law applied to the Lorentz torques,
the torque on the wires (τ_wire = r × (IL × B)) must be equal and opposite to the reactive
torque on the disk (τ_disk). If the wire mass is m_w and disk mass is m_d, the angular
accelerations must satisfy:
  τ_wire = I_wire × α_wire
  τ_disk = I_disk × α_disk = -τ_wire

Step 3 (observed): The disk rotates opposite to the wires at an angular rate inconsistent
with the wire mass being the sole source of the reactive torque. The disk torque exceeds
what the wire-mass reaction can account for.

Step 4 (Drew's interpretation): Either electromagnetic field angular momentum is mediating
a torque transfer that bypasses the Newtonian equal-and-opposite constraint, or the coupling
between the rotating current distribution and the disk involves a non-local force law.

### Reference to Known Physics: EM Field Angular Momentum

The Poynting vector S = (1/μ₀)(E × B) carries energy flux. The electromagnetic field also
carries angular momentum density:
  l_em = ε₀(r × (E × B)) = (1/c²)(r × S)

In systems with crossed electric and magnetic fields, this angular momentum is non-zero even
in static configurations. Drew's experiment may be observing the transfer of stored
electromagnetic angular momentum to the disk — a known but often overlooked classical EM
effect. This would not actually violate Newton's 3rd Law but would represent angular momentum
exchange between the field and the disk that is not mediated by the wire mass alone.
