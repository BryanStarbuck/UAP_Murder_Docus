## Beyond Newton's 3rd Law: Mass Recoil Suppression via Homopolar Dynamo

Drew identifies two distinct reaction forces and claims both can be suppressed:

1. "Mass recoil" — the standard Newton's 3rd Law translational reaction on the stator frame (equal and opposite to the thrust force on the magnet)

2. "Magnetic recoil" — a magnetic-specific reaction force on the stator from the spinning magnet's rotating field interacting back on the stator coils

Claim: a self-exciting homopolar dynamo in liquid metal (galinstan), spinning opposite to the applied 3-phase power, cancels both forms of recoil. (Source: tweet note_tweet 1983835595828072898)

The "mass recoil" suppression claim is the most radical. In classical mechanics, any internal force that lifts the magnet within the device must push the device body downward by an equal amount (Newton's 3rd Law). The only way to avoid this is if the force is not internal — i.e., if an external field (the rotating magnetic field from the coils, which is anchored to the stator) exerts a force on the magnet that does not have a reaction on the stator. This is precisely what "Newton's 3rd Law does not apply to a spinning Lorentz force" implies: the Lorentz force on the spinning magnet from the stator's field does not produce an equal-and-opposite force on the stator from the magnet's field.

In classical electrodynamics, this would violate the equality:
  F_{magnet on stator} = -F_{stator on magnet}

which holds for static and quasi-static cases. For highly dynamic, rotating cases, the field momentum term p_field = ε_0 ∫(E × B) dV could potentially account for apparent asymmetry in mechanical forces — but only if the field carries a large momentum (requiring strong E × B, i.e., large E and B simultaneously). In a device with permanent magnets and low-voltage AC coils, E × B field momentum is likely negligible. Drew's claim would require a different mechanism.
