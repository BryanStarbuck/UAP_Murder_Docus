## Magnetic Influence on the High-Voltage Ionized Air Experiment

The experimental observable:
* Air ionized at 8 kV to 8.8 kV AC
* A rotating magnetic field applied (either via 3-phase coils at 40/50 Hz or via a spinning permanent magnet)
* The effect on the ionized air motion is observed and recorded on video

The control conditions tested in order:
1. No 3-phase power (stationary configuration)
2. 3-phase power at 40 Hz
3. No 3-phase power (control repeat)
4. 3-phase power at 50 Hz
5. Stationary magnet at 8 kV AC (no rotation)
6. Spinning wood (no magnet) at 8.8 kV AC (mechanical rotation only, no magnetic field)
7. Spinning magnet at 8 kV AC
8. Spinning magnet at 8.8 kV AC (two repetitions)

The purpose: to separate the effects of (a) mechanical rotation alone, (b) static magnetic field alone, and (c) rotating magnetic field on the behavior of the ionized air plasma. The specific motion of the ionized air under the rotating magnetic field is the phenomenon being studied.

Physics context: ionized air (plasma) contains free charge carriers. A rotating magnetic field B_rot(t) = B_0(cos(ωt), sin(ωt), 0) (rotating in the XY-plane at angular frequency ω) exerts a Lorentz force F = q(v × B) on moving charges. For electrons and ions already in motion from the high-voltage ionization, this adds an azimuthal force component that can induce rotational flow in the plasma and potentially Z-axis (vertical) flow depending on the geometry.

## Magnetically Driven 3-Axis Gyroscope (Proposed Experiment)

Proposed experiment explicitly connecting gyroscopic precession physics to the magnetic excitation mechanism:

* Begin with a 3-axis gimbal gyroscope (described as the same type Feynman used in teaching, per video transcript 13_1942536906681769984)
* A 3-axis gimbal allows the gyroscope to spin on all three axes simultaneously
* Property demonstrated: "no matter how much we turn the rest of the gimbal, or what it's sitting on, the gyroscope will keep pointing in the same direction" — conservation of angular momentum / gyroscopic rigidity
* Proposed modification: make the spinning disc of the gyroscope magnetic
* Apply 3-phase magnetic field to drive it at high rotation rate

The rationale (from note_tweet 1942537540801814931):
  "To test for an anti-gravity effect, I will have to make the spinning disc of that gyroscope 'magnetic', then apply a 3-phase magnetic field to make it spin and rise."

The 3-axis requirement is physically essential: a 2-axis (toy) gyroscope cannot be spun by the 3-phase rotating field because the field sweeps through all orientations and requires the spinning disc to be free to orient itself along the field's rotation axis. A 2-axis gimbal constrains this freedom.

Magnetic coupling mechanism: the magnetized disc, subjected to a rotating field B(t) whose rotation axis is vertical (Z), experiences a torque τ = m × B (where m is the magnetic dipole moment of the disc). If the disc is already spinning rapidly (angular momentum L large), the gyroscopic response to this torque is precession rather than direct alignment — the precession dynamics interact with the rotation to potentially produce a net Z-axis force.

## Magnetic Recoil as a Distinct Reaction Force Category

Drew distinguishes two separate Newtonian reaction forces in the levitation mechanism:

1. Mass recoil — equal-and-opposite translational reaction to the Z-axis thrust force (standard Newton's 3rd Law for translational motion)

2. Magnetic recoil — a separate reaction force arising from the magnetic interaction between the spinning permanent magnet and the stator coils

The explicit claim: "Newton's 3rd Law does not apply to a spinning Lorentz force" (tweet 2007971400427110683). This claim, if valid, would eliminate or reduce the magnetic recoil component specifically — not the mass recoil. The mass recoil is proposed to be canceled separately by the counter-rotating homopolar liquid-metal dynamo.

The distinction matters: in a conventional electric motor, there is both electromagnetic torque on the rotor and an equal-and-opposite torque on the stator (the stator wants to rotate in the opposite direction; it is held fixed by its mounting). In Drew's device, the analogous forces are the Z-axis thrust on the spinning magnet and the equal-and-opposite downward Z-axis force on the stator coil frame. The claim is that when the Lorentz force is acting in a spinning geometry, the equal-and-opposite reaction on the stator is partially or fully suppressed.

  "I can't use a 'standard' iron ball bearing, because it affects the magnetism."
  (Source: tweet 2027640787765498028)

Stainless steel (austenitic grades, e.g., 304 or 316) is weakly magnetic to non-magnetic depending on grade — Drew's choice of stainless steel for the tube vs. standard steel is consistent with the need to minimize magnetic perturbation of the flywheel field.

## Homopolar Dynamo in Liquid Metal: Magnetic Field Generation for Reaction Cancellation

The proposed internal counter-rotating subsystem:

* Material: liquid metal, e.g., galinstan (Ga-In-Sn eutectic alloy, melting point approximately -19°C, liquid at room temperature)
* Geometry: a homopolar (Faraday) dynamo configuration
* Rotation direction: opposite to the applied 3-phase power rotation direction
* Function: "self-exciting" — generates its own sustained current via the homopolar EMF of a conducting disc or cylinder rotating in an axial magnetic field

A homopolar dynamo generates EMF by the rotation of a conducting disc in its own axial magnetic field (Faraday disk principle). The EMF is:

  ε = (1/2) ω B r²

where ω is the angular velocity of the disc, B is the axial magnetic field (self-generated), and r is the radius of the disc.

For a self-exciting dynamo, this EMF must be sufficient to drive the current that maintains B, requiring ω > critical angular velocity ω_c. Drew claims the liquid-metal homopolar dynamo runs in self-exciting mode, sustaining its own field and generating the magnetic recoil cancellation force without external power input beyond the initial spin-up.

The counter-rotation direction means the dynamo's magnetic field contribution is anti-aligned with the stator's applied field in the azimuthal sense, producing a force on the stator in the direction opposite to the stator's reaction to the levitating magnet — thus tending to cancel the magnetic recoil on the stator frame.
