## Lorentz Force on a Spinning Magnetic Body

Drew claims: "Newton's 3rd Law does not apply to a spinning Lorentz force."
(Source: tweet 2007971400427110683)

The Lorentz force on a charge q moving with velocity v in electromagnetic fields E and B:

  F = q(E + v × B)

For a magnetized body (permanent magnet) spinning with angular velocity Ω in an applied field B_ext:

The velocity of a charge at position r within the body (in lab frame):
  v = Ω × r

The Lorentz force on this charge from B_ext:
  F_Lorentz = q(v × B_ext) = q((Ω × r) × B_ext)

Expanding the triple cross product:
  (Ω × r) × B_ext = Ω(r · B_ext) - B_ext(r · Ω)

When integrated over the entire spinning magnetized body (all charges), the net force on the body is:

  F_net = ∫ ρ_charge [(Ω × r) × B_ext] dV

where ρ_charge is the charge density. For a neutral body with equal positive and negative charges, many terms cancel. The net Lorentz force on a spinning neutral magnetic body in an external field is in general non-zero because the magnetization M = (1/V)∫(r × J) dV contributes a current distribution that interacts with B_ext.

For a magnetic dipole m = M V in an external field B:
  F = ∇(m · B)    [force on a magnetic dipole in a gradient field]
  τ = m × B       [torque on a magnetic dipole]

These are the standard equations. Drew's claim requires an additional Z-axis force term that is not derivable from these expressions in classical electrodynamics — it would require either:
  (a) A field momentum contribution: p_field = ε_0 ∫(E × B) dV ≠ 0 under rotation
  (b) A new force term arising from the spinning geometry

## Three-Phase Rotating Field Mathematics

Six coils arranged as UVW, UVW (three phases, two coils per phase, in a ring).

For phase U at angle φ_U = 0:
  B_U(t) = B_0 cos(ωt) [cos(0) x̂ + sin(0) ŷ] = B_0 cos(ωt) x̂

For phase V at angle φ_V = 2π/3:
  B_V(t) = B_0 cos(ωt - 2π/3) [cos(2π/3) x̂ + sin(2π/3) ŷ]

For phase W at angle φ_W = 4π/3:
  B_W(t) = B_0 cos(ωt - 4π/3) [cos(4π/3) x̂ + sin(4π/3) ŷ]

Summing all three:
  B_total(t) = (3/2) B_0 [cos(ωt) x̂ + sin(ωt) ŷ]

This is a field of constant magnitude (3/2)B_0 rotating at angular frequency ω in the XY-plane (horizontal plane). At ω = 2π × 40 rad/s (40 Hz) or ω = 2π × 50 rad/s (50 Hz):
  |B_total| = (3/2) B_0 = constant
  Phase: φ(t) = ωt (linear in time)

Note: with six coils in two UVW pairs at 60° spacing (not 120°), the resultant field magnitude is still constant but the calculation includes both pairs. The field rotates at the electrical frequency.

## Torque on the Spinning Permanent Magnet

Torque on the magnet's dipole moment m in the rotating field B_total:
  τ = m × B_total

If m makes angle θ with B_total:
  |τ| = m B_total sin(θ)

For sub-synchronous operation (induction motor analogy), the slip angle δ = θ is maintained by the back-EMF of the spinning magnet. The continuous torque drives:
  I α = τ - τ_friction

where I = moment of inertia of magnet and α = angular acceleration. At steady state (α = 0):
  τ = τ_friction = constant = m B_total sin(δ_steady)

This determines the slip angle at steady-state spin.

## Magnetic Force on the Spinning Magnet: Z-Axis Component

For a magnetic dipole in a non-uniform field (the coil ring is at finite distance below the magnet, so dB/dz ≠ 0):

  F_z = (m · ∇) B_z + m_x (∂B_z/∂x) + m_y (∂B_z/∂y) + m_z (∂B_z/∂z)

For a magnet with dipole moment primarily in the Z-direction (m = m_z ẑ) in the rotating XY-plane field:
  F_z = m_z (∂B_z/∂z)

If the rotating field B_total is entirely in the XY-plane at the coil location, then B_z = 0 at that plane, but dB_z/dz may be non-zero above or below the coils. For a ring of coils carrying current I_coil at height z = 0, the axial field component at height z above the coil plane is:

  B_z(0, z) = (μ_0 I_coil R²) / [2(R² + z²)^(3/2)]

where R = coil ring radius. The gradient:
  ∂B_z/∂z = -3μ_0 I_coil R² z / [2(R² + z²)^(5/2)]

This is the classical gradient term. For z > 0 (magnet above the coil), ∂B_z/∂z < 0, so if m_z > 0:
  F_z = m_z (∂B_z/∂z) < 0    [downward force for magnet above coils, dipole pointing up]

This is the conventional result: a magnetic dipole in a gradient field is attracted toward the stronger field — which for the coil arrangement means downward toward the coils, not upward away from them. For the magnet to rise, a mechanism beyond this simple gradient force is required.

Drew's claim is that spinning the magnet changes this result — i.e., the Lorentz force on the spinning charge distribution generates an additional Z-axis force not captured by F_z = m·∇B.

## Anti-Gravity Force Measurement Equations

From tweet 1934367052594397363 (referenced parent tweet):
  "Small and reproducible anti-gravity effects of 30 to 50 grams, yet much of the upward spinning force of 500 grams (for this small magnet at 20-30 Hertz)."

Force unit conversion (gram-force to Newtons):
  30 g-force = 0.030 kg × 9.81 m/s² = 0.294 N
  50 g-force = 0.050 kg × 9.81 m/s² = 0.490 N
  500 g-force = 0.500 kg × 9.81 m/s² = 4.91 N

Efficiency (net anti-gravity / total upward spinning force):
  η = F_net_antigravity / F_total_upward = (30 to 50 g) / 500 g = 6% to 10%

This leaves 90-94% of the upward spinning force unaccounted for as net lift — dissipated as heat (eddy currents, hysteresis losses), friction, or canceled by stator reaction forces. The path to scaling up the device requires either (a) increasing efficiency to where F_net > mass of device, or (b) adding the homopolar dynamo reaction cancellation to recover the cancelled 90-94%.

## Homopolar Dynamo EMF Equation

Faraday/homopolar dynamo EMF:
  ε = (1/2) ω B r²

where:
  ε = electromotive force [V]
  ω = angular velocity of the conducting disc [rad/s]
  B = axial magnetic field threading the disc [T]
  r = radius of the disc [m]

For a self-exciting dynamo, the current generated by ε drives the coil that produces B:
  I = ε / R_circuit = ω B r² / (2 R_circuit)

The self-sustaining condition requires:
  B = μ_0 N I / L_coil    [Ampere's law for the coil]
  I = ω B r² / (2 R_circuit)    [Ohm's law + Faraday EMF]

Combining:
  B = μ_0 N ω B r² / (2 R_circuit L_coil)

Self-sustaining condition (critical angular velocity):
  1 = μ_0 N ω r² / (2 R_circuit L_coil)
  ω_c = 2 R_circuit L_coil / (μ_0 N r²)

For ω > ω_c, the dynamo is self-sustaining. For ω < ω_c, the field decays to zero and the dynamo dies. The liquid-metal (galinstan) dynamo must spin above ω_c for Drew's reaction-cancellation mechanism to function.

Galinstan properties relevant to this calculation:
  Electrical resistivity: ρ_galinstan ≈ 2.9 × 10^-7 Ω·m (at room temperature)
  This sets R_circuit ∝ ρ_galinstan × geometry.

For galinstan (density ≈ 6440 kg/m³), the viscous drag on the spinning liquid-metal disc sets a lower bound on the drive power needed to maintain ω > ω_c.

## Dual-Counter-Rotating Flywheel Angular Momentum

Inner flywheel (clockwise, angular velocity +ω_inner):
  L_inner = I_inner ω_inner ẑ

Outer flywheel (anticlockwise, angular velocity -ω_outer):
  L_outer = -I_outer ω_outer ẑ

Total angular momentum:
  L_total = (I_inner ω_inner - I_outer ω_outer) ẑ

For L_total = 0 (no net angular momentum, device does not precess):
  I_inner ω_inner = I_outer ω_outer

This is the design condition for a non-precessing levitation device. Drew's outer flywheel has larger magnets (30 mm vs. the inner's smaller magnets if 16 are used) and larger radius, so I_outer > I_inner. For balance, ω_outer < ω_inner — the outer flywheel must spin slower than the inner.

The 8 inner magnets and 8 outer magnets, driven by 6 three-phase coils arranged as UVW UVW, produce a slip frequency:
  f_slip = f_rotating_field - f_magnet_rotation
  τ ∝ sin(2π f_slip t)

The geometry of 8 magnets in a ring (8-fold symmetry) interacting with 6-coil 3-phase field (6-fold symmetry) produces a beating pattern at the least-common-multiple frequency, which could produce pulsed torque rather than constant torque — a detail that would affect smooth vs. pulsed levitation.

## Magnet Count: Allowed Combinations

Drew states: "eight magnets in each flywheel" for six coils; "12 or 24 magnets for nine coils."

The pattern: number of magnets must be a multiple of the number of coil pairs (i.e., a multiple of the phase number × 2 for a 3-phase system). For 6 coils (3-phase, 2 coils/phase): multiples of 2 × (something that divides evenly into 6). For 9 coils (3-phase, 3 coils/phase): multiples of 3 × something.

Specific stated values:
  6 coils: 8 magnets per flywheel (or 16 = 2×8)
  9 coils: 12 or 24 magnets per flywheel (24 = 2×12)

The underlying requirement: the number of magnets must produce alternating N-S pole faces that, when rotating, present a pole-face spatial frequency matching the spatial harmonic of the coil ring. For a 6-coil ring with 3-phase excitation, the fundamental spatial harmonic is the 3rd (3 N poles and 3 S poles alternating). 8 magnets produce 4 N-S pairs (4th harmonic), which does not exactly match the 3rd harmonic — this would produce sub-harmonic beating. Drew may be working with a configuration that is not at the spatial-harmonic resonance condition, which would explain why efficiency is low (6-10%).

## 3I/ATLAS Jet Power Conservation

From tweet note_tweet 2005590873623416947:
  "The total power of two jet-exhausts emerging from 3I/ATLAS remains conserved."
  "When the anti-tail jet (left) is weak, the other jet (right) is strong. When the anti-tail jet (left) is strong, the other jet (right) is weak."

Mathematical statement:
  P_jet1(t) + P_jet2(t) = P_total = constant

where P_jet1, P_jet2 are the powers of the two opposed jets. This anticorrelation (when one goes up, the other goes down, sum constant) is a strict conservation law behavior. For a natural sublimating comet, one expects only anti-solar activity P_jet1(t) ∝ illumination(t), with P_jet2 ≈ 0 at all times.

The observed anticorrelation would imply an active mechanism distributing a fixed internal power budget between two opposed jets — consistent with a propulsion/braking system as Drew claims, not consistent with solar-driven cometary outgassing.

Momentum delivered to object by jets over time dt:
  dp/dt = P_jet1/v_exhaust × n̂_1 + P_jet2/v_exhaust × n̂_2

where n̂_1 and n̂_2 are the unit vectors of the two jets (opposed: n̂_2 = -n̂_1). If P_jet1 ≠ P_jet2:
  dp/dt = (P_jet1 - P_jet2) / v_exhaust × n̂_1 ≠ 0

The object accelerates when P_jet1 ≠ P_jet2, and coasts at constant velocity when P_jet1 = P_jet2. This is the "accelerator and brake" analogy stated in the tweet.
