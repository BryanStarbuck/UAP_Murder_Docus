## 1. Newton's 3rd Law Violation Claims

Source: 1983881901372010784.yaml, 2007971953236423039.yaml

CLAIM 1 — Counter-rotating homopolar dynamo reduces F_down:
"If Newton's 3rd Law was strictly observed, then it would never leave the ground, so long as F-up = F-down. But that homopolar dynamo seems to reduce F-down so that it becomes less than F-up."

Statement: F_down < F_up in the described system (3-phase disc-magnet + counter-rotating liquid-metal homopolar dynamo). If verified, this would mean the system generates net external linear momentum without interaction with an external body -- violating both Newton's 3rd Law and conservation of linear momentum.

Standard physics position: Conservation of linear momentum for a closed system is a fundamental consequence of translation invariance (Noether's theorem: continuous translational symmetry → conserved linear momentum). Any apparent violation must arise from: (a) interaction with the electromagnetic field carrying momentum (Poynting vector stress), (b) measurement error, or (c) failure to account for all parts of the system.

The source asserts this is a genuine effect based on experimental observation ("seems to reduce F-down"). No measurement of the reaction force on the coil assembly (which would be the reaction partner per Newton's 3rd Law) is reported in the source texts.

CLAIM 2 — Corbino disc: Newton's 3rd Law "does not apply":
"Newton's 3rd Law does not apply to a Corbino disc, because an inward electric force is redirected by N/S magnetism to another force-direction 90 degrees away."

The force-redirection argument is physically correct in its description of the Corbino disc geometry (Lorentz force on radial current in axial B field does produce azimuthal force). However, the conclusion that Newton's 3rd Law "does not apply" is the non-classical claim. In standard EM theory:
* The total system including EM field momentum is fully compliant with momentum conservation
* The electromagnetic angular momentum (L_EM = epsilon_0 * integral of r x (E x B) dV) accounts for the angular momentum transferred from the power supply (electric field) to the magnet (magnetic field) via the current carriers
* What is accurately described: the mechanical force reactions are distributed between different subsystems (power supply vs. magnet vs. current carriers), making it non-intuitive where the reaction force goes
* What is not accurate: that Newton's 3rd Law is violated -- it is satisfied when all systems (including EM fields) are included

The non-classical content: the Corbino disc, if embedded in a closed craft, can be used to redirect force internally. An inward electrical force (from a battery) is converted to azimuthal force on the disc, with the reaction going to the magnetic field source (also internal to the craft). The result is internal angular momentum in the disc, and in principle a net linear momentum if the geometry is asymmetric. Whether this generates net external linear momentum (true reactionless drive) requires accounting for all EM field momentum, which the source does not do.

## 2. Size-Dependent Chirality Reversal in 3-Phase Induction

Source: 1970471190230937728.yaml (note_tweet), video 13_1970464668910231552_video.mdx

EXPERIMENTAL CLAIM:
"We find that a large aluminum disc (100 mm diameter) spins clockwise, in the SAME sense as a rotating magnetic field below. Whereas a small aluminum disc (70 mm diameter) spins anti-clockwise, in an OPPOSITE sense to the rotating field below. Why has no one noticed this before? Mainstream physicists today just memorize old equations from textbooks, and could not discover 'fleas on a dog'."

The author labels this "NEW PHYSICS" and invites theorists to explain it.

What is standard:
* An aluminum disc above a rotating 3-phase field will experience a torque due to eddy currents (induction motor principle)
* The induced current in the disc lags the driving field by a phase angle determined by omega*L/R
* Standard induction motor theory predicts the disc spins in the SAME direction as the field (co-rotation), with slip relative to synchronous speed
* The co-rotation of the 100 mm disc is consistent with standard induction motor behavior

What is potentially non-standard (the claim):
* The 70 mm disc spinning COUNTER to the driving field under identical conditions
* In standard induction motor theory, there is no mechanism for the induced eddy current disc to spin opposite to the driving field without an additional external influence
* A counter-torque would require the induced magnetic moment to be MORE than 90 degrees out of phase with the driving field, which would require the disc to act as an "over-excited synchronous motor" -- but aluminum has no remanent magnetism to produce this

Possible standard explanations not ruled out by the source:
* Experimental artifact: the 70 mm disc may be subject to a different air flow pattern, edge effect, or asymmetric positioning relative to the coils
* Skin depth effect near transition (R ≈ 2*delta): the current distribution becomes volumetric and may produce a torque reversal due to complex eddy current geometry in the transition regime
* Mutual inductance between the driving coils and the disc changes significantly as disc diameter changes relative to the coil geometry -- if the disc is smaller than the coil loop, the geometry of flux linkage inverts, which COULD invert the torque

The skin depth analysis (R/delta at 20 Hz):
delta_aluminum_20Hz ≈ 18.3 mm
R_100mm = 50 mm → R/delta = 2.73 (surface-dominated eddy currents)
R_70mm = 35 mm → R/delta = 1.91 (transitional; volumetric eddy currents)

At R/delta < 2, the radial distribution of eddy currents changes qualitatively. Standard theory for this regime predicts reduced torque magnitude, not torque reversal. The source's claim of REVERSAL (not just reduction) at 70 mm but not at 100 mm is potentially non-trivial and warrants experimental verification by independent parties.

The source explicitly frames this as evidence for a "vortex theory of magnetism" beyond standard Maxwell-Faraday theory.

## 3. Vortex Magnetism: Rotating Magnet Traps Another Regardless of Polarity

Source: 1957596048698011981.yaml

"It does not matter whether the North or South pole of that small cube magnet faces the big spinning magnet. The big spinning magnet creates a 'vortex' in spacetime, within which the small magnet spins and is held stably."

Standard analysis:
A spinning permanent magnet at angular velocity omega creates a time-varying magnetic field at nearby points. At a fixed observation point, the field oscillates at frequency omega. The field is NOT spherically symmetric but has a specific spatially-rotating structure.

In standard magnetostatics/magnetodynamics:
* A small magnet near a large spinning magnet experiences an oscillating field at frequency omega
* The torque on the small magnet: tau = m_small x B_spinning(t) = m_small x [B_rotating(omega*t)]
* In a rotating field (similar to AC magnetic trap): the small magnet tends to align with the rotating field, which would spin it at frequency omega (synchronous capture), OR it escapes if the interaction is too weak
* The polarity should matter: if North pole toward the spinning magnet, the alignment is one way; if South pole, the other way. The EQUILIBRIUM ORIENTATION relative to the driving field would differ.

The claim that polarity is IRRELEVANT is non-trivial. If confirmed, it implies:
* The trapping mechanism is not dipole-field alignment (which is polarity-sensitive)
* The trap is a potential energy minimum that is independent of magnetic polarity
* This would be analogous to a magnetic trap for NEUTRAL atoms (Zeeman slower, magnetic bottle traps for plasma -- but those trap via |B| gradient, not field direction)

The "vortex in spacetime" language suggests the rotating field creates a rotating frame of reference in which the trapped magnet is confined by the equivalent of a centrifugal barrier plus magnetic bottle. In the rotating frame (rotating at omega), the trapped magnet sees a static magnetic field, and the centrifugal effective potential V_eff = -(1/2)*I*omega^2 provides additional binding. The polarity-independence would follow if the trapping energy is dominated by kinetic (centrifugal) terms rather than magnetic dipole terms -- though this requires the trapped magnet to actually co-rotate.

The source does not provide a detailed theoretical model; the "vortex in spacetime" is a qualitative label for the observed behavior.

## 4. Helical Magnetic Field Producing Net Axial Thrust (Beyond Rotating Field Theory)

Source: 2044925361457398171.yaml (note_tweet), video 13_2044924356170854400_video.mdx

"A disc magnet spins in a 3-phase helical magnetic field, and rises against the vacuum"

The transition from "rotating magnetic field" (standard AC motor theory: XY-plane only) to "helical magnetic field" (rotating + propagating in Z) is the step that goes beyond standard rotating machine physics.

In standard AC motor theory:
* Stator produces a field rotating in the transverse (XY) plane
* The rotor spins in the XY-plane but does NOT translate in Z
* There is no net axial force for a uniformly wound 3-phase stator

For a helical field to produce net axial thrust on a synchronously spinning magnet, the field must have a non-zero axial wavenumber k_z:
B(r,z,t) = B_0(z) * [cos(k_z*z - omega*t)*x_hat + sin(k_z*z - omega*t)*y_hat]

This is different from a standard motor stator field. How three coils below a disc magnet create a helical field:
* The three wire coils are not planar rings but may be helical or positioned at different heights
* OR the field of the three disc-shaped coils has a natural axial structure that creates helicity

In practice, the field from three flat coils stacked in a Z-configuration (separated along Z) does have a Z-dependent phase structure. If coil 1 is at z=0, coil 2 at z=d, coil 3 at z=2d, with phase offsets corresponding to U-V-W, then the total field along the Z-axis has a helical phase structure:
B_total(z,t) ∝ cos(omega*t - phi(z)) where phi(z) varies with z

This produces a helical field structure without special coil geometries, simply from the physical separation of the three coils along Z.

Whether this arrangement provides a net time-averaged axial force on a spinning magnet requires the synchronous coupling to extract net momentum from the axial phase gradient -- analogous to a linear synchronous motor (LSM), except in the Z-direction (vertical) rather than horizontal.

A vertical linear synchronous motor driven by 3-phase current through three Z-separated coils is in principle possible and would produce net vertical (Z-axis) force on a synchronously moving rotor (the disc magnet). This is NOT standard rotating-machine physics but IS consistent with standard linear motor physics.

The "beyond Newtonian mechanics" claim: if the vertical linear motor force accounts for the rise, Newton's 3rd Law is satisfied (the reaction force is on the coils, which push up on the disc and are pushed down). The "beyond Newtonian" claim only applies if the counter-rotating dynamo actually reduces this reaction force -- which is the separate, unverified claim.

## 5. Gyroscopic Inertia: Laithwaite's Claim Extended to Space Propulsion

Source: 1953212976233906225.yaml

Eric Laithwaite's claim (Royal Institution Christmas Lectures, 1974):
* A spinning gyroscope undergoing precession appears to have reduced weight
* He demonstrated lifting heavy gyroscopes by hand that were too heavy to lift when not spinning
* Laithwaite interpreted this as partial overcoming of gravity by gyroscopic inertia

Standard physics explanation (Laithwaite was wrong):
* The apparent weight reduction is because the precessing gyroscope is continuously accelerating (centripetally, due to precession)
* The support force changes direction throughout the precession cycle
* The time-averaged support force equals the full weight of the gyroscope (W = mg, as required by Newton's laws)
* The ease of lifting a precessing gyroscope is a genuine mechanical advantage because one uses the gyroscope's angular momentum to redirect the gravitational force into precession rather than fighting it directly -- but the gyroscope is not lighter

The source extends Laithwaite's (incorrect) interpretation to space propulsion:
* A spinning magnetic assembly ("magnetic space propellor") precesses due to external torques from the ambient space magnetic field
* The precession generates net linear thrust (proposed as extension of Laithwaite's claimed weight reduction)
* This would violate conservation of linear momentum if no reaction partner exists

The interplanetary magnetic field (~5 nT near Earth's orbit) could provide an external torque on a magnetic gyroscope (tau = m x B_space). The reaction torque is on the interplanetary field (i.e., on the Sun's magnetic source). This IS momentum exchange with an external body (the solar magnetic field is "anchored" to the Sun's plasma). However, the thrust from tau = m x B on a 1 m^2 magnetic moment disc in 5 nT would be:
tau ≈ m * B = 1 A*m^2 * 5e-9 T = 5e-9 N*m

This is negligibly small for macroscopic propulsion. The mechanism only becomes significant for very large magnetic moments (superconducting magnets, large spinning ferromagnets) or very strong space magnetic fields (near magnetars, etc.).
