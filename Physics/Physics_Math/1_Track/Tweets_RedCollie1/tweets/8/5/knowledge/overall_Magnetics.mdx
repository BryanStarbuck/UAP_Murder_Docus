Source: 1945272410573541660.yaml (note_tweet), 1970471190230937728.yaml (note_tweet), 2039176288649592984.yaml, video 13_1970464668910231552_video.mdx

3-phase power (attributed to Nikola Tesla) drives three wire coils spaced 120 degrees apart (or in multiples: 9 coils in U-V-W-U-V-W-U-V-W configuration), producing a spatially rotating magnetic field:

B_total(t) = B_1*cos(omega*t) + B_2*cos(omega*t - 2*pi/3) + B_3*cos(omega*t - 4*pi/3)

Where B_1, B_2, B_3 are the field vectors from each phase coil (pointing at 0°, 120°, 240° in the XY-plane). The superposition produces a resultant B vector of constant magnitude rotating at angular frequency omega in the XY-plane (for a balanced 3-phase system):

|B_total| = (3/2) * B_peak (constant magnitude)
Angle of B_total rotates at omega rad/s

This is a spatially uniform but temporally rotating field in the XY-plane -- the standard stator field of an AC induction motor.

With 9 coils phased U-V-W-U-V-W-U-V-W arranged in a ring, the field is a 3-fold symmetric rotating structure. For this to couple to disc magnets, the flywheel requires 12 or 24 magnets (matching spatial harmonic content: 9 coil poles require pole counts that are multiples of 3, so 12=9+3 or 24=9*2+6 provide spatial periodicity matching).

## Lenz's Law / AC Induction Levitation of Aluminum Discs

Source: video 13_1970464668910231552_video.mdx

Aluminum is a non-ferromagnetic conductor. In a time-varying magnetic field, eddy currents are induced per Faraday's law:
EMF = -dΦ_B/dt

The induced eddy current J(r,t) creates an induced magnetic field B_induced that by Lenz's law opposes the change in the driving field. The interaction between B_driving and B_induced produces:
* Repulsive force (levitation) when the induced B is 180 degrees out of phase with the driving B (AC levitation / jumping ring geometry)
* Rotational torque when the driving field is rotating (as in 3-phase geometry)

Experimental results documented:

Configuration 1 (100 mm aluminum disc, 3-phase coils, 20 Hz):
* Disc spins CLOCKWISE -- same direction as the rotating 3-phase field
* This is consistent with induced eddy currents lagging the driving field (inductive phase lag), producing a drag torque that pulls the disc in the field's direction

Configuration 2 (70 mm aluminum disc, 3-phase coils, 20 Hz):
* Disc spins ANTI-CLOCKWISE -- opposite to the rotating 3-phase field
* This reversal is presented as anomalous and not explained by standard induction motor theory

Configuration 3 (aluminum disc + large external wire coil with AC, then 3-phase below, ~30-45 Hz):
* Lenz repulsion from the AC coil provides a vertical repulsive force
* The aluminum disc spins ANTI-clockwise (against the 3-phase field)
* Described as: "the aluminum disc spins against the three-phase magnetic field due to its own induced magnetic field which is 180 degrees out of phase with that wire coil"

The 180-degree phase relationship:
* The external AC coil induces a specific eddy current pattern in the aluminum disc
* This induced eddy current's magnetic moment is 180 degrees out of phase with the driving coil
* Lenz's law demands that the induced B oppose the driving B: B_induced ≈ -B_coil
* When this 180-degree-out-of-phase induced field then interacts with the 3-phase rotating field, the torque direction reverses relative to what it would be without the coil

The source explicitly claims this size-dependent chirality reversal (large disc: clockwise same as field; small disc: anti-clockwise opposite to field) is not accounted for in textbook induction physics and requires a "vortex theory of magnetism."

Frequency range tested: 20 Hz to 45 Hz (variable frequency drive, 3-phase power supply)

The stability criterion would then depend on matching the Larmor precession frequency or gyroscopic resonance rather than dipole orientation.

## Permanent Disc Magnet in 3-Phase Field: Rising Dynamics

Source: 1970471190230937728.yaml (note_tweet), video 13_1970464668910231552_video.mdx, video 13_2044924356170854400_video.mdx

* Rising continues until the field gets weaker with distance and the magnet "collapses again"

The polarity-independence of rising direction:
"It turns clockwise along the field and it rises up... We have turned that magnet upside down so that the blue south pole is facing up rather than the red north pole -- does exactly the same thing. It follows the field clockwise. It rises up on the pole."

This is anomalous by classical rotating machine analysis. In standard induction motor theory:
* A permanent magnet rotor in a rotating stator field couples to the field via the synchronous torque, which depends on the relative angle between rotor dipole moment and stator field
* Reversing the magnet polarity reverses the torque at a given phase angle
* However, the rotating field provides a restoring synchronous torque in both orientations -- the magnet synchronizes to the field in either polarity, simply at a different phase angle

The rising force (Z-axis thrust): not explained by standard motor theory. An induction motor torque is purely azimuthal. The axial force would require:
* A gradient in the field along Z: F_z = nabla(m · B) = m * dB_z/dz (force on a dipole in a field gradient)
* OR a helical component in the rotating field (non-zero k_z wavenumber in the field Fourier spectrum)

The source describes the field as "helical" -- three coils below create a field that has both rotating (XY) and axial (Z) components as one moves along Z, constituting a helical rotating field. This is distinct from a purely rotating (2D) field.

## Corbino Disc: Magnetic Force Redirection by 90 Degrees

Source: 2007971953236423039.yaml

"Newton's 3rd Law does not apply to a Corbino disc, because an inward electric force is redirected by N/S magnetism to another force-direction 90 degrees away."

Corbino disc (Orso Mario Corbino, 1911): a conducting disc in an axial magnetic field B_z, with electrical current driven radially (from center to rim or rim to center).

Lorentz force analysis:
* Radial current density: J = J_r * r_hat
* Axial field: B = B_z * z_hat
* Lorentz force on current: F = J x B = J_r * r_hat x B_z * z_hat = J_r * B_z * (r_hat x z_hat) = J_r * B_z * (-phi_hat)

So the inward radial electric force (driving the current) is redirected into the azimuthal (phi) direction by the magnetic field. The output force direction is 90 degrees from the input force direction.

The claim about Newton's 3rd Law:
* Input: radial electric force (from battery/power supply driving current inward)
* Output: azimuthal force (Lorentz force on current carriers)
* The reaction to the azimuthal force is an azimuthal force on the external magnetic field source (in the opposite direction)
* The reaction to the radial electric force is on the power supply (in the opposite radial direction)
* Because the input and output forces point in different directions (90 degrees apart), there is no single axis along which action and reaction are collinear
* This is argued to constitute a violation (or at minimum, inapplicability) of Newton's 3rd Law in the conventional sense for this geometry

The Corbino disc is proposed as the internal component of the counter-rotating system in the UFO that achieves force direction steering and Newton's 3rd Law mitigation.
