## 1. Magnetic Dipole Field Equations

Source: 3_1915353757367623681.txt (image description of dipole field diagram)

IMPLICIT (standard dipole physics described):

The magnetic field of a magnetic dipole with moment m located at origin:

B(r) = (mu_0 / 4*pi) * [ 3*(m · r_hat)*r_hat - m ] / r^3  (SI units, in free space)

Components in spherical coordinates (m = m_0 * z_hat):
B_r     = (mu_0 / 4*pi) * (2 * m_0 * cos(theta)) / r^3
B_theta = (mu_0 / 4*pi) * (m_0 * sin(theta)) / r^3

The zero-field nodal plane (equator, theta = pi/2): B_r = 0, B_theta ≠ 0
The poles (theta = 0, pi): B_r maximum, B_theta = 0

The two-lobe iron-filing pattern traces the B_r component:
* Dense fillings near theta ≈ 0 and theta ≈ pi (near the poles, large B_r)
* Sparse fillings near theta ≈ pi/2 (equatorial plane)

The crop circle two-lobe structure is claimed to topologically reproduce this angular distribution.

## 4. Skin Depth in Conducting Disc (Eddy Current Penetration)

Source: video 13_1970464668910231552_video.mdx (aluminum disc experiments)
IMPLICIT (required to understand size-dependent chirality reversal):

Skin depth for a conductor:
delta = sqrt(2*rho / (omega*mu))

For aluminum at 20 Hz:
rho (aluminum) = 2.65 x 10^-8 ohm*m
omega = 2*pi*20 = 125.7 rad/s
mu ≈ mu_0 = 4*pi x 10^-7 H/m (aluminum is non-magnetic)

delta = sqrt(2 * 2.65e-8 / (125.7 * 4*pi*1e-7))
     = sqrt(5.3e-8 / 1.579e-4)

## 5. Lorentz Force on Current in Magnetic Field (Corbino Disc)

Source: 2007971953236423039.yaml
EXPLICIT in qualitative form: "inward electric force is redirected by N/S magnetism to another force-direction 90 degrees away"

Complete derivation:

Corbino disc geometry:
* Disc in XY-plane, inner radius r_inner, outer radius r_outer
* External magnetic field B = B_0 * z_hat (axial)
* Voltage applied between center and rim: V, creating radial electric field E_r(r) = V / (r * ln(r_outer/r_inner))
* Radial current density: J_r(r) = sigma * E_r(r) = sigma * V / (r * ln(r_outer/r_inner))

Lorentz force on current carriers (charge q, drift velocity v_r):
F = q * (E + v x B) = q * (E_r * r_hat + v_r * r_hat x B_0 * z_hat)
                    = q * E_r * r_hat + q * v_r * B_0 * (r_hat x z_hat)
                    = q * E_r * r_hat - q * v_r * B_0 * phi_hat

The force has two components:
* Radial: F_r = q * E_r (driving force from applied voltage)
* Azimuthal: F_phi = -q * v_r * B_0 (Lorentz force perpendicular to both v_r and B_0)

The azimuthal force per unit volume on the disc current:
f_phi = J_r x B_0 = J_r * B_0 * (r_hat x z_hat) = -J_r * B_0 * phi_hat

Net azimuthal torque on disc:
tau = ∫_V r * f_phi dV = ∫ r * J_r * B_0 * (2*pi*r*h) dr  (for disc of thickness h)
    = 2*pi*h*B_0 * ∫_{r_inner}^{r_outer} J_r * r^2 dr
    = 2*pi*h*B_0 * sigma*V/ln(r_outer/r_inner) * ∫_{r_inner}^{r_outer} dr / r... (substituting J_r)
    
Wait, J_r(r) = sigma * V / (r * ln(r_outer/r_inner)), so J_r * r = sigma*V/ln(r_outer/r_inner) = constant

tau = 2*pi*h*B_0 * sigma*V/ln(r_outer/r_inner) * (r_outer - r_inner)

This is the torque on the disc due to the Hall/Lorentz effect -- the Corbino disc acts as an electromagnetic motor when current is driven radially in an axial B field. The reaction torque is on the magnet providing B_0.

The force-direction claim: the electrical input drives a radial current (r direction); the magnetic field deflects this into azimuthal motion (phi direction), 90 degrees away. The reaction torque on the magnet is azimuthal (phi direction), not radial. The reaction on the power supply is radial. Therefore the action-reaction pair partners are geometrically separated.

## 6. Force on Magnetic Dipole in Field Gradient (Disc Magnet Rise)

Source: videos 13_1970464668910231552, 13_2044924356170854400
IMPLICIT:

Force on a magnetic dipole m in a non-uniform B field:
F = nabla(m · B)

Components:
F_x = m_x * dB_x/dx + m_y * dB_y/dx + m_z * dB_z/dx
F_y = m_x * dB_x/dy + m_y * dB_y/dy + m_z * dB_z/dy
F_z = m_x * dB_x/dz + m_y * dB_y/dz + m_z * dB_z/dz

For the helical rotating field B(z,t) = B_0 * cos(k*z - omega*t) * x_hat + B_0 * sin(k*z - omega*t) * y_hat:

dB_x/dz = -B_0 * k * sin(k*z - omega*t)
dB_y/dz =  B_0 * k * cos(k*z - omega*t)

For a disc magnet synchronized to the field: m(t) = m_0 * cos(omega*t + phi_0) * x_hat + m_0 * sin(omega*t + phi_0) * y_hat:

F_z = m_x * dB_x/dz + m_y * dB_y/dz
    = m_0 * cos(omega*t + phi_0) * (-B_0*k*sin(k*z - omega*t))
    + m_0 * sin(omega*t + phi_0) * (B_0*k*cos(k*z - omega*t))

Time-averaging:
<F_z> = m_0 * B_0 * k * <-cos(omega*t+phi_0)*sin(k*z - omega*t) + sin(omega*t+phi_0)*cos(k*z - omega*t)>
      = m_0 * B_0 * k * <sin(omega*t+phi_0 - k*z + omega*t)>
      = m_0 * B_0 * k * <sin(2*omega*t + phi_0 - k*z)>

For a truly time-averaged (slow rise compared to omega), the <sin(2*omega*t + ...)> term averages to zero unless there is a phase lock between the magnet precession and 2*omega.

Actually, let's reconsider: for a synchronous disc rotating at omega in the XY-plane, and the field also rotating at omega:
m · B = m_0*B_0 * [cos(omega*t+phi_0)*cos(k*z-omega*t) + sin(omega*t+phi_0)*sin(k*z-omega*t)]
       = m_0*B_0 * cos(omega*t+phi_0 - k*z + omega*t)
       = m_0*B_0 * cos(2*omega*t + phi_0 - k*z)

F_z = d(m·B)/dz = m_0*B_0*k * sin(2*omega*t + phi_0 - k*z)

This oscillates at 2*omega. The time-averaged force is zero in this case.

For the force to be non-zero on average, the disc magnet must be precessing (not just spinning in XY), or there must be a static (non-rotating) Z-component:

If m = m_z * z_hat (static Z component from the permanent magnet aligned with Z):
F_z = m_z * dB_z/dz

For a solenoid-like arrangement, B_z decreases with z above the coils: dB_z/dz < 0, giving F_z < 0 (downward) for m_z > 0. This does not produce upward force.

Alternatively, for a Gradient force from the modulus |B(z)|:
A spinning disc magnet in a field that decreases in Z experiences a force that depends on the gradient of |B|, NOT on the specific direction of B. The force on a paramagnetic or diamagnetic sphere (or disc) in a gradient field:
F_z = chi * V * (B_z / mu_0) * dB_z/dz (for uniform susceptibility chi, volume V)

For chi < 0 (diamagnetic): F_z > 0 when dB_z/dz < 0 (repelled from high-field region) -- upward if field is strongest near coils below
For chi > 0 (paramagnetic): F_z < 0 (attracted toward high-field region) -- downward

Permanent ferromagnets (chi >> 1 effectively) are attracted to field maxima. The upward rise of a permanent disc magnet ABOVE the field source (where the field decreases with increasing z) would normally require the magnet to be pushed downward, not upward.

The source's claim of upward rise is experimentally documented (video evidence). The theoretical explanation within standard EM remains incomplete in the source texts, consistent with the claim that this is "new physics."

## 7. Homopolar Generator EMF

Source: 1983881901372010784.yaml (homopolar dynamo reference)
IMPLICIT:

Homopolar (unipolar) generator (Faraday disc):
A conducting disc of radius R rotates at angular velocity omega in uniform axial field B:
EMF = integral from center to rim of (v x B) · dr

At radius r: v = omega * r (azimuthal), B = B_z (axial)
v x B = omega*r * phi_hat x B_z * z_hat = omega*r*B_z * (phi_hat x z_hat) = -omega*r*B_z * r_hat

EMF = -integral_0^R (-omega*r*B_z) dr = omega*B_z*R^2/2

If the output current I is fed back to create B_z (self-exciting configuration):
B_z = mu_0*n*I/L (for solenoid with n turns per length L)
EMF = omega * (mu_0*n*I/L) * R^2 / 2

Output current: I = EMF / (R_internal + R_external) = omega*mu_0*n*R^2*I / (2*L*(R_int + R_ext))

For self-excitation, we need: omega * mu_0 * n * R^2 / (2*L*(R_int+R_ext)) > 1 (positive feedback)
This defines a critical angular velocity omega_crit = 2*L*(R_int+R_ext) / (mu_0*n*R^2)

Above omega_crit, the dynamo is self-exciting -- it amplifies its own field. This is the same criterion as for the Earth's geodynamo, where the critical magnetic Reynolds number Rm = mu_0*sigma*v*L > Rm_crit ≈ 10-100.

For a liquid metal homopolar dynamo (as claimed for the UFO internal component):
* Conducting fluid (e.g., mercury, liquid aluminum, liquid sodium): high electrical conductivity sigma
* Rotating in the internal magnetic field of the craft
* Counter-rotating relative to the 3-phase drive
* Self-exciting above omega_crit
* Spinning in counter-direction generates counter-angular momentum, claimed to reduce Newton's 3rd Law reaction forces on the craft

## 9. Lenz's Law Force (Jumping Ring)

Source: 1934367758671896655.yaml, referenced tweet 1934194761935770022
IMPLICIT:

The jumping ring force on a conducting ring in an AC solenoid field:

Time-averaged force:
<F_z> = (1/2) * I_primary * I_ring * (dM/dz) * cos(phi_lag)

where:
M = mutual inductance between solenoid and ring
phi_lag = phase lag of ring current relative to solenoid current (from ring's RL impedance)
phi_lag = arctan(omega*L_ring / R_ring)

For maximum force: phi_lag → 0 (resistive-dominated ring, high R relative to omega*L)
For minimum force: phi_lag → pi/2 (inductive-dominated ring, high omega*L relative to R)

The source reports: "small and reproducible anti-gravity effects of 30 to 50 grams, yet much of the upward spinning force of 500 grams (for this small magnet at 20-30 Hertz)" -- the discrepancy between 500g force and 30-50g net anti-gravity effect on scale. The 500g force would be the total electromagnetic upward force, of which 30-50g exceeds what standard Lenz repulsion predicts for the geometry. The remaining ~450g would be accounted for by standard Lenz forces; only the 30-50g excess is presented as anomalous.

## 10. Synchronous Torque on Permanent Magnet Rotor

Source: video 13_1970464668910231552_video.mdx
IMPLICIT:

For a permanent magnet of moment m = m_0 * n_hat in a rotating field B = B_0*(cos(omega*t)*x_hat + sin(omega*t)*y_hat):

If the magnet has rotated to angle phi_m, n_hat = cos(phi_m)*x_hat + sin(phi_m)*y_hat:

Torque: tau_z = m x B = m_0*B_0 * sin(omega*t - phi_m) (z-component)

This torque tends to pull phi_m toward omega*t (synchronization). In steady state, phi_m = omega*t - delta (lagging by constant angle delta):
tau_z = m_0 * B_0 * sin(delta) (constant synchronizing torque)

For steady rotation: tau_z = tau_drag (friction, air resistance)
m_0 * B_0 * sin(delta) = tau_drag

Maximum torque (pull-out torque): tau_max = m_0 * B_0 (at delta = pi/2)

The observation that both North-up and South-up magnets synchronize and rise in the same direction is consistent with this model: reversing polarity reverses m_0, which shifts delta by pi (the synchronizing torque becomes -m_0*B_0*sin(delta)), but the magnet still synchronizes -- just at a different equilibrium angle delta' = pi - delta. The rotation direction (same as field) is preserved in both polarity cases.
