## Specific Physics Advances Enabling the Claimed Benefit

### Advance 1: Homopolar Motor + 3-Phase Field Combination (Experimental, Not Purely Theoretical)

A conventional homopolar motor (Faraday disc, also called a "unipolar dynamo") spins under DC current through a radial conductor in an axial magnetic field. The torque arises from the Lorentz force F = q(v × B), where v is the radial carrier velocity and B is the axial field. The result is azimuthal torque: τ = r × F = r × (IL × B), where IL is the current element.

What has been tested (YAML 1984099193573798291, video 13_1984095599403397120): A homopolar disc motor where wires rotate in one direction and the disc rotates in the opposite direction, but wire mass is insufficient to account for disc counter-rotation. The question posed: "Where are the torques interacting? This is not equal and opposite torques." This anomalous torque distribution constitutes the experimental observation claimed to be non-Newtonian.

### Advance 2: 3-Phase Rotating Magnetic Field Causing Vertical Displacement ("Vortex-Lifting")

Three-phase AC power applied to three wire coils generates a rotating magnetic field UVW. As shown experimentally (video 13_1984095569347014657): when 3-phase power is applied at approximately 10 Hz, a spinning magnet rises to the top of its constraint (pole). When the orientation of the test magnet is reversed, instead of being repelled downward (as magnetic repulsion would predict), it still rises. This is stated to falsify the "magnetic repulsion" interpretation: "Instead it rises like a top as it gets spinning again" (YAML 2010654478794732001 video, 13_2010653338162061313).

The 3-phase field creates an inner clockwise rotation of the field UVW at the center of the coils and an anti-clockwise rotation in the external region between adjacent coils, as visualized using a compass needle (video 13_2014966308069376003). This spatial reversal of field rotation direction is a structural feature of the three-phase field geometry claimed to be essential to the vortex-lifting mechanism.

The distinction between vortex-lifting and ordinary magnetic repulsion is critical: vortex-lifting acts on a spinning disc/magnet in a rotating field and produces vertical displacement not predicted by static magnetic repulsion calculations.
