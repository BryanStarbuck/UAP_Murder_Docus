## 2. Distinction Between Scale Weight and Vertical Force

Critical experimental finding: the Z-axis rise (upward displacement of the magnet) does NOT produce a change in the reading of a scale on which the apparatus rests. This means:

- The vortex-lift force F_vortex acts upward on the magnet.
- An equal and opposite reaction force acts downward on the support structure (coils, pole, scale platform).
- Net force on (magnet + apparatus) system = 0.
- Scale reading unchanged.

This is what Drew calls "mass recoil" — the Newtonian reaction to the upward force on the magnet is transmitted through the magnetic field to the coil assembly and downward to the scale. It is also partly "magnetic recoil" — the Lenz's Law induced opposing field in the coils.

The distinction is between:
A. Internal redistribution of forces (magnet goes up, coils pushed down equally) → scale unchanged. This is ordinary physics.
B. External force with no equal and opposite reaction transmitted to support → scale changes. This is the anti-gravity claim.

## 5. Vortex-Lifting vs. Magnetic Repulsion: Orientation Independence Test

Decisive experimental test distinguishing vortex-lifting from magnetic repulsion (video 13_2010653338162061313):

"We turn on the three-phase power, it starts spinning and if it was magnetic repulsion pushing it up that way in the other orientation we would expect this magnet to go down as it spins. Instead it rises like a top as it gets spinning again."

Observed result: the magnet still rises. This result is inconsistent with pure magnetic repulsion and consistent with a spin-dependent, orientation-independent mechanism.

## 7. Z-Axis Rise Mechanism: XY-Plane Rotation Coupling

How does XY-plane spinning (azimuthal rotation in the horizontal plane) couple to Z-axis translation (vertical rise)?

Drew does not derive this coupling from first principles in the available tweets, but the phenomenon is experimentally confirmed. The implied mechanism by analogy with a gyroscope: a spinning disc in a gravitational field and in a non-uniform field (the rotating 3-phase field has Z-axis gradient near the coil edges) experiences a precession-like force. The interaction of the rotating magnetic moment with the spatial gradient of the rotating field (∇B) produces a force in the direction of increasing field magnitude, which is along the Z-axis for the coil geometry used:

    F = ∇(μ · B)

For the disc magnet with moment μ aligned with the rotating field B(t):

    F_z = ∂/∂z (μ_z B_z + μ_x B_x + μ_y B_y)

When the magnet is spinning in synchrony with the field, the time-averaged force:

    <F_z> = <μ · ∂B/∂z>

The time average is non-zero if the magnet spin phase is locked to the field rotation. This is the standard ponderomotive/gradient force on a rotating magnetic dipole, which can produce a net axial force toward or away from the coil plane depending on geometry.

Drew's description of the rise as "like a top" (gyroscope-like stable rise) is consistent with the magnet acting as a spinning gyroscope that precesses axially in the field gradient.
