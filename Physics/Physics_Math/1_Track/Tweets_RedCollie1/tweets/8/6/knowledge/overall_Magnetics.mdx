## 1. Three-Phase Rotating Magnetic Field

### Structure and Generation

Three wire coils (coils U, V, W) driven by three-phase AC power generate a rotating magnetic field. The three phases are spatially separated by 120° in the winding geometry and temporally offset by 120° (T/3 of the AC cycle). The resultant magnetic field vector rotates in the plane of the coils at the AC frequency.

Experimental visualization (video 13_2014966308069376003): A compass placed between two adjacent three-phase coils spins anti-clockwise. The same compass placed at the geometric center of the three coils spins clockwise. This demonstrates that the rotating field vector reverses its circulation sense between the internal and external regions of the three-phase coil array:

- CENTER of coils: field rotates CLOCKWISE (UVW sequence)
- EXTERIOR between coils: field rotates ANTI-CLOCKWISE

This spatial inversion of circulation sense is a structural consequence of the geometry of the three-phase winding and is directly identified by Drew as functionally important to the vortex-lifting mechanism.

Source (video 13_2014966308069376003): "We can visualize the magnetic field around one, two, three wire corals given three-phase power using a simple compass. If we place that compass in the crevice outside between two of those corals, you can see that it spins very rapidly. Anti-clockwise. Next, we take that same compass and place it in the middle at the center of the corals. You can see that it spins clockwise. The magnetic field UVW is rotating clockwise in this instance. We'll put it back out around the outside and again it spins anti-clockwise."

### Effect on a Permanent Magnet in the Field

A permanent disc magnet placed inside the three-phase coil array (centered) is acted on by the rotating field UVW. The magnetic torque τ = μ × B (where μ is the magnetic dipole moment of the permanent magnet and B is the rotating applied field) causes the permanent magnet to spin synchronously with the rotating field — this is the induction motor principle applied to a permanent-magnet rotor.

At approximately 10 Hz rotation rate (moderate power), the spinning magnet rises vertically. This is the "vortex-lifting" effect.

Source (video 13_1984095569347014657): "Now when I turn on the three-phase power, you can see the magnets are spinning in vortex and as we get to moderate power like about 10 hertz, it rises up to the top. It looks like it's pushing on the pole, however, that doesn't create any weight on the scale. No change of weight on the scale when we do that, even if we go fast to look."

### Key Experimental Finding: No Scale Weight Change

The upward force observed during 3-phase spin-rise does not correspond to a change in weight measured on a scale. Drew attributes this to "mass recoil" and "magnetic recoil" (see Lenz's Law section below). This null result on the scale is the critical problem: genuine anti-gravity would require a reduction in scale weight.

## 2. Magnetic Repulsion vs. Vortex-Lifting

Two distinct mechanisms are distinguished:

A. MAGNETIC REPULSION (static, scalar): A fixed magnet held above or below another fixed magnet, with poles oriented to repel. This produces a static levitation force that IS measurable on a scale (reduced scale weight). Demonstrated in initial experiment (YAML 1915359976211833311): "This is just 'magnetic repulsion' holding it up, but you can see why we need 'spin'?" The text indicates that repulsion alone is insufficient; spin is required for the specific UAP-like levitation mechanism.

B. VORTEX-LIFTING (dynamic, rotating field): A spinning magnet in a rotating 3-phase field rises by a mechanism that is NOT purely magnetic repulsion. Evidence: when the magnet orientation is reversed (poles swapped), if the mechanism were simple repulsion, the magnet should go DOWN. Instead it still rises (YAML 2010654478794732001 video, 13_2010653338162061313): "We turn on the three-phase power, it starts spinning and if it was magnetic repulsion pushing it up that way in the other orientation we would expect this magnet to go down as it spins. This falsification of the magnetic-repulsion explanation for vortex-lifting is a central experimental result. The mechanism for vortex-lifting is thus not yet theoretically determined within classical EM; Drew presents it as empirical evidence for a beyond-classical magnetic effect.

## 3. Lenz's Law as "Magnetic Recoil"

Lenz's Law: An induced EMF in a conductor creates a current whose magnetic field opposes the change in flux producing it (Faraday's law with sign: EMF = -dΦ/dt). In the context of the 3-phase spin-rise experiment, the rising spinning magnet induces opposing currents in the coil windings (or in conductive surroundings), which create a magnetic field opposing the applied rotating field. Drew terms this effect "magnetic recoil."

The self-excited dynamo mechanism is proposed to cancel magnetic recoil: the homopolar DC-driven spinning conductor acts as a "self-excited dynamo" (referencing Tesla's notes on the unipolar dynamo, https://teslauniverse.com/nikola-tesla/articles/notes-unipolar-dynamo) by generating a magnetic field that co-aligns with and reinforces the applied 3-phase field. This reinforcement is claimed to reduce or cancel the Lenz's Law opposition.

This should reduce or cancel 'magnetic recoil', which creates an opposing magnetic field by Lenz's Law."

## 5. Homopolar Motor Magnetic Field Structure

The homopolar (Faraday disc / unipolar) motor is a DC machine in which:

- An axial magnetic field B_z is provided by a permanent magnet or electromagnet.
- A radial current I_r flows from center to rim (or rim to center) in a conducting disc.
- The Lorentz force F = I dl × B acts azimuthally on each current element, producing rotational torque τ = ∫ r × (J × B) dV.
- No commutator is needed (hence "unipolar" or "homopolar") — the torque direction is constant regardless of the angular position of the rotor.

Key property identified by Drew (YAML 1966827826566623641 note_tweet): "The spin of homopolar motor does not follow Newton's 3rd law, since it is powered by a 'sideways' Lorentz force."

The implication is that because the driving force is always perpendicular to both the current and the field (azimuthal Lorentz force, with no component in the direction of motion of the external stator/magnet), there is no equal and opposite reaction force in the azimuthal direction that would be transmitted to the external support structure — unlike a conventional motor where stator reaction torque is transmitted through bearings to the frame.

The liquid-metal implementation uses galinstan as the conducting disc, allowing large currents (up to ~1000 A stated) without commutator wear. At 12 V DC, the wires get very hot: galinstan has near-zero electrical resistance compared to copper wire, so nearly all the resistance is in the connecting cables. Engineering solution: add ~0.1 ohm nichrome resistor in series.
