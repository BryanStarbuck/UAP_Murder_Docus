## 1. Lorentz Force — Foundational Equation

    F = q(v × B)

For a current element:

    dF = I (dl × B)

In cylindrical coordinates for the homopolar disc (B = B_z ẑ, dl = dr r̂):

    dF = I dr (r̂ × B_z ẑ) = I B_z dr (-φ̂)

Torque about the Z-axis from the whole disc, integrating from r = 0 to r = R:

    τ_z = ∫₀ᴿ r · (I B_z) dr = I B_z · (R²/2)

Units: τ in N·m; I in amperes; B_z in tesla; R in meters.

Experimental parameters stated (YAML 1995468258527588830 note_tweet): 1 volt DC, 1000 amps. At I = 1000 A, a modest B_z = 0.1 T, R = 0.05 m:

    τ_z = 1000 × 0.1 × (0.05²/2) = 1000 × 0.1 × 0.00125 = 0.125 N·m

This is a substantial torque for a small-scale apparatus.

## 2. Three-Phase Rotating Magnetic Field

Phase voltages:

    V_U(t) = V₀ cos(ωt)
    V_V(t) = V₀ cos(ωt - 2π/3)
    V_W(t) = V₀ cos(ωt - 4π/3)

Resulting magnetic field at the center of a three-phase symmetric winding:

    B_x(t) = B₀[cos(ωt) + cos(ωt - 2π/3) · (-1/2) + cos(ωt - 4π/3) · (-1/2)]
    B_y(t) = B₀[0 + cos(ωt - 2π/3) · (√3/2) + cos(ωt - 4π/3) · (-√3/2)]

After trigonometric reduction:

    B_x(t) = (3/2)B₀ cos(ωt)
    B_y(t) = (3/2)B₀ sin(ωt)

The magnitude |B| = (3/2)B₀ = constant. The field vector traces a circle in the XY-plane at angular frequency ω = 2πf_AC.

Experimental frequency: f_AC ≈ 10 Hz → ω ≈ 62.8 rad/s. At this frequency, the permanent magnet rises to the top of its constraint pole.

Five-phase extension (hypothesized for Buga orb-sphere, YAML 1942555034761535568 and 1923577273351053674):

    V_k(t) = V₀ cos(ωt - 2πk/5),  k = 0, 1, 2, 3, 4

Resulting field:

    |B_5phase| = (5/2)B₀ = constant

A 5-phase field has smaller torque ripple than 3-phase and a 67% larger field amplitude for the same coil current.

## 3. Magnetic Torque on Permanent Magnet

    τ = μ × B

where μ = m · V̂ is the magnetic dipole moment (m = magnetization, V̂ = volume vector).

For a disc magnet with axial dipole moment |μ| = μ_0 in field |B| = (3/2)B₀:

    |τ| = μ_0 · (3/2)B₀ · sin(δ)

where δ is the angle between μ and B(t). At steady synchronous rotation, δ → 0 (magnet tracks the field). The power input equals:

    P = τ · ω

## 4. Angular Momentum of the Spinning System

For a disc magnet rotating at angular velocity ω_m about the Z-axis:

    L_magnet = I_m · ω_m

where I_m = (1/2)m_magnet · R_magnet² (solid disc approximation).

For the galinstan Corbino counter-spin at angular velocity ω_g (opposite sign):

    L_galinstan = I_g · ω_g

Anti-gravity condition:

    L_total = L_magnet + L_galinstan = 0
    I_m · ω_m + I_g · ω_g = 0
    ω_g / ω_m = -I_m / I_g

The ratio of counter-spin frequency to main spin frequency equals the inverse ratio of moments of inertia. If both discs are similar in size and density, ω_g ≈ -ω_m.

## 5. Gyroscopic Precession

For a spinning disc with angular momentum L = I · ω in a gravitational field:

    dL/dt = τ_gravity = r × (mg ẑ)  [if spin axis is not vertical]

Precession angular velocity:

    Ω_precession = τ / L = (m · g · r) / (I · ω)

At ω ≈ 20-30 Hz (= 125-188 rad/s), r ≈ 0.01 m, I ≈ (1/2)m R² with R = 0.03 m:

    I ≈ (1/2)(0.01 kg)(0.03)² ≈ 4.5 × 10⁻⁶ kg·m²
    Ω_precession ≈ (0.01 × 9.8 × 0.01) / (4.5 × 10⁻⁶ × 125) ≈ 0.98/5.6 × 10⁻⁴ ≈ 1750 rad/s

(These values are order-of-magnitude estimates given the parameters.) The wobble (precession) at 20-30 Hz is expected and consistent with classical gyroscope mechanics. ## 6. Faraday's Law and Lenz's Law (Magnetic Recoil)

Faraday's Law:

    EMF = -dΦ/dt = -d/dt ∫∫ B · dA

Lenz's Law: The induced EMF drives a current I_induced such that the magnetic field generated by I_induced opposes the change in flux Φ.

For a conducting coil in the rising magnet's field, the induced current generates a field B_induced that opposes the driving 3-phase field B_applied. This is the "magnetic recoil" that reduces the net upward force:

    F_net_up = F_vortex_lift - F_Lenz_recoil

The self-excited dynamo enhancement proposes:

    B_total = B_applied + B_dynamo

where B_dynamo is generated by the spinning galinstan/homopolar conductor. If B_dynamo co-aligns with B_applied, the Lenz-opposing current now must work against a larger effective field, reducing the relative magnitude of the Lenz opposition.

The Tesla unipolar dynamo reference (https://teslauniverse.com/nikola-tesla/articles/notes-unipolar-dynamo) establishes that a spinning conducting disc in its own field self-amplifies — the disc generates EMF = ω · B_z · R²/2, and in a closed circuit this drives current that adds to B_z.

## 8. AC Induction Levitation (Lenz Levitation) of Aluminum Disc

For a flat conducting disc (conductivity σ, thickness d) in an alternating field B = B₀ e^(iωt) ẑ:

Eddy current surface density (thin disc approximation):

    K_eddy = σ d × (∂A/∂t)

where A is the vector potential. The time-averaged levitation force per unit area:

    <F_z/A> = (σ d ω B₀²) / (2√(1 + (σ d ω μ₀ R)²))  [order of magnitude]

For aluminum (σ ≈ 3.8 × 10⁷ S/m), d = 3 mm, ω = 2π × 50 Hz, B₀ = 0.1 T:

    σ d ω ≈ 3.8 × 10⁷ × 0.003 × 314 ≈ 3.6 × 10⁷

This is large, meaning the disc is in the strong-screening (perfect-conductor) limit, and the levitation force approaches maximum for the given field. In the three-phase case, the additional rotational component of the field provides azimuthal torque on the eddy currents, causing the disc to spin. The directionality (clockwise vs. anti-clockwise) depends on the phase sequence UVW vs. UWV.

Drew labels the observation that three-phase excitation makes the Lenz-levitated disc spin in two different directions as "NEW PHYSICS" (YAML 1970621393122533480). The directionality is determined by which phase sequence (and hence which rotational direction of the field) is applied.
