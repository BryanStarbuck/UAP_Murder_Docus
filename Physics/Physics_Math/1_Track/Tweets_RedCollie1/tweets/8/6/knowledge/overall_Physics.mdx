## 1. Lorentz Force in the Homopolar Motor

The foundational force law operative in the homopolar (Faraday disc) motor:

    F = q(v × B)

For a current-carrying conductor element:

    dF = I (dl × B)

In the homopolar geometry:
- B = B_z ẑ  (axial field from permanent magnet, along Z)
- dl = dr r̂  (radial current element in the disc)
- dl × B = dr r̂ × B_z ẑ = B_z dr (r̂ × ẑ) = -B_z dr φ̂

So dF = I (-B_z dr φ̂), which is purely azimuthal. Integrating from center (r=0) to rim (r=R):

    τ = ∫₀ᴿ r × dF/dr dr = ∫₀ᴿ r * I * B_z dr = I * B_z * R²/2

This azimuthal torque drives the disc to spin in the φ direction. The key property: the torque vector τ_φ is perpendicular to both the current direction r̂ and the field direction ẑ. Because the torque is Lorentz-force-derived ("sideways"), it does not couple to the axial (Z) direction in the standard configuration.

## 2. Magnetic Torque on a Permanent Magnet in a Rotating Field

A permanent magnet with magnetic dipole moment μ placed in an external rotating field B(t):

    τ = μ × B(t)

For a three-phase field: B(t) = B₀[cos(ωt) x̂ + sin(ωt) ŷ] (rotating field in XY plane at angular frequency ω = 2πf_AC).

## 5. Gyroscope Dynamics and the Wobble Claim

Source (YAML 1934370163694195145 text): "The central spinning magnet is SUPPOSED to wobble. Any spinning disc wobbles in the presence of a downward gravity field."

This refers to gyroscopic precession: a spinning disc (gyroscope) with angular momentum L in the presence of an external torque τ_gravity = r × (mg ẑ) will precess:

    dL/dt = τ_external

For a disc spinning about an axis that is not perfectly vertical, gravity exerts a torque that causes the spin axis to precess around the vertical. The precession angular velocity:

    Ω_precession = τ / L = mgr / (Iω)

where r is the distance from the pivot to the center of mass, I is the moment of inertia, and ω is the spin angular velocity. At low ω, precession is fast and wobble is large. At high ω, precession is slow and the spin axis is stable.

The context (from the referenced tweet in YAML 1934370163694195145): the experiment shows "small and reproducible anti-gravity effects of 30 to 50 grams, yet much of the upward spinning force of 500 grams (for this small magnet at 20-30 Hertz) seems to" [be lost/cancelled]. The anti-gravity effect of 30-50 g is reproducible but only a fraction (6-10%) of the 500 g upward spin force — the rest is absorbed by mass recoil and magnetic recoil.
