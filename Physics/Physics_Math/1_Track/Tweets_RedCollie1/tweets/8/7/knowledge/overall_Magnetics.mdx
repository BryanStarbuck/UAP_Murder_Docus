ROTATING FIELD GEOMETRY — XY-PLANE BEHAVIOR:
* The three-phase or six-phase rotating magnetic field rotates in the horizontal plane (XY-plane), generating a helical or screw-like field in the region above and below the stator midplane.
* A permanent disc magnet placed at the center couples magnetically to this rotating field and is driven to rotate in the same direction (or opposite direction depending on configuration and speed).
* At low rotation rates, the magnet follows the rotating field synchronously or near-synchronously (induction-motor-like drag coupling).
* At high rotation rates (25-30 Hz), the field speed exceeds any stable synchronous locking; the magnet then enters a precessing top-like gyroscopic regime.

ROTATING FIELD GEOMETRY — Z-AXIS (VERTICAL) DYNAMICS:
* The rotating magnetic field of the stator extends vertically above and below the coil plane; its helical field lines act as a "magnetic screw" on a spinning permanent magnet.
* At low frequency (12-15 Hz): The magnet is pushed toward the repulsive lower magnet (downward), causing a Newton's 3rd law upward reaction force in the central rod — purely Newtonian.
* At high frequency (25-30 Hz): The magnet behaves as a gyroscopic top and rises vertically (upward along the Z-axis), decoupling from the simple Newtonian rod-reaction pattern.
* "As we add a rotating magnetic field to that central magnet it will start going downward toward the repulsive magnet. And as that happens the central rod above will lift up as a Newtonian third law reaction." [Experimental observation at ~12 Hz, MDX 13_1915645568136994817]
* "We go to 15 hertz, 25 hertz, it rises up like a top. And now there's no Newtonian force in the rod at all just hovering there freely in space." [Experimental observation at 25 Hz, MDX 13_1915645568136994817]

HOMOPOLAR MOTOR — SOLID-STATE, TWO-BRUSH CONFIGURATION:
* A solid homopolar motor uses a single permanent magnet, current fed via two brushes (one at the pole face, one at the cylindrical equator, or top and bottom contacts).
* Operating principle: DC current flowing radially (inward or outward) in the presence of an axial magnetic field experiences a Lorentz force F = qv × B in the azimuthal direction, producing rotation without commutation.
* Key anomalous claim: "A 'solid' homopolar motor does not follow Newton's 3rd Law of action-reaction. Because its spin-motive energy is a sideways Lorentz force."
* The torque delivered to the rotor (magnet) does not produce an equal and opposite torque in the stator frame in the same straightforward way as a conventional motor, because the Lorentz force acts laterally on charge carriers moving in the magnetic field of the same magnet — i.e., the source of the field and the source of the force are co-located in the same object.
* Experimental demonstration: wires rotate in one direction; the disc magnet rotates in the opposite direction. "But the wires are not enough mass to start the disc rotating in the opposite direction yet the disc starts rotating in the opposite direction. Where are the torques interacting? This is not equal in opposite torques." [MDX 13_1966828665024704512]
