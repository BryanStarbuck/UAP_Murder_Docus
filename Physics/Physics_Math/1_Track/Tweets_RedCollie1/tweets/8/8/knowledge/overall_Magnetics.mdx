## 1. Dipole Magnetism vs. Spinning Magnetism: The Foundational Distinction

The author explicitly contrasts two regimes:

### 1a. Dipole (Static) Magnetism — Classical
- Two stationary magnets, one oriented N-S, the other S-N, produce equal and opposite repulsive forces: F_down on one side, F_up on the other.
- Net force on a test object: repulsion along the axis connecting the poles.
- This is the magnetism "taught in schools" and constitutes the classical dipole field described by the Biot-Savart law and the magnetostatic Maxwell equations.
- The field is described by the magnetic scalar potential Φ_m satisfying ∇²Φ_m = 0 in source-free regions, with the dipole term dominant at r >> d (separation d).

### 1b. Spinning Magnetism — Novel Regime
- A permanent magnet placed in the field of a three-phase wire coil array (UVW phasing) is set into rotation.
- The rotating magnetic dipole of the spinning permanent magnet interacts with the rotating field of the three-phase coil array in a regime not reducible to quasi-static dipole approximations.
- The interaction is time-varying at the rotation frequency f (Hz) and its harmonics.
- At low f (≲15 Hz): force behavior is approximately Newtonian; the magnet exhibits net up or net down force consistent with dipole-dipole coupling. Forces as large as 1000 grams (≈9.8 N) are reported in this regime.
- At high f (25–30 Hz): non-Newtonian force behavior emerges. A strong upward force (≈1 kg) appears, accompanied by an approximately equal downward force from a different mechanism ("magnetic recoil"), resulting in near-zero net weight change on a scale unless the rod is forcibly held down.

### 1c. Polarity Independence at High Frequency
- Experimental result: at high spin frequency, both dipole polarities of the permanent magnet (N-S and S-N relative to the downward gravity vector) produce identical behavior.
- Both directions of rotation (clockwise and anti-clockwise) produce identical behavior.
- This polarity and chirality independence is characteristic of the high-frequency (non-Newtonian) domain and has no counterpart in classical static dipole magnetism.

## 2. Three-Phase Rotating Magnetic Field Configuration

### 2a. Standard 3-Phase Configuration (UVW)
- Three wire coils labeled U, V, W, energized with AC power at phase offsets: 0°, 120°, 240° respectively.
- This produces a continuously rotating magnetic field in the plane of the coil array — the same principle underlying the three-phase induction motor invented by Nikola Tesla.
- The rotating field vector B(t) traces a circle in the coil plane at angular frequency ω = 2πf, where f is the supply frequency in Hz.
- The field magnitude is constant: |B(t)| = B₀ (ideal balanced three-phase supply).

### 2b. Double-Layer Staggered Configuration
- Two sets of three-phase coils, spatially staggered relative to each other, arranged in two tiers (one above, one below the rotor plane).
- The crop-circle diagram (2009) referenced by the author shows: 3 wire coils on Level 1 + 3 wire coils on Level 2 (staggered) + spinning magnet in the middle.
- Physical model built: 70 mm permanent magnet with 14 mm central hole, seated on a 6.3 mm aluminum rod; three coils below, three above, six total.
- Purpose of staggered arrangement: to produce a more axially symmetric rotating field environment around the spinning rotor magnet, potentially increasing the coupling efficiency and the ratio of upward to downward force.

### 2c. Six-Coil Configuration (U-V-W-U-V-W)
- A ring of 6 wire coils driven as U-V-W-U-V-W produces two counter-rotating spin directions simultaneously:
  - Clockwise rotation inside the ring.
  - Anti-clockwise rotation outside the ring.
- The author states this dual counter-rotation is "essential (apparently) for generating UFO flight."
- Mechanism proposed: the inner clockwise field couples to the spinning rotor magnet to produce upward lift; the outer anti-clockwise field cancels the "magnetic recoil" that would otherwise prevent net levitation.

### 2d. Five-Phase Configuration
- Five wire coils at equal angular separations (72° apart), driven with five-phase AC power (0°, 72°, 144°, 216°, 288° phase offsets).
- Produces a smoother rotating field envelope than three-phase (fifth-order harmonics are suppressed relative to three-phase).
- Referenced in the context of the "Buga orb-sphere" — a physical UAP artifact with five wire coils (described as "lunar crescents") embedded in a thick aluminum spherical shell.
- Also referenced as a "5-bladed space propeller" in crop-circle diagrams.
- The five-phase geometry means five-fold rotational symmetry, producing a rotating magnetic field that more closely approximates a pure rotating dipole (circular polarization of B) than three-phase.

## 3. Magnetic Recoil: The Key Barrier to Net Levitation

### 3a. Operational Definition
- "Magnetic recoil" is the downward force on the external coil/magnet assembly that accompanies the upward force on the spinning rotor magnet, by Newton's third law applied to the magnetic coupling.
- Analogized to: the rise of an aluminum disc over a wire coil with AC power (Lenz's law induction lifting); or the rise of a disc over a ring of spinning N-S magnets in a commercial hoverboard.
- In both analogies, the device producing the repulsive upward field itself experiences an equal downward reaction force.

### 3b. Mass Recoil
- A second distinct downward force term, labeled "mass recoil," attributed to the inertial reaction of the spinning permanent magnet as it accelerates upward under the magnetic driving force.
- Net effect: the spinning magnet given 3-phase power creates both a mass recoil term and a magnetic recoil term, both oriented downward, nearly canceling the upward force on the scale.

### 3c. Proposed Cancellation Mechanism
- A liquid, self-excited dynamo (using mercury, galinstan, or liquid gallium) is proposed to cancel both recoil terms.
- Hypothesis: The spinning conducting liquid, when it reaches a dynamo instability threshold, generates its own magnetic field that partially opposes the external field in a way that reduces the recoil without reducing the upward force.
- This is an open research question; no successful demonstration is reported in these sources.

## 4. Homopolar Motor / Generator Physics

### 4a. Device Description
- Faraday disc geometry: an inverted stainless steel bowl (conducting disc) rotating on a central axis, with electrical contact made via the central hub and the outer rim.
- Demonstrated by "Roobert33" (YouTube): two copper bus bars connected to the rim and hub, with DC current flow.
- Operation as motor: current flows radially (from hub to rim or vice versa) in the presence of an axial magnetic field, producing a tangential Lorentz force F = IL × B, rotating the disc.
- Operation as generator: rotation of the conducting disc in an axial field induces an EMF ε = ½ω B R² (for a disc of radius R rotating at angular frequency ω in axial field B) — the homopolar EMF.

### 4b. Role in UAP Energy System
- The author identifies the homopolar generator not as the anti-gravity component but as the "energy recovery system" of a UAP orb.
- The spinning conducting mass of the UAP (the main flywheel or rotor) generates DC power via the homopolar effect, which is then used to drive the 3-phase coils that maintain the rotation and produce lift.
- This constitutes a partially self-sustaining loop (not over-unity, but energy-efficient by recovering kinetic energy of the rotor into electrical power for the drive coils).

## 5. MHD / Lorentz Force Fluid Drive

### 5a. Device Description
- Flat copper coil (15–18 cm outer diameter) at the bottom of a clear acrylic container, with a central copper rod/electrode (~1 cm diameter) running vertically through the coil center.
- Conductive liquid (mercury, or galinstan) fills the container.
- Current supply: 90 A DC through the circuit (from the outer ring electrode to the central rod, or vice versa).
- Result: the radial current density J (flowing radially between rod and ring in the conductive liquid) crossed with the axial magnetic field B (generated by the current loop itself, plus any external magnet) produces a tangential Lorentz force: F = J × B, driving the liquid into vortex rotation.

### 5b. Physics
- The Lorentz force on the fluid: **f** = **J** × **B** (force per unit volume, in N/m³)
- For radial current density J_r and axial field B_z: f_θ = J_r × B_z (tangential force, driving circular flow)
- At 90 A in the demonstrated geometry, visible spiral vortex rotation is produced.
- The vortex spirals outward from the central rod to the perimeter, consistent with J_r flowing outward and B_z pointing upward (or inward current with downward B).

### 5c. Galinstan and Liquid Gallium Behavior
- Galinstan (gallium-indium-tin alloy, liquid at room temperature): demonstrated under Lorentz force drive (tweet 1995658795968827493: "The Lorentz force spin of galinstan").
- Liquid gallium: demonstrated being set into spin by rotating N-S magnets below. The liquid gallium swirls into a vortex over the spinning magnet array, forming a disc shape (tweet 13_2014994659647590400_video.mdx: "liquid gallium being reduced to spin by the magnets below and draw a big disc like shape").
- Both galinstan and gallium behave analogously to aluminum in the solid state (eddy current coupling and Lorentz force coupling), but in liquid form their response is fluidic (vortex formation rather than disc repulsion).
