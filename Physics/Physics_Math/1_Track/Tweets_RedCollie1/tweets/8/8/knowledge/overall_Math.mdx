## 2. Rotating Magnetic Field Equations

### 2a. Three-Phase Field (UVW, 120° spacing)
For three coils at spatial positions θ = 0°, 120°, 240° carrying currents:
I_U(t) = I₀ cos(ωt)
I_V(t) = I₀ cos(ωt − 2π/3)
I_W(t) = I₀ cos(ωt − 4π/3)

Resultant rotating field in the coil plane:
**B**(t) = B₀ [cos(ωt) x̂ + sin(ωt) ŷ]

where ω = 2πf (angular frequency, rad/s), f = frequency in Hz.

Magnitude: |**B**| = B₀ = constant (balanced supply)
Phase: arg(**B**) = ωt (linearly increasing — the field rotates at angular rate ω)

### 2b. Five-Phase Field (72° spacing)
For five coils at angular offsets 0°, 72°, 144°, 216°, 288°:
I_k(t) = I₀ cos(ωt − 2πk/5),  k = 0, 1, 2, 3, 4

Resultant:
**B**(t) = (5/2) (μ₀ N I₀ / 2R) [cos(ωt) x̂ + sin(ωt) ŷ]  [for coils of N turns, radius R]

The five-phase field has identical rotating-field structure to three-phase but reduces the 5th-harmonic torque ripple:
ΔT_5ph / T₀ < ΔT_3ph / T₀

### 2c. Six-Coil Ring (U-V-W-U-V-W)
A ring of 6 coils phased as U-V-W-U-V-W produces two spatial harmonics of the rotating field:
- Fundamental (p=1 pole pairs): rotates clockwise inside the ring at ω
- Second harmonic (p=2 pole pairs): rotates anti-clockwise outside the ring at ω (in the reference frame of the fundamental)

Mathematical decomposition via spatial Fourier series: for 6 coils at angular positions φ_k = kπ/3 (k=0..5) with phases U,V,W,U,V,W:
B_spatial(φ, t) = B₁ cos(φ − ωt) + B₂ cos(2φ + ωt) + higher harmonics

The cos(φ − ωt) term is the clockwise-inside rotating field.
The cos(2φ + ωt) term is the anti-clockwise-outside counter-rotating field.

## 3. Homopolar Generator EMF

EMF of Faraday disc (radius R, axial field B_z, angular velocity ω):
ε = ½ ω B_z R²

Derivation: for a radial conductor element at radius r moving with tangential velocity v = ωr in field B_z:
dε = (v × B_z) · dr = ωr B_z dr
ε = ∫₀^R ωr B_z dr = ½ ω B_z R²

Power output at load resistance R_L:
P = ε² / (R_internal + R_L)

## 4. Gyroscopic Precession

Angular momentum:
**L** = I **ω**_spin

For solid disc: I = ½mR²
For hollow cylinder (shell approximation for UAP flywheel): I = mR²

Applied torque **τ**:
**τ** = d**L**/dt

For **τ** perpendicular to **L** (gyroscopic case):
Precession rate ω_p = |**τ**| / |**L**|

Vertical lift force from precession (gyroscopic anti-gravity):
When horizontal torque τ_H acts on spinning body with angular momentum L = Iω_s:
The precession converts τ_H into a vertical angular velocity ω_p (about axis perpendicular to both τ_H and L)
The associated vertical force F_z = ω_p × L_horizontal_component

Key relationship: larger L (higher spin rate ω_s or larger moment of inertia I) → lower precession rate for same applied torque → but the force F_z scales as:
F_z ~ τ_H (independent of L in first approximation)
The direction of F_z depends on the chirality of spin and the direction of applied τ_H (hence the polarity and direction independence observed at high frequency).

## 5. Lorentz Force on Conducting Fluid

Force per unit volume:
**f** = **J** × **B**  (N/m³)

For radial current density J_r (A/m²) in axial field B_z:
f_θ = J_r B_z  (tangential force → circular vortex)

Total tangential force on fluid column of height h and inner radius r₁, outer radius r₂:
F_θ = ∫_{r₁}^{r₂} ∫₀^h J_r(r) B_z(r) · 2πr h dr

For uniform J_r = I / (2πr h) (total current I distributed over the cross-section):
F_θ = (I · B_z / 2π) ∫_{r₁}^{r₂} (1/r) · 2πr dr = I B_z (r₂ - r₁)

This drives the fluid in circular motion. The demonstrated current is I = 90 A.

## 6. Torque on Magnetic Dipole in Applied Field

Torque **τ** on dipole **m** in field **B**:
**τ** = **m** × **B**

For dipole **m** = m_z ẑ in rotating field **B** = B₀[cos(ωt) x̂ + sin(ωt) ŷ]:
**τ** = m_z B₀ [-sin(ωt) x̂ + cos(ωt) ŷ]

This is a rotating torque vector in the XY-plane with magnitude:
|**τ**| = m_z B₀ = constant

At synchronous rotation (magnet spin = field rotation), the torque is zero (ideal synchronous motor, no load). Under load (upward force), a slip angle δ develops:
|**τ**| = m_z B₀ sin(δ)

Power transferred: P = **τ** · **ω**_spin = m_z B₀ ω_spin sin(δ)
