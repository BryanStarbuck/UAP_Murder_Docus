## 1. Classical Electrodynamics Background (Baseline Assumed)

### 1a. Lorentz Force (Core to Multiple Experiments)
**F** = q(**E** + **v** × **B**)

For a volume element of conducting fluid with current density **J**:
**f** = **J** × **B**  (force per unit volume, N/m³)

Applied in:
- MHD fluid vortex experiments (mercury, galinstan, liquid gallium)
- Homopolar motor operation
- Eddy-current levitation of aluminum disc over AC coil

### 1b. Biot-Savart Law (Source of B from Current Loops)
**B**(**r**) = (μ₀/4π) ∫ (I d**l** × r̂) / r²

For the three-phase coil array: each coil contributes a term; the vector sum at the rotor position produces the rotating field **B**(t).

### 1c. Faraday Induction (Homopolar Generator)
ε = -dΦ_B/dt

For a homopolar (Faraday disc) generator with disc of radius R rotating at angular velocity ω in axial field B_z:
ε = ½ ω B_z R²

This is the open-circuit EMF. The disc acts as a unipolar source of DC EMF without commutation.

### 1d. Lenz's Law (Aluminum Disc Levitation — Baseline Reference)
When an AC coil produces a time-varying B through an aluminum disc, induced eddy currents in the disc create a magnetic field opposing the change (Lenz's law), producing a repulsive force:
F_rep ∝ (σ t²) (dB/dt)²  [where σ = conductivity, t = disc thickness]

This is classical levitation — referenced as the "magnetic recoil" analogy.

## 2. Three-Phase Rotating Field Physics

### 2a. Rotating Field Vector
For balanced three-phase supply with amplitude B₀ and angular frequency ω:
B_x(t) = B₀ cos(ωt)
B_y(t) = B₀ cos(ωt − 2π/3)    [120° phase delay for V-phase]
B_z(t) = B₀ cos(ωt − 4π/3)    [240° phase delay for W-phase]

The resultant field in the coil plane rotates as a vector of constant magnitude B₀ at angular frequency ω:
**B**(t) = B₀ [cos(ωt) x̂ + sin(ωt) ŷ]   (for UVW → rotating in the xy-plane)

### 2b. Torque on Spinning Permanent Magnet in Rotating Field
The torque on a magnetic dipole **m** in an applied field **B**:
**τ** = **m** × **B**

When the rotating field **B**(t) leads the magnet's dipole **m** by angle δ (slip angle), the torque drives the magnet to synchronize with the field rotation — this is the synchronous motor principle.

At synchrony (no slip): the magnet rotates at exactly ω = 2πf, and the torque is zero (purely reactive). Under load (upward force extraction), a slip angle δ develops, and the torque T = m B₀ sin(δ) provides the mechanical power.

### 2c. Five-Phase Extension
For five-phase supply with amplitude B₀ and angular frequency ω (phases at 0°, 72°, 144°, 216°, 288°):
**B**(t) = B₀ [cos(ωt) x̂ + sin(ωt) ŷ]

Identical rotating field envelope to three-phase, but with five coils the torque ripple (deviation from constant torque) is reduced because the fifth-harmonic terms cancel more completely. Five-fold spatial symmetry produces a smoother magnetic environment for the rotor.

## 3. Gyroscope Physics: Horizontal Force → Vertical Displacement

### 3a. Angular Momentum of Spinning Disc
**L** = I **ω**_spin

where I = ½mR² (for a solid disc of mass m and radius R) and **ω**_spin is the spin angular velocity vector along the disc axis.

### 3b. Gyroscopic Precession
Applied horizontal torque **τ**_H perpendicular to **L**:
**τ**_H = d**L**/dt

The rate of precession ω_p about the vertical axis:
ω_p = τ_H / |**L**| = τ_H / (I ω_spin)

### 3c. Vertical Displacement Mechanism (Observed)
- When the spinning disc (rotating away from observer) is touched from the side (horizontal torque applied), the blue side (top) rises — i.e., the torque vector is rotated 90° by the angular momentum to produce a vertical rotation.
- The conversion: horizontal input torque **τ**_H → vertical output angular velocity ω_V (about an axis 90° from the applied force direction).
- Author states: "A horizontal rotation gets shifted to a vertical rotation about an axis 90 degrees away from where the force was applied."

### 3d. Required External Force
- Without spin: a horizontal push simply rotates the disc in the horizontal plane; no vertical lift.
- With spin: the same horizontal push produces vertical lift (the gyroscopic precession converts horizontal torque to vertical angular velocity).
- Author explicitly states: "We require an outside force to make it lift up."
- This is identified as the gyroscopic anti-gravity principle applicable to internally-spinning UFOs: "internally-spinning UFOs can lift up, by pushing against the empty vacuum of space."

## 4. Horizontal and Vertical Dynamics of the Spinning Magnet Experiment

### 4a. Vertical Force Balance at Low Frequency (Newtonian Regime)
Net vertical force F_net = F_up − F_down − Mg

where:
- F_up = upward magnetic force from rotating field coupling
- F_down = downward dipole reaction force (magnetic recoil)
- Mg = weight of rotor assembly

At low f (~10–15 Hz): F_up ≈ F_down, so F_net ≈ −Mg (net downward; scale reads normal weight or heavier).

### 4b. Vertical Force Balance at High Frequency (Non-Newtonian Regime)
At high f (25–30 Hz):
F_up >> F_up(low f)   [non-Newtonian upward force emerges]
F_down ≈ F_up − ε    [a nearly equal downward term appears from "magnetic recoil" and "mass recoil"]

Net force: F_net ≈ ε (small net upward force)
Scale reading: approximately zero net weight change unless the rod is forcibly depressed.

Forcibly depressing the rod at 30 Hz: resistance force > 1 kg downward push required → implies F_up ≈ 1 kg upward.

### 4c. Mass Recoil Term
When a spinning body of mass m accelerates upward with acceleration a_z:
F_mass_recoil = m a_z (downward on the driving coil assembly by Newton's third law)

This term accompanies the upward acceleration of the rotor and adds to the magnetic recoil to produce the near-cancellation observed on the scale.

### 4d. Stabilization by Silicone Bead

- The bead prevents the magnet from flying off while allowing the upward force to be measured as pressure on the bead.

## 5. XY-Plane Interactions and Their Effect on the Z-Axis

### 5a. Rotating Field in the XY-Plane
The three-phase coil array produces a field that rotates in the XY-plane (horizontal plane) at frequency f. The spinning permanent magnet, aligned along the Z-axis (vertical), couples to this rotating XY field.

The torque on the rotor's magnetic dipole **m** = m ẑ in the rotating field **B**(t) = B₀[cos(ωt) x̂ + sin(ωt) ŷ]:
**τ** = **m** × **B** = m ẑ × B₀[cos(ωt) x̂ + sin(ωt) ŷ]
     = m B₀ [cos(ωt)(ẑ × x̂) + sin(ωt)(ẑ × ŷ)]
     = m B₀ [cos(ωt) ŷ − sin(ωt) x̂]

This is a rotating torque in the XY-plane, which at synchronous spin speed becomes steady torque (the synchronous motor driving torque).

### 5b. Z-Axis Force Generation Mechanism (Non-Newtonian)
The emergence of a net Z-axis force at high frequency is the key non-classical observation. The author does not provide a full mathematical derivation but proposes it arises from:
- The same gyroscopic coupling mechanism by which horizontal torque (XY-plane) converts to vertical displacement (Z-axis).
- At high spin frequency, the angular momentum |**L**| = I ω_spin is large enough that the conversion efficiency of XY-torque to Z-displacement is significantly enhanced.
- This is an analogy to the gyroscopic precession equation: ω_p = τ_H / |**L**|; as |**L**| increases (higher spin frequency), the precession rate for a given applied torque decreases, but the associated vertical force increases because the torque is being "stored" in the angular momentum rather than dissipated as heat.
