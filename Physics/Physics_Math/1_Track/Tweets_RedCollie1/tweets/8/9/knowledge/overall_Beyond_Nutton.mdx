# Beyond Newtonian Mechanics: Non-Classical Claims and Experimental Evidence

## 1. Claimed Violation of Newton's Third Law via Counter-Spin

### The Claim

Newton's Third Law (lex tertia): For every action force F_{AB} exerted by body A on body B,
there exists an equal and opposite reaction force F_{BA} = -F_{AB} exerted by body B on body A.

In electromagnetic propulsion: The upward electromagnetic force on the spinning magnet (body A)
is produced by the drive coils (body B). By Newton's Third Law, the coils must experience an
equal and opposite downward force.

Drew's claimed exception (tweets 1927937215071670353, 2039489935842156754):
* Spin the central magnet CW + outer ring of magnets CCW
* The counter-rotation configuration eliminates the downward back-reaction on the drive coils
* Net result: 200 g upward force on magnet with zero (or reduced) downward force on coils

Drew's exact statement:
"We're going to see if that changes Newton's third law so that upward 200 grams of force can
become purely upward force like for UFO without as much or any downward force due to Newton's
third law of action reaction."

And: "If the rising magnet and pole do not create any downward back-reaction in those six,
3-phase wire coils, then we will have an authentic 'UFO'."

### Physical Context

In classical electrodynamics, Newton's Third Law holds for mechanical forces but can appear
to be violated for electromagnetic forces when field momentum is included. The total momentum
of a system is:
    p_total = p_mechanical + p_EM
where p_EM = ε₀ ∫ (E × B) dV is the electromagnetic field momentum.

If the field momentum p_EM changes during the experiment, the mechanical momenta can appear
non-conserved. This is the hidden field momentum effect (first noted by Furry 1969, Griffiths
and Hnizdo 2013). In a rotating EM configuration, p_EM can be non-zero and can change over
time, transferring momentum between fields and mechanical bodies in non-obvious ways.

Drew's proposal, if successful, would be the first controlled demonstration of using rotating
field momentum to achieve sustained net thrust without reaction mass — well beyond standard
Newtonian mechanics.

## 2. Claimed Absence of Magnetic Dipole in Spinning State

### The Claim

"There is no 'magnetic dipole', as evidenced by both North (red) and South (blue) sides of
the central spinning magnet behaving identically."
(note_tweet 1927937215071670353)

### Analysis

A static permanent magnet has a well-defined dipole moment μ = μ ẑ, producing a field:
    B_dipole(r,θ) = (μ₀/4πr³)[3(μ·r̂)r̂ - μ]
with North and South poles distinguishable by field direction.

When the magnet spins about its own symmetry axis (ẑ) at angular velocity ω:
* In classical electrodynamics, the field of an axially magnetized, axially spinning disc
  is identical to its static field (no time-varying dipole field because the rotation is about
  the symmetry axis — the charge and current distribution is azimuthally symmetric and remains
  so during rotation).
* Therefore classically, the spinning state should NOT eliminate the dipole character.

Drew's experimental observation that North and South sides behave identically (both producing
lift, not one attracting and one repelling) is consistent with a classical interpretation:
the upward force depends on B² or |∂B/∂z| (which is polarity-independent), not on the
signed polarity of the field.

The non-classical interpretation Drew implies: that rapid spin creates a topologically
modified field in which the dipole structure is suppressed. This would require physics beyond
classical magnetostatics — potentially a quantum gyromagnetic or spin-orbit effect at
macroscopic scale.

## 3. QED and QCD Fundamental Error Claim

### The Claim

"As I said, Grok is not intelligent, and does not even realize that QED and QCD are
fundamentally in error (as Dirac said)."
(tweet 1953355302453043415)

### Historical Context

Paul Dirac's critiques of renormalization in QED:
* In his 1963 Scientific American article and in later writings, Dirac stated:
  "This is just not sensible mathematics. And to those who feel that, this is a sign that
  the theory is fundamentally wrong."
* Dirac objected specifically to the subtraction of infinities (renormalization) that is
  required to obtain finite, observable quantities in QED.
* Dirac maintained throughout his career that a mathematically rigorous theory should produce
  finite results from first principles without renormalization.

Drew aligns with the Dirac critique, implying that:
* A correct quantum field theory of EM interactions (corrected QED) would naturally explain
  the anomalous spinning-magnet lift without ad hoc vacuum-coupling postulates.
* The corrections to QED/QCD relevant to his experiments involve the behavior of EM fields
  in rotating frames — an area where QED and GR are notoriously difficult to reconcile.

Relevant physics: The interaction of quantum vacuum fluctuations with rotating systems is
not fully resolved in standard QFT. The Unruh effect (acceleration → thermal bath at
T_U = ℏa/2πck_B) and its rotational analog (rotation → thermal bath at T_rot related to
angular acceleration) are derived results, but their energy transfer implications for
macroscopic rotating magnets remain disputed.

## 4. Magnetic Repulsion vs. Spin-Based Lift: Distinguishing Classical from Non-Classical

### tweet 1915717905691664490 (referenced_tweet 1915359976211833311)

Referenced text: "This is just 'magnetic repulsion' holding it up, but you can see why we
need 'spin'?"

Drew explicitly acknowledges that static magnetic repulsion (standard physics: F = ∇(μ·B))
CAN levitate objects without spin. But he distinguishes this from his proposed spin-based
mechanism:

* Static magnetic levitation: Requires careful pole alignment and is passively stable only
  under specific geometries (Earnshaw's theorem: a static arrangement of charges cannot be
  stable in free space, but can be stable with current loops / diamagnets / spinning bodies).
* Spin-based lift (Drew's claim): Requires rotation above threshold frequency; lift scales
  with ω; stable along the rod axis dynamically.

The key "beyond Newton" claim is that static repulsion requires both North and South poles
to be correctly oriented (repulsion works only when like poles face each other), while
Drew's spin-based lift works regardless of orientation (reversal test confirmed). This
behavioral difference is the empirical distinction Drew claims separates his mechanism from
ordinary magnetic repulsion.

## 6. Galinstan as Non-Classical Rotor: Magnetohydrodynamic Regime

For liquid galinstan driven by a rotating magnetic field (referenced_tweet 2014994939780932026):

The magnetic Reynolds number Rm = μ₀ σ v L determines whether the conducting fluid is in
the MHD (Rm >> 1) or low-Rm (Rm << 1) regime:

For galinstan at v ~ 0.1 m/s, L ~ 0.01 m:
    Rm = (4π×10⁻⁷)(3.46×10⁶)(0.1)(0.01) = 4.35×10⁻³ << 1

At Rm << 1, the magnetic field is not significantly distorted by the fluid flow (low-Rm MHD).
This is the standard regime for liquid metals in industrial applications. The lift force from
galinstan MHD is computable classically from:
    F = σ v × B² × Volume [estimated order of magnitude for body force × volume]

This is within classical electrodynamics. Drew's proposed beyond-classical contribution would
require Rm ~ 1 or higher, achievable only at much higher flow velocities or larger length scales.
