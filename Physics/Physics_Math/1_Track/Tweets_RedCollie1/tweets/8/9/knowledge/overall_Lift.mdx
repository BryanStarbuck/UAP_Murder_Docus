# Lift and Propulsion: Z-Axis Rise Mechanisms and Gravity Claims

## Core Lift Claim

Dr. Horace Drew (RedCollie1) claims that a permanently magnetized disc spinning rapidly about
a vertical axis (Z-axis), driven by three-phase AC magnetic fields, experiences a net upward
force (Z-axis lift) that exceeds the gravitational force on the disc and added payload mass.
This is the primary experimental result reported across this set of tweets.

## Experimental Lift System: Detailed Description

### Physical Configuration

* Aluminum vertical rod: 8 mm diameter, oriented along Z-axis (vertical)
* Disc magnet: 70 mm diameter × ~3 mm thick, 14 mm central bore, mass = 80 g, sitting on and
  free to slide along the rod
* Three coils (UVW) positioned below the magnet, each R ≈ 18 Ω, connected to a
  variable-frequency three-phase power source
* Shaft collars (iron) added above the magnet to vary total payload mass

### Lift Onset Data

The magnet begins rising when the rotating field frequency exceeds a threshold:

    80 g total → rise at ~15 Hz
    120 g total (magnet + 4 shaft collars, 40 g collars) → rise at ~20 Hz
    200 g total (magnet + collars, 120 g collars) → rise at ~25 Hz

The inference from this data: lift force F_z increases monotonically with applied frequency f.
The system must reach a minimum spin rate before sufficient F_z to overcome gravity.

### Reversal Test

Magnet inverted 180° (South where North was and vice versa), total load ~150 g:
* System still rises.
* Drew's interpretation: lift does not depend on magnetic pole orientation; it depends on
  the spin state. "So we know this pushed against the vacuum not to pull itself."

This reversal test rules out a specific-pole-attraction mechanism and is consistent with
either a classical gyroscopic effect OR Drew's claimed vacuum-coupling hypothesis.

## Z-Axis Rise Mechanism: Drew's Proposed Physics

Drew uses multiple phrasings of the mechanism:

1. "Pushing against the vacuum" — analogous to a spinning top or gyroscope that rises when
   spinning fast enough, Drew claims the spinning magnet couples to the quantum vacuum and
   generates a reaction force upward.

2. "Just like a top rises" — comparison to gyroscopic precession. A top spinning at high ω
   precesses about the vertical axis; under gravity, precession is:
       Ω_precession = τ/L = (m g r) / (I ω)
   where r is the distance from pivot to center of mass. This is not upward motion of the
   top itself but precession — so the analogy is imprecise at the classical level. Drew's
   claim is that the spinning magnet rises vertically rather than merely precessing.

3. "Anti-gravity" / "UFO levitation" — Drew equates this mechanism with the claimed
   propulsion of UAP craft, framing it as the laboratory-scale demonstration of alien
   anti-gravity technology.

The vacuum-coupling claim specifically:
   "A spinning magnet will rise up by pushing against the vacuum"
   The quantum vacuum (ZPF, zero-point field) has energy density:
       ρ_vac = ½ ħ ∫₀^{ω_cutoff} ω³ / (2π²c³) dω   [divergent without cutoff]
   A rotating EM field pattern could in principle couple to vacuum fluctuations (analogous to
   dynamical Casimir effect or Unruh effect), but no quantitative mechanism is provided by Drew.

## XY-Plane Interactions Causing Z-Axis Lift

The mechanism by which horizontal field rotation (XY-plane) produces vertical lift (Z-axis):

Step 1: Three-phase coils UVW produce a rotating horizontal field B_h(t) at angular frequency ω.

Step 2: B_h(t) exerts torque τ = μ × B_h on the disc magnet's axial moment μ, spinning the
disc about the Z-axis.

Step 3: Once spinning at angular velocity ω_disc, the disc generates its own time-varying
field pattern. Drew claims this spinning field state, not the static dipole field, is what
produces the Z-axis force.

Step 4: The force F_z manifests. In classical induction motor theory, there is no net Z-axis
force for a free rotor on a frictionless guide rod — the only forces are the torque (rotational)
and any induced axial force due to field gradient in Z. If the coils are positioned below the
magnet and the field gradient ∂B_z/∂z > 0 below the magnet, then the paramagnetic force:
    F_z = μ · ∂B_z/∂z   [force on magnetic dipole in non-uniform field]
is upward. This IS a classical explanation for the observed rise, and Drew's system is
consistent with this interpretation for the initial lift.

However, Drew claims the magnitude of the observed lift exceeds what field-gradient paramagnetic
force alone can account for, and that the reversal test eliminates the static-dipole
field-gradient explanation. He attributes the excess to the vacuum-coupling mechanism.

are used interchangeably.

   enabling propulsion without reaction mass. This is the threshold claim for "UFO physics."
