# Magnetic Field Content: Theory and Applied

## Apparatus Configurations Observed/Described

### 1. Ring Homopolar Motor (tweet 1915717905691664490 + video mdx 13_1915717464387969024)

Configuration:
* Large toroidal/ring ferrite permanent magnet, approximately 20–25 cm outer diameter,
  3–4 cm thick, 8–10 cm inner bore diameter.
* Magnet mounted on a yellow/gold mounting block.
* Thin metal shaft (8 mm stainless steel rod, by analogy to the levitation experiment)
  passing through the center bore axially.
* Two brushes added diametrically across the shaft/disc to provide electrical contact at
  center and rim.
* Red wire connects to external DC circuit.

Physics: This is the Faraday disc / homopolar motor configuration.
* The ring magnet produces an axial (Z-direction) magnetic field B_z through the bore.
* When DC current I is driven radially through a conducting disc rotating in B_z, the
  Lorentz force F = IL × B acts azimuthally, producing torque τ = r × (IL × B_z), causing
  the disc to spin.
* Alternatively, when the disc is spun mechanically, it generates a radial EMF:
  EMF = (1/2) ω B_z (r_outer² - r_inner²)
  where ω is angular velocity, r_outer and r_inner are outer and inner radii of the
  conducting disc. This is the unipolar induction (homopolar generator) regime.

Drew's statement (video transcription): "This is a ring homopolomotor and when I add two
brushes across the diameter like this, it spins."

Mechanism: DC current flows radially; Lorentz force F = qv × B acts azimuthally on charge
carriers, producing continuous rotation without commutation (hence "unipolar" — no polarity
reversal needed because field direction is constant along the rotation axis).

### 2. Three-Phase Magnetic Levitation of Spinning Disc Magnet (tweet 2039489935842156754
   and associated video 13_2039489617280507904)

Configuration:
* Three wire coils arranged below the magnet, labeled UVW (three-phase notation).
* Each coil: approximately 18 Ω resistance.
* Disc magnet: 70 mm diameter, 14 mm central hole (bore), approximately 3 mm thick.
* Magnet mass: 80 g.
* Vertical aluminum rod: 8 mm diameter stainless steel (aluminum rod stated in text for
  lighter coil variant).
* The magnet sits on and slides along the vertical rod.

Magnetic field geometry:
* Three coils UVW driven at 120° phase offset produce a rotating magnetic field in the
  horizontal plane (XY-plane rotation of B vector).
* The rotating B field interacts with the permanent magnet's axial dipole moment μ,
  producing a torque that spins the magnet at the field rotation frequency (below magnetic
  slip → synchronous operation regime) or below it (asynchronous/induction regime).
* Once spinning at sufficient ω, a net upward force F_z develops along the rod axis (Z-axis).

Observed parametric data (experimental results, treated as empirical):

| Total mass (magnet + collars) | Applied frequency | Observed behavior |
|-------------------------------|-------------------|-------------------|
| 80 g (magnet only)            | ~15 Hz            | Begins to rise    |
| 120 g (magnet + 4 shaft collars ~40 g) | ~20 Hz  | Rises             |
| 200 g (magnet 80 g + shaft collars 120 g) | ~25 Hz | Rises          |
| ~150 g (magnet reversed 180°) | Not specified     | Still rises       |

The reversal test: "We have turned the magnet over about 180 degrees. So any mechanical
force could push it downward. Now we've still got about 150 grams on top of the magnet.
And it still flies off. So we know this pushed against the vacuum not to pull itself."

Drew's interpretation: reversal eliminates any purely mechanical rod-friction or air-drag
explanation, establishing that the upward force is magnetic/field-mediated, not mechanical.

Force balance analysis (classical framing):
* F_magnetic_upward ≥ m_total × g
* At 15 Hz, F_up ≥ 0.080 kg × 9.81 m/s² = 0.785 N
* At 20 Hz, F_up ≥ 0.120 × 9.81 = 1.177 N
* At 25 Hz, F_up ≥ 0.200 × 9.81 = 1.962 N

Efficiency concern noted by Drew: total coil mass ~1 kg, lifted mass ~200 g. Proposed
resolution: switch copper coils to aluminum (lower mass density, 2.70 g/cm³ vs 8.96 g/cm³
for copper) with higher-temperature insulation to allow higher power dissipation and thus
higher field strength at lower total coil mass.

## XY-Plane Dynamics (Horizontal Field Rotation)

* The three-phase coil system UVW, powered at mutual 120° phase offset, produces a field
  vector that rotates continuously in the horizontal (XY) plane.
* B_x(t) = B₀ cos(ωt)
* B_y(t) = B₀ cos(ωt - 2π/3) [for standard 3-phase 120° offset]
* B_z component from the permanent magnet itself (axial, aligned with rod axis)
* The rotating horizontal B field acts on the permanent magnet's moment to produce spin
  torque (XY-plane torque → Z-axis angular momentum growth).

## Z-Axis Dynamics (Vertical / Lift)

* Once the permanent magnet achieves sufficient angular velocity ω_z about the Z-axis, the
  upward force F_z develops.
* Drew's claim: F_z = f(ω_z, B_magnet, B_coil, m) where the dependence on ω_z is
  supralinear — heavier loads require higher frequencies (15 Hz for 80 g, 20 Hz for 120 g,
  25 Hz for 200 g).
* The scaling approximately follows: ω_threshold ∝ m^(1/2) based on the three data points,
  though Drew does not state an explicit functional form.

## Lorentz Force as Spin Mechanism

Drew states explicitly (note_tweet, 1966850678472753648):
"There are only two ways to make something spin: by the Lorentz force and/or by 3-phase power."

The Lorentz force in the homopolar motor context:
F = q(E + v × B)

For a current-carrying conductor of length L in field B:
dF = I(dL × B)

Integrated over the disc radius, this produces the azimuthal torque that drives rotation in
the DC homopolar motor without needing commutation.

## Claim: No Magnetic Dipole in Spinning State

Drew asserts (note_tweet 1927937215071670353) that when the magnet spins rapidly, both the
North and South poles behave identically — i.e., the spinning state destroys the static dipole
asymmetry. In classical electrodynamics, a spinning magnetized sphere or disc does produce
modified field structure:
* The Barnett effect (spinning uncharged body acquires magnetization proportional to ω) is
  the classical analogue but acts in the opposite direction.
* A spinning magnetic dipole radiates at the spin frequency (classical rotating dipole
  radiation).
* Drew's claim that "there is no magnetic dipole" in the spinning state is non-standard and
  contradicts classical magnetostatics for any finite spin rate well below relativistic.
  It is an experimental claim to be tested.
