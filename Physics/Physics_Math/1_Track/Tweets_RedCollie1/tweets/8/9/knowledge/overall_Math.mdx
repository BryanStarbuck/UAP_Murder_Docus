# Mathematical Content: Equations, Components, Relationships

## 1. Homopolar Generator EMF

For a conducting disc rotating at angular velocity ω in a uniform axial magnetic field B_z
(produced by the toroidal ring magnet in the experimental setup):

    EMF = (1/2) ω B_z (r₂² - r₁²)

Components:
    ω   = angular velocity of disc [rad/s]
    B_z = axial magnetic flux density [T]
    r₂  = outer radius of conducting disc [m]
    r₁  = inner radius of conducting disc (bore radius) [m]
    EMF = open-circuit terminal voltage [V]

Derivation: The radial velocity of a point at radius r on the spinning disc is v = ωr.
The motional EMF on a charge carrier at that point is dε = (v × B_z) · dr = ω r B_z dr.
Integrating from r₁ to r₂:
    EMF = ∫_{r₁}^{r₂} ω r B_z dr = ω B_z [r²/2]_{r₁}^{r₂} = (1/2) ω B_z (r₂² - r₁²)

This is the Faraday disc / homopolar generator EMF, classical result (Faraday, 1831).

## 2. Lorentz Force on Current-Carrying Conductor

    F = I L × B     [vector form, for straight conductor]
    dF = I (dL × B) [differential form, for arbitrary conductor geometry]

In the homopolar motor geometry:
* I = radial current through disc
* dL = radial element vector dr̂
* B = B_z ẑ (axial field from ring magnet)
* dF = I dr (r̂ × ẑ) = I dr (-φ̂)  [azimuthal force, producing rotation]

The torque element:
    dτ = r × dF = r × I dr (-φ̂) ẑ

Integrated torque:
    τ = I B_z ∫_{r₁}^{r₂} r dr = (1/2) I B_z (r₂² - r₁²)

Note the symmetry with the EMF formula — this is the manifestation of energy conservation
(power = τω = EMF × I).

## 3. Three-Phase Field Decomposition

Three coils at 120° phase offset produce a rotating magnetic field vector. For a balanced
three-phase system with amplitude B₀:

    B_U(t) = B₀ sin(ωt)
    B_V(t) = B₀ sin(ωt - 2π/3) = B₀ sin(ωt - 120°)
    B_W(t) = B₀ sin(ωt - 4π/3) = B₀ sin(ωt - 240°)

Resolving into x,y components (for coils spatially oriented at 0°, 120°, 240° in the
horizontal plane):

    B_x(t) = B_U cos(0°) + B_V cos(120°) + B_W cos(240°)
            = B₀[sin(ωt) - (1/2)sin(ωt-2π/3) - (1/2)sin(ωt-4π/3)]
            = (3/2) B₀ cos(ωt)  [by phasor addition]

    B_y(t) = B_U sin(0°) + B_V sin(120°) + B_W sin(240°)
            = (3/2) B₀ sin(ωt)

Resultant rotating vector:
    B_total(t) = (3/2) B₀ [cos(ωt) x̂ + sin(ωt) ŷ]

Magnitude: |B_total| = (3/2) B₀ = constant
Phase: θ(t) = ωt (continuous rotation at angular frequency ω)

This is the mathematical basis for why 3-phase power creates a smoothly rotating field with
constant magnitude — no pulsation, no dead zones in torque production.

## 4. Torque on Permanent Magnet from Rotating Field

The permanent disc magnet has magnetic moment vector μ (along its axis, ẑ direction when
horizontal field is applied):

## 7. Aluminum vs. Copper Coil Mass Ratio (Engineering Math)

For identical coil geometry (same wire cross-section and length):
    Mass_Al / Mass_Cu = ρ_Al / ρ_Cu = 2.70 / 8.96 = 0.301

Copper coil mass: ~1000 g
Aluminum coil mass at same geometry: ~301 g

Resistance ratio (same geometry, different conductivity):
    R_Al / R_Cu = σ_Cu / σ_Al = (5.96 × 10⁷) / (3.50 × 10⁷) = 1.703

Aluminum coils are ~70% more resistive at same geometry. To maintain the same field B at
same current I (same power dissipation):
    B ∝ N I (for solenoid, N = number of turns, I = current)
    P = I² R
    At fixed P: I = √(P/R)
    B ∝ N √(P/R) → B ∝ √(P σ / geometry)

Aluminum coil produces: B_Al = B_Cu × √(σ_Al/σ_Cu) = B_Cu × √(3.50/5.96) = 0.767 B_Cu

So at same power, aluminum coils produce ~23% less field. With higher-temperature insulation
allowing more power (higher I before melting), this deficit can be overcome. The net gain is
in mass: 1000 g → 301 g coil mass while retaining lift capacity.
