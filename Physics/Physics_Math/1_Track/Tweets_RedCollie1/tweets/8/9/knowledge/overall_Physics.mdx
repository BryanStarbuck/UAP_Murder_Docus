# Physics: Equations, Reasoning Chains, and Non-Classical Claims

## 1. Homopolar Motor / Faraday Disc Physics (Classical Baseline)

The experimental starting point is the ring homopolar motor (video 13_1915717464387969024,
tweet 1915717905691664490).

### Governing Equations

Faraday disc EMF for a conducting disc rotating at angular velocity ω in axial field B_z:

    EMF = ∫_{r_inner}^{r_outer} (v × B) · dr
        = ∫_{r_inner}^{r_outer} (ω r B_z) dr
        = (1/2) ω B_z (r_outer² - r_inner²)

For motor operation (driving current I radially):

    Torque τ = ∫_{r_inner}^{r_outer} r × (I/A)(dr × B_z)

where A is the cross-sectional area of the conductor. The integrated torque becomes:

    τ = I B_z (r_outer² - r_inner²) / 2  [for uniform current density]

The Lorentz force per unit volume on conducting material carrying current density J in field B:

    f = J × B

For radial current density J_r in axial field B_z:
    f_φ = J_r B_z   (azimuthal force component → rotation)
    f_z = 0          (no axial force in purely radial current + axial field geometry)

Key point: in the pure Faraday disc geometry, no Z-axis force (lift) is produced. The
homopolar motor is therefore the classical base from which Drew's claimed anomalous Z-axis
lift departs.

## 2. Three-Phase Rotating Magnetic Field Physics (Classical)

Three coils UVW at 120° spatial offset, driven at 120° temporal phase offset:

    B_U(t) = B₀ cos(ωt)         [spatially at 0°]
    B_V(t) = B₀ cos(ωt - 2π/3) [spatially at 120°]
    B_W(t) = B₀ cos(ωt - 4π/3) [spatially at 240°]

The resultant horizontal field vector magnitude is constant and rotates at angular frequency ω:

    |B_total(t)| = (3/2) B₀ = constant

    θ_field(t) = ωt

This is the standard result for a balanced three-phase winding (Nikola Tesla's rotating
magnetic field, cited by Drew as the inventor).

The rotating field interacts with the axial magnetic moment μ of the permanent disc magnet:

    τ = μ × B_total(t)

This torque, applied continuously as the field rotates, accelerates the magnet to spin at or
near ω (synchronous operation) or below ω (induction/asynchronous operation with slip).

## 3. Spin-Up Dynamics and the Lift-Frequency Relationship

Empirical data from the levitation experiment (video 13_2039489617280507904):

    m_total = 80 g  →  f_threshold ≈ 15 Hz  →  ω_threshold ≈ 94.2 rad/s
    m_total = 120 g →  f_threshold ≈ 20 Hz  →  ω_threshold ≈ 125.7 rad/s
    m_total = 200 g →  f_threshold ≈ 25 Hz  →  ω_threshold ≈ 157.1 rad/s

Dimensional analysis of a hypothetical force law F_z = k ω^α m^β:

At lift threshold, F_z = m g:
    k ω^α m^β = m g
    k ω^α = m^(1-β) g

From the data:
    m₁ = 0.080 kg, ω₁ = 94.2 rad/s
    m₂ = 0.120 kg, ω₂ = 125.7 rad/s
    m₃ = 0.200 kg, ω₃ = 157.1 rad/s

If k ω^α = m^(1-β) g:
    ω₂/ω₁ = (m₂/m₁)^((1-β)/α) = (120/80)^((1-β)/α) = 1.5^((1-β)/α)
    125.7/94.2 = 1.334

    1.5^((1-β)/α) = 1.334
    (1-β)/α = log(1.334)/log(1.5) = 0.288/0.405 = 0.711

Similarly for the third pair:
    ω₃/ω₁ = (200/80)^((1-β)/α) = 2.5^((1-β)/α)
    157.1/94.2 = 1.667
    2.5^((1-β)/α) = 1.667
    (1-β)/α = log(1.667)/log(2.5) = 0.511/0.916 = 0.558

The exponent ratio is not consistent across the three data points, suggesting either

## 4. The Reversal Test: Distinguishing Mechanical vs. Field-Mediated Lift

Drew inverts the magnet 180° and observes that lift is preserved with ~150 g load. His
reasoning:

* If lift were due to mechanical contact forces (rod friction, pneumatic drag pushing up),
  reversing the magnet orientation would not change those forces — they would remain upward.
* If lift were due to a purely mechanical gyroscopic effect (conservation of angular momentum
  of a spinning body), reversal of the magnet would not affect it — the angular momentum L = Iω
  continues in the same direction.
* HOWEVER: if the source of lift were, say, a specific polarity of the magnetic field
  interacting with the driving coils (North pole preferentially pulled up), then reversing
  would push the magnet DOWN, not up. The reversal test shows lift persists → the lift is NOT
  due to a specific pole orientation.

Drew's conclusion: "So we know this pushed against the vacuum not to pull itself."

Physics interpretation: The lift is associated with the spinning state itself (angular
velocity ω, moment of inertia I, and field configuration), not with the polarity orientation
of the static dipole moment. This is consistent with a classical gyroscopic Magnus-type effect
OR with Drew's non-classical vacuum-interaction claim.

## 5. Force Balance and Scaling to Full-Size Propulsion

Drew explicitly addresses the coil-mass vs. lift-mass efficiency problem:

    System mass: ~1000 g (copper coils) + 80 g (magnet) + mechanical structure
    Useful lift at 25 Hz: 200 g (magnet + collars)
    Efficiency: 200 g lift / 1000 g coil mass = 0.20 (below break-even for flight)

Proposed engineering solution:
* Replace copper coils (ρ = 8.96 g/cm³) with aluminum coils (ρ = 2.70 g/cm³):
  mass reduction factor ≈ 8.96/2.70 = 3.32 for identical coil geometry.
  Aluminum coil mass ≈ 1000/3.32 = 301 g.
* Use higher-temperature insulation to allow P = I²R operation at higher current and power
  without conductor degradation → higher B field → higher F_z at same or lower frequency.

This is a standard engineering optimization (material selection + thermal management), with
the goal of achieving mass efficiency η = F_z / (m_total × g) > 1.

## 6. Counter-Spin and Newton's Third Law

Classical statement (Newton's Third Law applied to magnetic propulsion):
For any electromagnetic system producing an upward force F_up on the magnet, there exists
an equal and opposite downward force F_down on the field-producing coils:

    F_up = -F_down   (Newton's Third Law)

This is enforced by conservation of momentum and is not violated in any known classical or
quantum field theory for systems with no expulsion of mass or radiation.

Drew's proposed exception mechanism:
* A counter-rotating outer ring of magnets generates a counter-field that, Drew claims,
  cancels the downward reaction on the coils while preserving the upward force on the
  central magnet.
* The counter-spin is specifically modeled on the Edwin Fuhr (Canada, 1974) UAP observation
  where the craft itself rotated clockwise while the surrounding grass rotated counter-clockwise
  (video 13_1995658911228329985, transcript: "It's going counterclockwise. Grass is moving one
  way and the machine was going the other way.").

Stated test (tweet 1927937215071670353, note_tweet): "If the rising magnet and pole do not
create any downward back-reaction in those six, 3-phase wire coils, then we will have an
authentic 'UFO'."

## 8. Galinstan Spin via 3-Phase Linear Induction (tweet reference 2014994939780932026)

Proposed liquid-metal rotor:
* Galinstan (Ga-In-Sn eutectic, liquid at room temperature, electrical conductivity
  σ ≈ 3.46 × 10⁶ S/m) as a liquid-metal rotor.
* Drive mechanism: 3-phase linear induction motor principle, producing a traveling EM wave:
    B(x,t) = B₀ cos(kx - ωt)
* Induced EMF in the liquid: ε ∝ (dB/dt) × volume
* Induced current density: J = σ E_induced (Ohm's law in the conductor frame)
* Lorentz body force on the liquid: f = J × B (body force per unit volume, drives bulk flow)

Alternatively, Lorentz force ("two crossed lizards" = two mutually perpendicular currents in
crossed B fields):
    F = I₁(L₁ × B) + I₂(L₂ × B)

The "right-hand rule" describes the cross product geometry for force direction.

This is a claim about anomalous astrophysical observations, not a laboratory physics result.

## 10. Spin Mechanisms: Two Fundamental Routes (Drew's Taxonomy)

Drew asserts (note_tweet 1966850678472753648):
"There are only two ways to make something spin: by the Lorentz force and/or by 3-phase power."

Formalization:

Route 1 — Lorentz Force:
    F = q(v × B)
    Integrated as dF = I(dL × B) for current-carrying conductor
    Produces torque τ = r × dF → angular acceleration α = τ/I_moment

Route 2 — 3-Phase Rotating Magnetic Field:
    B_rotating(t) = (3/2)B₀ [cos(ωt) x̂ + sin(ωt) ŷ]
    Torque on magnetic moment: τ = μ × B_rotating
    → Synchronous spin at field frequency ω or asynchronous with slip

These two routes are not independent: the rotating field itself acts via Lorentz forces on
the rotor. Drew's taxonomy distinguishes DC Lorentz-driven rotation (homopolar motor) from
AC 3-phase-driven rotation (induction/synchronous motor). Both are classical EM.
