## 1. Primary Lift Mechanism Claim: Non-Newtonian Spin Phase of Rotating Magnet

Source: 13_1916095047227084800_video.mdx (transcription), 1916095385363230993.yaml (referenced tweet)

The central lift hypothesis of this experimental series is:

A spinning magnet in a 3-phase (or N-phase) rotating magnetic field undergoes a phase transition from Newtonian mechanical behavior to "non-Newtonian" gyroscopic behavior at a critical frequency. In the Newtonian phase, the rotating field screw-drives the magnet downward, creating a reaction upward force on the support structure (increasing scale weight). In the non-Newtonian phase, the magnet behaves as a gyroscope ("top"), decoupling from the axial gradient force — and the author asserts that in this phase, upward force is somehow generated by interaction with the field geometry without a reaction on the support structure.

Exact quote from referenced tweet (1916093709445194077): "If we could just CAPTURE as much upward force from the non-Newtonian phase, as from the Newtonian phase, then the great problem of 'UFO propulsion' would be [solved]."

Critical evaluation: In the non-Newtonian (gyroscopic) phase as demonstrated, the scale reads ~0 g — meaning the apparatus as a whole does NOT generate net upward force; rather, it stops generating downward force on the scale. The scale drop from ~250 g to ~0 g does not represent lift generation; it represents cessation of the mechanical-screw downward push. The transition is NOT a demonstration of net upward force (antigravity or lift exceeding weight). The author acknowledges this gap ("if we could just CAPTURE...") — the current experiment does not demonstrate lift, only the removal of downward force.

## 2. Z-Axis Rise Mechanism — Detailed Analysis

Source: 13_1916095047227084800_video.mdx

The rod (and attached disc magnet) physically RISES in both regimes:

Newtonian regime Z-motion: The rotating field's helical gradient drives the disc magnet toward the lower repulsive magnet; the rod is pushed UP against the steel-beam mechanical stop. The magnet tries to go DOWN but pushes the rod UP via the mechanical coupling. This is purely Newtonian: force balance between helical field force (pushing magnet down) and rod/stop (constraining magnet down motion, transmitting reaction force upward).

Non-Newtonian (top/gyroscope) regime Z-motion: The magnet RISES OFF the upper stop (steel beam) and floats. The rod drops. The magnet is now levitated in place by the combination of:
1. The lower repulsive magnet (provides baseline upward magnetic force)
2. The rotating field (provides azimuthal torque keeping the magnet spinning)
3. Gyroscopic stabilization (prevents the magnet from tipping / falling off the repulsive equilibrium point)

This levitation IS occurring in the non-Newtonian regime — the magnet rises off the stop and is magnetically levitated in the air above the repulsive lower magnet. However, this levitation is passive magnetic + gyroscopic, not a new propulsive force; the magnet's weight is supported by the magnetic repulsion from below, not by any new principle.

## 3. XY-Plane Interactions Causing Altitude Change — Gyroscopic Precession

Source: 13_1916095047227084800_video.mdx, 1916095385363230993.yaml

When the spinning magnet is in the gyroscopic regime:
* Angular momentum vector L = I * omega (along spin axis, approximately vertical for a disc magnet spinning about its symmetry axis)
* The rotating field exerts a torque tau in the horizontal plane (attempting to track the field)
* This torque, applied to the angular momentum vector L, causes PRECESSION in the vertical plane (Euler's equation: dL/dt = tau)
* Precession in vertical plane changes the tilt angle of the spin axis, which changes the axial projection of the magnetic dipole moment
* This in turn changes the force between the spinning magnet and the lower repulsive magnet (force depends on axial separation AND relative orientation of dipole)

In this way, the XY-plane dynamics (rotating field torque) couple to the Z-axis position of the magnet through gyroscopic precession — a fully classical Euler-equation effect. The transition in Z-axis behavior (rise vs. no rise) is not a new physics effect but a consequence of gyroscopic decoupling from the axial gradient force.

## 4. Pole-Ball Device — Spherical Lift Architecture

Source: 1923735136211959909.yaml

"I was shown this 'pole-ball' device for UFO levitation in a dream months ago."

Concept: a sphere (diameter unspecified) with internal coils arranged around an internal pole structure. The sphere levitates by operating as a self-contained motor: the internal coils create a rotating field that interacts with the pole structure (or with an outer conducting shell) to produce rotation of the sphere itself. Rotation of the sphere body generates gyroscopic angular momentum that — combined with the internal magnetic configuration — is claimed to produce lift.

This is analogous to the NASA EM Drive controversy or to inertial propulsion claims, but using a spinning magnetic body rather than microwave resonance. No quantitative parameters for the "pole-ball" are provided in the tweets; it is described as a prototype in development.

Physical feasibility note: A spinning sphere that generates its own angular momentum from internal coils does not violate conservation of angular momentum (the motor torque and counter-torque are internal, but reaction against the external field is possible if the field interacts with an external medium or field). However, lift generation without reaction mass violates conservation of linear momentum unless there is a medium to push against or a gravitational interaction mechanism.

## 7. Gravity Interaction Claims — Vacuum Pushing Analogy

Source: 1953572321861808352.yaml (note_tweet.text)

"Can we spin a disc magnet using a rotating magnetic field, so that it pushes against the vacuum, like a spinning boat or airplane propeller?"

This is the core theoretical claim: that a spinning magnet in a rotating magnetic field can interact with the vacuum (or spacetime, or quantum vacuum field) as a propulsive medium, analogous to how a propeller pushes against air or a ship propeller pushes against water. The author draws an analogy to macroscopic fluid mechanics propulsion (F_thrust = rho * A * v * delta_v for a propeller/actuator disk theory), but applied to the quantum vacuum.

Physical context: The quantum vacuum (zero-point field) has a non-zero energy density in QED. The Casimir effect demonstrates that vacuum fluctuations exert measurable forces. However, no peer-reviewed mechanism exists for a macroscopic rotating magnet to generate directional thrust by interacting with vacuum fluctuations. This claim is speculative; it relates to concepts in the Haisch-Rueda-Puthoff (1994) "stochastic electrodynamics" inertia hypothesis (Physical Review A 49, 678) where inertia and gravity are attributed to interaction between matter and the zero-point electromagnetic field. However, even within that framework, propulsion by a rotating magnet has not been derived.
