## 1. Rotating Magnetic Field Production via Multi-Phase AC Coils

Source: 13_1916095047227084800_video.mdx, 1953572321861808352.yaml, 1970987567299559428.yaml

### Spatial Configuration for Rotating Field
* N coil sets arranged at equal azimuthal intervals (360/N degrees apart)
* Driven by N-phase AC, each phase offset by 2*pi/N radians in time
* Resultant magnetic field vector B(t) rotates in the azimuthal (XY) plane at angular velocity omega = 2*pi*f
* For 3-phase (N=3): coils at 0, 120, 240 degrees; phase offset 120 degrees; field rotates continuously
* For 5-phase (N=5): coils at 0, 72, 144, 216, 288 degrees; phase offset 72 degrees; smoother rotation (less ripple)

### Field Vector Rotation (3-phase)
B_x(t) = B_0 * [sin(omega*t) + sin(omega*t - 2*pi/3)*cos(120) + sin(omega*t - 4*pi/3)*cos(240)]
B_y(t) = B_0 * [0 + sin(omega*t - 2*pi/3)*sin(120) + sin(omega*t - 4*pi/3)*sin(240)]
Net: |B(t)| = constant = (3/2)*B_0 (for balanced 3-phase), rotating at omega
The field vector traces a circle in the XY plane (for a symmetric 3-phase arrangement with equal coil spacing and equal currents).

### Five-Phase vs Three-Phase Field Quality
Author's claim (1970987567299559428.yaml note_tweet.text): "Five-phase AC power (72 degree differences of AC phase). This works better than 3-phase AC power (120 degree differences of phase)."
Physical basis: With 5 phases (N=5), the torque ripple on the spinning magnet is reduced (ripple frequency = N*f in an N-phase motor), producing smoother spin. Also, 5-phase produces a more circular (less hexagonally perturbed) rotating field. In industrial motor design, higher phase counts reduce torque ripple and cogging.

## 2. Spinning Disc Magnet in Rotating Magnetic Field — Two Dynamical Regimes

Source: 13_1916095047227084800_video.mdx (primary experimental data)

### XY-Plane Dynamics (Azimuthal / Transverse)
* The rotating magnetic field exerts a torque on the disc magnet: tau = m x B (cross product of magnetic dipole moment m and local B field)
* This torque synchronizes the magnet spin with the field rotation (synchronous motor behavior)
* The disc magnet spins in the XY plane at the field frequency omega_field
* At low omega_field (below transition), the dipole follows the field with minimal slip

### Z-Axis Dynamics (Axial / Vertical)
Two regimes determined by spin frequency:

Regime 1 — Low frequency (Newtonian mechanical screw):
* At low omega_field, the spinning magnet on the rod behaves non-gyroscopically
* The repulsive lower magnet (fixed below, same polarity orientation) exerts an upward force on the disc magnet
* The helical field gradient from the rotating 3-phase field creates a net downward force component on the disc magnet (analogous to a magnetic screw thread)
* The disc magnet is driven downward against the repulsive lower magnet
* The reaction force pushes the rod upward against the mechanical stop (steel beam)
* Z-axis force on scale increases (downward on lower components, tracked as weight increase)
* Progression: 12 Hz (+70 g), 16 Hz (+190 g), 18 Hz (+250 g)

Regime 2 — High frequency (Gyroscopic top behavior):
* Above ~25–30 Hz, the disc magnet acquires angular momentum L = I * omega sufficiently large that gyroscopic rigidity dominates
* Gyroscopic rigidity resists the tilting/deflecting force that previously drove the magnet downward toward the repulsive magnet
* The magnet behaves as a "top" — stabilized by its own angular momentum, no longer following the axial gradient force
* The helical rotating magnetic field now provides effective axial support (the magnet rides the rotating field like a particle in a helical potential)
* Z-axis force transmitted to rod: ~0 g on scale
* The transition between regimes is the "non-Newtonian" behavior referenced in tweet 1916093709445194077

## 5. Magnetic Levitation — Stable Two-Spinning-Cube-Magnet System

Source: 1963904283214254226.yaml
"Stable levitation using two fast-spinning cube magnets" (demonstrated by RedCollie1 in 2022, YouTube: https://youtu.be/ZlJCe6g99QU)

Physical principle:
* Two cube magnets in close proximity, both spinning rapidly
* Normally, a static permanent magnet cannot be stably levitated above another permanent magnet (Earnshaw's theorem prohibits stable static levitation in any combination of static electric, magnetic, or gravitational fields from static sources)
* However, dynamic systems (spinning magnets) are exempt from Earnshaw's theorem
* When both magnets spin, the gyroscopic rigidity of each provides stabilizing torques
* The magnetic interaction between the two spinning magnets produces an equilibrium levitation gap
* Stability: the spinning axis provides gyroscopic resistance to perturbation, analogous to spinning top on a surface
* This is established dynamical stabilization (not anomalous physics); similar to "Levitron" toy (stable magnetic levitation achieved only when spinning top is within a narrow rotational speed window)

XY-plane: magnetic repulsion provides radial equilibrium (centering)
Z-axis: balance between gravitational force (downward) and magnetic repulsion (upward) at equilibrium gap; gyroscopic stabilization prevents tipping that would otherwise collapse the levitation

## 8. Corona / Plasma Discharge Around High-Voltage Electromagnetic Components

Source: 13_1923735068603817984_preview.txt (image description)

"Plasma/corona discharge around metallic components in near-dark conditions" — alternative interpretation of glowing orbs in photograph.
Physical basis: At sufficiently high voltage gradients (E > breakdown threshold of ~3 MV/m in air at STP), air ionizes (Townsend avalanche), producing a visible glow discharge (corona). Around sharp metallic components of a high-voltage electromagnetic device, this appears as glowing halo or orb patterns. The visible spectrum of air corona depends on excited-state transitions of N2, O2, and their ions (predominantly violet-blue in normal air; blue-white at higher energies).

The Paschen curve describes breakdown voltage as a function of pressure*gap distance (p*d). At elevated frequencies (AC corona), the breakdown threshold is lower than DC due to residual ions from previous half-cycle.
