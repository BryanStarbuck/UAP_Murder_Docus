## 1. Multi-Phase AC Waveforms

Source: 1953572321861808352.yaml, 1970987567299559428.yaml

### N-Phase AC Voltage Waveforms
General form for k-th phase in an N-phase AC system:
  V_k(t) = V_peak * sin(omega * t - 2*pi*(k-1)/N),  k = 1, 2, ..., N

* omega = 2*pi*f (angular frequency, rad/s)
* f = line frequency (Hz), variable via VFD (Variable Frequency Drive)
* V_peak = peak voltage amplitude
* Phase offset between adjacent phases = 2*pi/N radians = 360/N degrees

Three-phase case (N=3):
  V_U(t) = V_peak * sin(omega*t)
  V_V(t) = V_peak * sin(omega*t - 2*pi/3)
  V_W(t) = V_peak * sin(omega*t - 4*pi/3)

Five-phase case (N=5):
  V_1(t) = V_peak * sin(omega*t)
  V_2(t) = V_peak * sin(omega*t - 2*pi/5)
  V_3(t) = V_peak * sin(omega*t - 4*pi/5)
  V_4(t) = V_peak * sin(omega*t - 6*pi/5)
  V_5(t) = V_peak * sin(omega*t - 8*pi/5)

Phase separation: 3-phase = 120 degrees; 5-phase = 72 degrees (per author's explicit statement: "72 degree differences of AC phase")

## 2. Rotating Magnetic Field Vector

Source: Implied by 3-phase motor theory underlying experiment in 13_1916095047227084800_video.mdx

For N coils placed at equal azimuthal angles theta_k = 2*pi*(k-1)/N in the XY plane, each driven by V_k(t) above, producing axial (Z-directed) flux density B_k(t) = B_0 * sin(omega*t - 2*pi*(k-1)/N) at azimuthal angle theta_k:

The resultant field in the plane:
  B_x(t) = sum_{k=1}^{N} B_0 * sin(omega*t - 2*pi*(k-1)/N) * cos(2*pi*(k-1)/N)
  B_y(t) = sum_{k=1}^{N} B_0 * sin(omega*t - 2*pi*(k-1)/N) * sin(2*pi*(k-1)/N)

For N >= 3 (balanced symmetric system):
  B_x(t) = (N/2) * B_0 * cos(omega*t)
  B_y(t) = (N/2) * B_0 * sin(omega*t)

Resultant: B(t) = (N/2)*B_0 [cos(omega*t) x_hat + sin(omega*t) y_hat]
Magnitude: |B(t)| = (N/2)*B_0 = constant
Direction: rotates at angular velocity omega in XY plane

Note: For N=3, amplitude = (3/2)*B_0; for N=5, amplitude = (5/2)*B_0 (for equal coil strengths).
The N=5 case has larger amplitude and smoother rotation (torque ripple frequency = N*f = 5f vs 3f for N=3).

## 3. Magnetic Torque on Spinning Magnet

Source: Underlying physics of experiment, 13_1916095047227084800_video.mdx

Magnetic dipole moment of disc magnet:
  m = M_s * V * z_hat (at equilibrium, where M_s is saturation magnetization, V is magnet volume)
  V = pi * r^2 * t = pi * (0.035)^2 * (0.003) = pi * 0.001225 * 0.003 = 1.155e-5 m^3
  (using r = 35 mm = 0.035 m, t = 3 mm = 0.003 m as stated in video)

Torque on dipole in field:
  tau = m x B(t) = |m| * |B| * sin(phi) * n_hat
  where phi = angle between m and B(t), n_hat = unit vector normal to both

At synchronous spin (phi = constant, lock-in torque):
  tau_synch = |m| * |B| * sin(delta)
  where delta is the torque angle (lag angle between m and B)

For synchronous operation (no slip), the magnet spins at exactly omega_field:
  omega_magnet = omega_field = 2*pi*f

## 4. Angular Momentum and Gyroscopic Equations

Source: Underlying the transition described in 13_1916095047227084800_video.mdx

Angular momentum of spinning disc magnet:
  L = I * omega_magnet (directed along spin axis)
  I = (1/2) * m_disc * r^2 (moment of inertia of solid disc about symmetry axis)
  = (1/2) * m_disc * (0.035)^2
  (mass m_disc not stated; diameter 70 mm, 14 mm hole, 3 mm thick — for NdFeB density ~7500 kg/m^3:
   V_ring = pi*(0.035^2 - 0.007^2)*0.003 = pi*(0.001225 - 0.000049)*0.003 = 1.109e-5 m^3
   m_disc ~ 7500 * 1.109e-5 = 0.083 kg = 83 g)
  I ~ (1/2) * 0.083 * (0.035)^2 = 5.08e-5 kg*m^2

At f = 32 Hz: omega = 2*pi*32 = 201 rad/s
  L = 5.08e-5 * 201 = 0.0102 kg*m^2/s = 10.2 mN*m*s

Euler's equation (gyroscope precession):
  dL/dt = tau_external
  where tau_external is the torque from the rotating magnetic field

Precession angular velocity (if tau perpendicular to L):
  Omega_precession = tau / L = tau / (I * omega_spin)

For the transition to "top" behavior: when L is large enough that Omega_precession << omega_field, the gyroscope is in the fast-spin (gyroscopic) regime and resists the deflecting force from the axial gradient.

Critical condition for transition (approximate):
  I * omega_critical^2 >> F_axial * r_characteristic
  where F_axial is the axial gradient force from the field-screw interaction

The transition observed at ~25–30 Hz in the experiment represents this condition being satisfied.

## 5. Scale Force Equations — Mechanical Screw Regime

Source: 13_1916095047227084800_video.mdx

In the Newtonian (mechanical screw) regime, the disc magnet is pushed downward by the rotating field with force F_screw. The rod is simultaneously pushed upward against the stop with reaction force F_reaction = F_screw.

The scale reads: F_scale = M_device * g + F_reaction (if device assembly sits on scale)
Or if scale is tared: Delta_F_scale = F_reaction = F_screw

Measurements:
  12 Hz: Delta_F = 0.070 kg * 9.81 = 0.687 N
  16 Hz: Delta_F = 0.190 kg * 9.81 = 1.865 N
  18 Hz: Delta_F = 0.250 kg * 9.81 = 2.453 N
  32 Hz: Delta_F ~= 0 N

F_screw as function of omega_field (empirical, from data points):
Approximate fit between 12 and 18 Hz: F_screw ~ alpha * omega_field^2 (quadratic, consistent with magnetic force scaling with B^2 and B ~ omega*I/R)

Beyond ~20–25 Hz: F_screw collapses as gyroscopic decoupling begins.

## 7. Coil Count and Phase Architecture — Mathematical Summary

Source: 2027850978926727258.yaml, 1970987567299559428.yaml

Configuration 1 (3-phase, 9 coils total):
  Upper ring: 6 coils, driven as two interlaced 3-phase sets: (U1-V1-W1-U2-V2-W2)
    Azimuthal spacing: 60 degrees between adjacent coils
    Phase offset within each 3-phase set: 120 degrees
    Total field: combination of two 3-phase fields offset by 0 and 60 degrees -> produces 6-pole rotating field
  Lower ring: 3 coils, driven as U-V-W (single 3-phase set)
    Azimuthal spacing: 120 degrees
    Phase offset: 120 degrees
    Produces 2-pole or 4-pole rotating field depending on winding

Configuration 2 (5-phase, Buga sphere model):
  5 coils at 72-degree spacing, driven with 72-degree phase offset
  Produces smoother rotating field (see Section 2 above)

Pole pair number p determines relationship between field rotation rate and rotor spin:
  omega_rotor = omega_field / p (for p pole pairs)
  For p=1 (2-pole field): omega_rotor = omega_field directly
  For p=3 (6-pole field from 6-coil arrangement): omega_rotor = omega_field / 3
