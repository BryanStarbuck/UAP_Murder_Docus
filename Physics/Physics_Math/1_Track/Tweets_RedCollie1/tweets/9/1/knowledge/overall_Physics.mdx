## 1. Newtonian Mechanical Screw Effect of Spinning Magnet in Three-Phase Rotating Field

Source: 13_1916095047227084800_video.mdx (transcription) + 1916095385363230993.yaml (referenced tweet text)

### Device Configuration (Experimental, not theoretical)
* Central magnet: disc, 70 mm diameter, 14 mm hole, 3 mm thick, mounted on 8 mm aluminium rod
* Lower holding magnet: 60 mm diameter, repulsive orientation, fixed below to hold rod in place
* Rod can translate axially (up/down); upper travel limited by a heavy mass (steel beam)
* Three-phase wire coils: 3 sets arranged to produce rotating magnetic field around spinning magnet
* Power source: variable frequency drive (VFD), frequency swept from 0 Hz to ~32 Hz and back
* Scale: digital precision scale (tared), measuring apparent weight change of the assembly

### Observed Phenomenology and Frequency-Dependent Dynamics (Experimental Results)

* 0 Hz: No spin, no weight change on scale (baseline)
* ~12 Hz: Rod rises to mechanical stop (steel beam above); scale reads +70 g (Newtonian push-up via screw effect)
* 16 Hz: scale reads +190 g (magnet spinning faster, mechanical screw torque increasing)
* 18 Hz: scale reads +250 g (peak of mechanical-screw regime)
* ~20 Hz: (transition zone, approaching gyroscopic/top regime)
* 30 Hz: scale weight begins decreasing — magnet rising off upper stop, gyroscopic stabilization beginning
* 32 Hz: scale reads ~0 g — magnet behaves as a "top" (gyroscope), no longer pushing rod upward against stop; the helical magnetic field from the rotating 3-phase field now supports the spinning magnet

### Physical Interpretation: Two Regimes

Regime 1 — Newtonian Mechanical Screw (low frequency, below ~20–25 Hz):
* The rotating magnetic field drags the disc magnet into synchronous spin with the field
* The gyroscopic precession at low RPM is insufficient to maintain the magnet at a fixed axial position
* The disc magnet is deflected toward the lower repulsive magnet by the screw-like helical field topology
* This deflection pushes the rod upward against the steel-beam stop
* Net effect: downward force on lower repulsive magnet translated to upward force on rod, producing weight INCREASE on scale (reaction force)
* Mechanism: F_scale_increase = F_reaction = m_rod * a, where a is determined by helical field gradient and spin frequency

Regime 2 — Gyroscopic / Non-Newtonian Top Behavior (high frequency, above ~25–30 Hz):
* At sufficient angular velocity omega, the spinning magnet acquires gyroscopic rigidity (gyroscopic stiffness L = I * omega, where I = moment of inertia of disc magnet and omega = angular velocity)
* The magnet becomes a gyroscope — it resists the lateral force that previously drove it toward the lower repulsive magnet
* Instead, it rides the helical rotating magnetic field as a "top" — levitated / held up by field geometry
* No force is transmitted to the rod; rod no longer pushed against stop
* Scale reading drops toward zero
* The magnet is "supported by helical magnetic fields" — meaning the rotating field gradient provides effective axial support against gravity

## 3. Three-Phase AC Power and Rotating Magnetic Field

Source: 1953572321861808352.yaml, 1970987567299559428.yaml, 2027850978926727258.yaml

### Three-Phase Power (3-phase AC)
* Three sinusoidal voltage waveforms, each 120 degrees out of phase: V_U, V_V, V_W
  - V_U(t) = V_peak * sin(omega * t)
  - V_V(t) = V_peak * sin(omega * t - 2*pi/3)
  - V_W(t) = V_peak * sin(omega * t - 4*pi/3)
* When passed through coil sets arranged at 120-degree spatial intervals, produces a rotating magnetic field vector rotating at angular frequency omega (= 2*pi*f)
* Standard industrial 3-phase used in all induction motors

### Five-Phase Power (5-phase AC)
Source: 1970987567299559428.yaml (note_tweet.text)
"Five-phase AC power (72 degree differences of AC phase). This works better than 3-phase AC power (120 degree differences of phase)."

* Five sinusoidal waveforms, each 72 degrees (= 2*pi/5 radians) out of phase
  - V_k(t) = V_peak * sin(omega * t - 2*pi*k/5), k = 0,1,2,3,4
* When passed through 5 coil sets arranged at 72-degree spatial intervals, produces a smoother rotating field (less torque ripple than 3-phase)
* Smaller angular step between phases produces closer approximation to ideal continuous rotating field
* Author claims 5-phase is superior to 3-phase for the proposed UFO propulsion mechanism

### Coil Configuration Prescription (from 2027850978926727258.yaml note_tweet.text)
"Place 6 wire coils in a circle, with 3 more wire coils below. Give them both 3-phase AC power as 6 (U-V-W-U-V-W) or 3 (U-V-W). A central magnet, given 3-phase power from those 3 lower wire coils, will spin up through a 'thin tube'. While it spins up through the center with 3-phase power ('trident' shape), we can add 'bearings' to the outside of that 'tube', and use those 6 other wire coils to make lots of small magnets spin in the opposite direction. A magnet with 3 big wire coils rises up. Many small magnets spin in plane below, in the opposite direction."

Architecture:
* Upper ring: 6 coils arranged in azimuthal circle, powered U-V-W-U-V-W (two sets of 3-phase)
* Lower ring: 3 coils arranged below, powered U-V-W (one set of 3-phase)
* Central element: single large magnet on axial shaft; 3-phase from lower coils causes it to spin and rise axially ("up through thin tube")
* Peripheral element: multiple small magnets outside the tube, driven by upper 6 coils to counter-rotate relative to central magnet
* This counter-rotation is analogous to the angular momentum cancellation claimed in dual-flywheel designs

## 4. Electromagnetic Lorentz Force on Liquid Metal — Galinstan

Source: 1995975540717887662.yaml (referenced tweet text)
"I just love this video. Liquid metal galinstan spinning by an electromagnetic Lorentz force! Working on its relation to UFO physics now."

Galinstan = Ga-In-Sn eutectic alloy (gallium-indium-tin), liquid at room temperature.
Lorentz force on conducting fluid: F = J x B * dV (body force density = current density cross magnetic field, integrated over volume)
For a current-carrying conductive liquid in an external magnetic field B, the body force rotates the fluid in the plane perpendicular to B (azimuthal Lorentz force when B is axial and J is radial, or vice versa).
This is MHD (magnetohydrodynamics) rotation: the principle behind electromagnetic stirring in metallurgy.
Author suggests the spinning liquid metal may be an analog of the proposed UFO propulsion mechanism using rotating magnetic fields.
