1. CLAIMED VIOLATION OF NEWTON'S 3RD LAW (ACTION-REACTION) — LORENTZ SPIN
============================================================================================

Source (1976079428825514470, note_tweet):
"Spin by a Lorentz force does not create action-reaction by Newton's 3rd Law, because its
vector of spin lies 90 degrees away from the vector of input DC voltage or current."

Source (2001577665980174511, note_tweet):
"The new idea (to solve that problem) is that we might add a counter-spinning Lorentz force
in liquid metal (galinstan), so as to violate Newton's 3rd law of action-reaction for two

Analysis of the claim:
Newton's 3rd Law states: for every force F_AB exerted by body A on body B, there is an
equal and opposite force F_BA = -F_AB exerted by B on A.

Drew's proposed violation rests on the geometric argument that the DC Lorentz force on
the liquid metal (F = J × B) has its reaction force orthogonal to the generated motion:
    - Input: DC current J in XY-plane, static field B in Z-direction
    - Output: Lorentz force J × B in φ-direction (azimuthal) → liquid metal spin (L_z)
    - Reaction: force on current-carrying electrodes and on the magnet face producing B
    - Drew claims this reaction is in the XY-plane (on the radial current source), not in Z
    - Therefore: L_z of liquid metal is generated WITHOUT a compensating -L_z reaction

Standard classical electromagnetism DOES satisfy Newton's 3rd Law in the form of conservation
of total momentum (mechanical + electromagnetic field momentum):
    d(p_mech)/dt + d(p_EM)/dt = F_external
The J × B force is balanced by reaction forces on the current source and magnet, but these
reactions act through the electromagnetic field — not necessarily as direct contact forces.

Drew's claim is that the 90° geometric separation allows the Z-component of angular momentum
to be generated freely. Whether this holds under full electromagnetic stress tensor analysis
(Maxwell stress tensor T_ij = ε_0[E_iE_j - (1/2)δ_{ij}E²] + (1/μ_0)[B_iB_j - (1/2)δ_{ij}B²])
is not addressed in these sources. The claim is theoretical/experimental hypothesis.

Status: Proposed experiment. "We'll find out whether that's true or not." (video
13_2001574820505620480_video.mdx). Not experimentally confirmed in these sources.
