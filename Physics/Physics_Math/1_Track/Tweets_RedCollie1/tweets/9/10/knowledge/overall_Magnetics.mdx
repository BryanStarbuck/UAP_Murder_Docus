5. HOMOPOLAR MOTOR SPIN CONFIGURATION
============================================================================================

Source (1989474122372714627, retweeted text):
"A disc-magnet which is spinning as a 'homopolar motor' will be 'lifted up' by an applied
3-phase magnetic field."

The homopolar (Faraday disk) motor operates by the Lorentz force on radial current flow in
a rotating conductor in an axial magnetic field: F = q(v × B). In a homopolar motor, the
current I flows radially outward (or inward) across the conducting disc, the axial magnetic
field B_z provides the force, and the resultant Lorentz force F = I·L × B is tangential,
producing torque. Drew proposes the same disc-magnet serves both as the rotating magnetic
source (permanent magnet) and the driven element, with the 3-phase external coils providing
the time-varying B that drives it.

6. LORENTZ FORCE IN LIQUID METAL FOR REACTION CANCELLATION
============================================================================================

Source (1976079428825514446, note_tweet):
"The key to understanding UFO levitation is therefore to find some way to keep that
fast-spinning magnet from creating action-reaction with the rest of the device, through a
need for conservation of spin angular momentum. That is what I am working on now, using
liquid metal which can spin by a DC Lorentz force!"

Source (1976079428825514470, note_tweet):
"Spin by a Lorentz force does not create action-reaction by Newton's 3rd Law, because its
vector of spin lies 90 degrees away from the vector of input DC voltage or current."

Geometric argument: In a DC Lorentz drive, the input force vector (F = J × B, current
density J crossed with static B field) is orthogonal to both the current direction and the
field direction. If the static B field is axial (Z) and the applied DC current flows radially
(XY-plane), the Lorentz force is azimuthal (φ-direction), producing azimuthal flow in the
liquid metal. Drew claims this azimuthal angular momentum has its vector along Z, and
because the DC driving source is in the XY-plane, the action-reaction for the driving circuit
is in the XY-plane, not in Z — thus the Z-axis angular momentum of the liquid metal is not
cancelled by the drive circuit. This is the mechanism he proposes to circumvent conservation
of angular momentum in the lift axis.

Note: This argument conflates distinct axes and requires more rigorous tensor analysis to
evaluate. It is presented here as Drew's stated theoretical reasoning, not as confirmed physics.

7. CONSERVATION OF ANGULAR MOMENTUM — EXPERIMENTAL RESULT
============================================================================================

Video transcription (13_1976075981111828480_video.mdx):
"Here I have a motor spinning a blade placed on a floating boat so there's no friction to
interfere. Right now the boat isn't moving meaning its total angular momentum is zero. But

This demonstration confirms standard conservation of angular momentum: L_total = 0 requires
L_blade + L_boat = 0, so L_boat = -L_blade at all times. When the spinning magnet rises in
the 3-phase coil arrangement, the coil assembly acquires an equal and opposite angular
momentum — the scale measures no net weight change because the total system angular momentum
is conserved and no net external force has been generated in the lab frame.

This null result (zero change in system weight on a scale) is the key experimental datum
motivating the liquid-metal Lorentz counter-spin proposal.
