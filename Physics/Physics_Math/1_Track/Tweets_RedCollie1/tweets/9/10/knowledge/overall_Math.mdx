1. GYROSCOPIC PRECESSION EQUATION
============================================================================================

[DERIVED FROM CLAIM — source 1946057554548912446, note_tweet]

Precession angular velocity:
    Ω_prec = τ_grav / |L_spin|
            = (M · g · r_cm) / (I_spin · ω_spin)

Variables:
    Ω_prec  = precession angular velocity [rad/s]
    τ_grav  = gravitational torque about pivot [N·m]
    M       = mass of spinning body [kg]
    g       = gravitational acceleration = 9.81 m/s² (Earth surface)
    r_cm    = horizontal distance from pivot point to center of mass [m]
    I_spin  = moment of inertia about spin axis [kg·m²]
    ω_spin  = spin angular velocity [rad/s]
    L_spin  = I_spin · ω_spin = spin angular momentum [kg·m²/s]

Relationship encoded in sources:
    As ω_spin → ∞:  Ω_prec → 0, spin axis → vertical (anti-gravity transition)
    As ω_spin is low: Ω_prec is large, axis is tilted, less upward force

Drew's claim: the upward "anti-gravity" force occurs during the TRANSITION from low ω_spin
to high ω_spin, as the precession axis tilts from horizontal toward vertical. The rate of
change of L (vector) generates the net upward force:
    F_up ∝ dL/dt (change of angular momentum vector direction)
            = τ_reaction (reaction torque on support → upward force)

For a disc (radius R, mass M):
    I_disc = (1/2) · M · R²

3. ELECTROMAGNETIC TORQUE ON DISC-MAGNET
============================================================================================

[DERIVED FROM CLAIM]

Torque on a magnetic dipole μ in external field B:
    τ = μ × B

Magnitude:
    |τ| = |μ| · |B| · sin(δ)
where:
    μ  = magnetic dipole moment of disc-magnet [A·m²]
    B  = applied rotating field magnitude [T]
    δ  = angle between μ and B (slip angle)

At steady-state spin-up, δ > 0 (magnet lags the field), net positive torque accelerates
the magnet. At synchronous speed, δ → 0, net torque → 0.

Relation to angular momentum:
    dL/dt = τ_em − τ_load
At steady levitation (ω_spin = const):
    τ_em = τ_load  (electromagnetic torque balances frictional and gravitational load torques)
