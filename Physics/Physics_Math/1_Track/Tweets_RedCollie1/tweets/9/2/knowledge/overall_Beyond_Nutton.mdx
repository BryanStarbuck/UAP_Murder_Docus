## 1. Claimed Newton's 3rd Law Violation: Lorentz Force on Liquid Metal

### Source: tweet 1971034425413812434, note_tweet
"The fast spin of that liquid metal does not follow Newton's 3rd Law, so if we could
direct its 'Lorentz force' upward, then we would have 'anti-gravity'."

Classical expectation (Newtonian):
* Every action has an equal and opposite reaction.
* If liquid metal galinstan is pushed upward (or azimuthally) by J × B, the field
  source (magnet + current supply) must experience an equal and opposite force.
* In standard MHD (magnetohydrodynamics), the current-carrying wire and magnet
  both experience reaction forces consistent with Newton's 3rd Law in the full
  electromagnetic system.

Drew's observation:
* The galinstan vortex spins strongly under Lorentz force.
* No visible equal-and-opposite mechanical counter-rotation is observed on the
  container, magnet, or external circuit.
* Drew interprets this absence as a Newton's 3rd Law violation in the mechanical domain.

Assessment as "beyond Newtonian":
* If real, this would imply that electromagnetic body forces on conducting fluids can
  produce net linear or angular momentum in the fluid without an equal reaction on
  any identifiable mechanical body — a violation of Newtonian mechanics.
* Standard physics explanation: the reaction IS present but distributed across the
  electromagnetic field (Maxwell stress) and the current-supply circuit. Drew's setup
  may not instrument these sufficiently to observe the reaction.
* Drew treats the apparent absence of observable counter-reaction as beyond-Newtonian.

## 2. Frequency-Dependent Force Anomaly in Spinning Magnet

Non-classical observation 1 — Unpredictable directionality at low frequency (< 20 Hz):
* Over a dozen disc magnets (or magnetic flywheels), the direction of axial force (up vs.
  down) under low-frequency 3-phase spin cannot be predicted from any measured magnetic
  or geometric characteristic of the specimen.
* Classical magnetostatics and standard motor theory would predict that the force direction
  on a spinning magnetic dipole in a known field configuration is deterministic, determined
  by the dipole orientation and field geometry.
* Drew states: "I have not found any characteristic of the magnet (or magnetic flywheel)
  which might predict which way it goes, over a dozen different examples."
* This unpredictability (if not attributable to hidden variables like flux asymmetries or
  mechanical contact friction) represents a non-classical result.

Non-classical observation 2 — High-frequency convergence to upward motion:
* All specimens converge on upward motion at 25-30 Hz regardless of their low-frequency
  behavior.
* This frequency-dependent force reversal (from unpredictable direction at low frequency
  to universally upward at high frequency) has no obvious explanation in classical rotating
  magnetic field theory.
* A classical rotating field exerts a torque on the spinning magnet (drive torque), not
  a net axial force. The axial force arises from field non-uniformity (gradient force:
  F = ∇(m · B)), which depends on the specific field geometry and would not obviously
  be frequency-dependent in the same way Drew observes.

Non-classical observation 3 — Gyroscopic decoupling:
* "Such a 'gyroscopic' motion has never been seen before using iron-free, 3-phase, copper
  electromagnets to create spin."
* The magnet hovers in air when spinning under 3-phase field — decoupled from the pole.
* Classical gyroscope theory: a gyroscope supported on a pivot precesses; it does not
  LEVITATE without a support point.
* Drew's observation: the 3-phase spinning magnet hovers without mechanical support at
  high frequency — suggesting the electromagnetic field itself provides a vertical
  restoring force previously unknown in this geometry.

## 4. Proposed Non-Classical Gyroscopic Levitation Force

### Source: note_tweet 1916293690764759043
"It would be of great interest if we could 'harness' that high-frequency, upward spinning
force, which does NOT cause a central pole to move upward or downward."

At high frequency:
* The spinning magnet exerts ZERO net force on the central pole.
* Yet the spinning magnet itself is pushed upward by the electromagnetic field.
* Classical expectation: Newton's 3rd Law demands equal and opposite forces between
  magnet and pole. If the magnet pushes upward, the pole should push downward equally.
  Zero force on the pole from the magnet means either:
  (a) The upward force on the magnet has a different origin than the magnet-pole interaction
      (i.e., the force comes from the electromagnetic field directly, not from the pole),
  (b) The mechanical coupling becomes negligible at high speed (loose contact) and the
      upward force is truly from the rotating field — not from the pole reaction.
* Option (b) is consistent with classical physics: the reaction is on the stator coils
  (not the pole), which are fixed and experience the downward reaction from the field
  on the magnet. Drew's apparent paradox dissolves if the reaction is on the coils,
  not the pole.
* However, Drew characterizes this as novel and previously unobserved behavior.
