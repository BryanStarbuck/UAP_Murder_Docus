## Z-Axis Force Generation: Frequency-Dependent Progression

### Experimental sequence (video 13_1916293139054350336, 13_1916293193240612864;
### note_tweet 1916293690764759043)

A 70 mm disc magnet (14 mm center hole, 6-10 mm shaft/pole) spinning on a pole under
3-phase electromagnetic drive:

Stage 1 — Mechanical screw effect (low frequency, ~< 20 Hz):
* As the magnet spins, it reaches a stage that pushes DOWN on the magnet below it,
  and this pushes the ROD UPWARD.
* Described as "a lot of force on the rod like a spring going upward."
* This is characterized as "like a mechanical screw effect."
* At this stage, when the rotating force is removed, "the magnet goes up and the rod
  has no tendency to rise at all" — suggesting the force is dynamic, not static.

Stage 2 — Higher frequency (25 Hz):
* "It's rising up quite a lot, but you can't push it down any further."
* The upward force on the magnet itself is now strong.

Stage 3 — 28 Hz:
* "Quite firmly bound up there, you can't push it down if you don't break down."
* The magnet is firmly held upward.

Stage 4 — 30 Hz:
* "Not much flow for 30 hertz" (from video 13_1916293193240612864 — the second video,
  showing a different, thicker/heavier magnet that rises less due to greater mass).
* Despite the magnet being larger and heavier, strong upward binding persists.

## Two Distinct Upward Force Mechanisms

### From note_tweet 1916293690764759043
Drew distinguishes:

Mechanism 1 (low-frequency):
* Upward force exerted ON THE CENTRAL POLE by the spinning magnet.
* At 20 Hz: ~300 grams force on the pole.
* This is the "mechanical screw" or push-type interaction.
* The force is transmitted mechanically through the pole-magnet contact.

Mechanism 2 (high-frequency):
* The spinning magnet decouples from the pole.
* Force on the pole from the magnet → ZERO.
* The magnet itself is pushed upward by a "top-like gyroscopic force."
* Feels like a strong upward push when hand is placed on the spinning magnet.
* Drew estimates: 500-1000 grams upward force at high frequency.
* The magnet HOVERS in the air like a gyroscope — "spinning magnetic levitation."

Key transition: as frequency increases from <20 Hz to 25-30 Hz, Mechanism 2 overtakes
Mechanism 1 in magnitude. The second force "decouples" the magnet from the pole.

## Gravity: Claimed Interactions and Anti-Gravity Framing

### Gravitational potential energy increase (tweet 1963915629892899295, note_tweet)
"It is easy to get increased gravitational 'potential' energy, but not decreased total-
weight on a scale (unless the central spinning flywheel goes into 'free fall')."

This distinguishes:
* Increased gravitational PE: when flywheel rises on a pole (height increases → PE = Mgh
  increases). This is normal mechanical behavior.
* Decreased total weight: a reduction in the scale reading below the resting weight —
  actual anti-gravity. This has not been achieved by Drew in lab experiments as of the
  source dates.

"Free fall" caveat: if the flywheel disconnects from the pole and falls freely, it
experiences zero weight by inertial mechanics. Drew notes this as the only case where
decreased weight is observed — but this is trivial free fall, not sustained anti-gravity.
