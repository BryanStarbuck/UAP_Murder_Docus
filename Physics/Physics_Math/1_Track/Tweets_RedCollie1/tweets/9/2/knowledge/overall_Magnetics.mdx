## 3-Phase Magnetic Spin Force: Two Distinct Regimes

### Claim (Drew, tweet 1916293690764759043, note_tweet)
"There seem to be two kinds of 3-phase, magnetic spin-force (in general). Something like
for 'chaotic' versus 'laminar' flow."

### Low-Frequency Regime (< 20 Hz)
* A disc magnet spinning on a bare aluminum or stainless steel rod under 3-phase excitation
  will move STRONGLY DOWNWARD in one orientation or STRONGLY UPWARD in the other.
* Direction (up vs. down) cannot be predicted from any measured characteristic of the
  magnet or magnetic flywheel across a dozen different specimens tested.
* This unpredictability is anomalous from the standpoint of classical magnetostatics,
  where the Lorentz force on a spinning magnet in a known field configuration should be
  deterministic given the pole orientation.

### High-Frequency Regime (25-30 Hz)
* At 25 Hz: magnet rises considerably; cannot be pushed downward by hand.
* At 28 Hz: firmly held upward; cannot be pushed down without breaking the setup.
* At 30 Hz: strong upward binding force persists.
* ALL specimens (regardless of low-frequency direction behavior) converge on UPWARD
  motion at high frequency in a top-like gyroscopic fashion.
* The second force (high-frequency upward) becomes stronger than the first (low-frequency
  directional force) as frequency increases.

### Gyroscopic Decoupling at High Frequency
* At high rpm, the spinning magnet decouples from the central pole.
* The upward or downward force previously exerted ON THE POLE goes to ZERO.
* The spinning magnet hovers in air in a gyroscopic state.
* Drew states: "Such a 'gyroscopic' motion has never been seen before using iron-free,
  3-phase, copper electromagnets to create spin."
* A central pole is required for stability; without it, the system is unstable.
* Using small permanent magnets to create spin (an alternative approach) introduces
  paramagnetism which "confuses the issues."

### Force Magnitude Estimates
* At 20 Hz: force on central pole ~300 grams (upward, low-frequency regime)
* At high frequency: predicted upward force 500 to 1000 grams (estimate by Drew)
* The high-frequency upward force is felt physically: "I can 'feel' with my hand that
  the spinning magnet at high-frequency is being pushed strongly upward."

## Lorentz Force on Liquid Metal (Galinstan)

### Tweet 1971034425413812434, note_tweet; Video 13_1971031656783020036

  a stainless steel container.

* DC power applied: 12 volts, ~50 amps (total electrical power ~600 W).

Observed behavior:
* A strong toroidal vortex flow develops in the galinstan under Lorentz force: F = J × B,
  where J is the current density from DC power and B is the magnetic field from the
  embedded and external magnets.
* Direction of vortex reverses when DC leads are reversed (consistent with F = J × B
  sign change under J reversal).
* With the magnet embedded inside the galinstan, the magnet can be made to react with
  3-phase power applied externally — spinning the magnet while the Lorentz force spins
  the liquid metal.
* Advantage of embedded magnet: simultaneously applying 3-phase power to the magnet
  AND Lorentz (DC) power to the galinstan makes BOTH spin at the same time.

## Spin and Counter-Spin Physics

### Tweet 1996203901125837155
"If you spin a magnet by 3-phase forces, you see both spinning and counter-spinning forces
of equal spin angular momentum. Not so for the spinning Lorentz force!"

Key distinction:
* For a magnet spun by 3-phase electromagnetic forces: angular momentum is conserved;
  equal and opposite counter-spin is produced (Newton's 3rd Law applies; total angular
  momentum = 0 in CoM frame).
* For liquid metal spun by Lorentz force: the spinning Lorentz force does NOT produce
  equal counter-spinning forces. This is the claimed anomaly — asymmetric angular
  momentum generation.
* Physical basis: the Lorentz force F = J × B on a fluid element is a body force that
  can produce toroidal flow without a mechanical reaction couple, provided the current
  source (external battery/supply) absorbs or provides the reaction. Drew appears to
  interpret the absence of an obvious mechanical counter-rotation as a Newton's 3rd Law
  violation, though the reaction is actually carried by the current source and circuit.
