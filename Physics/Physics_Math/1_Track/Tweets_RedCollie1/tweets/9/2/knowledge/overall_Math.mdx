## Lorentz Force Equation (core to all spin experiments)

    F = q(E + v × B)       [vector form, single charge]
    f = J × B              [volumetric body force in conducting fluid, N/m^3]

Components in galinstan MHD experiment:
    J = sigma (E + v × B)      [Ohm's law for moving conductor; sigma = conductivity]
    f_azimuthal = J_r × B_z    [radial current × axial field → azimuthal (toroidal) force]
    f_axial = J_azimuthal × B_r [azimuthal current × radial field → axial force — key for lift]

Where:
    J_r = radial component of current density [A/m^2]
    B_z = axial (vertical) component of magnetic flux density [T]
    J_azimuthal = azimuthal current component (induced by motion)
    B_r = radial magnetic field component [T]

The dominant drive in Drew's galinstan experiment is f_azimuthal = J_r × B_z, producing
toroidal vortex flow. The proposed anti-gravity extension requires generating a significant
f_axial = J_azimuthal × B_r component.

Experimental parameters:
    V_DC = 12 V
    I_DC ≈ 50 A
    P_electrical = V × I = 12 × 50 = 600 W

## 3-Phase Rotating Field Mathematics

Three-phase current set at frequency f (Hz), angular frequency omega = 2 pi f (rad/s):
    I_U(t) = I_0 sin(omega t + 0)
    I_V(t) = I_0 sin(omega t - 2pi/3)
    I_W(t) = I_0 sin(omega t - 4pi/3)

For N coils per phase producing magnetic field B_i in the i-th coil:
    B_total(theta, t) = Sum_i B_i cos(theta - theta_i) I_i(t)

For a balanced 3-phase, 6-coil system (coils at theta = 0, 60, 120, 180, 240, 300 degrees,
wired U,V,W,U,V,W):
    B_total(theta, t) = B_0 cos(theta - omega t)      [rotating field result]

This is a field of constant amplitude B_0 rotating at angular velocity omega in the
azimuthal plane. This is the standard induction motor rotating field result.

## Gyroscope / Angular Momentum

Spin angular momentum of rotating magnetic flywheel:
    L = I_moment × omega_spin    [kg m^2 / s]

where:
    I_moment = (1/2) M R^2     for a solid disc of mass M, radius R

Gyroscopic stability (precession equation):
    d L / dt = tau_applied     [vector equation]
    omega_precession = |tau| / |L| = tau / (I_moment omega_spin)

At high omega_spin (high frequency drive), L is large:
    omega_precession → 0   as omega_spin → infinity (for fixed tau)

This means large L resists axis changes — consistent with Drew's observation that at
25-30 Hz the magnet "cannot be pushed down" (resistive force to axis displacement is
proportional to L and therefore to omega_spin).

## 3I/ATLAS Trajectory Deviation (Avi Loeb, cited by Drew)

Angular deviation:
    Delta_alpha = 4 arcseconds in Right Ascension
    1 arcsecond = pi / (180 × 3600) ≈ 4.848 × 10^-6 radians
    Delta_alpha = 4 × 4.848 × 10^-6 = 1.939 × 10^-5 radians

At 3I/ATLAS distance from Earth at time of measurement (not precisely given in source),
the linear displacement would be:
    Delta_x = d × Delta_alpha  [m]
    (where d is distance in meters)

Mass loss calculation (Loeb, quoted by Drew):
"The nucleus of 3I/ATLAS should have lost at least a sixth of its mass, to accelerate
that much by cometary outgassing."
    Delta_M / M_total ≥ 1/6 ≈ 0.1667

This is derived from the rocket equation for outgassing propulsion:
    Delta_v = v_exhaust × ln(M_initial / M_final)
    Delta_v = v_exhaust × ln(M / (M - Delta_M))
    ≈ v_exhaust × (Delta_M / M)  for Delta_M << M

For the 4-arcsecond deviation to require Delta_M/M ≥ 1/6, the implied non-gravitational
acceleration significantly exceeds what cometary sublimation typically produces.

## Coil Wiring Topology (6-coil 3-phase)

For 6 coils in a ring, labeled 1-6 at positions:
    theta_k = (k-1) × 60 degrees, k = 1 to 6

Wiring assignment (U-V-W, U-V-W):
    Coil 1: phase U (0°)
    Coil 2: phase V (120° lag)
    Coil 3: phase W (240° lag)
    Coil 4: phase U (0°)  — opposite side, creates 2-pole field
    Coil 5: phase V (120° lag)
    Coil 6: phase W (240° lag)

This creates a 2-pole rotating field (P = 2 poles), synchronous speed:
    N_sync = 120 f / P = 60 f [RPM] = f [revolutions per second]

At f = 25 Hz: N_sync = 25 RPS = 1500 RPM
At f = 28 Hz: N_sync = 28 RPS = 1680 RPM
At f = 30 Hz: N_sync = 30 RPS = 1800 RPM

These are the RPM values at which the magnet hovers/binds upward in Drew's experiments.
