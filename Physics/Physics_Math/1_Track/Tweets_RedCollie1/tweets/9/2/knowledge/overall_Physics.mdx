## Magnetohydrodynamics (MHD): Lorentz Force on Conducting Fluid

### Setup (tweet 1971034425413812434; video 13_1971031656783020036)
Liquid metal galinstan (eutectic alloy: gallium, indium, tin; electrical conductivity
~3.46 × 10^6 S/m at room temperature) placed in stainless steel container.

Permanent disc magnet embedded in the galinstan. External DC current applied.

### Lorentz Force Body Force
The volumetric Lorentz body force on the conducting fluid:
    f_L = J × B    [N/m^3]

where:
    J = current density vector [A/m^2]
    B = total magnetic flux density [T] (combination of embedded magnet field + external magnet)

For applied current I ~50 A at 12 V through galinstan:
    J = I / A_eff   (cross-sectional area A_eff depends on container geometry)

B from embedded permanent magnet and external magnet produces a primarily axial or radial
field component. J (from DC leads, radial injection) crossed with axial B produces
azimuthal (toroidal) force -> drives toroidal vortex flow.

Reversal of DC polarity: J → -J, therefore f_L → -f_L, producing reversed vortex
(confirmed experimentally in video 13_1971031656783020036: vortex reverses direction
when DC leads reversed).

### MHD Navier-Stokes with Lorentz Body Force
Full equation of motion for conducting fluid:
    rho (Du/Dt) = -∇p + eta ∇²u + (J × B) + rho g

where:
    rho = fluid density (galinstan: ~6440 kg/m^3)
    u = velocity field
    p = pressure
    eta = dynamic viscosity (galinstan: ~0.0024 Pa·s, much less than mercury)
    g = gravitational acceleration (9.81 m/s^2, downward)

For the toroidal vortex geometry, the dominant balance is:
    rho (u · ∇)u ≈ J × B   (inertial convection balanced by Lorentz body force)

Drew's claim of Newton's 3rd Law violation in this context:
Standard MHD: the total momentum change of the fluid is compensated by reaction forces
on the current-carrying wires and the external circuit (which carries the return current).
In a standard MHD pump, there IS a reaction force on the magnet/coil system.
Drew interprets the absence of a visible counter-rotating solid body as a Newton's 3rd
Law anomaly. The physics interpretation: the reaction momentum is distributed across
the electromagnetic field and the external circuit, not concentrated in a single
mechanical counter-rotation — hence not obviously visible but not actually violating
Newton's 3rd Law in classical MHD.

Drew's stated anti-gravity mechanism: if the Lorentz body force can be directed upward
(axially, along Z), then net upward momentum is imparted to the fluid without a
corresponding downward reaction on a mechanical structure. He proposes this as the
mechanism by which a combination of embedded magnet + liquid metal could produce lift.

## 3-Phase Rotating Magnetic Field Physics

### Rotating Field Production
Three-phase sinusoidal currents produce a spatially rotating magnetic field:
    I_U(t) = I_0 sin(omega t)
    I_V(t) = I_0 sin(omega t - 2pi/3)
    I_W(t) = I_0 sin(omega t - 4pi/3)

For 6 coils wired U-V-W, U-V-W in phase (Drew's configuration), the resulting magnetic
field rotates in the XY plane (azimuthal plane) at angular frequency omega = 2 pi f,
where f is the supply frequency in Hz.

The rotating field drives the central disc magnet at synchronous (or near-synchronous)
speed omega_rotor ≈ omega (like a synchronous motor), subject to load.

### Force on Spinning Magnet: Two Regimes

At f < 20 Hz (low frequency):
* The interaction between the spinning magnet and the central pole produces a net axial
  (Z-direction) force. Direction (up or down) varies across specimens — unpredictable
  from magnetic properties alone.
* Possible mechanism: the rotating field produces helical flux lines (B field with both
  azimuthal and axial components). At slow rotation, the axial component of the Lorentz
  force on the spinning magnet (F_z = q v × B evaluated for the magnet's moving charges,
  or equivalently the gradient force on a magnetic dipole in a non-uniform field) is
  non-zero and specimen-dependent.

At f = 25-30 Hz (high frequency):
* All specimens shift to upward motion.
* The spinning magnet decouples from the central pole (mechanical contact forces go to
  zero because the contact becomes "very loose at high frequency").
* The magnet hovers in a gyroscopic state.
* Force on pole from magnet → ZERO.
* Drew characterizes this as a "top-like gyroscopic force."

### Gyroscope Physics Applied
A spinning disc magnet of moment of inertia I spinning at angular frequency omega_spin
has spin angular momentum:
    L = I * omega_spin   [kg m^2 / s]

For gyroscopic stability, the precessional frequency:
    omega_precession = tau / L = (torque) / (I omega_spin)

At high omega_spin (high frequency 3-phase drive), L is large. Small applied torques
produce slow precession rather than tipping. The magnet resists displacement from its
spin axis (gyroscopic rigidity). This is consistent with Drew's observation that at high
frequency the magnet "cannot be pushed down" — large L means high resistance to axis
change.

However, the UPWARD force (not merely gyroscopic stability) is the anomaly. Classical
gyroscope theory does not predict net axial levitation force from spin alone (without
precession around a fixed point). Drew's observation that there is a detectable upward
push (felt by hand, and that previously large pole forces go to zero) suggests additional
electromagnetic coupling — possibly:
* Helical flux line geometry creates an axial gradient in B, and the force F = ∇(m · B)
  on the spinning magnetic dipole m has a net Z-component.
* Or the decoupling itself (loss of mechanical contact) means the magnetic levitation
  force (diamagnetic or gradient-force) is unmasked once friction/contact forces are gone.

## Spin and Counter-Spin Angular Momentum

### Tweet 1996203901125837155
"If you spin a magnet by 3-phase forces, you see both spinning and counter-spinning forces
of equal spin angular momentum. Not so for the spinning Lorentz force!"

Classical interpretation:
For a magnet spun by a rotating electromagnetic field (stator), angular momentum
conservation requires: L_magnet + L_stator = L_total (constant if no external torque).
The stator (coils, power supply, mounting structure) experiences equal and opposite torque
to that applied to the magnet. This is the classical counter-spin.

For Lorentz force on liquid metal:
The MHD Lorentz force (J × B) acts as a body force on the fluid. The current J is
supplied externally (battery). The magnetic field B is from a fixed embedded magnet.
The reaction force IS on the magnet and the external circuit — but these are rigid
or external, not obviously spinning in the counter-direction. Drew interprets this
asymmetry as anomalous.

## Nickel-Cyanide Coating Chemistry: Material Analysis of 3I/ATLAS

### Tweet 1961229463917924584 (and referenced tweet 1961226935750815857)
Spectroscopic observation: nickel AND cyanide being released from 3I/ATLAS as it
approaches the Sun. No iron detected.

Chemical interpretation:
"This combination can only come from the heat-related decay of a nickel-cyanide coating,
which is often used to protect other metals (such as iron) from corrosion."

Electroplating chemistry: nickel electroplating using nickel-cyanide baths (e.g., 
Ni(CN)4^2- complex) deposits Ni metal on iron or other substrates. Thermal decomposition
of the Ni-CN coating releases both Ni vapor and CN (cyanide radical / HCN) when heated.
The absence of iron in the coma is explained by the coating protecting iron substrate.

Drew's inference: the nickel-cyanide coating is of artificial origin (electroplating is an
industrial process with no known natural analog), therefore 3I/ATLAS is a manufactured
object rather than a natural comet.
