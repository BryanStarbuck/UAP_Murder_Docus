## Conditional Angular Momentum Conservation — Non-Classical Claim

Source tweet: 2039678934200283365 (note_tweet)

The author's central non-Newtonian claim in this batch:

Standard Newtonian/Lagrangian mechanics: in a closed system with no external torques, the total angular momentum L_total = Σ I_i ω_i is conserved at all times and for all initial conditions:
  dL_total/dt = τ_external = 0 → L_total = constant ∀ t

Author's proposed modification: "spin angular momentum mvr is only conserved in the starting condition of v = 0 for his body and feet, not moving in either direction."

This explicitly states that angular momentum conservation is conditional on the initial velocity of the body being zero. When the body is already rotating (v_body ≠ 0), a different conservation rule (or no conservation) applies.

Why this would be beyond Newtonian mechanics:
* Newtonian mechanics has no such conditional: the conservation law is absolute, following from the rotational symmetry of the Lagrangian (Noether's theorem). If the Lagrangian is rotationally symmetric, angular momentum is conserved unconditionally.
* For the author's claim to be physically correct, the Lagrangian of the spinning system would need to have explicit velocity-dependent terms that break the rotational symmetry when v_body ≠ 0 — i.e., a term like L_interaction = f(ω_body) × L_wheel that is not present in classical mechanics.
* Such a term could arise from: coupling to a background field (gravitational, geomagnetic), spin-spin interaction with the vacuum (analogous to spin-orbit coupling in atomic physics but at macroscopic scale), or a torsion field in an extension of GR (Einstein-Cartan theory).

The experimental test proposed: "I will try to test these ideas by experiment soon." — This establishes that the author intends to perform a laboratory test of the conditional angular momentum hypothesis, which would be a direct test of a beyond-Newtonian prediction.

## Anti-Precession as Non-Classical Force

Source: video transcription 13_2039678248859422720 and tweet 2039678934200283365

Standard Newtonian precession: dL/dt = τ
  For a gyroscope: τ = r × F_gravity, producing precession Ω_p = τ/L

Anti-precession: forcing the gyroscope axis against the precession direction.
* Laithwaite's claim (1974 Royal Institution Christmas Lectures): a gyroscope precessing freely appears to weigh less than when held stationary. Measured weight reduction reported of several pounds.
* The standard explanation: the precessing gyroscope distributes its weight over a longer time interval (slower effective downward acceleration), which a bathroom scale integrates differently — not a true weight reduction.
* Laithwaite's counter: the measured force over the full precessional period integrates to less than the static weight, which cannot be explained by time-averaging alone.

Beyond-Newtonian implication: if anti-precession produces a net upward force component not accounted for by the vector sum of gravitational and centrifugal pseudo-forces, then there exists a coupling between angular momentum L and linear momentum p that is not present in the classical Hamiltonian:
  H_classical = L²/(2I) + mgh
  
A beyond-classical term would be:
  H_beyond = H_classical + λ(L · ∇φ_gravity)
  where φ_gravity is the gravitational potential and λ is a coupling constant.
  
This spin-gravity coupling term would produce an additional force:
  F_beyond = -∇H_beyond = λ ∇(L · ∇φ_gravity)
  
For a precessing gyroscope in a uniform gravitational field (∇φ = g ẑ, ∇∇φ = 0): F_beyond = 0.
For a non-uniform field or during anti-precession (rapid change in L direction): F_beyond ≠ 0.

This is the mathematical structure of Woodward's Mach effect (transient mass fluctuations) or Tajmar's gravitomagnetic frame-dragging proposals, both of which seek beyond-Newtonian spin-gravity coupling.

The beyond-Newtonian claim: a system with L_total = 0 but T > 0 can generate a net force without reaction mass.   where δm = (P_transient)/(4πGρ_0 c²) × (∂²E/∂t²) is the transient mass fluctuation and a is the acceleration.

For the counter-spinning magnetic disc: the rotating field modulates the local electromagnetic energy density, which via E = mc² contributes a time-varying mass density δm(t), producing a Mach effect force.

## Newton's Third Law and Counter-Spin

Source tweet: 2039678934200283365 (note_tweet): "How might this be relevant to Newton's Third Law?"

Newton's Third Law: F_AB = -F_BA (equal and opposite reaction).

For conventional propulsion: thrust requires expelling reaction mass (rocket equation: F = v_exhaust × dm/dt).

The counter-spin hypothesis circumvents Newton's Third Law by:
1. Generating force from an interaction with the external field (vacuum, gravitational field) rather than with expelled mass.
2. The "reaction" force is borne by the external field (gravitational field, vacuum), which has effectively infinite mass — so the recoil on the external field is negligible.

This is exactly analogous to how an electromagnet can produce a force on a ferromagnetic object without expelling mass: the "reaction" is borne by the electromagnetic field, which does work on both objects. For UAP propulsion, the claim is that the gravitational field plays the role of the electromagnetic field — the craft pushes against gravity itself.

Classical mechanics forbids this for gravity (unlike EM, gravity is not shielded and has no negative charge to cancel). Beyond-Newtonian frameworks that allow it include:
* Metric engineering (Alcubierre drive): the spacecraft distorts spacetime such that it is in free fall in a locally curved region.
* Gravitomagnetism: frame-dragging in GR creates gravitomagnetic fields that can exert forces on rotating masses analogously to magnetic forces on moving charges. The gravitomagnetic force: F_gm = 2m(v × B_grav) where B_grav = -(G/c²) ∇ × (J/r) and J is the mass-current density.
* Torsion fields (Einstein-Cartan): spin-torsion coupling provides an additional term in the geodesic equation: d²x^μ/dτ² + Γ^μ_νρ (dx^ν/dτ)(dx^ρ/dτ) = -(ℏ/2m) S^μν ∇_ν T where S^μν is the spin tensor and T is the scalar torsion.
