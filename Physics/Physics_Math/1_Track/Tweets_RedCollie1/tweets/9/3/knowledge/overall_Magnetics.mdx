### DC Motor Commutation vs. Three-Phase AC — Historical and Physical Contrast

Source tweet: 1923903179806396615 (note_tweet)

* Early DC motor design: used a split-ring commutator to reverse the sense of magnetism halfway through every rotary cycle. This reversal is a mechanical analog of polarity inversion required to maintain unidirectional torque.

  The commutator performs a discrete half-cycle commutation: at every 180° of rotation, it swaps the current direction through the armature winding, maintaining the condition that the torque T = N·I·A·B·sin(θ) always acts in the same rotational direction.

* Tesla's three-phase power innovation: replaced the commutator with a continuously rotating magnetic field produced by spatially offset coils driven by phase-offset currents. This eliminates the mechanical switching discontinuity and allows smoother torque at arbitrary frequencies.

  The torque in a three-phase induction motor is:
  T = (3 × V² × R₂/s) / (ωs × [(R₁ + R₂/s)² + (X₁ + X₂)²])
  where s = slip, ωs = synchronous angular velocity, R and X are resistance and reactance of stator (subscript 1) and rotor (subscript 2).

* Context posed by the author: this progression from DC commutation → three-phase AC is used as an analogy for a further progression toward "anti-gravity" — implying that just as three-phase power superseded commutated DC by eliminating the mechanical half-cycle reversal, a similarly revolutionary principle might govern UAP propulsion by eliminating the thrust-reaction constraint of Newtonian mechanics.
