## 1. Compact "Hot" Nuclear Fusion as Primary Energy Source

Source: 1928228490572353673.yaml (note_tweet)

The central energy claim in this corpus is a compact nuclear fusion device operating
at 50 mm scale:

"plans have also been drawn for 'compact' nuclear fusion to make energy. Not 'cold'
fusion, but 'hot' fusion on a very small scale (50 mm)."

The claimed device uses standard hot-fusion physics (D-D or D-T thermonuclear reactions
requiring collision energies in the keV to MeV range), not cold fusion (which claims
room-temperature nuclear reactions via lattice confinement — not supported by mainstream
physics). The scale distinction is critical: conventional tokamaks and inertial confinement
systems are large (meter-to-building scale). The author claims a 50 mm cylindrical device
achieves the same physics.

### Energy Produced per D-D Reaction
  D + D --> ^3He + n + 3.27 MeV     (branch 1, ~50% probability)
  D + D --> T + p + 4.03 MeV         (branch 2, ~50% probability)
  Average Q per D-D reaction: ~3.65 MeV = 5.85 * 10^-13 J

### Claimed Enhancement: AC Magnetic Confinement Off-Electrode
The device is described as working "somewhat like a Farnsworth fusor, but with an AC
magnetic field surrounding the cylindrical gas chamber (given 40 kV DC power)."

Experimental power parameters documented:
  - 40 kV DC applied to inner electrode (ion acceleration voltage)
  - Surrounding AC electromagnet (separate power supply)
  - Gas chamber: cylindrical, ~50 mm scale
  - Test gas: argon (diagnostic proxy for deuterium ion paths)

The author claims this configuration achieves "two-way head-on collisions" off-electrode,
effectively doubling the center-of-mass energy:
  E_CM = 2 * q * V = 2 * e * 40 kV = 80 keV

Deuterium-deuterium fusion cross-section sigma(E) at 80 keV (center-of-mass) is
significantly higher than at 40 keV. The cross-section sigma_DD peaks around 100-250 keV:
  sigma_DD(80 keV) ≈ 10^-29 m^2 to 10^-28 m^2 (order of magnitude from evaluated nuclear data)

The claim that the white luminous region in the argon experiment shifts off-electrode
under AC magnetic influence is presented as empirical support (not yet with deuterium).

### Video Evidence
The MDX transcription of video 13_1928225927152160832_video.mdx records: "AC power 40 kV, 6 kW."
These are the electrical parameters of the experimental compact fusion device while operating:
  - Accelerating voltage: 40 kV
  - Input power: 6 kW = 6000 W

For a Farnsworth-style fusor, electrical power input of 6 kW at 40 kV implies:
  Current: I = P/V = 6000 W / 40000 V = 150 mA

At 150 mA, the number of ions hitting the inner electrode per second (if all current is
ionic): n_ions = I/e = 0.15 / 1.602e-19 ≈ 9.4 * 10^17 ions/second

## 6. Energy from E = MC^2 as Crop Circle Code

Source: 1928228490572353673.yaml (note_tweet)

"How to misinterpret 'E = MC^2'? Of course they mean 'nuclear fusion'!"

The author claims that crop circles encoding "E = MC^2" symbolism are intended to
communicate nuclear fusion (mass-energy conversion through nuclear reactions), not
simply Einstein's rest-mass-energy equivalence. The broader implication: the "plans"
drawn by ETs in crop circles are engineering instructions for fusion energy devices.

The D-D fusion energy yield:
  E = Delta_m * c^2
  Delta_m ≈ 0.003510 u per reaction
  c = 2.998 * 10^8 m/s
  c^2 = 8.988 * 10^16 J/kg

  E_per_reaction = 0.003510 * 1.6605e-27 kg * 8.988e16 J/kg = 5.24 * 10^-13 J = 3.27 MeV

For comparison, deuterium mass available per gram of heavy water (D2O):
  Molar mass D2O ≈ 20 g/mol
  Moles D per gram D2O: 2/20 = 0.1 mol/g
  Atoms D per gram: 0.1 * 6.022e23 = 6.022e22
  Pair reactions per gram: 3.011e22
  Energy per gram D2O: 3.011e22 * 3.27e6 * 1.602e-19 J = 1.576e10 J ≈ 15.76 GJ/g

This is approximately 4.4 million kWh per gram of heavy water — a high energy density
comparable to fission (~82 GJ/g for U-235), representing the motivation for the
"lots of free energy for the world" claim if the compact 50 mm fusion device works.
