## 3. Counter-Spinning Flywheel: Two Novel Lift Mechanisms

Source: 2045269852815016297.yaml (note_tweet)

### Mechanism A: Spinning Magnet Rising + Force Applied to Counter-Spinning Flywheel

"Firstly, the rise of a spinning magnet up a pole, which then applies its upward force
to the center of a counter-spinning, magnetic flywheel (rather than say to a stationary
'bead'). Could this bypass Newton's 3rd Law?"

Physical scenario:
  - A permanent magnet spins about its axis (omega_mag) while translating upward along a
    vertical pole guide (constrained translational DOF along Z).
  - A separate magnetic flywheel spins at omega_fw in the opposite direction at the top
    of the pole.
  - As the spinning magnet approaches the counter-spinning flywheel from below, it
    exerts an upward force on the flywheel center.

Why this might produce net lift: If the interaction transfers upward linear momentum to
the flywheel while the spinning magnet decelerates (transferring rotational energy), the
net system could experience a net upward linear impulse without equal downward reaction
on the ground — this is the Newton's 3rd Law question. (Classical mechanics requires
no net impulse on an isolated system; this experiment tests whether this holds.)

### Mechanism B: Two-Phase Pulsed Magnetic Cycle

"Secondly, the preliminary motion of a spinning magnet downward, away from the center of
an attractive magnetic flywheel, followed by its upward force or pulsed motion as a
second step, when the flywheel spins rapidly in an opposite direction."

Phase 1 (downward stroke): Spinning magnet descends below the flywheel center.
                            The flywheel is attractive; the magnet is attracted upward
                            but constrained to move downward first (mechanical setup).
                            Work is stored in the attractive potential energy.

Phase 2 (upward stroke / impulse): The flywheel rapidly counter-spins or reverses.
                            The magnetic interaction switches from attractive to
                            repulsive (or changes phase), producing a strong upward
                            force pulse on the spinning magnet.

Net cycle: if the upward impulse in Phase 2 exceeds the downward displacement force in
Phase 1, the magnet gains net upward linear momentum each cycle.

"This second mechanism seems novel, and has never been studied before (on Earth)."

Author claims this is unprecedented in terrestrial physics literature. He is developing
it toward "novel space propulsion."

  (a) Rotating magnetic field in XY-plane (from N-phase coils): creates the time-varying
