## 1. Mutual Inductance and AC Induction Force — Complete Mathematical Chain

### Mutual Inductance Definition
For a solenoid (primary) and coaxial ring (secondary), the mutual inductance M(z) is a
function of the axial separation z:

  M(z) = (mu_0 * pi * a^2 * b^2 * N) / (2 * (a^2 + z^2)^(3/2))   [simplified Neumann formula]

where:
  a = solenoid radius (m)
  b = ring radius (m, b ≈ a for tight coupling)
  N = number of solenoid turns
  z = axial separation (m)
  mu_0 = 4*pi * 10^-7 T*m/A (permeability of free space)

The gradient of M with z:
  dM/dz = -3 * M(z) * z / (a^2 + z^2)

This gradient is negative (M decreases as ring moves away from solenoid) and drives the
expression for axial force.

### Primary Current
  I_p(t) = I_0 * cos(omega*t)

### Induced EMF in Ring
  EMF = -M * dI_p/dt = M * I_0 * omega * sin(omega*t)

### Phasor Form of Induced Current in Ring
  I_s = (j*omega*M*I_0) / (R + j*omega*L_s)

  |I_s| = (omega*M*I_0) / sqrt(R^2 + omega^2*L_s^2)

  Phase angle (relative to I_p): phi_s = pi/2 - arctan(omega*L_s/R)

### Instantaneous Axial Force
  F_z(t) = I_s(t) * I_p(t) * (dM/dz)

### Time-Averaged Axial Force (the "lift")
  <F_z> = (1/2) * Re[ I_s_phasor * I_p_phasor* ] * (dM/dz)

  = (1/2) * (omega^2 * M^2 * I_0^2 * L_s) / (R^2 + omega^2*L_s^2) * |dM/dz|

Note: since dM/dz < 0 and the ring is above the solenoid (z > 0), the product gives a
positive (upward) force: <F_z> > 0.

### Frequency Dependence
Low-frequency regime (omega*L_s << R):
  <F_z> ≈ (omega^2 * M^2 * I_0^2 * L_s) / R^2  ~ omega^2

High-frequency regime (omega*L_s >> R):
  <F_z> ≈ (M^2 * I_0^2) / (2*L_s)  = constant (independent of omega)

### Optimal Frequency for Maximum Lift
Differentiate <F_z> with respect to omega^2 and set to zero:
  d<F_z>/d(omega^2) = 0

  (R^2 + omega^2*L_s^2) * L_s - omega^2*L_s^2 * 2*L_s = 0   [simplifying]
  R^2 = omega^2 * L_s^2
  omega_optimal = R / L_s

At omega_optimal, the maximum time-averaged lift force is:
  <F_z>_max = (M^2 * I_0^2) / (4*L_s) * |dM/dz|

## 2. Rotating Magnetic Field from N-Phase AC System

### N-Phase Current Set
For an N-phase system with phase-separated currents:
  I_k(t) = I_0 * cos(omega*t - k * 2*pi/N),  k = 0, 1, ..., N-1

### Magnetic Field Vector from N-Phase Winding
If each phase produces a field B_k in direction (cos(k*2*pi/N), sin(k*2*pi/N), 0) in
the XY-plane:

  B_total(t) = sum_{k=0}^{N-1} B_k(t)
             = B_0 * sum_{k=0}^{N-1} cos(omega*t - k*2*pi/N) * (cos(k*2*pi/N) * x_hat + sin(k*2*pi/N) * y_hat)

  B_x(t) = B_0 * sum_{k=0}^{N-1} cos(omega*t - k*2*pi/N) * cos(k*2*pi/N)
          = (N*B_0/2) * cos(omega*t)    [using orthogonality of the sum]

  B_y(t) = (N*B_0/2) * sin(omega*t)

  |B_total| = N*B_0/2 = constant

The N-phase system creates a rotating field vector of constant magnitude (N*B_0/2)
rotating at angular velocity omega in the XY-plane. This is the mathematical basis for:
  - 3-phase (N=3): B_total rotates at omega with |B| = 3*B_0/2
  - 5-phase (N=5): B_total rotates at omega with |B| = 5*B_0/2

The 5-phase system has lower torque ripple because the individual phase contributions
overlap more smoothly — the harmonic content of |B_total(t)| decreases as N increases.

## 3. Dipole-Dipole Interaction Between Two Spinning Magnetic Dipoles

### Static Case (Non-Spinning)
Two magnetic dipoles m1 (at origin) and m2 (at position r = r*z_hat):

  U(r) = (mu_0 / 4*pi*r^3) * (m1 . m2 - 3*(m1 . z_hat)*(m2 . z_hat))

For m1 and m2 aligned along Z-axis (m1 = m2 = m * z_hat):
  U = (mu_0 / 4*pi*r^3) * (m^2 - 3*m^2) = -2*mu_0*m^2 / (4*pi*r^3)  [attractive]

For m1 and m2 anti-aligned (m1 = +m*z_hat, m2 = -m*z_hat):
  U = +2*mu_0*m^2 / (4*pi*r^3)  [repulsive]

Axial force (Z-component):
  F_z = -dU/dr = -6*mu_0*m1*m2 / (4*pi*r^4) * (cos_alignment_factor)

### Spinning Case (Counter-Rotating Dipoles)
If dipole 1 spins about Z at omega_1 and dipole 2 spins at omega_2 = -omega_1 (counter-rotation):

  m1(t) = m * (sin(alpha)*cos(omega_1*t + phi_1) * x_hat + sin(alpha)*sin(omega_1*t + phi_1) * y_hat + cos(alpha) * z_hat)

  m2(t) = m * (sin(alpha)*cos(-omega_1*t + phi_2) * x_hat + sin(alpha)*sin(-omega_1*t + phi_2) * y_hat + cos(alpha) * z_hat)

The time-averaged interaction energy <U> includes:
  (a) A static term from the Z-components of both dipoles (constant contribution)
  (b) Oscillating cross-terms at 2*omega_1, which time-average to zero for the field

  <m1 . m2> = cos^2(alpha)*m^2 + sin^2(alpha)*m^2 * <cos(2*omega_1*t + phi_1-phi_2)>

For counter-rotation, phi_1-phi_2 evolves at 2*omega_1*t — the oscillating term averages
to zero. The static contribution:
  <m1 . m2>_static = cos^2(alpha)*m^2

  <U> = (mu_0/4*pi*r^3) * [m^2*cos^2(alpha) - 3*m^2*cos^2(alpha)]
      = -2*mu_0*m^2*cos^2(alpha) / (4*pi*r^3)

This is always attractive (for the Z-aligned components). However, the instantaneous
force oscillates at 2*omega_1, and for specific phase relationships between the spinning
elements, the author proposes there may be periods of net upward (repulsive) force impulse.

The "pulsed motion" mechanism exploits the non-zero AC component of the interaction:
the instantaneous force F_z(t) has peaks at intervals of pi/omega_1, and if the geometry
is arranged so that upward force pulses are larger than downward force pulses, the
time-integrated impulse over a cycle may be non-zero.

## 4. Center-of-Mass Frame Kinetic Energy for Head-On Ion Collisions

Source: 1928228490572353673.yaml (note_tweet)

For two ions each accelerated through voltage V with charge q and mass m:

  Speed after acceleration: v = sqrt(2*q*V/m)

For head-on collision (velocity vectors anti-parallel):
  Relative velocity: v_rel = 2*v

  Center-of-mass energy:
    E_CM = (1/2) * mu_reduced * v_rel^2
    where mu_reduced = m/2 (for identical particles)

  E_CM = (1/2) * (m/2) * (2*v)^2 = (1/2) * (m/2) * 4 * (2*q*V/m)
       = (1/2) * (m/2) * 8*q*V/m = 2*q*V

  For V = 40 kV, q = e:
    E_CM = 2 * e * 40 kV = 80 keV

The "effective 40 + 40 = 80 kV" in the note_tweet corresponds exactly to this formula:
E_CM = 2*q*V where V = 40 kV — doubling the effective collision energy compared to a
single-beam fixed-target geometry.

The key advantage over a single-beam (one ion hitting a stationary target) geometry:
  E_CM (beam-target) = (1/2) * mu * v^2 = (1/2)*(m/2)*(2qV/m) = q*V/2 = 20 keV

  Ratio: E_CM (head-on) / E_CM (beam-target) = 80 keV / 20 keV = 4

Head-on collisions quadruple the center-of-mass collision energy compared to a fixed-target
geometry at the same accelerating voltage.

## 8. Gyroscope Precession — Angular Velocity Mathematics

Source: 1943083067482190123.yaml (tweet)

  Omega_precession = tau / L_spin = (M*g*r) / (I*omega_spin)

  d(L)/dt = tau (torque equation: gravitational torque drives precession)

  For a disk gyroscope: I = (1/2)*M*R_disk^2
  Omega_precession = (M*g*r) / ((1/2)*M*R_disk^2 * omega_spin)
                   = 2*g*r / (R_disk^2 * omega_spin)

"At low rates of spin": omega_spin small --> Omega_precession large (fast precession)
The non-inertial (Euler angle) description in body frame requires the full Euler equations:

  I_1 * dOmega_1/dt - (I_2 - I_3) * Omega_2 * Omega_3 = tau_1
  I_2 * dOmega_2/dt - (I_3 - I_1) * Omega_3 * Omega_1 = tau_2
  I_3 * dOmega_3/dt - (I_1 - I_2) * Omega_1 * Omega_2 = tau_3

For an axially symmetric body (I_1 = I_2 = I_perp, I_3 = I_parallel):
The nutation frequency is:
  omega_nutation = (I_parallel - I_perp) * omega_spin / I_perp

At low omega_spin: omega_nutation is small, and the simple precession formula above applies.

## 9. Oberth Maneuver — Delta-V and Energy Mathematics

Source: 1984594883047473303.yaml (note_tweet)

  Specific orbital energy: epsilon = v^2/2 - mu/r
  At perihelion (r = r_p): v_p = sqrt(mu * (2/r_p - 1/a))  [vis-viva equation]

  For a hyperbolic trajectory (3I/ATLAS is interstellar, so e > 1):
    v_infinity^2 = v_p^2 - 2*mu/r_p  (excess hyperbolic velocity squared)

  After a prograde burn Delta_v at perihelion:
    v_p' = v_p + Delta_v
    New v_infinity'^2 = (v_p + Delta_v)^2 - 2*mu/r_p
                      = v_infinity^2 + 2*v_p*Delta_v + Delta_v^2

  The kinetic energy gain at infinity:
    Delta(KE_infinity) = (1/2)*(v_infinity'^2 - v_infinity^2)
                       = v_p * Delta_v + (1/2)*Delta_v^2

  For small Delta_v: Delta(KE_infinity) ≈ v_p * Delta_v

  Comparing to the same Delta_v burn at infinity (v_approach ≈ v_infinity):
    Delta(KE_at_infinity) = v_infinity * Delta_v + (1/2)*Delta_v^2

  Ratio of energy gains:
    Oberth efficiency = (v_p * Delta_v) / (v_infinity * Delta_v) = v_p / v_infinity > 1

  For 3I/ATLAS, v_infinity ≈ 60 km/s (interstellar), v_p at perihelion near Sun
  (r_p ~ 0.4 AU) can be estimated from energy conservation:
    v_p = sqrt(v_infinity^2 + 2*mu_sun/r_p)

    = sqrt(3.6e9 + 4.44e9) = sqrt(8.04e9) ≈ 89.7 km/s

  Oberth efficiency for 3I/ATLAS: 89.7/60 ≈ 1.49 -- 49% more efficient than burning at infinity
