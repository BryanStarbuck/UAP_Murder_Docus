## 5. XY-Plane Interactions Causing Z-Axis Altitude Change

The mechanism proposed operates as follows:

Step 1 (XY-plane): The three-phase stator coils apply a rotating magnetic field B(t) = B_0
rotating in the XY-plane at frequency ω = 2πf (f = 40-50 kHz). This field is confined to
the horizontal (XY) plane at each instant.

Step 2 (XY-plane → helical): The spatial arrangement of the three coils at 120° separation
in XY produces a field with a helical component along Z as the field rotates. This is because
the rotating field vector, when sampled along the Z-axis of the coil stack, traces a helix.

Step 3 (Z-axis torque): The helical field component applies a torque to the spinning magnet
that has both an XY component (maintaining spin in the XY-plane) and a Z component (the
"upward torsional force"). The Z-component of the torque arises because the helical field
at different Z positions along the magnet pushes different parts of the magnet's pole faces
in slightly different directions — net result is a force along Z.

Step 4 (Z-axis translation): The Z-component of torque, when applied to a gyroscopically
stable spinning magnet (with high angular momentum L = Iω along Z), produces a net upward
force (translation along Z) rather than just precession, because the fast-spin mechanism
suppresses the reaction.

This XY→Z force conversion is the key proposed mechanism, and it is analogous to how
astrophysical jets eject matter along Z from a rotating (XY-plane) accretion structure.

## 7. Gyroscopic Precession → Upward Translation

For the spinning disc magnet in the device:
  L = I × ω  (angular momentum along the spin axis, initially vertical = Z)

When the 3-phase helical field applies a horizontal torque τ_horiz to the tilted magnet:
  dL/dt = τ_horiz

This causes precession of L about the Z-axis. If L is tilted at angle θ from Z:
  Precession rate: Ω_prec = τ_horiz / (L sin θ)

The precession is in the horizontal (XY) plane for a gravity-dominated gyroscope. However,
in the proposed device, the helical magnetic field also applies a Z-directed torque τ_z,
which does NOT cause precession but instead causes the spin axis to tilt further or produces
a net Z-direction force via:
  F_z = τ_z / L × (something depending on geometry)

The exact mechanism by which τ_z → F_z (torque → translation) is the "physics problem"
that Drew states remains to be fully understood: "the underlying physics remains hidden
(for now)." (tweet 1938895917509419031)

## 10. Ball Joint Requirement for Full 3D Precession

Tweet 1943115252838863247:
"I may need an 'axial ball joint' to get the spinning magnetic UFO motion, drawn in crops 2018?"

For the spinning magnet to respond optimally to the rotating helical field and generate
maximum Z-axis lift force:
  * The magnet's spin axis must be free to precess in 3D (not constrained to one axis)
  * A simple fixed bearing allows rotation about one axis only → cannot precess → gyroscopic
    response is blocked → reduced Z-force
  * An axial ball joint (ball-and-socket bearing) allows the spin axis to tilt and precess
    freely in any direction → full gyroscopic response → maximum Z-force generation

Hardware (txt file 3_1943114565853810689.txt): ball-and-socket joint, threaded stud end,
rubber seal washer, polished stainless steel spherical ball housing, flanged cup socket.
All components machined to aerospace tolerances to minimize friction losses at the ball
surface, which would damp the precession and reduce the net upward force.
