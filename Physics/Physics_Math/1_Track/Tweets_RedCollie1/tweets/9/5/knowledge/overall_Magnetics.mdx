## 5. Counter-Spin Magnetic Field Phenomenon

Tweet 2015150280770490491 (referenced parent tweet, context):
"Teaching lesson, UFO physics 101. Why does this 3-phase magnetic field counter-spin in
certain places?"
Response by Drew: "Study in which direction the local electric current flows."

This refers to the well-known phenomenon in polyphase rotating fields where, depending on
the phase sequence (ABC vs. ACB), the field rotates either clockwise or counter-clockwise
as seen from a fixed observation point. In a multi-coil stator, the local field direction
in the gaps between coils can appear to counter-rotate relative to the primary rotation sense
due to the superposition of adjacent phase contributions. This is a classical electromagnetism
result but is presented here as foundational to understanding the upward torque mechanism.

## 7. AC Magnetic Field for Compact Fusion (Magnetic Confinement Analog)

Tweet 2032946669650878643 (note_tweet):
"Deuterium oxide can release deuterium gas, when reacting with metals. Free D2 gas can then
fuse, by adding 50 kV inward DC voltage, when surrounded by an AC magnetic field."
"Slide 2 > Lorentz spin motion when surrounded by a DC magnetic field."
"Slide 3 > two oscillating spin motions when surrounded by an AC magnetic field."

Magnetic field roles in compact fusion:
  * DC magnetic field → Lorentz force produces circular/helical ion orbit (cyclotron motion).
    Ion follows spiral path toward center: radius r = mv/(qB), where m = deuteron mass,
    v = ion velocity, q = 2e (deuterium charge), B = applied DC field magnitude.
  * AC magnetic field → two oscillating spin motions: ions undergo alternating left-hand and
    right-hand helical paths due to the time-varying B direction. This creates two distinct
    curved approach paths for deuterium ions heading toward the central negative electrode.
    The ions collide slightly off-center (avoiding the electrode), maximizing gas-gas fusion
    events and minimizing unproductive electrode collisions.

The AC magnetic field in this context functions as an alternating-gradient magnetic lens,
continuously refocusing the ion trajectories to increase collision cross-section between
deuterium nuclei. This is distinct from standard magnetic mirror or tokamak confinement but
is qualitatively similar to alternating-gradient focusing used in particle accelerators.
