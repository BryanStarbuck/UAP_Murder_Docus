## 3. Lorentz Force and Homopolar Motor Physics

The Lorentz force law:
  F = q(E + v × B)

For a conductor carrying current I in magnetic field B (force per unit length):
  dF/dl = I(dl̂ × B)

In a homopolar motor:
  * A conducting disc rotates about the axis of a coaxial permanent magnet
  * Radial current I flows from center to rim (or rim to center) of the disc
  * Axial magnetic field B_z from the permanent magnet
  * Lorentz force on each current element: dF = I dr × B_z ẑ
  * Net torque: τ = ∫(r × dF) produces azimuthal acceleration of the disc

The relevant equation for homopolar motor torque:
  τ = (1/2) × B_z × I × (R_outer^2 - R_inner^2)

where R_outer and R_inner are the outer and inner radii of the conducting disc.

The author's claim: in the spinning-disc + 3-phase field configuration, the torque experienced
by the disc exceeds τ predicted by the above formula for the wire mass alone, suggesting an
additional torque contribution from the interaction of the disc's own spin angular momentum
with the applied field.

## 4. Gyroscopic Precession and Upward Force

For a spinning gyroscope (disc magnet) with angular momentum vector L = I_disc × ω:
  * I_disc = (1/2) m R^2 (moment of inertia of solid disc, radius R, mass m)
  * ω = spin angular velocity (rad/s)
  * |L| = I_disc × ω

Precession under applied torque τ_applied:
  τ_applied = dL/dt = Ω_prec × L

where Ω_prec is the precession angular velocity vector.

For precession due to gravity (standard gyroscope on a pivot):
  Ω_prec = M × g × r_cm / (I_disc × ω)   [horizontal precession about vertical axis]

For precession due to an applied torque from the rotating 3-phase magnetic field:
  Ω_prec = τ_field / (I_disc × ω)

If the applied torque τ_field has a tilted direction relative to L (which it does, because
the helical magnetic field has both radial and axial components), then the precession Ω_prec
has a vertical component. The vertical component of the precession of a disc whose L is
tilted relative to the vertical axis corresponds to an angular velocity of the disc's axis
sweeping out a cone — this is the "upward spin and precess" behavior described in the 1991
crop picture interpretation.

The author proposes that this gyroscopic precession, when driven by the helical rotating
magnetic field rather than by gravity, produces a net upward force component because the
reaction force to the field torque is reduced by the fast-spin mechanism described above.

## 5. Astrophysical Jet Physics: Faraday Rotation Measure

Faraday rotation of linearly polarized radio emission propagating through a magnetized plasma:

  Δθ = RM × λ^2

where:
  * Δθ = rotation of polarization angle (radians)
  * RM = Rotation Measure (rad/m^2) = (e^3 / 2π m_e^2 c^4) × ∫ n_e B_∥ dl
  * e = electron charge, m_e = electron mass, c = speed of light
  * n_e = electron number density (cm^-3)
  * B_∥ = component of B along the line of sight (μG)
  * dl = path length element (pc)
  * λ = wavelength of observation (m)

RM is positive if B_∥ points toward the observer, negative if away (by convention).

  * The helix pitch angle α satisfies: tan(α) = B_φ / B_z

For a relativistic jet with Lorentz factor γ, the helical field pitch is modified by special
relativistic aberration and Doppler boosting, explaining the asymmetry between jet (approaching,
brighter) and counterjet (receding, fainter) rotation measure maps.

## 9. Thermodynamics: Carnot Limit as Baseline Comparison

From txt file 13_1923907846153928704_preview.txt (steam engine diagram description):
Thermodynamic efficiency of a heat engine:
  η = 1 - T_C / T_H

where T_C = cold reservoir temperature, T_H = hot reservoir temperature (both in Kelvin).

The Rankine/Carnot cycle implemented in a steam engine:
  1. Isobaric heating in boiler: Q_H input raises water to steam
  2. Adiabatic (approximately polytropic) expansion in cylinder: steam does work W on piston
  3. Piston rod → crank → flywheel: linear mechanical work converted to rotational kinetic energy
  4. Flywheel stores energy between power strokes
