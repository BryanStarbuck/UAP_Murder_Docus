## "Transitional Magnetism": Proposed New Physical Regime

Source: tweet 1924058591545454850, note_tweet.

The author reports receiving the concept of "transitional magnetism" from "unknown sources" in
five "lucid physics dreams." The key claims about this regime:

2. It requires THREE-PHASE ELECTROMAGNETS to apply the spin force (NOT permanent magnets).
3. Personal antigravity devices are claimed possible (referencing the CARET document studies).

Relationship to known magnetomechanical effects:

A. Barnett Effect (1915): A ferromagnet rotating at angular velocity omega acquires a
   magnetization M = chi * omega / gamma, where chi is the magnetic susceptibility and
   gamma = ge/(2m_e) is the gyromagnetic ratio. This is an established effect: rotation induces
   magnetization through spin-orbit coupling in the crystal lattice.

B. Einstein-de Haas Effect (1915): Angular momentum is transferred from spin polarization to
   mechanical rotation. When a ferromagnet is demagnetized, mechanical angular momentum appears.
   Conversely, applying a magnetic field to align spins can induce mechanical rotation.

Both A and B are established and well-characterized. "Transitional magnetism" as described by
the author appears to be a regime where these magnetomechanical effects become strong enough
at high angular velocity to produce macroscopic translational forces beyond those predicted by
classical magnetostatics.

A candidate theoretical framework: At high omega, the Coriolis force in the rotating frame of
the magnet acts on the magnetic domain structure and conduction electrons:
  F_Coriolis = -2m * (omega x v)
This could distort the current distribution in the magnet's crystal lattice, producing an
asymmetric charge distribution and thus a non-zero net force on the magnet in the lab frame.

The claimed requirement for three-phase (rotating external field) rather than DC spin-up suggests
the synchronized rotating external field and magnet spin create a resonant coupling condition
not present with DC-only spin-up. This resonance between the precessing magnetic moments (at the
Larmor frequency omega_L = gamma * B) and the externally imposed rotation (omega) could produce
parametric amplification of the magnetomechanical coupling.

## Counter-Spin Action > Reaction

Source: tweet 2039849887341396035.

"Can we greatly reduce the impact of Newton's 3rd Law on a nearby object, by setting it into
rapid counter-spin, before spinning another object, so as to produce action > reaction?"

This is stated as "a problem from classical physics [that] does not seem to be in a textbook
anywhere."

Physical context: The De Laval nozzle (rocket) and Magnus effect show that rotation combined
with translational motion produces asymmetric forces not captured by naive N3L bookkeeping.
More relevant: the gyroscopic precession case, where a torque on a spinning body produces
precession rather than the expected acceleration in the torque direction.

For counter-spinning bodies:
* Body 1 spinning at omega_1 (CW)
* Body 2 pre-spun at -omega_2 (CCW, counter-spin)
* Body 1 then exerts a torque on Body 2.

In classical rigid-body mechanics, the interaction between two bodies through contact forces
obeys N3L exactly. But for non-contact magnetic interactions between spinning magnetic bodies,
the coupling is through the field (not through direct contact), and the field itself carries
momentum. The Abraham-Lorentz-Dirac force allows for non-N3L-local interactions mediated by
fields.

The specific claim: if Body 2 is pre-spun CCW, and Body 1 exerts a CW torque on Body 2,
the final CW spin rate of Body 2 is GREATER than what N3L classical prediction gives for the
transferred angular momentum. The "extra" angular momentum comes from the de-spinning of Body 2
from -omega_2 to +omega_final, and by energy conservation (E = mv^2), this allows a larger
final velocity than if Body 2 started at rest.

This is not truly action > reaction -- it is a case where the pre-stored kinetic energy of
counter-spin augments the coupling. But the author frames it as producing a net asymmetric
translational force, which IS a beyond-N3L claim.

## Spin-Dependent Positional Shift: Beyond Static Magnetostatics

Source: tweet 2045383245861392487, video transcription 13_2045371465424568321_video.mdx.

Classical magnetostatics predicts:
* The equilibrium position of the central S magnet inside the 4N flywheel ring is at z = 0
  (the plane of the flywheel ring), regardless of the magnet's spin rate.
* Magnetostatics deals with static charges and steady currents; the internal spin state of the
  magnet (its rotation about Z) does not enter into the static force calculation.

Observed: when the central magnet and outer flywheel are counter-spinning, the central magnet
shifts to z < 0 (below the flywheel plane).

This spin-dependent positional shift is BEYOND MAGNETOSTATICS. Possible mechanisms:

1. Gyromagnetic levitation: the spinning permanent magnet is a gyroscope. In the field gradient
   of the flywheel ring, the gyroscopic response to the radially-directed restoring force
   (which would classically push the magnet back toward z=0) is a precession -- the magnet
   precesses rather than returning to z=0. The precession orbit could have a non-zero time-
   averaged z-displacement.

2. Radiation pressure of rotating fields: the spinning magnet radiates electromagnetic energy
   (as a rotating dipole, power P = (mu_0 * omega^4 * mu^2) / (6*pi*c^3)). The radiation
   carries momentum. In the presence of the flywheel's field, the radiation pattern is asymmetric,
   and the radiation pressure on the central magnet has a net Z-component.

3. Magnetohydrodynamic drag: if the spinning magnet displaces the adjacent rotating electromagnetic
   field (like a spinning propeller displacing air), the reaction of the field on the magnet has
   a Z-component related to the field momentum flow. This is the "propeller in space" model
   used by the author (compare to tweet 1928347152150896786: "magnetic spinning propeller").

## Polarity-Independent Upward Force: Non-Classical Force Law

Source: tweet 1928347152150896786.

Classical Stern-Gerlach force: F_z = mu_z * (dB_z/dz)
* mu_z = +|mu| for N-up magnet: F_z = +|mu| * (dB_z/dz) -- could be positive or negative
  depending on whether d B_z / dz is positive or negative.
* mu_z = -|mu| for S-up magnet: F_z reverses sign.

The claim that BOTH orientations give the same upward force requires:
  F_z(mu_z = +|mu|) = F_z(mu_z = -|mu|) > 0

This is impossible in the classical Stern-Gerlach framework, since F_z is linear in mu_z.

A force law consistent with polarity-independence:
  F_z = f(mu_z^2, omega^2, B_rot^2, ...) = f(|mu|^2, omega^2, |B_rot|^2, ...)

Such a quadratic dependence on the dipole moment would arise if the force is a second-order
(ponderomotive) effect -- for example, the ponderomotive force on a magnetic dipole in a
rapidly oscillating field:
  F_ponderomotive = -(mu_z^2) / (2*m) * gradient(|B|^2)

This is negative (downward) and proportional to mu_z^2 (polarity-independent) but would push
the magnet toward weaker field, not toward stronger field.

An upward polarity-independent force consistent with spinning could arise from:
* A spin-Hall effect for magnetic moments: the coupling of spin angular momentum and orbital
  momentum of the magnet in the rotating field produces a transverse (Z-direction) force
  proportional to omega^2 and |mu|^2.
* Such effects are observed at the quantum scale (spin Hall effect in condensed matter) but not
  yet demonstrated for macroscopic permanent magnets at laboratory scales.

## Newton's 3rd Law: Scope and Known Limits

Beyond what Newtonian mechanics formally covers:

1. Field-mediated forces: N3L applies to contact forces between particles. For electromagnetic
   forces mediated by fields, N3L applies to the SYSTEM of particles + fields, not to the
   particles alone. The electromagnetic field carries momentum, and the total momentum
   (mechanical + field) is conserved. But the mechanical sub-system alone can violate N3L,
   with the field taking up the slack.

2. Non-inertial reference frames: In the rotating frame of the spinning magnet, there are
   fictitious forces (Coriolis, centrifugal) that have no Newtonian reaction partner in the lab
   frame. The transformation between rotating and inertial frames is via:
     F_inertial = F_rotating + m * omega x (omega x r) + 2m * omega x v_rotating + m * d(omega)/dt * r

3. Abraham-Lorentz force: A radiating charge experiences a self-force:
     F_AL = (mu_0 * q^2) / (6*pi*c) * da/dt  (where a = acceleration)
   This is the radiation reaction force. It has no clear Newtonian "partner force" -- it is the
   electromagnetic field's back-reaction on the accelerating charge.

4. Quantum vacuum effects: Casimir force between conducting plates is an attractive force arising
   from zero-point vacuum fluctuations. It has no classical Newtonian reaction partner (the
   "reaction" is against the quantum vacuum). At macroscopic scales, these forces are tiny, but
   they demonstrate that forces without conventional N3L pairs exist.

The author's "pushing down against space" framing maps conceptually onto #1 above: the spinning
magnetic system transfers reaction force to the electromagnetic field / vacuum structure.

## Astronomical Jet Analogy: Helical Fields and Axial Acceleration

Source: tweet 1924058591545454850.

"Helical magnetic fields like in an astronomical jet."

In astrophysical MHD jets, the acceleration mechanism (Blandford-Payne):
* Magnetic field lines thread a rotating accretion disc.
* Field line footpoints are anchored in the disc; as the disc rotates, field lines wind into
  a helix above and below the disc plane.
* The tension in the helical B_phi component exerts a centrifugal force on plasma along the
  field line:
    F_centrifugal along field = (v_phi^2 / R) * (component along field line)
* If the field line is inclined > 60° from the disc rotation axis (Blandford-Payne criterion),
  plasma is centrifugally ejected along the field line -- effectively launched up the helix.
* This is a non-Newtonian acceleration in the sense that the driving force is the centrifugal
  force in the rotating frame, not a rocket exhaust or contact force.

Applied to the spinning magnet: the rapid rotation of the permanent magnet in the three-phase
field winds up the field lines around the magnet into a helical topology. If the helical winding
geometry satisfies an analogous criterion to the Blandford-Payne 60° threshold, the rotating
plasma / field momentum is launched upward along the spin axis, and the reaction on the magnet
is upward (the magnet is pushed by the reaction of the field momentum it is launching upward).

This is a non-Newtonian propulsion in a vacuum: the driving force is the field's own tension and
topology, not any expelled reaction mass.
