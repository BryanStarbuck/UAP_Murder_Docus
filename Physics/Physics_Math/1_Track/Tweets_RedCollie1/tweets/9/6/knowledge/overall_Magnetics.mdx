## Three-Phase Rotating Magnetic Field (Primary Mechanism)

Source: tweets 1928347152150896786, 1964077861780017653, 2045383245861392487, MDX transcriptions.

Three-phase AC power is the central driver of the spinning-magnet propulsion system. Three-phase
power delivers three sinusoidal voltage waveforms at phases 0°, 120°, and 240° (i.e., displaced
by 2π/3 radians). When applied to three electromagnet coils arranged symmetrically (120° apart in
the XY plane), the superposition of their individual magnetic fields produces a resultant field
vector that rotates continuously in the XY plane at the AC frequency.

Mathematical structure of the rotating field (standard three-phase induction theory):
  B_x(t) = B_0 * cos(omega * t)
  B_y(t) = B_0 * cos(omega * t - 2*pi/3)
  B_z(t) = 0  (ideally, for a planar coil arrangement)

The resultant field magnitude is constant: |B| = (3/2) * B_0, rotating at angular frequency omega.

The rotating field exerts a torque on the permanent magnet's dipole moment mu:
  tau = mu x B_rotating

At synchronous speed (magnet spins with the field), the magnet locks in and experiences no
further torque. Below synchronous speed, the slip produces a net torque that accelerates the
magnet. This is the operating regime of the device: AC-driven asynchronous or synchronous
rotation of the permanent magnet.

Experimental configuration (tweet 1928347152150896786):
* Six wire coils of 0.5 mm copper wire
* Three-phase AC power supply
* Central permanent disc magnet: 70 mm diameter, 14 mm hole (annular), 3 mm thick
* Mounted on 6.3 mm aluminum rod (rod diameter << 14 mm hole to avoid mechanical coupling)
* 20 mm silicone bead coupling between magnet and rod (transmits vertical force without
  transmitting rotational torque to the rod, so the rod does NOT spin when the magnet rises)

## Annular Permanent Magnet Configuration

Central magnet: annular (ring) type, South pole up (or North pole up).
Outer flywheel ring: four North pole permanent magnets arranged in a ring.

Magnetic attraction geometry between S central and 4N outer:
* In the non-spinning configuration, the central S magnet sits in the plane of the outer N ring.
  Attraction is purely planar (radial); the Z-component of the attraction force is zero at the
  equilibrium position (magnet centered in the ring plane).
* If the central magnet rises above the ring plane, the off-axis dipole-dipole attraction has a
  downward Z-component pulling it back: F_z = -d(U)/dz where U is the dipole-dipole potential
  energy, which is negative (attractive) and has a restoring minimum at z=0.
* If the central magnet drops below the ring plane, the same analysis yields an upward Z-component
  restoring force.

This creates a magnetic "trap" -- the flywheel ring stabilizes the central magnet vertically
near z=0 plane.

## Lenz's Law / Annular Induction Motor (Outer Ring Mechanism)

Source: tweet 2015155597822558312.

The outer ring of a UFO is proposed to function as an annular induction motor:
* A ring of outer electromagnets generates a rotating magnetic field around the circumference.
* A conducting ring (or ferromagnetic outer shell) inside this rotating field has eddy currents
  induced in it by the changing flux (Faraday's law: EMF = -d(Phi_B)/dt).
* The induced currents, interacting with the rotating field, produce a tangential Lenz force
  that drags the outer shell to rotate in the direction of field rotation.
* This is the operating principle of a linear induction motor, applied to an annular geometry.

The net mechanical configuration: outer annular induction motor spins the UFO's outer shell;
inner three-phase system spins the central magnet in the opposite direction. The counter-rotating
inner magnet and outer shell produce the net anti-gravity lift.

Relevant EM law chain:
  dB/dt (from rotating outer field) -> EMF in ring (Faraday) -> I_eddy in ring (Ohm) ->
  F_Lenz on ring = I * L x B (Lorentz force on induced current) -> tangential rotation of ring

## Lorentz Force / Homopolar Motor Variant

Source: tweet 1971040932478001503.

Alternative spin-up mechanism: homopolar motor using liquid galinstan (Ga-In-Sn eutectic,
liquid at room temperature, highly electrically conductive ~3.46 x 10^6 S/m).

* The permanent magnet (disc or sphere) is surrounded by liquid galinstan.
* DC current flows radially through the magnet body via the liquid metal contacts.
* The radial current I crosses the axial magnetic field B of the magnet: F = qv x B produces
  a tangential (azimuthal) force on the current-carrying fluid and magnet.
* For a disc magnet: F_phi = I_r * B_z * delta_r integrated over the disc radius.
* This produces continuous rotation without mechanical brushes or commutation (the hallmark of
  the homopolar motor, first demonstrated by Faraday 1831).

The advantage of galinstan over solid brushes: zero friction, continuous liquid contact,
no wear, enables very high rotational velocities needed for the proposed "transitional magnetism"
regime.

The author also notes the magnet can be spun simultaneously by both Lorentz/homopolar and
three-phase mechanisms, allowing independent control of spin rate.

## XY-Plane and Z-Axis Dynamics

* XY plane (horizontal): The three-phase rotating magnetic field drives rotation of the
  central permanent magnet about the Z-axis. This is the driven (powered) degree of freedom.
  The outer flywheel (when present) counter-rotates in the XY plane about the same Z-axis.
* Z-axis (vertical): The claimed propulsion effect. The fast-spinning central magnet experiences
  a net upward force (positive Z-direction) with no corresponding downward force on the support.
  This is the anomalous claim.
* The equilibrium Z-position of the central magnet relative to the flywheel depends on the
  spin state: at rest, the magnet sits at z ≈ 0 (flywheel plane); during counter-spin, it shifts
  to z < 0 (below the flywheel).
* The net Z-force on the assembly (central magnet + flywheel) is then the magnetic push of the
  (z < 0) central magnet upward on the flywheel, plus the reaction on the central magnet from
  the flywheel field.

Scale measurement result (video 13_1964076811551477760_video.mdx):
"Now when I turn on the scale, no change of weight on the scale when we do that even if we go fast."
Interpretation: total system weight on scale does not change when spin is activated. If there were
a downward reaction to the upward magnetic force, the scale reading should increase. It does not.

## "Transitional Magnetism" (Claimed New Regime)

Source: tweet 1924058591545454850 (note_tweet field).

Term described as given to the author in "lucid physics dreams from unknown sources."
Requirements stated:
1. High angular velocity for whatever is spinning.
2. Use of three-phase electromagnets (NOT permanent magnets) to apply the spin force.
   Permanent magnets alone cannot generate the required rotating field for the spin-up.

The author distinguishes "transitional magnetism" from normal electromagnetism:
normal EM = static or slowly varying fields, classical Lorentz force.
transitional magnetism = high-angular-velocity regime where the rotating spin creates
a qualitatively different coupling between mechanical rotation and magnetic field topology.

This is analogous in spirit to (though distinct from) known effects in rotating magnetic
systems such as the Barnett effect (magnetization induced by mechanical rotation of a
ferromagnet: M = chi * omega / gamma, where gamma is the gyromagnetic ratio) and the
Einstein-de Haas effect (angular momentum transferred from spin alignment to mechanical
rotation). Both demonstrate real magnetomechanical coupling. The author's claim extends this
to a propulsive regime not described by current theory.
