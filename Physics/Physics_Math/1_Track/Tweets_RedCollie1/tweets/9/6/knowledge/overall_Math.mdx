## Author's Explicit Mathematical Formulation

### Kinetic Energy of Spinning Objects (tweet 2039849887341396035 and quoted 2039847044744118505)

The author's formulation:
  E = mv^2

where v is described as the velocity of the spinning disc.

Standard rotational KE for a rigid body:
  E_rot = (1/2) * I * omega^2

For a uniform disc, moment of inertia: I = (1/2) * m * R^2
  E_rot = (1/4) * m * R^2 * omega^2 = (1/4) * m * v_rim^2

The author's E = mv^2 uses v = rim velocity = R*omega:
  E_author = m * v_rim^2 = m * R^2 * omega^2 = 4 * E_rot

This is a factor-of-4 rescaled kinetic energy, but it is a monotonically increasing function
of omega^2, so it correctly tracks which objects have more or less rotational energy.

The conservation condition:
  E_total = sum_i m_i * v_i^2 = constant

Applied to the counter-spin problem:
* Upper disc given spin energy E_1 = m_1 * v_1^2, v_1 = -2 (anticlockwise)
* By classical Newton's 3rd Law, the lower disc gets opposite spin: v_2 = +X such that
  total angular momentum is conserved.
* The question: if total energy E_total = m_1*(−2)^2 + m_2*v_2^2 must be conserved,
  and m_1 = m_2, then v_2^2 = 4, so v_2 = ±2.
* Classical result: both discs spin at equal and opposite speeds. Action = reaction.
* Proposed result: if the upper disc is pre-spun to v = -2 BEFORE the lower disc is spun,
  can the lower disc achieve v > 2? The author's claim is yes, via some mechanism that allows
  the pre-loaded counter-spin to reduce the reaction coupling.

## Three-Phase Rotating Field Mathematics

Three-phase voltage set (balanced):
  V_a(t) = V_0 * cos(omega * t)
  V_b(t) = V_0 * cos(omega * t - 2*pi/3)
  V_c(t) = V_0 * cos(omega * t - 4*pi/3)

For three coils oriented at 0°, 120°, 240° in the XY plane, the magnetic field contributions:
  B_a = B_0 * cos(omega*t) * cos(0°) x_hat + B_0 * cos(omega*t) * sin(0°) y_hat
       = B_0 * cos(omega*t) * x_hat

  B_b = B_0 * cos(omega*t - 2*pi/3) * cos(120°) x_hat
      + B_0 * cos(omega*t - 2*pi/3) * sin(120°) y_hat

  B_c = B_0 * cos(omega*t - 4*pi/3) * cos(240°) x_hat
      + B_0 * cos(omega*t - 4*pi/3) * sin(240°) y_hat

Summing x-components:
  B_x = B_0 [cos(omega*t)*1 + cos(omega*t - 2pi/3)*(-1/2) + cos(omega*t - 4pi/3)*(-1/2)]
      = (3/2) * B_0 * cos(omega*t)

Summing y-components:
  B_y = B_0 [cos(omega*t)*0 + cos(omega*t - 2pi/3)*(sqrt(3)/2) + cos(omega*t - 4pi/3)*(-sqrt(3)/2)]
      = (3/2) * B_0 * sin(omega*t)   [with appropriate sign convention]

Resultant field:
  B_rot = (3/2) * B_0 * [cos(omega*t) x_hat + sin(omega*t) y_hat]

Magnitude: |B_rot| = (3/2) * B_0 = constant (does not vary with time)
Direction: rotates at angular frequency omega in XY plane.

This is the standard result for a balanced three-phase system producing a constant-amplitude
rotating field vector.

## Five-Phase Rotating Field Mathematics

For five balanced phases at 2*pi/5 = 72° separation:
  B_k = B_0 * cos(omega*t - 2*pi*k/5) for k = 0,1,2,3,4

Coils oriented at angles 2*pi*k/5 in XY plane.

X-component:
  B_x = sum_{k=0}^{4} B_0 * cos(omega*t - 2*pi*k/5) * cos(2*pi*k/5)
      = (5/2) * B_0 * cos(omega*t)  [using the identity sum_{k=0}^{N-1} cos(2pi*k/N)^2 = N/2]

Y-component:
  B_y = (5/2) * B_0 * sin(omega*t)

Resultant: |B_rot_5phase| = (5/2) * B_0

Higher than three-phase result ((3/2)*B_0) by factor 5/3, giving 67% higher rotating field
amplitude for the same per-coil field strength B_0.

## Dipole-Dipole Potential Energy

For two magnetic dipoles mu_1 and mu_2 separated by vector r:
  U = (mu_0 / 4*pi) * [mu_1 dot mu_2 / r^3 - 3*(mu_1 dot r_hat)*(mu_2 dot r_hat) / r^3]

For the central S magnet (mu_1 = -mu z_hat, pointing down) and one N flywheel magnet
(mu_2 = +mu z_hat, pointing up), with the flywheel magnet at position (R, 0, 0) and the
central magnet at position (0, 0, z):

  r = sqrt(R^2 + z^2)
  r_hat = (R x_hat - z z_hat) / sqrt(R^2 + z^2)

  mu_1 dot mu_2 = (-mu)(+mu) = -mu^2
  mu_1 dot r_hat = (-mu) * (-z / sqrt(R^2+z^2)) = mu*z / sqrt(R^2+z^2)
  mu_2 dot r_hat = (+mu) * (-z / sqrt(R^2+z^2)) = -mu*z / sqrt(R^2+z^2)

  U = (mu_0/4*pi) * [-mu^2/(R^2+z^2)^(3/2) - 3*(mu*z/sqrt(R^2+z^2))*(-mu*z/sqrt(R^2+z^2)) / (R^2+z^2)^(3/2)]
    = (mu_0/4*pi) * [-mu^2/(R^2+z^2)^(3/2) + 3*mu^2*z^2/(R^2+z^2)^(5/2)]
    = (mu_0*mu^2/4*pi) * [-1/(R^2+z^2)^(3/2) + 3z^2/(R^2+z^2)^(5/2)]

At z = 0 (central magnet in the ring plane):
  U(0) = (mu_0*mu^2/4*pi) * [-1/R^3] < 0  (attractive)

Z-force:
  F_z = -dU/dz

  dU/dz = (mu_0*mu^2/4*pi) * [3z/(R^2+z^2)^(5/2) + 6z/(R^2+z^2)^(5/2) - 15z^3/(R^2+z^2)^(7/2)]
  (simplified: full derivative of the z-dependent expression)

At z = 0: dU/dz = 0 (force is zero at the equilibrium plane -- the magnet is in the ring plane).
For z < 0: F_z = -dU/dz > 0 (upward restoring force).

With 4 flywheel magnets: total U and F_z are 4x the single-dipole values (by superposition for
symmetric arrangement).

## Lorentz Force for Homopolar Motor

Current density in radial direction: J_r = I / (2*pi*r*h) [for radial current in a disc of
height h at radius r]

Force per unit volume: f = J x B = J_r * B_z * phi_hat

Torque per unit volume: dT/dV = r * f_phi = r * J_r * B_z

Total torque:
  T = integral_V r * J_r * B_z * dV
    = B_z * integral_0^R integral_0^{2*pi} integral_0^h r * (I/(2*pi*r*h)) * r * dr * d*phi * dz
    = B_z * I * integral_0^R r * dr
    = B_z * I * R^2 / 2

For a disc magnet as its own field source, B_z is not uniform but depends on r and z. For the
axial field at the mid-plane of a disc magnet (magnetization M, thickness t, radius R):

  B_z(r=0, z=t/2) = mu_0 * M * [t / sqrt(t^2 + 4*R^2/4)]  (approximate for disc)

## Lenz Force (Annular Induction Motor)

Faraday's law for induced EMF in the annular ring at radius r:
  EMF = -d(Phi_B)/dt

For a rotating field B_rot = B_0 * cos(omega*t - k*phi) (where k is the pole pair number):
  Phi_B through a ring segment = B_0 * Area * cos(omega*t - k*phi_ring)
  EMF = B_0 * Area * omega * sin(omega*t - k*phi_ring)

Induced current (for ring resistance R_ring):
  I_ind = EMF / R_ring = B_0 * Area * omega * sin(omega*t - k*phi_ring) / R_ring

Lorentz force on induced current in field B_rot:
  F = I_ind * L x B_rot

Azimuthal force (tangential, causing rotation):
  F_phi proportional to I_ind * B_0 * cos(phase difference between I and B)

This is the standard induction motor torque, proportional to slip frequency (difference between
field rotation rate omega and ring mechanical rotation rate omega_mech):
  T_induction proportional to s * omega (where slip s = (omega - omega_mech) / omega)

described mathematically as:

Logarithmic spiral:
  r = a * e^(b*theta)
  where a and b are constants controlling scale and tightness of the spiral.

Multiple overlapping logarithmic spirals with angular offsets:
  r_k(theta) = a * e^(b*(theta - 2*pi*k/N))  for k = 0, 1, ..., N-1

mathematical resolution via electromagnetic momentum:

Maxwell stress tensor: T_ij = (1/mu_0)*(B_i*B_j - (1/2)*delta_ij*B^2)

Force on volume V:
  F_i = integral_V (J x B)_i dV + integral_S T_ij * n_j dA - (1/c^2) * d(integral_V S_i dV)/dt

where S = (E x B)/mu_0 is the Poynting vector (electromagnetic energy flux).

The last term: -(1/c^2) * dS_EM/dt represents the rate of change of electromagnetic momentum.

If the reaction force is entirely absorbed into the rate-of-change of the electromagnetic
momentum stored in the spinning field, the mechanical reaction on the scale is zero while
the upward force on the flywheel is real.

linear momentum at a rate equal to the claimed reaction force.
For F_up = 1 kg * g = 9.8 N, the required rate of electromagnetic momentum change:
  dP_EM/dt = 9.8 N  (in the -Z direction, i.e., the field gains downward momentum)

This is the Feser-type field-momentum anti-gravity proposal: the downward reaction is carried
by the field, not by the mechanical support. Whether this is consistent with the measured scale
invariance depends on the field configuration and requires detailed calculation.
