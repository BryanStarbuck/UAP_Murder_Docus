The Laithwaite effect: when a gyroscope is precessing rapidly, its apparent weight is reduced. This is not violation of Newton's law but is a redistribution of force — the person supporting the gyroscope feels reduced load because the gyroscope's angular momentum resists the gravitational torque by precessing rather than falling. The net force on the support (ground) is the same, but the subjective experience is of greatly reduced weight.

### The Flywheel as Proposed Lift System: Spin Rate vs. Lift Force

The spinning magnetic flywheel provides lift via gyroscopic precession. The key relationships:

**Gyroscopic lift force (proposed):**
    F_lift ∝ L × Ω_precession = (I · ω) × Ω

where:
- I = moment of inertia of flywheel
- ω = spin angular velocity of flywheel
- Ω = precession angular velocity (driven by 3-phase field)

At fixed precession rate, lift increases linearly with spin angular momentum L = Iω. Higher spin rate → more lift (to a limit determined by material strength, bearing friction, and field geometry).

**Disc magnet parameters (experiment):**

**Scaling to aircraft-scale:**

However, the 3rd Law cancellation problem must first be solved before such scaling is meaningful.

### Counter-Intuitive Direction Observation: N-Up and S-Up Both Rise

The most experimentally significant lift observation: the magnet rises for BOTH orientations (N-pole up and S-pole up), when spinning in the same rotational direction under three-phase power.

This eliminates:
- Simple magnetic repulsion (direction-dependent on polarity)
- Diamagnetic levitation (depends on field gradient, not spin)
- Eddy-current levitation of a permanent magnet (not applicable)

It is consistent with:
- Gyroscopic precession (direction depends only on spin sense and applied torque direction, not on magnetic polarity)
- Magnus-like effect (aerodynamic lift equivalent — but operating in magnetic field rather than fluid)

The Magnus effect: a spinning ball in a fluid experiences a lift force perpendicular to both its velocity and its spin axis. Direction of the Magnus force depends on spin direction, not on the ball's composition. Analogously, the spinning magnet in the rotating field experiences an axial force determined by the spin direction, not the polarity orientation — consistent with the experimental observation.

### AC Lenz Levitation Modification by Spin

From YAML 2039863168193999003:
> "Rapid spin also greatly reduces the upward force of repulsion applied to an aluminum disc, by an AC wire coil placed just below."

Standard Lenz levitation gives upward force F_Lenz when the disc is stationary. When disc spins at angular velocity Ω:

    F_lift(Ω) < F_lift(0)

The reduction is monotonically increasing with spin rate Ω. This is a practical constraint: for a UAP drive using a spinning magnetic flywheel above an AC coil for initial levitation, the spin reduces the AC levitation contribution. The three-phase rotating field + spinning permanent magnet configuration avoids this problem because the force mechanism is different (gyroscopic/precession rather than eddy-current repulsion).

This experiment is presented as an indirect confirmation that spinning modifies electromagnetic levitation forces in a non-trivial (non-additive) way, consistent with the broader claim that spin-field interactions produce non-standard force behaviors.
