### Angular Momentum Conservation Mathematics

Total system angular momentum (conserved in absence of external torque):

    L_total = L_wheel + L_body = constant

From YAML 2039863168193999003 (referenced tweet):
> "Our friend first reduces spin angular momentum around his vertical axis to zero, by lowering the spinning wheel to horizontal (times 0:12 to 0:20). That makes his body and feet move anti-clockwise with velocity v = -2, because..."

Let L_0 = initial total angular momentum (= L_wheel_initial when body is stationary).
When the wheel is reoriented so its angular momentum vector changes direction from +z to -z:

    ΔL_wheel = -2 · L_wheel_initial

To conserve total angular momentum:

    ΔL_body = +2 · L_wheel_initial

So the body acquires angular velocity ω_body:

    ω_body = ΔL_body / I_body = 2 · L_wheel_initial / I_body

In the described scenario, the body and feet rotate anti-clockwise with "velocity v = -2" — the notation is informal but consistent with a quantitative angular velocity relationship derived from conservation of angular momentum.

For the UFO counter-spin architecture:
    I_inner · ω_inner = I_outer · ω_outer

(opposing signs, so L_total = 0). If inner rotor has moment of inertia I_in and spins at ω_in, and outer ring has I_out and spins at ω_out:

    I_in · ω_in + I_out · (-ω_out) = 0
    → I_in / I_out = ω_out / ω_in

Larger inner moment of inertia requires proportionally slower spin for zero net angular momentum, or equivalently, more compact and higher-speed outer ring for a compact inner rotor.

### Three-Phase Synchronous Speed and Pole-Count Relationships

Synchronous speed of rotating magnetic field:

    n_s = (120 · f) / P   (RPM)

where f = electrical frequency (Hz), P = number of magnetic poles.

For 12 electromagnets in a circle (12-pole stator):

    n_s = (120 · f) / 12 = 10 · f   (RPM)

At 50 Hz: n_s = 500 RPM. At 60 Hz: n_s = 600 RPM.

The rotor magnet count determines the effective pole count seen by the rotating field:
- 8 magnets all N-up: creates a multipole pattern with 8 effective poles. Torque interaction depends on the common harmonic between 12-pole stator and 8-pole rotor fields.
  - gcd(12, 8) = 4 → 4th harmonic dominates → complex torque pattern
  - Net torque: anti-clockwise (from experiment)

- 16 magnets all N-up: 16-pole rotor with 12-pole stator.
  - gcd(12, 16) = 4 → 4th harmonic again, but with different phase relationship
  - Net torque: clockwise (direction reversal from 8-pole case)

- 12 magnets alternating NS: 24-pole (alternating N and S doubles the effective pole count) interacting with 12-pole stator.
  - 24 / 12 = 2: even harmonic, perfect cancellation possible
  - Result: no rotation (experimentally confirmed)

- 8 magnets alternating NSNSNSNS: 16 effective poles (alternating doubles count) vs. 12-pole stator.
  - Asymmetric interaction produces strong clockwise torque.

The pattern reveals that the direction of rotation is determined by the arithmetic relationship (harmonic matching or mismatching) between rotor effective pole count and stator pole count, rather than by magnet polarity alone.

> "We do see a slight loss of total weight by HALF the weight of the magnet (about 20 grams), because it goes into 'free fall' for HALF of the time."

    W_measured = m · g · (1 - t_ff / T)

- m = mass of magnet
- g = gravitational acceleration
- t_ff = time per cycle in free fall

    W_measured = m · g · 0.5 = m · g / 2

    m · g / 2 ≈ 0.196 N → m ≈ 40 g

### Electromagnetic Induction (Lenz Effect)

Standard Faraday-Lenz law: induced EMF in a conducting disc rotating in a magnetic field:

    ε = -dΦ_B / dt

where Φ_B = ∫ B · dA is the magnetic flux through the rotating disc.

For an aluminum disc (conductivity σ_Al ≈ 3.7 × 10⁷ S/m) in an AC field of frequency f and amplitude B₀:

Eddy current density: J = σ_Al · E_induced

Induced electric field in the disc (cylindrical coordinates, for a uniform axial AC field B_z = B₀ cos(ωt)):

    E_φ = (r/2) · dB_z/dt = -(ω · B₀ · r / 2) · sin(ωt)

Eddy current density:

    J_φ = σ_Al · E_φ

Upward (repulsive) force per unit volume on the current-carrying disc in the field:

    f_z = J_φ × B_z (cross product)

The time-averaged upward force is proportional to σ · ω · B₀² · r² (to first approximation), which is the standard Lenz levitation force.

**Claimed modification by spin:** When the aluminum disc rotates at angular velocity Ω (imposed by DC motor), the eddy currents experience the AC field in the rotating frame of the disc, which has a shifted effective frequency:

    ω_eff = ω ± Ω (slip frequency, analogous to induction motor slip)

At high Ω, the effective frequency seen by the conducting disc changes, altering the eddy current pattern and reducing the net levitation force. The claimed "non-Newtonian" behavior is proposed to arise when the slip frequency approaches zero (synchronous operation), at which point the eddy current component responsible for the levitation force vanishes — analogous to a synchronous induction motor at zero slip producing no net electromagnetic torque.

This is actually a classical electrodynamics effect (loss of eddy-current braking at synchronous speed), though Dr. Drew labels it "non-Newtonian." The "non-Newtonian" labeling may refer to the counterintuitive direction of the force change (spinning disc → less levitation force, not more) rather than a claim of violation of classical mechanics.
