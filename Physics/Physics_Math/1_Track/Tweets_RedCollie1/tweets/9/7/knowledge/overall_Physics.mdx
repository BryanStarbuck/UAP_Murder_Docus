### Gyroscopic Precession (Foundational Physics)

For a gyroscope with moment of inertia I spinning at angular velocity ω, total spin angular momentum:

    L = I · ω

Applied torque due to gravity (for a gyroscope tilted at angle θ from vertical, at radius r from pivot):

    τ = m · g · r · sin(θ)

Precession angular velocity:

    Ω_precession = τ / L = (m · g · r) / (I · ω)

Key physics: precession angular velocity Ω is inversely proportional to spin angular velocity ω. At high spin rates, precession is slow. The gyroscope does not fall; it precesses.

### The Lorentz Force as Governing Analogy

Lorentz force on a charge q moving with velocity v in electric field E and magnetic field B:

    F = q(E + v × B)

Key property: the magnetic component of the Lorentz force (q · v × B) is always perpendicular to v. It does no work on the charge (since F · v = 0) but continuously redirects the momentum of the charge.

### Motor Physics: Three-Phase Induction vs. Permanent Magnet Drive

**Standard three-phase induction motor:**
The rotating magnetic field produced by 3Φ stator windings (3 windings at 120° electrical separation, carrying currents 120° out of phase in time) rotates at synchronous speed:

    n_s = 120 · f / P

where f is electrical frequency (Hz) and P is number of poles.

The rotor (aluminum disc in induction configuration, or permanent magnet rotor in synchronous configuration) experiences a torque due to the difference between rotor speed and synchronous speed (slip, for induction) or due to the angular misalignment between rotor magnet poles and stator field poles (for synchronous PM motor).

- The NS alternating cancellation at 12 poles is consistent with a rotor that is perfectly phase-matched to the stator but with alternating polarity, so that net torque averages to zero.

### Horizontal Dynamics: Three-Phase Coil Torque Generation

The torque on a magnetic moment m in a magnetic field B:

    τ = m × B

For a disc magnet with magnetic moment m (vertical axis) placed in a rotating horizontal field B(t) = B₀[cos(ωt) x̂ + sin(ωt) ŷ]:

The field rotates in the XY-plane at angular frequency ω. The torque τ = m × B has a horizontal component that drives the disc magnet to follow the rotating field (synchronous operation) or lag behind (induction/slip operation).

The vertical (Z-axis) torque component (which would produce axial spin-up or spin-down of the disc about the vertical axis) is zero for a purely horizontal rotating field acting on a purely vertical magnetic moment. However, when the disc magnet is tilted slightly from vertical during precession, or when the field has a helical component (due to the spatial arrangement of the coils), a net axial force component can emerge.

The 3-phase coil arrangement in the experiments produces a rotating field that is primarily horizontal (in the plane of the coil array), but with a vertical component that varies with position above the coil plane. This spatial variation of B_z along the Z-axis creates a field gradient dB_z/dz that can exert an axial force on the disc magnet:

    F_z = m_z · (dB_z/dz)

The sign of this force depends on the magnet orientation and the gradient direction, consistent with the observation that the magnet rises for both N-up and S-up orientations (the field gradient direction reverses to match, since the field is driven externally).

### Magnetic Dipole vs. Spinning Magnet Physics

For a static magnetic dipole moment m in an external field B, the potential energy:

    U = -m · B

Force on dipole in non-uniform field:

    F = ∇(m · B)

For a dipole with fixed m, this force always has a Newton's 3rd Law partner: the source of B feels -F.

For a spinning magnet (gyromagnetic system), the effective magnetic moment is not fixed in direction — it precesses. The precessing magnetic moment interacts with the field differently from a static dipole. In NMR/MRI physics, a precessing nuclear magnetic moment in a transverse oscillating field absorbs energy when the oscillation frequency matches the Larmor precession frequency:

    ω_L = γ · B₀

where γ is the gyromagnetic ratio and B₀ is the static field. At resonance, the precessing spin continuously absorbs energy from the oscillating field.

Dr. Drew's experimental scenario is analogous: a precessing disc magnet in a rotating field that matches the precession frequency may enter a resonant energy-transfer regime where the rotating field continuously feeds angular momentum into the disc magnet's spin, and the reaction is not a simple Newton's 3rd Law downward force but a more complex redistribution of field momentum — potentially analogous to radiation pressure momentum transfer in quantum electrodynamics.

This connection is implied by the source material but not explicitly derived. It represents the most physically substantive framing for the claimed non-Newtonian behavior.
