## Ball Joint Requirement for Lift Capture

Source: tweet 1945756603879469354, note_tweet

"I suspect that a 3-phase spinning magnet has to be attached to a 'ball joint', in
order to capture the upward, anti-gravity force of a spinning gyroscope or top."

The role of the ball joint in the lift mechanism:

A spinning gyroscope precesses rather than tilts when a torque is applied. If the
magnet is rigidly attached to the rod, gravitational and magnetic torques would tilt
the magnet rather than causing precession -- the precession motion would be constrained.
A ball joint allows:
* Free precession in all directions about the ball center
* The precession angular velocity vector to reorient freely
* The upward component of the gyroscopic reaction force to be transmitted through the
  rod as an axial compression (lift), rather than being dissipated as bending moments
  in a rigid structure

This is analogous to the geometry of a spinning top with a pivot point: the axial
thrust of the precessing top is transmitted upward through its tip. If the tip were
glued to the table, the precession dynamics would be altered and the vertical thrust
would not freely express.

## Frequency Window for Lift (Experimental)

Source: video 13_1964084490902626304_video.mdx; tweet 1964085230987596272

* 25, 30, 32, 33 Hz: progressive spin-up, levitation active
* 34 Hz: magnet "falls" -- levitation collapses

This defines an operational bandwidth of approximately 20-33 Hz for the observed
anomalous lift in this apparatus. Above 34 Hz, the mechanism breaks down.

Physical interpretation of the upper frequency collapse:
(a) Motor pull-out: the permanent magnet rotor exceeds its synchronous pull-out torque
    angle and desynchronizes from the rotating field -- the rotor then experiences
    oscillating rather than unidirectional torque, reducing average lift
(b) Resonance crossing: at some critical frequency, the precession frequency of the
    spinning disc equals a resonance frequency of the mechanical system, causing
    instability and loss of lift
(c) Eddy current saturation: at high angular velocity, induced eddy currents in the
    aluminum rod and disc magnet assembly could interact with the applied field and
    produce opposing (damping) torques that reduce effective spin and lift
