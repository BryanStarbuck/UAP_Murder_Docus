## Core Mathematical Framework: Spin Energy and Newton's Third Law

Source: tweet 2039902746678362233, note_tweet

### Spin Energy Definition

E_spin = m * v^2

Where:
* m = inertial parameter (mass or effective moment of inertia divided by radius squared)
* v = spin velocity (rim speed or equivalent scalar spin parameter)

Note: This is NOT the standard kinetic energy formula E = (1/2)*m*v^2. The absence of
the factor of 1/2 may indicate:
(a) Rotational kinetic energy E = I*ω^2 rewritten with I = m*r^2 and v = ω*r, giving
    E = (m*r^2)*ω^2 = m*(r*ω)^2 = m*v^2 (which does carry the full factor, not 1/2, if
    the "m" here is not the full moment of inertia but half of it), or
(b) A non-standard definition intentionally used by the author

### Newton's Third Law Applied to Spin Energy Transfer

The author applies Newton's Third Law as a momentum/energy exchange rule:

Transfer scenario: spinning body (transmitter) with spin parameters (m_t, v_t) interacts
with a receiver body (m_r, v_r).

The transmitter imparts spin energy ΔE to the receiver:
ΔE = m_t * v_t^2

For the given numerical example:
m_t = 1, v_t = -2 (negative = opposite spin direction)
ΔE = 1 * (-2)^2 = 4

Receiver starts at rest (v_r0 = 0):
E_r_initial = m_r * v_r0^2 = 1 * 0 = 0
E_r_final = E_r_initial + ΔE = 0 + 4 = 4
v_r_final = sqrt(E_r_final / m_r) = sqrt(4/1) = +2

Result: v_r: 0 → +2, direction opposite to transmitter's spin.

### Spin Energy Addition with Pre-Existing Receiver Spin

Receiver initially spinning:
m_r = 1, v_r0 = +8
E_r_initial = m_r * v_r0^2 = 1 * 64 = 64

Newton's 3rd Law adds ΔE = 4:
E_r_final = 64 + 4 = 68
v_r_final = sqrt(68 / 1) = sqrt(68) ≈ 8.246

Summary: v_r: +8 → +8.246 (Δv ≈ 0.246)

### Comparison: Angular Momentum vs. Energy Conservation Predictions

Angular momentum naive prediction:
L_initial + ΔL = L_final
m_r * r * v_r0 + m_t * r * |v_t| = m_r * r * v_r_naive
v_r_naive = v_r0 + |v_t| = 8 + 2 = 10

Spin energy prediction (Drew's approach):
v_r_final = sqrt((v_r0^2 + v_t^2)) = sqrt(64 + 4) = sqrt(68) ≈ 8.246

The gap: 8.246 vs. 10.0 -- a factor of ~1.214 difference in predicted outcomes.

Drew's conclusion from this:
"As the prior spin velocity of that nearby object gets larger, then we may need
practically zero effect of Newton's 3rd Law on the nearby, fast-spinning object."

Mathematical formalization:
Let the fractional velocity change be:
δ = (v_r_final - v_r0) / v_r0
  = (sqrt(v_r0^2 + ΔE/m_r) - v_r0) / v_r0
  = sqrt(1 + ΔE/(m_r * v_r0^2)) - 1
  ≈ ΔE / (2 * m_r * v_r0^2)   [for ΔE << m_r * v_r0^2]

As v_r0 → ∞: δ → 0

This means: a very fast pre-spinning receiver is nearly inert to additional spin energy
from Newton's 3rd Law reaction. The transmitter (upper disc) gains upward momentum but
the pre-spinning receiver (lower counter-disc) gains essentially zero downward momentum.
Net result: an apparent unbalanced upward force.

### Limiting Case Analysis

Define the reaction efficiency η:
η = Δv_r / |v_t| (how much the receiver's speed changes relative to the transmitter speed)

For v_r0 = 0 (stationary receiver):
η = v_r_final / |v_t| = 2/2 = 1.0 (full reaction)

For v_r0 = 8, |v_t| = 2:
η = (8.246 - 8) / 2 = 0.246 / 2 = 0.123 (12.3% reaction)

For v_r0 → ∞:
η → 0 (zero reaction)

Application: The lower drive disc spinning at v_r0 >> |v_upper_disc_velocity| has
η ≈ 0, so the upper disc rising upward transfers essentially no downward momentum to
the lower disc. The system appears to violate Newton's Third Law from the outside.

## Gyroscopic Precession Mathematics

Source: tweet 1953771519701922259, referenced_tweet 1953767622644052357

Standard gyroscope precession rate:
Ω_precession = τ / L = (M * g * d) / (I * ω)

Where:
* τ = applied torque = M*g*d (gravitational torque about pivot, with d = pivot offset)
* L = angular momentum = I * ω
* M = gyroscope mass
* g = gravitational acceleration (9.81 m/s^2)
* d = distance from pivot to CM
* I = moment of inertia of spinning body about spin axis
* ω = angular spin velocity

Laithwaite anomaly: the effective weight W_eff measured by a scale under a precessing
gyroscope is claimed to be W_eff << M*g. Standard mechanics predicts W_eff = M*g at
all times (only precession direction changes, not weight). The discrepancy is the
claimed "incredibly light" effect.

If Laithwaite's effect were real, the correction term would be:
W_eff = M * g * (1 - f(ω, Ω, I))
where f is some function of the spin parameters that approaches 1 at high ω, giving
W_eff → 0. No accepted theoretical expression for f exists.

## Three-Phase Electromagnetic Drive: Frequency-Torque Relationship

Source: video 13_1964084490902626304_video.mdx; tweet 1964085230987596272

Observed frequency sweep (experimental):
f_drive: 20 → 25 → 30 → 32 → 33 → 34 Hz

At 34 Hz: magnet falls ("And it falls.")

For a 2-pole three-phase motor (p=1 pole pair):
ω_sync = 2π * f_drive / p [rad/s] = 2π * f_drive [for p=1]

At 20 Hz: ω_sync = 125.7 rad/s (1200 RPM)
At 34 Hz: ω_sync = 213.6 rad/s (2040 RPM)

The collapse at 34 Hz suggests a resonance or pull-out condition. For a synchronous
motor, the maximum (pull-out) torque is:
T_max = (3 * V_phase * E_f) / (ω_s * X_s)

where V_phase = phase voltage, E_f = back-EMF from rotor flux, X_s = synchronous
reactance. When the load angle exceeds 90°, the motor loses synchronism and the rotor
falls out of step -- consistent with the observed "falls" at 34 Hz.

Alternatively, the levitation regime may have an upper frequency bound where the
gyroscopic precession rate approaches the stator field rotation rate, causing a
resonance condition that destabilizes the levitation.

## Homopolar Motor Torque Equation

Source: tweet 1989465158549393867, note_tweet

Force on a current-carrying conductor in a magnetic field:
F = I * L x B (for a straight conductor of length L carrying current I in field B)

For a radial current element at radius r in an axial field B_z:
dF_phi = I * dr * B_z (azimuthal force)

Total torque:
T = integral_0^R (r * dF_phi) = B_z * integral_0^R (I * r * dr/R) = B_z * I * R/2
   (for uniform current density: J = I / (π*R^2), and integrating)

More precisely for uniform J_r:
T = integral_0^R [r * J_r * 2π*r * dz * B_z] 
  = 2π * J_r * B_z * integral_0^R r^2 dr
  = 2π * J_r * B_z * R^3 / 3
  = (2/3) * π * B_z * J_r * R^3

And since I_total = J_r * π * R^2:
T = (2/3) * B_z * I_total * R

(This is the standard homopolar motor torque expression up to geometric factors
depending on the current path geometry.)

## Orbital Mechanics: 3I/ATLAS Deviation

Source: tweet 1984803501550260409

For a hyperbolic orbit with specific energy ε:
ε = v^2/2 - μ/r = v_infinity^2/2 > 0

An impulsive ΔV applied to the object changes ε by:
Δε = V_0 * ΔV * cos(θ) + ΔV^2/2

where V_0 is the pre-burn speed and θ is the angle between velocity and ΔV vectors.

For small ΔV (ΔV << V_0):
Δε ≈ V_0 * ΔV * cos(θ)

Change in asymptotic speed:
Δv_∞ = Δε / v_∞

The brightness change proportional to the exhaust column:
L_exhaust ∝ T_exhaust^4 (Stefan-Boltzmann)
Color shift: peak wavelength λ_max = 2.898 × 10^-3 / T [m] (Wien's law)
For blue color (λ_max ≈ 450 nm = 4.5 × 10^-7 m):
T_exhaust ≈ 2.898 × 10^-3 / 4.5 × 10^-7 ≈ 6,440 K

This places the exhaust temperature in the range of a hot chemical rocket (~3000-4000 K)
to electric propulsion plume heated by RF or arc (>10,000 K). The deep blue observed
suggests very high temperature exhaust or broadband emission shifted by Doppler effect.
