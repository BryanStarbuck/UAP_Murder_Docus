EQUATION 1: LORENTZ FORCE ON CURRENT-CARRYING CONDUCTOR
Source: 3_1979663877190090753.txt (homopolar motor schematic); tweet 1989473708160029037

F = IL x B

where:
* F = force vector on the conductor [Newtons]
* I = current magnitude [Amperes]
* L = length vector of current-carrying element [meters], direction = direction of conventional current flow
* B = applied magnetic flux density vector [Tesla]
* x = vector cross product

In the Corbino disc / homopolar motor:
* I flows radially (in the r-hat direction of cylindrical coordinates)
* B is axial (z-hat direction)
* L x B = r-hat x z-hat = -phi-hat (tangential, azimuthal direction)
* Therefore F is tangential = torque-producing

Torque element: dN = r * dF = r * I * B * dr (for an infinitesimal ring at radius r, width dr)

Total torque (for uniform radial current density and uniform axial B):
N_z = integral from R1 to R2 of [r * I_line * B] dr
where I_line = I / (2*pi*r) for a disc with total current I, giving:
N_z = integral from R1 to R2 of [r * (I / 2*pi*r) * B] dr = (I * B / 2*pi) * (R2 - R1)

More precisely for a disc with uniform current density j_r = I / (2*pi*r*t) (t = thickness):
N_z = (I * B / 2) * (R2^2 - R1^2) / (R2 - R1) * pi
[exact form depends on current distribution; for uniform radial current: N_z = I*B*(R2^2 - R1^2)/2]

EQUATION 3: THREE-PHASE ROTATING FIELD PHASE RELATIONSHIPS
Source: tweets 1924248812933259674, 2015208388947488793

For a 3-phase supply with phases U, V, W:
* V_U(t) = V_peak * cos(omega*t)
* V_V(t) = V_peak * cos(omega*t - 2*pi/3)
* V_W(t) = V_peak * cos(omega*t - 4*pi/3)

where omega = 2*pi*f, f = supply frequency [Hz]

For 6 coils arranged U-V-W-U-V-W at angular positions 0, 60, 120, 180, 240, 300 degrees:
The rotating magnetic field vector at center rotates at angular frequency omega.
Each coil produces a pulsating field; the sum of all six produces a smoothly rotating resultant field.

For two counter-rotating sets (upper 6 coils CCW, lower 3 coils CW):
B_upper(t) = B_0 * [cos(omega*t)*x-hat + sin(omega*t)*y-hat] (CCW rotation)
B_lower(t) = B_0 * [cos(omega*t)*x-hat - sin(omega*t)*y-hat] (CW rotation, opposite y-component)

Total field at center:
B_total = B_upper + B_lower = 2*B_0*cos(omega*t)*x-hat

This is a linearly oscillating field in the x-direction (not rotating), explaining why the center shows cancellation of rotation.

At intermediate radii, the two sets' contributions are not equal in magnitude (due to geometric attenuation with distance), so the dominant one wins, producing partial rotation.

EQUATION 4: MAGNETIC TORQUE ON COMPASS NEEDLE IN ROTATING FIELD
Source: tweet 1976071049973465118 (note_tweet)

N = m x B

where:
* N = torque vector [N*m]
* m = magnetic dipole moment of compass needle [A*m^2]
* B = applied rotating magnetic field [T]
* x = vector cross product

If B rotates at angular frequency Omega (rad/s) and the needle has moment of inertia I_needle:
* Equation of motion: I_needle * (d^2*theta/dt^2) = |m||B| * sin(phi)
where phi = angle between m and B.

For slow rotation (Omega << omega_0, where omega_0 = sqrt(|m||B|/I_needle) is the natural oscillation frequency of the needle):
* Needle tracks B, oscillating back and forth.

For fast rotation (Omega >> omega_0):
* Needle cannot track B; the torque continuously drives the needle in one direction.
* Needle enters continuous rotation at angular speed proportional to B and m.
* This is the "compass spinning" effect described in the UFO detector context.
