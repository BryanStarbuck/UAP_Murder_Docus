## VI. Gyroscopic Precession as the Bridge to Lift

### Classical Gyroscope Mechanics (Baseline)

For a symmetric top (Euler angles θ, φ, ψ) in a gravitational field:

**L = (1/2)I₁(θ̇² + φ̇² sin²θ) + (1/2)I₃(ψ̇ + φ̇ cosθ)² − Mgl cosθ**

where I₁ = I₂ are equatorial moments of inertia, I₃ is axial moment of inertia, M = rotor mass, l = distance from pivot to center of mass.

Steady precession gives:
**φ̇ = Mgl / (I₃ω₃)** for large ω₃

Gravitational torque drives precession:
**τ_grav = dL/dt → precession Ω_p = Mgl sinθ / (I₃ω₃)**

**Gravity is "converted" from a downward Z force into a horizontal (XY) precession.** The device is in dynamic equilibrium where gravitational energy is stored as precession kinetic energy. As 3-phase power continues to add angular momentum (ω₃ increases), the precession rate decreases (Ω_p ∝ 1/ω₃). The gyroscope transitions from horizontal precession (θ = 90°) toward vertical sleeping-top configuration (θ → 0°).

This θ-angle decrease requires the spin axis to physically rotate from horizontal toward vertical, involving a translation of the center of mass in the Z direction:

**Δh = l(cosθ_f − cosθ_i)**

For θ_i = 90°, θ_f = 0°: Δh = l (maximum height gain)
