## 2. Three-Phase Rotating Magnetic Field (Primary Drive Mechanism)

### 2.1 Mathematical Formalism

Source: multiple YAML files across all 100 directories

A three-phase system consists of three sinusoidal AC currents of equal amplitude and frequency, mutually phase-shifted by 120° (2π/3 radians):

    I_U(t) = I_0 cos(ωt)
    I_V(t) = I_0 cos(ωt - 2π/3)
    I_W(t) = I_0 cos(ωt - 4π/3)

When these three currents are driven through coils arranged at 120° spatial intervals around a common axis, the superposition of the three individual fields produces a net magnetic field vector of constant magnitude that rotates uniformly in the XY plane at angular frequency ω:

    B_x(t) = B_0 cos(ωt)
    B_y(t) = B_0 sin(ωt)
    |B_total| = B_0 = constant

For a 3-phase system with N_coils and n turns per coil:

    B_rot = (3/2) · μ_0 · n · I_0   [rotating field amplitude, constant]

For a 6-coil configuration (two UVW sets):

    B_total(t) = B_0 · (3/2) · [cos(ωt) x̂ + sin(ωt) ŷ]

(the factor 3/2 is the amplitude enhancement from symmetric superposition).

Phase designations: U, V, W are standard IEC designations (equivalent to R, S, T in some national standards; L1, L2, L3 in others; A, B, C in US power systems). Three phases displaced by 120° each: 0°, 120°, 240°.

Standard wiring topology for the U-V-W-U-V-W ring: 6 coils in a ring, with phases assigned U, V, W, U, V, W (each phase represented twice, 60° apart mechanically). This produces a 6-pole, 3-phase rotating field or equivalently a 2-pole field with 6 coil slots.

### 2.5 Bloch Wall Placement Requirement

Source: tweet 2012993319564095886

A Bloch wall (domain wall) is the transition region between two magnetic domains of opposite orientation within a ferromagnetic material. In the context of the electromagnet ring, the boundary between N-pole and S-pole regions of adjacent electromagnets forms an analog of a domain wall. Placing a ring magnet precisely at this N/S boundary (Bloch wall position):
- The ring magnet experiences net zero tangential force (it sits at the symmetry point)
- It does not spin up with the rotating field
- It remains stationary while providing a fixed magnetic reference for the rising inner rotor

The Bloch wall in a physical ferromagnet has a characteristic width δ_B:

    δ_B = π √(A / K)

where A is the exchange stiffness constant (J/m) and K is the anisotropy energy density (J/m³). For typical NdFeB magnets, δ_B ~ 3–5 nm. In the macroscopic electromagnet context, the analog "Bloch wall" position is the geometric midpoint between adjacent coil faces.

Source: 1929053403268919622.yaml, 2034132025507254299.yaml, tweet 10/10 batch
