## Part I: Core Electromagnetic Drive Mathematics

### 1. Three-Phase Rotating Magnetic Field — Foundational Derivation

**Phase currents (balanced 3-phase system):**

    I_U(t) = I₀ cos(ωt)
    I_V(t) = I₀ cos(ωt − 2π/3)
    I_W(t) = I₀ cos(ωt − 4π/3)

where ω = 2πf, f = drive frequency (Hz), I₀ = peak current amplitude.

**Identity for balanced system:**

    I_U + I_V + I_W = 0  (for all t)

**Magnetic field from 3 coils at angular positions 0°, 120°, 240° in the XY-plane:**

    B_U(θ, t) = (B₀/2)[cos(θ − ωt) + cos(θ + ωt)]
    B_V(θ, t) = (B₀/2)[cos(θ − 2π/3 − ωt) + cos(θ − 2π/3 + ωt)]
    B_W(θ, t) = (B₀/2)[cos(θ − 4π/3 − ωt) + cos(θ − 4π/3 + ωt)]

**Superposition (backward-rotating terms cancel):**

    B_total(θ, t) = (3B₀/2) cos(θ − ωt)

Equivalently in Cartesian components:

    B_x(t) = B₀[cos(ωt) − ½cos(ωt − 2π/3) − ½cos(ωt − 4π/3)]
           = (3/2) B₀ cos(ωt)

    B_y(t) = (3/2) B₀ sin(ωt)

    B_total(t) = (3/2) B₀ [cos(ωt) x̂ + sin(ωt) ŷ]

**Key result:** |B_total| = (3/2) B₀ = constant. The field rotates at angular frequency ω with constant amplitude. This is the standard result for a balanced 3-phase rotating magnetic field from which AC induction and synchronous motor operation follow.

**General N-phase result:** For N coils at azimuthal angles θ_k = 2πk/N, each driven at I_k(t) = I₀ cos(ωt − 2πk/N), k = 0,...,N−1:

    B_total(t) = (N/2) B₀ [cos(ωt) x̂ + sin(ωt) ŷ]      (for N ≥ 3)

For N = 5 (five-phase system): amplitude = (5/2)B₀, torque ripple frequency = 5f (vs. 3f for N = 3). The Buga orb's 5-phase system thus produces 5/3 = 1.67× stronger rotating field amplitude than a 3-phase lab setup for equal current and coil geometry.

**Rotating MMF from N-turn 3-phase winding:**

    F(θ, t) = Σ_{k=1}^{3} N I₀ cos(ωt − (k-1)·2π/3) · cos(θ − (k-1)·2π/3)
             = (3/2) N I₀ cos(θ − ωt)

Amplitude: (3/2) N I₀; spatial wavelength: 2π (one pole pair per revolution).

**6-coil winding (U-V-W-U-V-W) at positions 0°, 60°, 120°, 180°, 240°, 300°:**

    B(φ, t) = B₀ [cos(ωt)cos(φ) + cos(ωt−2π/3)cos(φ−2π/3) + cos(ωt−4π/3)cos(φ−4π/3)
               + cos(ωt)cos(φ−π) + cos(ωt−2π/3)cos(φ−π−2π/3) + cos(ωt−4π/3)cos(φ−π−4π/3)]

Using product-to-sum: cos(A)cos(B) = ½[cos(A−B) + cos(A+B)]. Counter-rotating terms cancel by symmetry; net result:

    B(φ, t) = (3 B₀ / 2) cos(ωt − φ)    [dominant rotating component]

**12-coil (U-V-W repeated 4 times), coils at 0°, 30°, 60°, ..., 330°:**

The winding function has harmonic components at n = 1, 5, 7, 11, 13, ... (no even harmonics by symmetry; no multiples of 3 by 3-phase balance). Coils wired in series per phase (all 4 U coils in series, all 4 V coils in series, all 4 W coils in series). This produces field magnitude 4× that of a single coil per unit current, at the same rotation rate.

**5-phase field from N_c = 5 coils at 72° spacing:**

Fundamental component: (5/2)NI₀ cos(θ − ωt). Lowest significant space harmonic: 5th (vs. 3rd in 3-phase).

### 3. Homopolar (Faraday Disc) Motor/Generator — Complete Derivation

**Motional EMF of spinning conducting disc** (generator mode):

    ε = ∫₀ᴿ (v × B) · dr̂ dr

Velocity of disc element at radius ρ spinning at angular velocity ω: v = ωρ φ̂.
Applied axial field: B = B_z ẑ.
Cross product: v × B = ωρ B_z (φ̂ × ẑ) = −ωρ B_z r̂.
Dot with dr = dr r̂:

    dε = ωρ B_z dρ
    ε = ∫₀ᴿ ωρ B_z dρ = ½ ω B_z R²

where R = disc radius [m], ω = angular velocity [rad/s], B_z = axial field [T].

Key relationships: ε ∝ ω × B_z × R² — quadratic in disc radius, linear in field strength and rotation rate.

**Radial current density in disc** (motor mode, current I injected via brushes):

    J_r(r) = I / (2π r h)    [h = disc thickness, uniform conductivity assumed]

**Azimuthal Lorentz force per unit volume:**

    f_φ = J_r × B_z = [I/(2πr h)] × B_z

**Torque on disc** due to Lorentz force (integrating from inner radius a to outer radius b):

    τ_z = ∫_a^b r · dF_φ = −I B_z ∫_a^b r dr = −(I B_z / 2)(b² − a²)

For brushes at r = 0 (center) and r = R (rim), a → 0, b → R:

    τ = ½ I B_z R²    (standard homopolar motor torque formula)

This result is:
- Linear in current I
- Linear in field B_z
- Quadratic in radius R (larger disc → proportionally stronger)

**Power consistency:** P = ε × I = (½ ω B_z R²) × I = τ × ω ✓ (ideal efficiency).

**EMF sign convention:** Electrons driven radially outward (conventional current inward); reversal of I or B_z reverses torque direction.

**Corbino disc geometry (full Lorentz body force derivation):**

Current density radial: J = J_r r̂. Applied axial field: B = B_z ẑ.
Lorentz body force: f = J × B = J_r r̂ × B_z ẑ = J_r B_z (r̂ × ẑ) = −J_r B_z φ̂.

Total azimuthal torque on annular disc from r_inner to r_outer, thickness d:

    τ = −2π B_z J_r d · (r_outer³ − r_inner³) / 3

Time-averaged torque with AC fields B_z(t) = B_0 cos(ωt) and J_r(t) = J_0 cos(ωt + φ):

    ⟨τ⟩ ∝ (J_0 B_0 / 2) cos(φ)

At φ = π (180° phase shift from capacitive resonance): ⟨τ⟩ ∝ cos(π) = −1. Torque reverses — disc spins in the opposite direction.

**Newton's Third Law anomaly (Drew's experimental claim):**

The electromagnetic angular momentum stored in the field:

    g_em = ε₀ (E × B)    [electromagnetic momentum density, kg/(m²·s)]
    L_em = ε₀ ∫ r × (E × B) dV

The reaction torque is carried by L_em, not mechanically by the wires. For wires outside the magnetic field region (τ_wire ≈ 0 since B ≈ 0 at wire location), the Maxwell stress tensor stores the angular momentum — producing the observed apparent Newton's Third Law asymmetry.

### 4. Lorentz Force — Fundamental Equations

**General form:**

    F = q(E + v × B)

Terms: q = charge of carrier [C]; v = velocity [m/s]; B = magnetic field [T]; E = electric field [V/m].

**Cylindrical coordinate cross products:**

    r̂ × ẑ = −φ̂
    φ̂ × ẑ = r̂ (equivalently −r̂ depending on sign convention; use r̂ × ẑ = −φ̂ as primary)
    r̂ × φ̂ = ẑ
    φ̂ × r̂ = −ẑ
    ẑ × r̂ = φ̂
    ẑ × φ̂ = −r̂

**E×B drift:**

    v_drift = (E × B) / |B|²    [perpendicular to both E and B, same for all charge signs and masses]

**Cyclotron frequency and radius:**

    ω_c = qB / m    [rad/s]
    r_c = m v_⊥ / (qB) = v_⊥ / ω_c

For electrons (q = e = 1.602 × 10⁻¹⁹ C, m_e = 9.109 × 10⁻³¹ kg):
    ω_c = eB / m_e = 1.7588 × 10¹¹ × B  [rad/s]
    At B = 1 T: ω_c ≈ 28 GHz; at B = 0.1 T: ω_c ≈ 2.8 GHz.

**Z-axis Lorentz force component** — condition for sustained axial force:

For charge carrier with velocity v_carrier = v_r r̂ + ω_disc ρ φ̂ in field B = B_z ẑ + B_⊥(cos(Ωt) x̂ + sin(Ωt) ŷ), expanding in cylindrical coordinates:

    F_z = q (v_r B_⊥φ − ω_disc ρ B_⊥r)
        = −q B_⊥ [v_r sin(Ωt − φ) + ω_disc ρ cos(Ωt − φ)]

Time-averaged F_z = 0 for arbitrary phase. For non-zero ⟨F_z⟩, synchronous condition required: ω_disc = Ω. At synchronous speed (Ωt − φ = −φ₀ = constant):

    F_z(synchronous) = −q B_⊥ [v_r sin(−φ₀) + ω_disc ρ cos(−φ₀)] = constant ≠ 0

This is the mathematical condition for a sustained Z-axis force: the disc must spin synchronously with the 3-phase rotating field. At synchronous speed, F_z becomes a DC (constant) value. The sign and magnitude depend on the phase offset φ₀.

### 5. Helical Magnetic Field and Gradient Force

**Helical field parameterization:**

    B_helix(r, φ, z, t) = B₀ · [sin(kz − ωt) φ̂ + cos(kz − ωt) ẑ]

    B_φ(r, φ, z, t) = B₀(r) · cos(ωt − k_z·z − n·φ)

**Magnetic dipole potential energy in helical field:**

    U = −m · B_helix = −m₀ B₀ cos(kz − ωt − φ_m)

    F_z = −m₀ B₀ k sin(kz − φ₀)

### 7. Angular Momentum Conservation — Full System

**Standard conservation (no external torques):**

    d(L_mech + L_field)/dt = 0    [total conservation]
    d/dt(L_magnet + L_device) = −d/dt(L_field)

For an isolated system starting from rest (L_total = 0):

    I_m · ω_m + I_d · ω_d = 0
    ∴ ω_d = −(I_m / I_d) · ω_m

When the internal blade reverses (ω_m → −ω_m), ω_d must reverse sign too to maintain L_total = 0.

**Under Lorentz force (3-phase drive):**

The Lorentz force exchanges angular momentum between the mechanical system and the electromagnetic field:

    L_magnet_upward ≠ 0 (net upward spin)
    L_device_downward = 0 (no downward counter-spin of vehicle, if L_field absorbs reaction)
    L_field ≠ 0 (field carries the angular momentum reaction)

**Counter-rotating two-component system:**

    L_net = I_inner · ω_inner − I_outer · ω_outer

Zero angular momentum (cancellation) condition:

    I_inner · ω_inner = I_outer · ω_outer → L_net = 0

Implications of L_net = 0:
1. No net gyroscopic torque on the craft — free reorientation without gyroscopic resistance
2. No net reaction torque transmitted to the airframe
3. Anomalous field configuration at the interface where opposing momenta cancel

**Force cancellation analysis** (if F_z = k ω coupling holds):

    F_z_total = k ω_inner + k ω_outer = k ω_inner (1 − I_inner/I_outer)

For I_inner = I_outer: F_z_total = 0. For I_inner < I_outer: partial cancellation only — net upward force. To achieve net upward force, the counter-spinning element must have smaller moment of inertia, or the counter-spin must be produced via a mechanism (e.g., liquid metal) that does not couple to the lift force in the same way.

**Energy-velocity argument for suppressed Newton's Third Law reaction:**

For a body pre-spinning at v₀ receiving a counter-spin impulse Δv₀:

    v_new = √(v₀² + (Δv₀)²)
