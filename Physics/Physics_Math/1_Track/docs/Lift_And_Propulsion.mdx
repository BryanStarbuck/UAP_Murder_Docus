### B.3 Conservation of Momentum — Does Drew's Framework Close?

The total electromagnetic force on a system:

```
F_total = ∫(ρ_e E + J × B) dV + d/dt ∫ ε₀(E × B) dV    [field momentum term]
```

Drew's claim requires the field momentum term to be macroscopically significant — achievable at high frequencies (radiation pressure) or by resonance. For the force to produce net system lift, this field momentum term must sustain a non-zero rate of change, which in turn requires continuous net electromagnetic radiation from the system (radiation reaction → recoil → lift). At laboratory drive frequencies (10–50 Hz), the radiated power and associated field momentum flux are negligible.

The physically consistent route to system lift within standard electrodynamics requires one of:
1. The system exchanges momentum with an external field or body (Earth's magnetic field, for instance)
2. The system sustains continuous electromagnetic radiation that carries away momentum in a preferred direction (radiation pressure)
3. The vacuum absorbs distributed linear momentum from the device (Drew's "pushing against the vacuum" claim)

Routes 1 and 2 are physically possible in principle but have not been demonstrated at the measured force levels. Route 3 is speculative.

### B.4 The XY-to-Z Coupling: Physical Justification

If the magnetic force on the spinning disc has both horizontal and vertical components, the gyroscopic response to the horizontal component produces a net vertical force. Increases with spin rate as expected.


**Candidate 3 — Magnetic pressure gradient from helical field:**
For a 6-coil rotating field with axial displacement Δz between the two field sources:
```
F_z = m_z ∂B_z/∂z
```
The gradient ∂B_z/∂z at the inner magnet location is non-zero when two axially displaced rotating fields superpose, creating a helical field topology.

**Candidate 4 — Parametric resonance:**
Time-varying effective potential (due to counter-rotation of outer ring) + natural oscillation frequency of disc magnet in its magnetic trap → resonant energy transfer from rotation to Z-translation.

**Candidate 5 — Ponderomotive force:**

## Part C — Can It Scale? Ground Contact, Altitude, and Size

### C.1 Does the Mechanism Require Ground Contact?

**No — the proposed mechanism does not push against the ground.** The source of the upward force (in Drew's framework) is the electromagnetic field interaction between the spinning magnets and the rotating field, not a reaction against any surface. The mechanism is:

```
DC power + 3-phase AC → rotating EM field → torque on spinning magnets
→ [proposed coupling] → upward force on device
```

The downward reaction in the current prototype goes into the coil assembly (which sits on a table). If the coil assembly is mounted to the spinning magnet assembly as a single free-floating unit, the question becomes whether the system's own center of mass accelerates upward — which Drew acknowledges has not yet been demonstrated.

Control experiments demonstrate the rise is genuine thrust, not mechanical screw action:
1. Central hole pre-drilled in both CW and CCW directions (eliminates thread-screw coupling)

### C.3 Scaling Analysis

**Force scaling with frequency (from empirical data):**

F_z ∝ ω² (from threshold frequency data: lifting 200 g at 25 Hz vs. 80 g at 15 Hz implies F ∝ ω^1.5 to ω^2).

For F_z ∝ ω², to achieve levitation of the full device assembly (M_device ~ 1 kg):

```
ω_required ≥ ω_lab × √(M_device / m_magnet) ≈ 17 Hz × √(1000/100) ≈ 54 Hz
```

Within the experimentally demonstrated operating range (12–50 Hz confirmed, outer flywheel stable to 50 Hz).

**Force scaling with device size:**

From the homopolar motor torque formula:
```
τ = ½ I B_z R²    (quadratic in disc radius)
```

And the helical field gradient force:
```
F_z = m_z ∂B_z/∂z    with m_z ∝ R³ (for proportionally scaled disc magnet)
```

Net scaling: F_z ∝ R⁵ for proportionally scaled device. A 3× increase in device radius yields 3⁵ = 243× more force. This is very favorable for scale-up.

**Power scaling estimate:**

For a spinning disc at B = 0.1 T, ω = 200 Hz, and scaling from Drew's lab prototype (R_lab = 0.05 m) to UAP size (R_UAP = 10 m):

```
P_UAP ≈ P_lab × (R_UAP/R_lab)² = 1000 W × (10/0.05)² ≈ 400 MW
```

400 MW is within the range of large power generators and consistent with nuclear or advanced fusion energy sources being necessary for UAP-scale operation.

**Engineering constraints on scaling:**

1. **Materials:** Bearing loads scale as R⁴ (centrifugal force on outer ring × moment arm). At UAP scale, conventional bearing materials fail. Superconducting magnetic bearings or exotic metamaterials would be required.

2. **Power density:** Liquid metal vortex Ohmic losses scale as ω²; at high frequencies, galinstan becomes inefficient and permanent magnets become the preferred configuration (which Drew has already transitioned to in his laboratory work).

3. **Structural integrity:** Centrifugal force on outer ring magnets at high ω requires material tensile strength beyond available steel alloys. Drew proposes counter-rotating assemblies to zero net angular momentum, which eliminates gyroscopic torque but not centrifugal hoop stress.

## Part D — Verdict

**Is this a valid new propulsion system in principle?**

The following is established experimentally (single investigator, unreplicated):
- A spinning permanent magnet in a 3-phase rotating field generates an upward force of up to ~1 kg at 20–30 Hz
- The outer flywheel counter-rotates stably against the field
- The force is polarity-independent at frequencies ≥ 25 Hz (ruling out simple dipole-gradient mechanism)
- A small anomalous weight reduction (−30 to −50 g) is measurable with counter-rotating coil assembly

The following is **not** established:
- Net system center-of-mass acceleration upward
- Lift that persists in vacuum (aerodynamic contribution uncharacterized)
- Any independent replication of the anomalous force measurements

**For this to constitute a valid propulsion system, the following must be true:**

1. The anomalous weight reduction (−30 to −50 g) must survive vacuum conditions (ruling out aerodynamic origin)
2. The mechanism must produce net center-of-mass acceleration, not merely relative motion between rotor and stator
3. One of the three proposed configurations (A, B, or C above) must successfully transfer the rotor's upward force to the entire vehicle frame
4. The XY-to-Z coupling must be of sufficient efficiency that P_electrical / F_lift exceeds current electric propulsion benchmarks (currently achieving ~41 mN/W — within range of Hall-effect thrusters at 60–80 mN/W, but without propellant)

**What would confirm the core claim:** A precision scale measurement showing total system weight decrease ≥ m_device × g while the device is operating in vacuum, with no physical connection to the ground. Drew identifies this as Step 4 of his roadmap — his next experimental target.
