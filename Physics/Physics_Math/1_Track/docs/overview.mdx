# @RedCollie1 Research Wiki — Overview

## Introduction

Dr. Horace Drew (@RedCollie1) holds a PhD in x-ray diffraction crystallography from Caltech (1981) and conducted postdoctoral research in structural molecular biology at the MRC Laboratory of Molecular Biology in Cambridge, England. After a career in mainstream structural biology, he has spent over two decades investigating the physics of UAP propulsion, motivated in part by a personal close-encounter sighting in 1965 Florida. His research methodology is unconventional: he treats crop circles appearing in Italian fields between 2008 and 2011 as encoded ET engineering schematics, which he decodes using a quaternary base-4 alphabet derived from DNA codon structure, and attempts to replicate the implied devices in his laboratory.

Drew's central propulsion thesis is that UAPs achieve flight by spin — specifically, that a fast-spinning permanent magnet nested inside a counter-rotating flywheel ring, driven by a 3-phase rotating electromagnetic field (XY-plane), generates a net upward force along the Z-axis without expelling a reaction mass. He frames this as a "spin-dependent exception" to Newton's Third Law of action-reaction, in which the electromagnetic field or quantum vacuum absorbs the reaction momentum rather than any mechanical component of the vehicle. Laboratory experiments at bench scale have confirmed component-level behaviors — spinning magnets rise along a vertical rod under 3-phase drive, achieving forces up to ~1 kg at 17–20 Hz — but full system levitation (center of mass lift) has not yet been demonstrated as of April 2026.

This wiki synthesizes Drew's published research — compiled from approximately 100 tweet directories of @RedCollie1 — into structured investigative answers to three core questions about his physics program: whether his claims go beyond classical physics, whether the mechanism constitutes a valid new propulsion paradigm, and whether it constitutes a claim for a new energy source. All content is written at PhD level per project standards, preserving full mathematical formalism.
