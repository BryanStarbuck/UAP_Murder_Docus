## Document Content Summary

This 1984 document is a weapons assessment — not a theoretical physics paper — evaluating Soviet scalar weapons programs based on the Bohm-Aharonov effect. The CIA's decision to commission and circulate this as an internal intelligence document in 1984 establishes that the agency was taking Soviet scalar weapons claims seriously.

### The Bohm-Aharonov Effect

The foundational physics: Aharonov and Bohm demonstrated in 1959 that quantum mechanical charged particles are affected by electromagnetic potentials even in regions where the fields themselves are zero. In standard QED:

**B = ∇ × A**
**E = −∇φ − ∂A/∂t**

Classical electrodynamics treats only E and B as physically observable; A and φ are treated as mathematical conveniences with gauge freedom — changeable by gauge transformation without affecting observables. The Aharonov-Bohm paper (Physical Review 115(3):485–491, 1959) demonstrated that even when B = 0 in the region where a charged quantum particle travels, the vector potential A is nonzero and produces a measurable phase shift in the particle's wave function. This was experimentally confirmed. Implication: the potentials, not just the fields, are the fundamental physical entities in quantum mechanics.

### Bearden's Thesis

Bearden argues that Western physics relegated potentials to mathematical conveniences while Soviet scientists took the BA effect seriously as a basis for a new class of weapons. The key claim: scalar potentials can be engineered to produce interference patterns (scalar interferometry) at distant locations, creating energy deposition, force fields, or other physical effects at a target without detectable electromagnetic field propagation along the beam path.

The effect propagates through the potential (non-observable in classical EM) rather than the field (observable), making it undetectable by conventional electromagnetic sensors.
