1. TOP-LEVEL DESIGN ARCHITECTURE
====================================================================

Sub-approach A (Day 1 focus): Ground-coupled Kelvin lift
  * Rotating Halbach ring mounted as the rotor of the craft
  * Fixed ferromagnetic target disc (e.g., 3 mm mild steel plate)
    secured to the ground or a launch pad
  * Craft rises when Kelvin force on the ring from the ground disc
    exceeds the combined weight of ring + motor + frame

Sub-approach B (Day 2+ focus): Free-flight via time-asymmetric force
  * Same rotor, no external target
  * Investigates whether rotation creates a non-zero time-averaged
    force on the craft itself (requires field symmetry analysis)


====================================================================
2. ROTOR DESIGN — HALBACH RING
====================================================================

Configuration: 8 N52 cube magnets arranged in a horizontal ring.

Ring geometry:
  * Ring diameter: 20 cm (center of magnets at r = 10 cm from axis)
  * Number of magnets: 8 (angular spacing: 45° between centers)
  * Magnet dimensions: 20 mm × 20 mm × 10 mm (W × L × H)
    (20 mm tangential, 20 mm radial, 10 mm axial)
  * Magnet material: NdFeB N52, Br = 1.48 T

Halbach pattern for upward flux concentration:
  The magnetization direction rotates at twice the position angle
  (2× Halbach pattern). For position angle phi = k * 45° (k = 0..7):
    Magnetization angle = 2 * phi measured from the +z axis in the
    xz-plane (or from +z in the rz-plane).

  The 2× pattern creates a strong upward (+z) flux lobe above the
  ring and suppresses flux below the ring. This is the configuration
  that creates ∂B_z/∂z < 0 above the ring (field decreasing with
  height from a peak just above the ring surface).

Physical result: the region 1–3 cm above the ring surface has the
  largest field gradient and therefore the largest Kelvin force
  on any ferromagnetic body placed in that zone.


====================================================================
3. REACTION SURFACE (Sub-approach A)
====================================================================

Fixed launch plate:
  * Material: low-carbon mild steel (ASTM A36 or equivalent)
  * Saturation: B_sat ≈ 2.0 T, M_sat ≈ 1.59 × 10^6 A/m
  * Dimensions: 25 cm diameter disc, 5 mm thick
  * Position: centered below the Halbach ring at a gap distance z_gap
  * Not attached to the craft — fixed to ground

Note: the launch plate must be thicker than the skin depth at the
operating frequency to avoid eddy current losses reducing the
effective permeability. At 1 Hz (motor frequency), skin depth in
steel is ~250 mm, so a 5 mm plate is entirely sub-skin-depth and
eddy currents are negligible. At higher rotation speeds this changes.


====================================================================
4. ROTOR DRIVE SYSTEM
====================================================================

Motor type: Brushless DC (BLDC) or three-phase induction motor
  * Rotation axis: vertical (z-axis)
  * Target RPM range: TBD via simulation
    (low RPM for static coupling; higher for dynamic effects)
  * Torque reaction: counter-rotating flywheel required to prevent
    craft body spin. Mass of flywheel determined by motor torque spec.
  * Alternative: dual counter-rotating Halbach arrays
    (upper ring CW, lower ring CCW; net torque = 0)

Frame: carbon fiber composite (low mass, non-ferromagnetic)
  * Ring frame holds 8 magnets in precise angular alignment
  * Magnet retention: mechanical clamp + epoxy adhesive
  * Centrifugal stress at operating RPM must be verified (Section 5)


====================================================================
5. MECHANICAL CONSTRAINTS
====================================================================

Centrifugal stress on magnets at RPM:
  * Ring radius r = 0.10 m
  * Magnet mass m_mag: density of NdFeB ≈ 7400 kg/m³
    V_mag = 0.02 × 0.02 × 0.01 = 4 × 10^-6 m³
    m_mag = 7400 × 4 × 10^-6 = 0.0296 kg ≈ 30 g each
  * Centrifugal force per magnet at Ω rad/s:
    F_c = m_mag * r * Ω² = 0.0296 × 0.10 × Ω²
  * At 3000 RPM (Ω = 314 rad/s): F_c = 0.0296 × 0.10 × 314² ≈ 292 N
  * Tensile strength of frame material must exceed this.
    Carbon fiber frame: σ_tensile ~ 600 MPa. Adequate at 3000 RPM.
    At 10,000 RPM: F_c ≈ 3250 N — frame geometry must be designed
    to distribute this load.

Magnet demagnetization: NdFeB magnets are sensitive to elevated
temperature. Curie temperature of N52: ~80°C. At high RPM, eddy
current heating in the metal mounting hardware must be avoided.
Use non-conducting magnet holders (G10 fiberglass or PEEK).

Air gap to ground plate:
  * Minimum gap z_gap = 2 mm (mechanical clearance during lift-off)
  * Operating gap (hovering): TBD via force-vs-distance simulation
  * Ground effect: At z_gap < 1 cm, force increases sharply due to
    flux channeling through the iron plate. This is favorable for
    initial lift-off but requires gap control.
