1. CLAIMED LIFT MECHANISM
====================================================================

The Kelvin body force (ponderomotive force on magnetic matter) acting
on a ferromagnetic target body in the spatially non-uniform magnetic
field produced by a rotating asymmetric permanent magnet array. The
time-averaged force has a net upward component when the field geometry
is engineered to concentrate flux above the array and attenuate it
below, creating a spatial gradient whose vertical component does not
cancel over a full rotation cycle.


====================================================================
2. GOVERNING EQUATIONS
====================================================================

2.1 KELVIN BODY FORCE DENSITY

The body force per unit volume on a magnetically linear material in
an external field H is:

  f_i = mu_0 * M_j * (∂H_j / ∂x_i)                     [Eq. 2.1]

In vector notation:

  f = mu_0 * (M · ∇) H                                  [Eq. 2.2]

For a linear paramagnetic or ferromagnetic material:
  M = chi_m * H  (linear regime, chi_m = mu_r - 1)

  f = mu_0 * chi_m * (H · ∇) H = (mu_0 * chi_m / 2) * ∇(H²)  [Eq. 2.3]

The total force on a body of volume V is:

  F = ∫_V mu_0 * (M · ∇) H dV                          [Eq. 2.4]

For a ferromagnetic body at or near saturation (chi_m → ∞):
  M → M_sat (constant, independent of H)

  f_z ≈ M_sat * mu_0 * (∂H_z / ∂z) = M_sat * (∂B_z / ∂z)  [Eq. 2.5]

where B_z = mu_0 * (H_z + M_z) evaluated at the external field H_z.


2.2 EARNSHAW'S THEOREM — WHY IT DOES NOT PROHIBIT THIS DESIGN

Earnshaw's theorem: a static arrangement of magnetic monopoles
(or electric charges, or gravitational masses) cannot be in stable
equilibrium under purely conservative forces. For magnetostatics, the
theorem generalizes to: no body of fixed magnetization can be in
stable equilibrium in the field of external static magnets.

Why Attempt 1 circumvents Earnshaw:

(a) The array rotates — the field is time-varying. Earnshaw applies
    to static fields. Time-varying fields can create stable force
    profiles that static fields cannot.

(b) The target material responds nonlinearly (ferromagnetic saturation)
    — the effective chi_m is field-dependent. Nonlinear response creates
    force profiles not subject to Earnshaw's linear-field theorem.

(c) The configuration is not seeking equilibrium levitation of the
    target but rather lift force on the array (craft) against an
    external fixed target (ground surface). Earnshaw constrains the
    stability of free levitation, not the existence of force between
    a moving source and a fixed target.


2.3 SYMMETRY ARGUMENT FOR NET VERTICAL FORCE

A necessary condition for net lift: the field geometry must break
vertical (z-axis) symmetry. For a symmetric rotating array (same
flux above and below), the Kelvin force on targets above and below
cancel, and the net z-force on the array itself is zero.

Symmetry breaking mechanism: Modified Halbach array with vertical flux
bias. In a standard linear Halbach array, magnetization direction
rotates as a function of position to concentrate flux on one face.
For a ring Halbach array with the rotation period equal to half the
ring circumference, the flux is concentrated radially inward on one
axial face and radially outward (or suppressed) on the other.

The key design choice is the orientation of this concentration:
flux concentrated ABOVE the array creates ∂B_z/∂z < 0 above
(field decreasing with height) → upward Kelvin force on ferromagnetic
target placed above the array.

The array reaction: by Newton's third law, the force on the target
is equal and opposite to the force on the array. If the target is
fixed (external), the array (craft) is pushed away from the target.
If flux is concentrated upward and the target is below the craft,
the craft is pushed upward.

This is the Sub-approach A (ground-coupled) geometry.


2.4 TIME-AVERAGING FOR THE ROTATING CASE

For a rotating magnet array at angular velocity Ω_z (rotation about
the z-axis), the field at a fixed point in space is time-periodic
with period T = 2π/Ω_z. For an N-fold symmetric array:

  B(r, t) = Σ_n B_n * exp(i * n * Ω_z * t)             [Eq. 2.6]

The time-averaged force on a linear target:

  <F_z> = (1/T) ∫_0^T F_z[B(r, t)] dt                  [Eq. 2.7]

For the linear (unsaturated) case:
  F_z ∝ |B|² — which is positive definite.
  Time averaging of |B|² over a rotating field retains only the DC
  (zero-frequency) component of the field intensity. This is the
  ponderomotive force, analogous to the AC Stark shift in optics.

For the saturated ferromagnetic case:
  F_z = M_sat * (∂B_z / ∂z) — which changes sign with the sign of
  ∂B_z / ∂z. Time averaging over a symmetric rotation yields zero.
  Time averaging over an asymmetric rotation (flux concentrated
  upward during one half-cycle, less during the other) yields a
  non-zero time-averaged upward force.

CONCLUSION: Net lift in the rotating case requires the field
gradient ∂B_z/∂z to have a non-zero time-average over one full
rotation period. This requires spatial asymmetry that is preserved
or enhanced by rotation — not eliminated by it.
