====================================================================
2. POWER REQUIREMENTS
====================================================================

Magnet holding force (static):
  The Kelvin force exerted by the rotating array on the ground plate
  at z_gap = 20 mm is estimated at 5.87 N (from math_day_1.mdx).
  The plate is attracted toward the array. The array is pushed upward.
  This static configuration requires zero electrical power (permanent
  magnets provide the field). Power is required only for rotation.

Motor power for rotation:
  Rotational kinetic energy of ring at angular velocity Ω:
    I_ring = 8 × m_mag × R² = 8 × 0.030 × 0.10² = 0.0024 kg·m²
    KE = 0.5 × I_ring × Ω²

  At 1000 RPM: Ω = 104.7 rad/s → KE = 0.5 × 0.0024 × 104.7² = 13.2 J
  Start-up time 5 s → avg power = 13.2 / 5 = 2.6 W (negligible)

  Steady-state power is dominated by:
  (a) Eddy current losses in ground plate and nearby conductors
  (b) Air drag on magnets at operating RPM
  (c) Bearing friction

  Eddy current dissipation (rough estimate):
    At 1000 RPM (f_field = 8 × 1000/60 = 133 Hz for 8-pole array)
    Skin depth in steel: δ = √(2ρ / (ωμ)) = √(2×1.7×10^-7 / (838×4π×10^-7×1000))
    = √(3.4×10^-7 / 1.054×10^-3) = √(3.23×10^-4) = 0.018 m = 18 mm

    At 133 Hz, skin depth ≈ 1.8 mm in steel (μ_r = 1000).
    Eddy power density: P_eddy = (B²ω²δ²) / (2ρ_resistivity) × V_plate
    = (0.003² × 838² × 0.0018²) / (2 × 1.7×10^-7) × V_plate
    Rough estimate: < 50 W for this configuration at 1000 RPM.

  Total electrical budget: 100–200 W at operating RPM (motor + losses).
  Battery pack: 6S LiPo (~22.2 V, 5 Ah) → 111 Wh → ~33 min runtime.


====================================================================
3. MECHANICAL CONSTRAINTS AND SAFETY
====================================================================

Centrifugal stress at maximum RPM (from Design.mdx):
  At 1000 RPM: F_c per magnet = 0.030 × 0.10 × (104.7)² = 32.8 N
  At 3000 RPM: F_c = 295 N per magnet
  Frame must bear 8 × F_c in tension across its cross-section

  G10 fiberglass tensile strength: ~275 MPa
  Cross-section area of frame wall between pockets (minimum):
    A_min = F_c / σ_tensile = 295 / 275×10^6 = 1.07×10^-6 m² ≈ 1 mm²
  This is a very thin section — design wall thickness at ≥ 3 mm for
  safety factor of 3×.

  MAXIMUM SAFE RPM for 3 mm wall section (preliminary):
    F_c = σ_allowable × A = (275×10^6 / 3) × (3×10^-3 × 12×10^-3) = 3,300 N
    Ω_max = √(F_c / (m_mag × R)) = √(3300 / (0.030 × 0.10)) = 3317 rad/s
    RPM_max ≈ 31,700 RPM (well above any motor candidate)

  Structural constraint is not limiting. Rotor imbalance and bearing life
  are the practical speed limiters.

Magnet retention at temperature:
  Loctite 638 shear strength: 25 N/mm² at room temperature
  Bond area per magnet: 20×10 = 200 mm²
  Bond shear force: 25 × 200 = 5000 N (far exceeds centrifugal load)
  Temperature limit of Loctite 638: 150°C (well above Nd Curie limit)

Air gap control:
  Precision of z_gap critically affects lift force (see simulation).
  If lift force is a strong function of z_gap, a gap control servo
  (capacitive or Hall sensor + feedback loop) may be required for
  stable ground-plate hovering.
