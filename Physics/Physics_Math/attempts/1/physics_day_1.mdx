====================================================================
PHYSICS QUESTION FOR TODAY
====================================================================

What is the spatial distribution of the Kelvin body force on a
ferromagnetic target placed above a Halbach permanent magnet ring?
Specifically: does the 8-magnet, 20 cm diameter N52 Halbach ring
defined in Attempt.yaml generate a force sufficient to lift a 500 g
ferromagnetic target at any accessible height (1–10 cm above ring)?


====================================================================
SECTION 1 — FIELD PHYSICS OF THE HALBACH RING
====================================================================

1.1 SINGLE MAGNETIC DIPOLE FIELD

A permanent magnet of volume V_m, remanence B_r, magnetized along ẑ
has a dipole moment:

  m = (B_r / μ_0) * V_m                                 [Eq. 1.1]

On the symmetry axis (r = 0) at height z above the dipole center:

  B_z(0, z) = (μ_0 / 4π) * (2m / z³)                   [Eq. 1.2]

This is the far-field (z >> magnet dimension) approximation. For a
10 mm tall magnet evaluated at z = 10 cm, the aspect ratio is 10:1,
making this approximation accurate to ~5%.

For the 20×20×10 mm N52 magnet:
  V_m = 20 × 20 × 10 × 10^-9 m³ = 4 × 10^-6 m³
  m = (1.48 / 4π × 10^-7) * 4 × 10^-6 ≈ 4.712 A·m²    [Eq. 1.3]


1.2 HALBACH RING SUPERPOSITION

An N-magnet Halbach ring of radius R with magnetization direction
rotating at angular rate p (p = 2 for the 2× Halbach pattern) has
a field that, at height z on the symmetry axis, is the coherent
superposition of N individual dipole fields. For a 2× Halbach pattern,
the dipole moments projected onto ẑ are:

  m_z^(k) = m * cos(2 * (2πk/N))  for k = 0, 1, ..., N-1    [Eq. 1.4]

where the factor of 2 in the cosine comes from the 2× magnetization
rotation. For N = 8, the N ẑ-components sum as:

  M_z_total = m * Σ_{k=0}^{7} cos(2 * kπ/4)
             = m * Σ_{k=0}^{7} cos(kπ/2)
             = m * [1 + 0 + (-1) + 0 + 1 + 0 + (-1) + 0] = 0  [Eq. 1.5]

IMPORTANT RESULT: The net z-dipole moment of the 8-magnet 2× Halbach
ring is ZERO. The on-axis far-field (dipole approximation) B_z from
the ring is therefore zero at all z.

This does NOT mean the field is zero — it means the dipole term
vanishes and the field is dominated by quadrupole and higher multipole
terms. The on-axis field falls off faster than z^-3 (at least z^-5
for the quadrupole term), which means the Kelvin force also falls
off faster with distance.

IMPLICATION FOR DESIGN: The 2× Halbach ring does NOT create a strong
on-axis upward gradient force. The flux is concentrated RADIALLY in
the plane of the ring, not axially. For axial (vertical) Kelvin
force, the field gradient must be maximized along the z-axis.


1.3 REVISED GEOMETRY: AXIAL FLUX CONCENTRATION

To produce a large ∂B_z/∂z above the ring, the magnetic flux must
be directed upward. This requires each magnet to have a net upward
(+z) component of magnetization. Several options:

Option A — Simple axial array: All magnets magnetized in +z direction.
  Field on axis: N dipoles at radius R, all pointing +z.
  B_z(0, z) = N * (μ_0/4π) * m_z * f(z, R)
  where f(z, R) accounts for off-axis geometry (computed numerically).

Option B — Modified Halbach with axial bias: Magnets magnetized in a
  pattern that combines tangential Halbach concentration with a net
  upward (+z) component. Each magnet's magnetization has:
    M = M_0 * (sin(θ) r̂ + cos(θ) ẑ)  with θ = 2φ_k
  This creates both radial flux concentration and a net z-component.

Option C — Two-layer array: An upper Halbach ring (concentrates flux
  upward) above a lower Halbach ring (concentrates flux downward from
  below). The superposition creates a strong axial gradient between
  the two layers and above the upper layer.

Day 1 simulation tests Option A (simple axial array) and Option B
(2× Halbach ring). Option C is a Day 2 follow-on if A or B shows
insufficient gradient.


1.4 KELVIN FORCE ON FERROMAGNETIC TARGET — PHYSICAL ANALYSIS

For a ferromagnetic body at magnetic saturation, M_sat ≈ B_sat/μ_0:
  For mild steel: B_sat ≈ 2.0 T → M_sat ≈ 1.59 × 10^6 A/m

Body force density at saturation:
  f_z = M_sat * ∂B_z/∂z = (B_sat / μ_0) * ∂B_z/∂z      [Eq. 1.6]

For the body not at saturation (linear regime), with χ_m >> 1:
  f_z ≈ (χ_m / (1 + χ_m)) * (1/μ_0) * B_z * ∂B_z/∂z ≈ (1/μ_0) * B_z * ∂B_z/∂z  [Eq. 1.7]

The total lift force on a steel disc of mass m_target = 0.5 kg:
  Volume: V_target = m_target / ρ_steel = 0.5 / 7800 ≈ 6.41 × 10^-5 m³

  Required average force density to lift target:
    f_z_required = m_target * g / V_target = 4.905 / 6.41×10^-5 ≈ 76,500 N/m³  [Eq. 1.8]

  Required ∂B_z/∂z (at saturation):
    ∂B_z/∂z = f_z * μ_0 / B_sat = 76,500 * 4π×10^-7 / 2.0 ≈ 0.048 T/m  [Eq. 1.9]

This is a VERY achievable gradient. Permanent magnet assemblies
routinely produce gradients of 1–100 T/m at 1–5 cm distance.

CONCLUSION: The required field gradient for Kelvin lift of a 0.5 kg
steel target (∂B_z/∂z ≈ 0.048 T/m) is modest and well within the
range of N52 permanent magnet arrays. The geometric design question
is whether the Halbach ring can produce this gradient at the
operating height with the specified magnet count and dimensions.


====================================================================
SECTION 2 — DEPARTURE FROM STANDARD PHYSICS FRAMEWORK
====================================================================

This approach operates entirely within classical electrodynamics.
There is no claim of beyond-standard physics for Attempt 1.

The Kelvin body force is a well-established result derivable from
Maxwell's stress tensor applied to a polarizable medium. The relevant
equations are:

  T_ij = μ_0 * H_i * H_j + (1/μ_0) * B_i * B_j / 2 - δ_ij * (μ_0 H² + B²/μ_0) / 2

The force on a body is the surface integral of T_ij * n_j over the
body surface (or volume integral of ∂T_ij/∂x_j over the body volume).

The only "unusual" claim is that a rotating magnet array with
asymmetric geometry can produce a sustained net upward force on the
craft itself without an external reaction surface. That claim (Sub-
approach B) requires the time-averaged force on the current-carrying
system itself to be non-zero — which is not guaranteed by Noether's
theorem for a time-periodic, spatially asymmetric field configuration
interacting with its own mechanical frame through a nonlinear medium.

Day 1 does not make this claim. Day 1 establishes the static force
baseline using only Sub-approach A (external target fixed to ground).


====================================================================
SECTION 3 — WHAT SIMULATION MUST SHOW
====================================================================

From the physics above, the simulation must compute:

1. B_z(0, z) on the symmetry axis above the ring for z = 1–10 cm
2. ∂B_z/∂z as a function of z (numerical gradient of B_z)
3. F_z(z) = M_sat * (∂B_z/∂z) * V_target at each z
4. Whether F_z exceeds m_target * g at any accessible z
5. Comparison between Option A (all-axial) and Option B (2× Halbach)
   to determine which geometry produces the larger on-axis gradient

If Option A exceeds the target at any z, the Sub-approach A design
is viable as a ground-coupled launch system.
