====================================================================
2. MAIN SIMULATION — Halbach Ring Field and Kelvin Force
====================================================================

Software:    Magpylib 5.0.1
Script:      {ROOT_DIR}/simulation/magpylib/software/run_1_1_kelvin_force.py
Test:        Compare two ring geometries:
             Option A: 8 N52 magnets, all +z magnetization (axial array)
             Option B: 8 N52 magnets, 2× Halbach magnetization pattern

Geometry (both options):
  N:         8 magnets
  R:         0.10 m (10 cm radius, magnet centers)
  Magnet:    20×20×10 mm (W×L×H)
  Br:        1.48 T

Observing grid:
  z range:   0.005 m to 0.100 m (5 mm to 10 cm above ring plane)
  Points:    50 equally spaced on z-axis (0, 0, z)

Computed quantities:
  * B_z(z): z-component of field on symmetry axis
  * ∂B_z/∂z: numerical gradient (np.gradient with z_array)
  * F_kelvin(z): Kelvin force on 500 g steel target at each height
    F = V_target × B_sat × (∂B_z/∂z) / μ_0 × μ_0
      = V_target × M_sat × (∂B_z/∂z)
    where M_sat = B_sat/μ_0 = 2.0/(4π×10^-7) = 1.592×10^6 A/m
    V_target = 0.5/7800 = 6.41×10^-5 m³

Success criterion:
  F_kelvin(z) / (m_target × g) > 1.0 at any z in [0.005, 0.05] m

Comparison metric:
  Which option (A or B) produces larger F_kelvin?
  At what height z is the Kelvin force maximized?


====================================================================
3. INPUTS AND BOUNDARY CONDITIONS
====================================================================

All simulations run in free space (μ_r = 1 everywhere except magnets).
Ground plate is NOT included in the Magpylib model — Magpylib handles
the permanent magnet fields; the ground plate effect is captured by
treating the plate as the target body in the force calculation
(the Kelvin force on the plate = reaction force on the ring array).

No thermal, eddy current, or time-varying effects in Day 1.
This is a pure magnetostatic calculation.
