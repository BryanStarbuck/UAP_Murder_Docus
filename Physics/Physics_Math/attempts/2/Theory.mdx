1. THE PHYSICAL MECHANISM
====================================================================

A conductor at fixed electrostatic potential V in free space develops
a surface charge density sigma(r) determined by the geometry of the
conductor surface. The local surface charge density is not uniform
for an asymmetric conductor — it is higher where the surface has
greater curvature (sharper features, apices, edges).

The local electric field immediately outside the conductor surface is
perpendicular to the surface (boundary condition: tangential E = 0
for a conductor) and equal to:

    E_n = sigma / eps_0     [V/m]

where sigma is the local surface charge density [C/m^2] and eps_0 is
the permittivity of free space (8.854e-12 F/m).

The electrostatic pressure (Maxwell electrostatic stress normal to
the surface) acting on the conductor surface is:

    P = eps_0 * E_n^2 / 2 = sigma^2 / (2 * eps_0)     [Pa = N/m^2]

This pressure acts outward (away from the conductor, in the direction
of n_hat). For each surface element dA with outward normal n_hat(r),
the force contribution is:

    dF = P(r) * n_hat(r) * dA     [N]

The total net force on the conductor is the surface integral:

    F = integral over S of P(r) * n_hat(r) dA
      = integral over S of (eps_0/2) * E_n(r)^2 * n_hat(r) dA     [N]


====================================================================
2. WHY A SYMMETRIC CONDUCTOR HAS ZERO NET FORCE
====================================================================

For a perfect sphere of radius R at potential V, the charge distribution
is uniform: sigma = eps_0 * V / R everywhere on the surface. The field
is radially symmetric: E_n(r) = V/R, constant. The outward normal n_hat
is radially outward. By symmetry, for every surface element at position
r contributing force dF = P * n_hat(r) * dA, there is an equal and
opposite element at -r contributing -dF. The integral vanishes:

    F_sphere = (eps_0/2) * (V/R)^2 * integral over sphere of n_hat dA = 0

This is the symmetry cancellation result. A sphere in isolation has
zero net electrostatic force. This provides the validation test: any
electrostatic simulation code must return zero net force for a sphere.


====================================================================
3. WHY AN ASYMMETRIC CONDUCTOR HAS NONZERO NET FORCE
====================================================================

Consider a hemisphere of radius R at potential V, positioned with its
flat face on a grounded conducting plane (z = 0 plane, grounded to V = 0).

The hemisphere is at potential V. The plane is at V = 0. The boundary
conditions are:
  * On the hemisphere surface S_h: V = V_0
  * On the ground plane z = 0: V = 0
  * At infinity: V -> 0

The resulting E-field distribution is asymmetric. At the apex of the
hemisphere (topmost point, theta = 0), the field is concentrated because
the local radius of curvature is smallest and the charge density is
highest. At the equator of the hemisphere (theta = pi/2), the geometry
transitions to the flat plane, and the field gradient changes character.

The key asymmetry: the E-field at the apex is approximately:

    E_apex ≈ 3 * V_0 / R     [V/m]    (for hemisphere on ground plane)

This is 3× the field of a full sphere at the same potential. The field
is highly concentrated at the apex compared to a sphere. Meanwhile, the
base of the hemisphere (edge at z = 0) has a different field geometry
entirely, governed by the ground plane boundary condition.

The resulting net force integral:

    F_z = integral over S_h of (eps_0/2) * E_n(r)^2 * cos(theta) * dA

is nonzero because E_n(theta) is not symmetric about the midplane.
The apex has higher E^2 contribution and its n_hat has a larger z-component
(cos theta → 1 at apex). The equatorial regions have lower E^2 and
their n_hat has cos theta → 0. There is no opposing surface below (the
flat base is at the ground plane, with different boundary conditions).

Net result: F_z ≠ 0, directed upward (toward the apex).

This is the fundamental claim. It is NOT a violation of Newton's third law:
the reaction force is on the ground plane (and whatever is connected to it
through the circuit that maintains the potential difference).

4. THE MAXWELL STRESS TENSOR — FULL FORMULATION
====================================================================

The Maxwell stress tensor for electrostatics is:

    T_ij = eps_0 * (E_i * E_j - (1/2) * delta_ij * E_k * E_k)     [Pa]

where i, j, k are Cartesian indices, and summation over repeated indices
is implied (Einstein convention). In vector notation:

    T = eps_0 * (E ⊗ E - (1/2) * I * |E|^2)

The force on a volume V surrounded by a closed surface S is:

    F_i = integral over S of T_ij * n_j dA

where n is the outward unit normal to S. For a conductor, the surface
charge density determines the field just outside:

    E = (sigma / eps_0) * n_hat    at the conductor surface

Substituting into T_ij:

    T_ij * n_j = eps_0 * (E_i (E · n) - (1/2) * n_i * |E|^2)
               = eps_0 * E_n * (E_i - (1/2) * n_i * E_n)

For a conductor where E is purely normal (E_tangential = 0):

    T_ij * n_j = eps_0 * E_n^2 * n_i - (1/2) * eps_0 * E_n^2 * n_i
               = (eps_0 / 2) * E_n^2 * n_i

This confirms the pressure form P = eps_0 * E_n^2 / 2 acting in the
outward normal direction. No tangential components survive for a perfect
conductor in electrostatic equilibrium.

5. BUHLER EXPERIMENTAL RESULT AND THIS THEORY
====================================================================

Dr. Charles Buhler at NASA Kennedy Center demonstrated net thrust from
an asymmetric electrostatic device in hard vacuum. The critical design
element: asymmetric electrode geometry that creates a non-uniform
electrostatic pressure distribution over the device surface.

Buhler's result is in the millinewton range. The force scales as:

    F ~ eps_0 * V^2 * L    [rough dimensional analysis]

where L is the characteristic linear size of the conductor. For V = 10 kV
and L = 0.1 m:

    F ~ 8.854e-12 * (10e3)^2 * 0.1 = 8.854e-8 N ≈ 88 nN

This naive estimate is far below millinewton scale. The actual Buhler
result requires a more careful geometry with concentrated charge and a
specific asymmetry. Geometry is the key amplifier. The hemisphere apex
concentration factor and the ground plane interaction both amplify the
force beyond the naive estimate.

The connection to Cernohajev (Manual Section 3.5): Gene Sticco claims
Cernohajev independently derived the theoretical basis for this effect
from UAP craft analysis in the 1980s. Cernohajev specifies a charge
density of 0.2e-6 C/m^3 in the context of his solenoid array design.
Whether this connects directly to the surface-charge mechanism here
is unresolved but worth investigating in a later attempt.

7. DEPARTURE FROM CONVENTIONAL PHYSICS EXPECTATIONS
====================================================================

Standard electrostatics textbooks do not emphasize that asymmetric
conductors generate net forces on themselves. This is because in the
typical context, a conductor in free space (no ground plane, no external
fields) has zero net force — the electrostatic pressure is internal
stress that the conductor material supports. The body as a whole has
no net force.

The critical departure in the Buhler configuration: the conductor is
NOT in free space. It is in the presence of a ground plane. The ground
plane breaks the symmetry of the return path for the electric field.
The force on the asymmetric conductor relative to the ground plane is
nonzero.

In a circuit sense: the capacitor formed by the hemisphere and the
ground plane has a force between the plates. For a parallel-plate
capacitor, this force is attractive (toward the other plate). For an
asymmetric geometry, the force has a component that is NOT toward the
ground plane — it can have a net tangential or off-normal component.

This is the mechanism that Buhler measured: not a violation of
conservation of momentum, but an asymmetric distribution of the
electrostatic force between an asymmetric conductor and its circuit
ground. The reaction force is on the ground (the rest of the circuit).
If the ground is the Earth (a Faraday cage building, for example), the
force on the conductor is an effective thrust relative to ground.

For propulsion: the ground plane must be replaced by something. In
free space, you need a separate ground reference. This is why Buhler's
result, while real, requires careful thought about the closed system.
In vacuum, if the device includes its own internal ground (a capacitor
with asymmetric electrodes), the net force on the external package is
what matters. This is the Day 2+ question.
