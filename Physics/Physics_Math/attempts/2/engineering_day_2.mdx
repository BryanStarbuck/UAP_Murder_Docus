====================================================================
1. DESIGN OVERVIEW
====================================================================

The Day 2 device is a bench-scale Buhler-analog electrostatic thrust
demonstrator. Goal: demonstrate measurable net vertical force from
asymmetric electrostatic pressure in a geometry that is reproducible
and scalable.

Component list:
1. Aluminum hemisphere (the primary asymmetric conductor)
2. Aluminum base plate (the grounded reference)
3. PTFE (polytetrafluoroethylene) mounting post (insulator)
4. High-voltage DC power supply
5. Precision microforce balance (for thrust measurement)
6. Vacuum chamber (for clean measurement without ion wind)


====================================================================
2. PRIMARY CONDUCTOR — HEMISPHERE
====================================================================

Material: Aluminum 6061-T6 (or equivalent high-conductivity aluminum)
  * Conductivity: 2.4e7 S/m — effectively perfect conductor
  * Surface finish: polished to < 1 micron Ra to minimize field emission
    and corona discharge
  * Why: rough surfaces cause field enhancement at protrusions that
    can initiate breakdown at voltages much lower than smooth-surface
    estimates

Geometry:
  * Hemisphere: radius R = 10.0 cm ± 0.5 mm
  * Base diameter: 20.0 cm
  * Height (apex to base): 10.0 cm
  * Wall thickness: 2.0 mm (hollow to reduce mass)
  * Mass estimate: pi * R * t * rho_Al = pi * 0.1 * 0.002 * 2700 ≈ 1.7 g
    (hollow hemispherical shell)
  * Mounting flange: 4× M3 tapped holes at base perimeter, on 18 cm
    bolt circle, for insulated post attachment

Surface treatment:
  * Electropolish after machining to remove machining marks and oxide
  * Anodize with thin (5-10 micron) hard anodize for surface passivation
  * Do NOT anodize apex region: anodize is an insulator and would
    prevent charge distribution from reaching the apex. Keep apex
    region polished aluminum.

CRITICAL: The sharp edge at the hemisphere base (where flat base
meets the curved surface) must be rounded to R_edge ≥ 2 mm radius.
Sharp edges cause catastrophic field enhancement:
  E_edge ~ E_apex * (R_apex / R_edge) = 3 V/R * (R / R_edge)
  For R_edge = 0.1 mm: E_edge ~ 30 * E_apex → immediate breakdown


====================================================================
3. GROUND REFERENCE PLATE
====================================================================

Material: Aluminum 6061-T6 (same as hemisphere, matching conductivity)

Geometry:
  * Square plate: 50 cm × 50 cm × 3 mm thickness
  * The hemisphere is mounted at center of plate on insulating post
  * Ground connection: #12 AWG copper wire to true earth ground (or
    negative terminal of HV supply for a floating system)

Surface finish: Clean, no anodize (anodize is dielectric and would
  change the effective boundary condition).

Position: The plate is at z = 0 in our coordinate system. The hemisphere
  base sits on the PTFE post at z = z_post, with the hemisphere curved
  surface beginning above.

NOTE: For the simulation, the ground plane extends to infinity. In the
physical device, the 50 cm plate is a finite approximation. The plate
must be large enough that edge effects are negligible:
  * At plate edge (25 cm from center), the hemisphere field has fallen to:
    E(r=25cm) ~ V_0 * (R/r)^2 ~ 10kV * (10/25)^2 = 10kV * 0.16 = 1.6 kV/25cm
    = 6.4 kV/m — far from breakdown
  * The force contribution from the region r > 25 cm is negligible.
  * 50 cm plate is adequate for 10 cm hemisphere.


====================================================================
4. INSULATING MOUNTING POST
====================================================================

Material: PTFE (Polytetrafluoroethylene)
  * Dielectric strength: 20-24 MV/m
  * Dielectric constant: 2.1 (low — minimizes capacitive loading)
  * Volume resistivity: > 10^18 Ohm*cm (effectively perfect insulator)
  * Mechanically machinable, dimensionally stable

Geometry:
  * Cylinder: outer diameter 25 mm, inner bore 10 mm (for HV wire)
  * Height: 20 mm (separates hemisphere from ground plate by 20 mm)
  * Base flange: 60 mm diameter for bolted attachment to ground plate
  * Top flange: 60 mm diameter for bolted attachment to hemisphere base

The post must carry the electrostatic force load. From math_day_2.mdx,
maximum expected force is ~6 mN at 10 kV. PTFE in compression handles
~25 MPa; the post cross section is pi*(0.0125)^2 = 4.9e-4 m^2.
Maximum allowable load: 25e6 * 4.9e-4 = 12,250 N >> 6 mN. Post strength
is more than adequate. Thread engagement is the limiting factor.


====================================================================
5. HIGH-VOLTAGE POWER SUPPLY
====================================================================

Requirements:
  * Output voltage: 0-50 kV DC, continuously adjustable
  * Output current: 1-10 mA maximum (capacitive load, not resistive)
  * Polarity: positive (hemisphere at +V_0, ground plate at 0 V)
  * Regulation: < 0.1% ripple (clean DC for static measurement)
  * Leakage current measurement: ideally built-in to separate leakage
    from true electrostatic force

Capacitance of the device: C = 2 pi eps_0 R ≈ 2 pi * 8.854e-12 * 0.1
  = 5.6e-12 F = 5.6 pF (rough estimate for hemisphere near ground plane)
  More accurate: C ~ 10-20 pF for this geometry.

Charging energy: E = (1/2) C V^2 = (1/2) * 20e-12 * (10e3)^2 = 1e-3 J = 1 mJ.
  Charging time: < 1 ms with a 1 mA supply. Quasi-static measurement is valid.

Leakage concern: Any leakage current through the PTFE post creates an
  ion wind or corona effect that contaminates the force measurement.
  PTFE resistance at 10 kV across 20 mm: I_leak = V / R_post = 10e3 / (10^18 * 0.02 / 4.9e-4)
  = 10e3 / 4.1e13 = 2.4e-10 A = 0.24 nA. Negligible.

Vacuum requirement: At 10 kV, air breakdown occurs at the apex field
  E_apex = 3 * 10e3 / 0.1 = 300 kV/m. Air breakdown field is ~3 MV/m.
  Ratio: 300 kV/m / 3000 kV/m = 0.1. Safe in air at 10 kV, R = 10 cm.
  
  At V = 280 kV (lift force scale): E_apex = 8.4 MV/m > air breakdown.
  Vacuum required for lift-scale operation. For validation at 10 kV,
  air operation is safe.


====================================================================
6. FORCE MEASUREMENT SETUP
====================================================================

Measurement approach: Direct force measurement via precision balance.

Option A: Weigh the ground plate.
  * Place ground plate on a precision balance.
  * Apply HV to hemisphere.
  * If F_z acts upward on hemisphere, the reaction force F_z (downward)
    acts on the ground plate.
  * The balance reading increases by F_z when HV is applied.
  * Resolution needed: < 1 mN = 0.1 g force (0.1 g sensitivity balance)
  * Many lab balances read to 0.01 g = 0.1 mN. Adequate.

Option B: Suspend hemisphere from a torsion wire, measure deflection.
  * More complex setup. Not needed if Option A is available.

Option A preferred: it is simple, direct, and ground-plane reaction
confirms the force is electrostatic (if it were ion wind blowing
downward on the ground plate, the balance reading would also increase,
but ion wind can be eliminated by vacuum testing).

Vacuum testing:
  * Seal the entire assembly in a vacuum chamber.
  * Reduce pressure to < 1e-3 mbar to eliminate all ion transport.
  * If the ground plate force increase survives in vacuum, the mechanism
    is purely electrostatic (Maxwell stress). Ion wind is eliminated.
  * This is the critical test that distinguishes the mechanism.


====================================================================
7. BUILDABLE MINIMUM TEST CONFIGURATION
====================================================================

Minimum viable test (bench test in air, 10 kV):

* 1 aluminum hemisphere, R = 10 cm (can be machined or purchased as
  aluminum bowl/dome from hardware supply)
* 1 aluminum plate 50 × 50 cm
* 1 PTFE cylinder 25 mm OD × 20 mm height
* 1 HV supply 0-30 kV (available from Spellman or similar, $500-3000)
* 1 analytical balance, 0.01 g resolution (available for $200-500)
* Standard BNC/SHV coaxial cabling rated for 30 kV

Expected result in air at 10 kV:
  F_z ≈ 2-6 mN (from simulation prediction)
  Balance reading increase: 0.2-0.6 g

This is a detectable signal on a 0.01 g balance with 20-60× SNR.

Minimum cost: ~$1000-4000 for components.
Build time: 1-2 weeks (machining and assembly).
Clean room or special conditions: NOT required for validation test.

8. WHAT CAN BE SIMULATED VS. WHAT REQUIRES PHYSICAL BUILD
====================================================================

Simulatable:
  * E-field distribution over the hemisphere surface (scipy/numpy, Day 2)
  * Net force from Maxwell stress tensor integral (scipy/numpy, Day 2)
  * Scaling of force with voltage and geometry (parametric sweep, Day 2)
  * Dependence on ground plate distance and size (can model as finite plate)
  * Multiple hemisphere stack force (Day 3+)

Requires physical build:
  * Confirm absence of ion wind (vacuum test)
  * Measure actual leakage current (determines if mechanism is clean)
  * Confirm force magnitude agrees with simulation
  * Identify failure modes (field emission, corona onset, mechanical)
  * Validate that multiple elements add force without cancellation

The simulation results today will determine whether the physical build
is worth pursuing. If simulation shows force is too small to measure
with available equipment, redesign before building.
