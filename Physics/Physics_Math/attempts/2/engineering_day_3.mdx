1. ENGINEERING IMPLICATIONS OF DAY 3 PHYSICS RESULTS
====================================================================

The isolated conductor analysis in physics_day_3.mdx establishes that
the Buhler mechanism requires an external reaction body. This fundamentally
changes the engineering design space.

The following configurations are physically viable for a craft:


====================================================================
2. CONFIGURATION A: GROUND-REFERENCED PROPULSION SYSTEM
    (Near-surface craft with external ground reference)
====================================================================

2.1 System Description

The craft carries a high-voltage electrode array. The Earth (or any
large conducting body) serves as the external ground plane / reaction body.
The Maxwell stress forces the craft upward against the reaction force
transmitted to Earth via the electrostatic field.

2.2 Key Engineering Parameters

Electrode geometry: Hemisphere array, R = 10 cm, N = 100 elements
    * Voltage per element: 10 kV (manageable with commercial HV supplies)
    * Lift per element (V = 10 kV, R = 10 cm): F_z ≈ 9.5 mN
    * Total lift (N = 100): F_z,total ≈ 0.95 N
    * Lift mass: 0.95 / 9.81 ≈ 97 grams

For 0.5 kg lift:
    * N = 100 elements at 22.7 kV each, OR
    * N = 10 elements at 71.8 kV each, OR
    * N = 1 element at 227 kV

Power requirement:
    * Electrostatic system: P = V × I
    * I depends on leakage and corona discharge
    * In vacuum (no corona): I approaches zero, P → 0
    * In air: corona onset at E > 3 MV/m limits electrode geometry
    * At apex of hemisphere: E_apex = 3V/R = 3 × 10000/0.1 = 300 kV/m
    * This is below air corona threshold (3000 kV/m) — air operation possible!
    * However: air ion wind (NOT corona) contributes to force in air
    * The Buhler vacuum test is required to isolate the pure electrostatic effect

2.3 Altitude Limitation

The mechanism requires the craft to be close enough to a conducting surface
to have significant field coupling. The force falls off as the distance to
the ground plane increases.

For a hemisphere at height H above the ground:

    F_z(H) ≈ F_z(H=0) × (R / (R + H))²  (approximate, dipole falloff)

At H = 1 m, R = 10 cm: F_z(1m) ≈ F_z(0) × (0.1/1.1)² ≈ 0.83% of F_z(0)

This is a severe limitation. At 1 meter altitude, the force is < 1% of
the ground-reference value. This configuration is essentially limited to
surface skimming (H < R) or requires an extremely high voltage to compensate.

At H = R = 10 cm and V = 227 kV:
    F_z(H=R) ≈ F_z(H=0) × (1/2)² = 25% × F_z(0) = 25% × 0.5 × 9.81 = 1.23 N

Still sufficient for 0.5 kg lift at half-hemisphere-height.

Engineering verdict for Configuration A: VIABLE FOR NEAR-SURFACE FLIGHT ONLY.
Range limited to H ≲ 5R from ground. At R = 10 cm, max useful altitude ~ 50 cm.


====================================================================
3. CONFIGURATION B: STACKED MULTI-ELEMENT ARRAY
    (Higher voltage, more elements for increased lift)
====================================================================

3.1 Stack Design

Stack N hemispherical elements vertically, each at potential V relative
to the element below it. The bottom element is referenced to external ground.

The total force F_z,total = N × F_z,single if elements are spaced far enough
to avoid field cancellation between adjacent elements.

Required spacing: D_min ≈ 5R between element centers to avoid field interference.
For R = 10 cm: D_min ≈ 50 cm between centers → total stack height ≈ N × 50 cm.

For N = 10 elements at R = 10 cm:
    Stack height: 5 m (impractical for a compact craft)
    Total F_z: 10 × 9.5 mN at 10 kV = 95 mN at 10 kV per element
    For 0.5 kg: need V = 71.8 kV at 10 kV/element (7 elements at 10 kV each)
    Stack height for 7 elements: 3.5 m

Verdict: Stack configuration requires unacceptably large physical dimensions
for reasonable voltages. A flat array (2D, N elements side-by-side) is more
compact.

3.2 Flat Array Design

N = 100 hemispherical elements in a 10×10 array, each of radius R = 2 cm.
    Spacing: D = 5R = 10 cm center-to-center
    Array dimensions: 10 × 10 cm = 100 cm × 100 cm = 1 m²
    Each element: F_z = 9.5 mN at 10 kV (independent of R — confirmed Day 3)
    Total F_z = 100 × 9.5 mN = 950 mN ≈ 0.97 N
    For 0.5 kg: required F_z = 4.905 N → need ~500 elements at 10 kV
    5 m × 5 m array for 500 elements

For practical craft (1 m²):
    N = 100, V = 10 kV per element: F_z = 0.95 N → 97 g lift
    N = 100, V = 32 kV per element: F_z = 10 N → 1 kg lift

Engineering verdict for Configuration B:
    A 1 m² flat array at 32 kV achieves 1 kg lift.
    This is COMPACT and MANAGEABLE from a high-voltage perspective.
    The 32 kV supply is off-the-shelf laboratory HV.
    The electromagnetic emissions from such an array need analysis.

====================================================================
5. HIGH-VOLTAGE SYSTEM SPECIFICATIONS
====================================================================

5.1 Voltage Supply

Required: 10-100 kV DC, adjustable, 1-100 mA
Available off-shelf: Glassman, UltraVolt, Spellman HV supplies
    * Glassman ER series: 0-100 kV, 0-1 mA, compact
    * Cost: ~$2,000-5,000 per supply
    * One supply per element or shared supply with resistive divider network

5.2 Insulation Requirements

Between HV electrode and craft structure:
    Dielectric strength requirement: 100 kV over 5 cm gap = 2 MV/m
    PTFE (Teflon): dielectric strength 19.7 MV/m → 5 cm PTFE is safe
    Alumina ceramic: dielectric strength 10-20 MV/m → also safe

Electrode mounting post:
    * PTFE rod, 2 cm diameter, 5 cm height
    * No sharp edges on mounting structure (chamfer to avoid corona)
    * Surface resistivity of PTFE > 10¹⁵ Ω (minimal leakage)

5.3 Corona and Breakdown in Air

At V = 10 kV, R = 10 cm:
    E_apex = 300 kV/m = 0.3 MV/m
    Air breakdown field: 3 MV/m (10× higher)
    Corona onset for hemispherical geometry: ~1 MV/m
    → No corona at 10 kV, R = 10 cm in air

At V = 32 kV, R = 10 cm:
    E_apex = 960 kV/m = 0.96 MV/m
    → Still below corona onset in clean dry air
    → Marginal in humid air or near surfaces

At V = 100 kV, R = 10 cm:
    E_apex = 3 MV/m = air breakdown threshold
    → Corona discharge begins in air. Vacuum required above 100 kV / (R = 10 cm).

Design rule: For air operation, keep V < 50 kV at R = 10 cm.
For 0.5 kg lift in air with R = 10 cm: need N = 50 elements at 50 kV each.
Array: 7×7 elements, 0.49 m² footprint. Manageable.

5.4 Vacuum Chamber Requirements for Validation

For Buhler-mechanism validation (eliminating ion wind):
    * Chamber volume: 60 cm diameter, 60 cm height
    * Pressure: < 10⁻⁵ Torr (turbomolecular pump)
    * Feedthrough: 100 kV HV ceramic feedthrough (MDC Vacuum, Kurt Lesker)
    * Internal suspension: torsion balance or force transducer
    * Data: force vs. voltage, force vs. geometry, force vs. pressure (to show vanishing at 1 atm is NOT observed = confirms mechanism is not ion wind)

Estimated cost of minimum validation setup:
    * Vacuum chamber: $3,000-8,000
    * Turbomolecular pump + controller: $5,000-12,000
    * HV feedthrough: $500-2,000
    * HV supply: $2,000-5,000
    * Force measurement: $500-2,000
    Total: $11,000-29,000

This is a realizable laboratory experiment.

7. MINIMUM VIABLE PROTOTYPE FOR PROOF-OF-CONCEPT
====================================================================

Target: Demonstrate measurable upward force (> 1 mN) from electrostatic
pressure asymmetry in air, before committing to vacuum setup.

Components:
    * 10 cm radius aluminum hemisphere (machined or hand-formed)
    * 25 cm × 25 cm aluminum ground plate
    * PTFE mounting post (3 cm dia, 10 cm high)
    * 10-30 kV DC HV supply
    * Analytical balance (0.01 g resolution) as force sensor
    * Option: digital scale with 0.01 g resolution, isolated from HV

Test procedure:
    1. Place assembly on scale (not connected to HV)
    2. Record baseline weight
    3. Apply 10 kV to hemisphere
    4. Record weight change
    5. Repeat at 20 kV, 30 kV
    6. Plot weight vs. V² (should be linear if mechanism is Maxwell stress)
    7. Compare weight change to predicted F_z (9.5 mN = 0.97 g at 10 kV)

NOTE: In air (not vacuum), the measured force will include BOTH the
electrostatic Maxwell stress AND an ion wind contribution. Ion wind
will produce a larger, direction-dependent force. To isolate the
Maxwell stress component:
    * Ion wind is inversely proportional to air pressure
    * Maxwell stress is independent of pressure (it's a surface force)
    * Measuring force at multiple pressures (atmospheric → rough vacuum)
      and extrapolating to P = 0 isolates the Maxwell stress contribution

Cost of minimum prototype: $200-500.
This is a viable first step before investing in a vacuum chamber.
