====================================================================
1. PROBLEM STATEMENT AND COORDINATE SYSTEM
====================================================================

Geometry:
  * Hemisphere of radius R centered at origin, z >= 0
  * Flat base of hemisphere coincides with z = 0 plane
  * Grounded conducting plane at z = 0, extending to rho -> infinity
  * Hemisphere at electrostatic potential V_0
  * Vacuum permittivity eps_0 = 8.854187817e-12 F/m

Spherical coordinates (r, theta, phi):
  * r: radial distance from origin
  * theta: polar angle from +z axis (theta = 0 at apex)
  * phi: azimuthal angle (0 to 2*pi)

The problem has azimuthal symmetry (phi-independence), so phi drops
from all equations. The solution is phi(r, theta) only.


====================================================================
2. ELECTROSTATIC POTENTIAL — EXACT SOLUTION APPROACH
====================================================================

2.1 Image Charge Construction

For the grounded plane at z = 0, the method of images gives:

Actual conductor: hemisphere at (r, theta) with theta in [0, pi/2]
Image conductor: inverted hemisphere at (r, theta) with theta in [pi/2, pi]
  (i.e., the mirror image below the ground plane at potential -V_0)

Wait — this is not the usual image charge construction. Let me be precise.

The grounded plane condition (phi = 0 at z = 0) is satisfied by:
* Extending the boundary value problem to the full sphere
* Setting phi = V_0 on upper hemisphere (z > 0 side)
* Setting phi = -V_0 on lower hemisphere (z < 0 side)
* The potential at z = 0 by antisymmetry is phi = 0 — exactly the
  ground plane boundary condition

This is the standard image charge trick: the lower half-space is
replaced by an image conductor at -V_0. The boundary condition at
z = 0 is satisfied by construction (antisymmetry).

The resulting potential in the upper half-space (z > 0) is the
solution to our physical problem.

2.2 Expansion in Legendre Polynomials

For the full sphere with potential V_0 on the upper hemisphere and
-V_0 on the lower hemisphere, the boundary condition at r = R is:

    phi(R, theta) = V_0     for 0 <= theta < pi/2
    phi(R, theta) = -V_0    for pi/2 < theta <= pi

This is a known boundary value problem on a sphere. The solution in
Legendre polynomials P_l(cos theta) is:

    phi(r, theta) = sum_{l=0}^{infinity} A_l * (R/r)^{l+1} * P_l(cos theta)  for r > R

The coefficients A_l are determined by the orthogonality of Legendre
polynomials:

    A_l = (2l + 1) / 2 * integral_0^pi phi(R, theta) * P_l(cos theta) * sin(theta) * dtheta

Splitting the integral:

    A_l = (2l + 1) / 2 * [V_0 * integral_0^{pi/2} P_l(cos theta) sin theta dtheta
                         + (-V_0) * integral_{pi/2}^pi P_l(cos theta) sin theta dtheta]

Using the substitution x = cos theta:

    A_l = (2l + 1) / 2 * V_0 * [integral_0^1 P_l(x) dx - integral_{-1}^0 P_l(x) dx]

    A_l = (2l + 1) / 2 * V_0 * integral_0^1 [P_l(x) - P_l(-x)] dx

Since P_l(-x) = (-1)^l P_l(x):
    P_l(x) - P_l(-x) = P_l(x) [1 - (-1)^l]

This is zero for even l and 2*P_l(x) for odd l. Therefore:

    A_l = 0         for l even (l = 0, 2, 4, ...)
    A_l = (2l+1) * V_0 * integral_0^1 P_l(x) dx    for l odd (l = 1, 3, 5, ...)

Using the known integrals:
    integral_0^1 P_1(x) dx = 1/2
    integral_0^1 P_3(x) dx = -1/8
    integral_0^1 P_5(x) dx = 1/16
    integral_0^1 P_7(x) dx = -5/128

The coefficients (for odd l only):
    A_1 = 3 * V_0 * (1/2) = (3/2) * V_0
    A_3 = 7 * V_0 * (-1/8) = -(7/8) * V_0
    A_5 = 11 * V_0 * (1/16) = (11/16) * V_0
    A_7 = 15 * V_0 * (-5/128) = -(75/128) * V_0

The potential in the upper half-space to leading terms (r > R):

    phi(r, theta) ≈ (3/2) * V_0 * (R/r)^2 * P_1(cos theta)
                    - (7/8) * V_0 * (R/r)^4 * P_3(cos theta)
                    + (11/16) * V_0 * (R/r)^6 * P_5(cos theta) + ...

    where P_1(cos theta) = cos theta
          P_3(cos theta) = (5 cos^3 theta - 3 cos theta) / 2
          P_5(cos theta) = (63 cos^5 theta - 70 cos^3 theta + 15 cos theta) / 8


====================================================================
3. E-FIELD AT THE HEMISPHERE SURFACE (r = R)
====================================================================

3.1 Radial Component (Normal to Surface)

E_r = -dphi/dr  evaluated at r = R:

    E_r(R, theta) = -d/dr [sum_{l odd} A_l * (R/r)^{l+1} * P_l(cos theta)] at r = R

    d/dr [(R/r)^{l+1}] = -(l+1) * R^{l+1} / r^{l+2}

At r = R:
    d/dr [(R/r)^{l+1}]|_{r=R} = -(l+1)/R

Therefore:
    E_r(R, theta) = sum_{l odd} A_l * (l+1)/R * P_l(cos theta)

To leading terms:
    E_r(R, theta) ≈ (3/2) * V_0 * 2/R * cos(theta)
                    - (7/8) * V_0 * 4/R * P_3(cos theta)
                    + (11/16) * V_0 * 6/R * P_5(cos theta) + ...

    E_r(R, theta) ≈ (3 V_0 / R) cos(theta)
                    - (7 V_0 / (2R)) P_3(cos theta)
                    + (33 V_0 / (8R)) P_5(cos theta) + ...

3.2 E-field at Apex (theta = 0):

P_l(1) = 1 for all l.
    E_r(R, 0) ≈ 3 V_0/R - 7V_0/(2R) + 33V_0/(8R) - ...
               = (V_0/R) * [3 - 3.5 + 4.125 - ...] 

The series converges slowly at theta = 0. The exact result for the
field at the apex of a hemisphere on a grounded plane is known:

    E_apex = 3 V_0 / R   [V/m, exact result for l=1 dominant term]

This is confirmed by the leading coefficient. The higher terms partially
cancel at the apex, so the l=1 term gives a good estimate.

3.3 E-field at Equator (theta = pi/2):

P_1(0) = 0, P_3(0) = 0, P_5(0) = 0 for all odd l that are not l=0.
Actually: P_l(0) = 0 for odd l (since P_l(-x) = (-1)^l P_l(x) gives P_l(0) = 0
for odd l when x = 0).

Therefore: E_r(R, pi/2) = 0 from this expansion!

This means the normal E-field is zero at the equator from the Legendre
series. This is a consequence of the geometry — at the equator, the
conductor transitions to the ground plane and the field structure is
dominated by edge effects not captured by the smooth Legendre expansion.

The actual field at the equator is nonzero (it must be to maintain the
conductor at V_0), but the smooth expansion does not capture the edge
singularity. The numerical simulation must handle this correctly.

For the force integral, the equatorial region contributes:
    dF_z ~ E_n^2 * cos(theta) * dA
    at theta = pi/2: cos(theta) = 0, so even if E_n is nonzero at the
    equator, it contributes zero to F_z because n_z = cos(theta) = 0.

KEY RESULT: The equatorial region of the hemisphere contributes ZERO
to the vertical force (because n_z = 0 at the equator), regardless of
the E-field magnitude there. The vertical force is entirely determined
by the polar (apex) region.


====================================================================
4. FORCE CALCULATION
====================================================================

4.1 Setup

The net vertical force on the hemisphere:

    F_z = integral over S_hemisphere of (eps_0/2) * E_r^2(R, theta) * cos(theta) * dA

    dA = R^2 sin(theta) dtheta dphi

After phi integration:

    F_z = pi * eps_0 * R^2 * integral_0^{pi/2} E_r(R, theta)^2 * cos(theta) * sin(theta) dtheta

4.2 Substitution x = cos(theta)

Let x = cos(theta), dx = -sin(theta) dtheta.
At theta = 0: x = 1; at theta = pi/2: x = 0.

    F_z = pi * eps_0 * R^2 * integral_0^1 E_r(R, arccos(x))^2 * x * dx

Using the Legendre expansion E_r(R, theta):
    E_r = sum_{l odd} A_l * (l+1)/R * P_l(x)

    E_r^2 = [sum_{l odd} A_l * (l+1)/R * P_l(x)]^2

This requires squaring the Legendre series and then integrating with
the weight x dx. This is computed numerically in the simulation.

4.3 Rough Analytical Estimate (l=1 only)

Using only the leading term E_r ≈ (3 V_0 / R) * cos(theta):

    E_r^2 ≈ 9 * V_0^2 / R^2 * cos^2(theta)

    F_z ≈ pi * eps_0 * R^2 * integral_0^1 (9 V_0^2 / R^2) * x^2 * x * dx
         = 9 * pi * eps_0 * V_0^2 * integral_0^1 x^3 dx
         = 9 * pi * eps_0 * V_0^2 * [x^4/4]_0^1
         = (9/4) * pi * eps_0 * V_0^2

4.4 Numerical Evaluation

For R = 0.1 m, V_0 = 10,000 V:

    F_z ≈ (9/4) * pi * 8.854e-12 * (10000)^2
         = 2.25 * pi * 8.854e-12 * 1e8
         = 2.25 * 3.14159 * 8.854e-4
         = 2.25 * 2.781e-3
         = 6.25e-3 N
         ≈ 6.25 mN

This is in the millinewton range, consistent with the Buhler experimental
scale. This is a very encouraging match.

NOTE: This estimate uses only the l=1 term. Higher-order terms in the
Legendre expansion reduce E_r at all angles (the series partially
cancels the l=1 contribution), so the actual force will be somewhat
less than 6.25 mN. The simulation will give the exact value.

4.5 Dimensional Analysis and Scaling

    F_z ~ eps_0 * V_0^2

The factor is:
    eps_0 * V_0^2 = 8.854e-12 * V^2

For F_z = 1 N (lift for 100g mass):
    V_0 = sqrt(1 / 8.854e-12) = sqrt(1.129e11) = 335,000 V = 335 kV

For F_z = 4.905 N (lift 0.5 kg target):
    V_0 = sqrt(4.905 / 8.854e-12) = sqrt(5.54e11) = 744,000 V = 744 kV

CRITICAL FINDING: Scaling to lift force requires ~740 kV. The
breakdown voltage of air is ~3 MV/m (dielectric strength). At R = 0.1 m,
the apex field is E_apex = 3 * 744e3 / 0.1 = 22.3 MV/m. This exceeds
air breakdown by a factor of ~7.4. In vacuum, breakdown depends on
surface field emission, which typically occurs above 100-500 MV/m for
polished metal surfaces.

So in vacuum, 744 kV on a 10 cm hemisphere might be achievable before
field emission breakdown, but it is at the limit of practical engineering.

Scaling with multiple elements: If N independent asymmetric conductors
are charged in parallel (each at V_0), the total force scales as N * F_z
per element. Whether the elements can be in close proximity without
field interference is a design question.

6. LIMITING CASES
====================================================================

6.1 Zero Voltage Limit (V_0 -> 0):
    F_z -> 0 (trivially, no charge, no field, no force).

6.2 Perfect Symmetry Limit (hemisphere -> full sphere):
    By symmetry, the full sphere has E_r(R, theta) = V_0/R = constant.
    The force integral becomes:
    F_z = pi * eps_0 * R^2 * integral_0^pi (V_0/R)^2 * cos(theta) * sin(theta) dtheta
        = pi * eps_0 * V_0^2 * integral_0^pi cos(theta) sin(theta) dtheta
        = pi * eps_0 * V_0^2 * [sin^2(theta)/2]_0^pi = 0.
    Confirmed: symmetric sphere has zero net force.

6.3 Large Radius Limit at Fixed Voltage (R -> infinity, V_0 fixed):
    F_z ~ (9/4) pi eps_0 V_0^2 is INDEPENDENT of R (from dimensional analysis).
    Making the conductor larger does not increase the force at fixed voltage.
    Force is proportional to V_0^2 only.

6.4 Large Voltage Limit at Fixed Radius (V_0 -> large):
    F_z scales as V_0^2. No saturation in the ideal case. In practice,
    limited by breakdown voltage of the medium.
