1. LEGENDRE SERIES CONVERGENCE ANALYSIS
====================================================================

1.1 Series Expansion for Hemisphere on Grounded Plane

The electrostatic potential in spherical coordinates (r, θ, φ) for
the half-space z > 0 above a grounded plane, with a hemispherical
conductor of radius R at potential V:

    φ(r, θ) = Σ_{l=0}^{L_MAX} B_l r^{-(l+1)} P_l(cos θ)    for r > R

Boundary condition at r = R:

    φ(R, θ) = V      for 0 ≤ θ ≤ π/2  (hemisphere surface)
    φ(R, θ) = 0      for π/2 < θ ≤ π  (image of ground plane region)

Applying orthogonality of Legendre polynomials:

    B_l R^{-(l+1)} = (2l+1)/2 ∫_0^π φ(R, θ) P_l(cos θ) sin θ dθ

Split the integral:

    B_l = R^{l+1} (2l+1)/2 [ V ∫_0^{π/2} P_l(cos θ) sin θ dθ ]

Let x = cos θ, dx = -sin θ dθ:

    B_l = R^{l+1} (2l+1)/2 V ∫_0^1 P_l(x) dx

The integral I_l = ∫_0^1 P_l(x) dx has closed form for integer l:

    I_0 = 1
    I_1 = 1/2
    I_l = [ P_{l-1}(0) - P_{l+1}(0) ] / (2l+1)    for l ≥ 1

where P_l(0) = 0 for odd l, and for even l:

    P_l(0) = (-1)^{l/2} (l-1)!! / l!!

For l = 2:  P_2(0) = -1/2
    I_2 = [P_1(0) - P_3(0)] / 5 = [0 - 0]/5 = 0

For l = 3:  I_3 = [P_2(0) - P_4(0)] / 7 = [-1/2 - 3/8] / 7 = -7/8 / 7 = -1/8

For l = 4:  I_4 = [P_3(0) - P_5(0)] / 9 = [0 - 0]/9 = 0

The pattern: I_l = 0 for all even l ≥ 2. Only odd l terms contribute.
This means the series has non-negligible terms only at l = 1, 3, 5, 7, ...


1.2 Convergence Rate

For large odd l, the magnitudes of the Legendre integral coefficients:

    I_l ~ O(l^{-1/2})   (from asymptotic expansion of Legendre polynomials)

The surface E-field contribution at order l scales as:

    E_l(R, θ) ~ B_l (l+1) R^{-(l+2)} P_l(cos θ) ~ V (l+1) / R · O(l^{-1/2}) = O(l^{1/2})

This appears divergent, but the physical E-field is the SUM of all l
terms. The field AT THE EDGE (θ = π/2) has the worst convergence; the
Gibbs overshoot is ~9% of the voltage step = 0.09V, independent of L_MAX.

The FORCE integral involves:

    F_z = ∫_0^{π/2} (ε₀ E²_n / 2) cos θ · 2πR² sin θ dθ

The cos θ weighting suppresses the contribution from near θ = π/2
(where Gibbs is worst). The integrand vanishes at θ = π/2 because
cos(π/2) = 0. Therefore:

    * The force integral converges FASTER than the E-field itself
    * The convergence rate for F_z is governed by terms away from θ = π/2
    * Expected: F_z is 95%+ converged at L_MAX = 21


1.3 Quantitative Convergence Estimate

Sum the l-th order contribution to F_z:

    δF_z(l) ~ ε₀ V² R² · (2l+1) · I_l² · G_l

where G_l = ∫_0^{π/2} P_l²(cos θ) cos θ sin θ dθ is a geometric factor
that decreases for large l (Parseval-like estimate).

For l = 21 (the maximum in Day 2):

    I_{21} ~ C_21 / sqrt(21) ~ 0.22   (from computed values)
    G_{21} ~ O(1/21)  (geometric suppression)
    δF_z(21) / F_z ≈ fraction contributed by l = 21 term ≪ 1%

This supports the prediction that C at L_MAX = 21 is within 2-3% of
the true converged value. The Day 2 C = 10.73 should be C_∞ ≈ 10.7-11.5.

2. CONE GEOMETRY FACTOR DERIVATION
====================================================================

2.1 Coordinate System for Cone on Plane

Cone of half-angle α, height h, base radius r_base = h tan α.
Place apex at height h above the base plate (origin at apex).
The cone surface in spherical coordinates is: θ = α.

For the conical conductor at potential V on a grounded plane,
the boundary value problem becomes:

    φ = V    on cone surface (θ = α, 0 ≤ r ≤ r_base)
    φ = 0    on ground plane (z = 0, r > r_base)
    φ = 0    at r → ∞

Unlike the hemisphere (closed surface), the cone is an open surface.
The solution requires a different approach — a conical coordinate
Legendre expansion or a numerical Green's function method.

For the numerical simulation (run_2_3_cone_sweep.py), we use a direct
approach: place the conductor on a grounded plane, model the cone as a
surface at potential V, and integrate the Maxwell stress numerically.


2.2 E-field Concentration at Cone Apex

Near the apex of a cone with rounded tip radius ρ_t:

    E_apex ≈ V / (h · f(α))    where f(α) is the cone factor

For a hemisphere: α = 90°, f(α) = 1/3 (since E_apex = 3V/R for
hemisphere on plane, verified Day 2). More precisely:

    E_apex (hemisphere) = (1/R) · Σ_l (l+1) B_l P_l(1) / V = 3.0 V/R

This matches the Day 2 simulation: E_apex = V/R · (sum of l+1 coefficients).
At V = 10 kV, R = 0.10 m: E_apex = 3.0 × 10^5 V/m (300 kV/m),
consistent with the Day 2 Legendre series apex value.

For a cone of half-angle α < 90°, the apex field enhancement is larger:

    E_apex ~ V / (h · g(α))

where g(α) decreases as α → 0 (sharper apex → higher E).
The force at the apex:

    P_apex = ε₀ E²_apex / 2 ~ V² ε₀ / (2 h² g²(α))


2.3 Geometry Factor as Function of Cone Angle

Let C(α) = F_z / (ε₀ V²) be the geometry factor.
The cone results can be normalized by hemisphere result C(90°) = C_0.

Analytical scaling argument:

For a fixed-volume geometry at fixed V, as α decreases:
    * Apex E increases as ~ 1/sin(α) for moderate α
    * Apex pressure P ~ 1/sin²(α)
    * Apex area contributing to F_z ~ R² sin²(α) · Δα (solid angle element)
    * Product P · dA_z ~ (1/sin²α) · R² sin²α = R² (independent of α at apex!)

This suggests the apex contribution to C(α) is roughly independent of α.
The main variation in C(α) comes from the BASE region of the cone where
the charge distribution changes with α.

Prediction refinement: C(α) varies by less than 50% across cone angles,
with a maximum somewhere in the 40-60 degree range. The hemisphere at
α = 90° is NOT far from optimal.

This prediction will be tested by the Day 3 cone sweep simulation.

3. ISOLATED CONDUCTOR FORCE INTEGRAL
====================================================================

3.1 Green's Theorem Proof of Zero Self-Force

For an isolated conductor Ω with surface ∂Ω at uniform potential V
in vacuum (no external charges):

    The electrostatic potential φ satisfies:
        ∇²φ = 0    in R³ \ Ω  (Laplace outside)
        φ = V       on ∂Ω
        φ → 0       as |r| → ∞

    The Maxwell stress tensor in the exterior is:
        T_ij = ε₀ (E_i E_j - δ_ij |E|²/2)

    The net force on the conductor is:
        F_i = ∮_{∂Ω} T_ij n^{out}_j dA

    where n^{out}_j is the OUTWARD normal from the conductor.

    Now consider the divergence of T in the exterior region:
        ∂_j T_ij = ε₀ (E_j ∂_j E_i + E_i ∂_j E_j - ∂_i (|E|²/2))
                 = ε₀ (E_j ∂_j E_i + E_i (∇·E) - E_j ∂_i E_j)

    In vacuum (ρ = 0): ∇·E = 0, and ∇×E = 0 → ∂_j E_i = ∂_i E_j.
    Therefore:
        ∂_j T_ij = ε₀ (E_j ∂_i E_j - E_j ∂_i E_j) = 0   ✓

    So ∇·T = 0 in the exterior.

    By Gauss's divergence theorem over the region V_ext between ∂Ω and a
    large sphere S_R at radius R → ∞:

        ∮_{∂Ω} T_ij n_j dA + ∮_{S_R} T_ij n_j dA = ∫_{V_ext} (∂_j T_ij) dV = 0

    As R → ∞, the field E ~ Q/R² (monopole) if the conductor has net charge Q.
    The stress tensor T ~ E² ~ Q²/R⁴, and the sphere area ~ R². Therefore:

        ∮_{S_R} T_ij n_j dA ~ Q²/R⁴ · R² = Q²/R² → 0 as R → ∞

    Hence:
        ∮_{∂Ω} T_ij n_j dA = 0

    The net force on the isolated conductor is exactly ZERO.

    This result holds for ANY shape of conductor, regardless of asymmetry,
    provided no external charges or conductors are present.


3.2 Physical Interpretation

The result F = 0 for an isolated conductor means:

    * The electrostatic self-force on a conductor cannot propel it.
    * Any net force requires an EXTERNAL reaction.
    * The Buhler experiment measures force between the electrode and the
      chamber walls (which act as the ground plane / reaction body).

IMPORTANT DISTINCTION: The force between the craft electrode and an
external conductor IS real and is the Buhler mechanism. For a craft in
free space interacting with a planet's electric field, the mechanism
becomes:

    F_craft = (charge on craft) × (external E-field from planet)
            = Q · E_external

This is an ordinary Coulomb interaction with the planet — not a novel
thrust mechanism. For a small craft charge Q ~ 1 μC in Earth's fair-weather
E-field of ~100 V/m: F ~ 10⁻⁴ N. Insufficient for lift.


3.3 Can the Craft Carry Its Own Ground Plane?

If the craft carries both the apex emitter (+V) and a base plate (ground),
both separated by an insulator, the internal force structure is:

    Force on apex: F_up = ε₀ E²_apex · A_apex / 2    (upward)
    Force on plate: F_down = ε₀ E²_plate · A_plate / 2  (downward)

By Newton's 3rd law for internal forces:
    F_up + F_down = 0    (for the center-of-mass motion)

The two parts push against each other with equal and opposite forces.
The craft as a WHOLE experiences zero net force.

But: if the potential difference across the insulator drives a current
(e.g., corona discharge, field emission), charged particles are EJECTED
from the system. The ejected charge carries momentum, and the craft
recoils. This is ion thruster physics — requires consumable propellant
(the emitted charge).
