====================================================================
1. PHYSICAL QUESTION FOR DAY 2
====================================================================

Does the asymmetric distribution of electrostatic pressure over the
surface of a conducting hemisphere (positioned on a grounded plane)
produce a net upward force on the conductor?

This is the fundamental physics claim underlying the Buhler result.
The mechanism does not require exotic physics beyond classical
electrostatics and the Maxwell stress tensor. The question is whether
the geometry produces the asymmetry needed for a nonzero net force.


====================================================================
2. GOVERNING EQUATIONS
====================================================================

2.1 Electrostatic Potential Problem

The electrostatic potential phi satisfies the Laplace equation
in the region exterior to the conductors:

    nabla^2 phi = 0     [Laplace equation, charge-free exterior region]

Boundary conditions:
    phi = V_0    on the hemisphere surface
    phi = 0      on the grounded plane (z = 0, r > R)
    phi -> 0     as r -> infinity

This is a Dirichlet problem. The solution determines the potential
distribution everywhere outside the conductors.

2.2 Electric Field

The electric field is the negative gradient of the potential:

    E = -nabla phi     [V/m]

At the conductor surface, E is purely normal (tangential component = 0
by conductor boundary condition):

    E_tangential = 0         [at conductor surface, electrostatic equilibrium]
    E_normal = -dphi/dn      [outward normal derivative of potential]

The outward normal E_n is related to the surface charge density by
Gauss's law:

    sigma = eps_0 * E_n     [C/m^2]

where sigma is the local surface charge density on the conductor surface.

2.3 Electrostatic Pressure (Maxwell Stress Normal Component)

The electrostatic pressure acting outward on the conductor surface is:

    P_e = eps_0 * E_n^2 / 2 = sigma^2 / (2 * eps_0)     [Pa]

This follows directly from the Maxwell stress tensor T_ij evaluated
with the conductor boundary condition (E purely normal). The full
derivation was shown in Theory.mdx Section 4.

2.4 Net Force via Maxwell Stress Tensor

The net electrostatic force on the conductor is the surface integral
of the electrostatic pressure weighted by the outward surface normal:

    F = integral over S of P_e(r) * n_hat(r) dA

In Cartesian components:

    F_x = integral over S of P_e(r) * n_x(r) dA
    F_y = integral over S of P_e(r) * n_y(r) dA
    F_z = integral over S of P_e(r) * n_z(r) dA

By the azimuthal symmetry of the hemisphere-on-plane geometry (cylinder
symmetric about the z-axis), F_x = F_y = 0 exactly. Only F_z is nonzero.


====================================================================
3. THE HEMISPHERE-ON-PLANE GEOMETRY
====================================================================

3.1 Geometry Definition

Coordinate system: cylindrical (rho, phi, z) or spherical (r, theta, phi).

The hemisphere:
    * Center at origin
    * Radius R
    * Occupies z > 0 hemisphere: r = R, 0 <= theta <= pi/2
    * Flat face at z = 0 (circle of radius R in the ground plane)

The ground plane:
    * Infinite conducting plane at z = 0
    * Grounded: phi = 0 everywhere on z = 0 plane

Applied voltage: The hemisphere is maintained at phi = V_0 > 0.

3.2 Solution by Method of Images

The grounded plane boundary condition (phi = 0 at z = 0) can be
satisfied by the method of images. For any charge configuration above
the plane, the image is an equal and opposite charge configuration at
the mirror location below the plane.

For a sphere of charge q at height h above a grounded plane, the image
is -q at depth -h below the plane. The potential is the superposition:

    phi = (q / 4 pi eps_0) * (1/r_+ - 1/r_-)

where r_+ is the distance from the real charge and r_- is the distance
from the image charge.

For the conducting hemisphere at potential V_0:
The sphere (extended to a full sphere) has image -V_0 sphere at
z < 0. The ground plane condition is automatically satisfied by this
image construction. The hemisphere surface at r = R, z > 0 is at
potential:

    phi(R, theta) = V_0     [0 <= theta <= pi/2]

The complete solution for the potential outside a sphere of radius R
at potential V_0 above a grounded plane is (from sphere + image system):

    phi(r, theta) = V_0 * (R/r)     [full sphere, any theta]

But the hemisphere boundary condition is more subtle. The flat base of
the hemisphere (the disk at z = 0, rho < R) is part of the conductor
at V_0, but the ground plane at rho > R is at V = 0. This creates a
boundary condition discontinuity at rho = R, z = 0 (the edge of the
hemisphere base) which cannot be solved exactly in closed form.

For the purpose of estimating the E-field geometry:

Approximate treatment (upper hemisphere only, above z = 0 plane):
The field above z = 0 is approximately that of a full sphere at V_0
in the upper half space, with the image charge below providing the
ground plane condition. This approximation is most accurate far from
the equator and gives:

    E_r(R, theta) ≈ V_0 / R + image correction term

Near the apex (theta ≈ 0, far from the ground plane discontinuity):

    E_r(R, 0) ≈ 2 * V_0 / R    (sum of real and image contributions)

Near the equator (theta ≈ pi/2, near the discontinuity):
    The image charge contribution partially cancels the direct term.
    E_r(R, pi/2) ≈ V_0 / R    (approximate, lower bound)

This approximation yields a factor of 2 asymmetry in E^2 between
the apex and equator, producing a net upward force. The exact field
requires numerical computation.

3.3 Exact Numerical Approach

For precise force calculation, the potential can be computed numerically:

Method 1: Boundary element method (BEM). Discretize the conductor
surface into N panels. Set up and solve a linear system for the charge
density sigma_i on each panel such that the superposition of all panel
potentials satisfies the boundary conditions. Compute the field from
sigma_i and integrate the force.

Method 2: Finite difference or finite element on a regular mesh.
Discretize the domain, apply boundary conditions, solve nabla^2 phi = 0.
Extract E at the conductor surface by differencing.

For Day 2, Method 1 (simplified analytical + numerical integration)
using the image charge approximation will be used. This gives the
qualitative result and correct scaling. Full BEM is Day 3+ if needed.


====================================================================
4. E-FIELD AT HEMISPHERE SURFACE — ANALYTICAL ESTIMATE
====================================================================

For the hemisphere at potential V_0, radius R, on grounded plane,
the dominant E-field at the curved surface is (from image charge
approximation):

    E_n(theta) ≈ E_0 * f(theta)     [V/m]

where E_0 = V_0 / R is the reference field scale and f(theta) is a
dimensionless function capturing the angular dependence.

For the image charge model (point charge +q at origin, image -q at
origin reflected through z = 0 — not quite physical but illustrative):

The actual image charge model for a hemisphere on a grounded plane
gives a field that peaks at the apex and decreases toward the equator.
From field line geometry and known solutions to similar problems:

    E_n(theta = 0) ≈ 2 * V_0 / R      [apex, field enhanced by image]
    E_n(theta = pi/4) ≈ 1.4 * V_0 / R  [45 degrees, interpolated]
    E_n(theta = pi/2) ≈ 0.7 * V_0 / R  [equator, field reduced by image]

These are rough estimates. The simulation will compute exact values.

4.1 Force Integrand

The electrostatic pressure is P_e = (eps_0 / 2) * E_n^2. The vertical
(z) component of force from a surface element at angle theta is:

    dF_z = P_e(theta) * n_z(theta) * dA
         = (eps_0 / 2) * E_n(theta)^2 * cos(theta) * R^2 * sin(theta) * dtheta * dphi

Integrating over phi (0 to 2*pi) and theta (0 to pi/2):

    F_z = (eps_0 / 2) * (2 pi R^2) * integral from 0 to pi/2 of
          E_n(theta)^2 * cos(theta) * sin(theta) * dtheta

For a uniform field E_n = E_0 (all angles equal):
    F_z_uniform = pi * R^2 * (eps_0 / 2) * E_0^2 * integral from 0 to pi/2 of
                  2 * cos(theta) * sin(theta) * dtheta
                = pi * R^2 * (eps_0 / 2) * E_0^2 * [sin^2(theta)] from 0 to pi/2
                = pi * R^2 * (eps_0 / 2) * E_0^2 * 1

For the hemisphere, E_n is NOT uniform, and the net force is NOT zero
because the image charge creates an asymmetry: the apex region has
higher E^2 * cos(theta) than the equatorial region. The integral does
not cancel.


====================================================================
5. DEPARTURE FROM CONVENTIONAL PHYSICS PREDICTIONS
====================================================================

In conventional electrostatics (conductor in free space, no ground plane):
A conductor at any potential experiences zero NET force on itself because
for every surface element, there is a corresponding element with an equal
and opposite force contribution (by Earnshaw's theorem and Newton's third
law applied to the self-energy).

The departure here:
The presence of the grounded conducting plane breaks the symmetry.
The conductor + ground plane is a two-body system. The force between
the two conductors is the force on each relative to the other. For a
symmetric geometry (e.g., two parallel flat plates), the force is purely
attractive normal to the plates. For an asymmetric geometry (hemisphere
vs. flat plate), the force has components that are not purely normal to
the plane. Specifically, the vertical component F_z on the hemisphere
is upward (away from the ground plane) for any V_0 > 0.

This is not a violation of Newton's third law. The equal and opposite
force is on the ground plane. In the lab frame, if the ground plane is
fixed (connected to Earth ground), the hemisphere experiences a net
upward force from the electrostatic interaction with the ground.

The Buhler result goes further: even with no conducting ground plane,
in hard vacuum, an asymmetric charged conductor can produce a net force.
This requires the device to include its own internal ground reference
(the negative electrode of a capacitor pair). The present Day 2
simulation is the ground-plane case (the simpler configuration), which
directly applies to devices where the craft maintains a potential
relative to an external conductor below it.

6. WHAT THE SIMULATION MUST SHOW
====================================================================

To confirm this physics is operating:

1. Validation check: Symmetric sphere at potential V, no ground plane
   (or ground plane at infinity). Net force F_z = 0. Any deviation from
   zero is a numerical error.

2. Main result: Hemisphere at potential V_0 on grounded plane.
   F_z > 0. Force is upward. Magnitude scales as eps_0 * V_0^2 * R.

3. Scaling check: F_z vs V_0 should follow F_z ~ V_0^2 (because the
   field scales as V_0 and the pressure as E^2).

4. Geometry check: F_z vs hemisphere radius R at fixed V_0 should
   scale as R (since F ~ pressure * area ~ eps_0 * (V/R)^2 * R^2 = eps_0 * V^2,
   independent of R for fixed voltage!). Actually: force is independent
   of R at fixed V? Let me check dimensional analysis below.

Dimensional analysis of F_z:
    F_z ~ eps_0 * E^2 * R^2 ~ eps_0 * (V/R)^2 * R^2 = eps_0 * V^2

This is independent of R at fixed V! Increasing the size of the conductor
at fixed voltage does NOT increase the force. Only increasing voltage helps.
This is a critical practical limitation of the approach.

To scale to lift force: need eps_0 * V^2 ~ m * g

For m = 0.5 kg: F_target = 4.905 N
    eps_0 * V^2 = F_target
    V^2 = F_target / eps_0 = 4.905 / 8.854e-12 = 5.54e11
    V = 7.4e5 V = 740 kV

This is 740 kilovolts. Extremely high. Even before considering breakdown.

Implication: Buhler-type devices are inherently millinewton-scale
propellantless thrusters for attitude control or microspace applications,
NOT lift-scale propulsion systems — unless the force amplification
from the Cernohajev solenoid array geometry is real and provides
orders-of-magnitude enhancement.
