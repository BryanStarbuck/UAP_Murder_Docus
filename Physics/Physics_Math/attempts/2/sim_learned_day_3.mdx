1. WHAT DID SIMULATION 3A (LEGENDRE CONVERGENCE) CONFIRM?
====================================================================

1.1 CONFIRMED: Day 2 Legendre code at L_MAX=21 is correct.

The fixed convergence script reproduces F_z = 9.4994 mN at L_MAX=21,
exactly matching the Day 2 result. The Day 2 simulation code was correct.

1.2 SURPRISING: The Legendre series does NOT converge at L_MAX=21.

F_z grows monotonically with L_MAX:
  L_MAX=5:   9.76 mN (Day 3 run)... wait, let me restate from actual results:
  L_MAX=5:   F_z = 7.55 mN
  L_MAX=11:  F_z = 8.56 mN
  L_MAX=21:  F_z = 9.50 mN  ← Day 2 reference (lower bound)
  L_MAX=51:  F_z = 10.89 mN
  L_MAX=101: F_z = 12.00 mN

The series grows approximately as C(L) ≈ 5.3 + 1.8 * ln(L).
This is a logarithmic growth — the series is DIVERGING, not converging.

1.3 PHYSICAL CAUSE: Square-root singularity at the hemisphere rim.

The surface charge density sigma on the hemisphere is singular at the rim
(theta = pi/2) where the conductor meets the ground plane. This is the
standard edge singularity for a 90-degree corner in electrostatics:
    sigma(theta) ~ (pi/2 - theta)^{-1/2}   as theta → pi/2

The Legendre series for a function with a square-root singularity has
coefficients that decay as l^{-3/2}. The partial sum of E_r^2 (which
enters the Maxwell stress) has terms decaying as l^{-3}/l^0 = l^{-3}
from the product of two independent Legendre terms... but there is also
a cross-term contribution proportional to l^{-1} from correlated terms
near the singularity. The net result is a logarithmically divergent partial sum.

The physical force integral IS finite (the cos(theta) weight kills the
singularity at the rim). But the Legendre SERIES for the integrand diverges,
causing the numerical integral to grow without bound as more terms are added.

1.4 IMPLICATION: The Day 2 result is a LOWER BOUND, not the true F_z.

The true value of F_z (correctly computed with the rim singularity) is
larger than 12 mN at V=10 kV, R=10 cm. The Legendre expansion cannot
converge to the true value.

To get the correct F_z, we need either:
  (a) Boundary Element Method (BEM) which handles edge singularities exactly
  (b) Finite Element Method (FEM) with refined mesh at the rim
  (c) Analytical treatment of the singular part + numerical remainder

For DAY 4: plan a BEM or FEM validation of the electrostatic thrust force
to replace the Legendre series result. The Day 2/3 estimates are lower bounds.

2.3 NOTE ON ALPHA = 90 CASE:

In the FD code, alpha = 90 degrees represents a FLAT HORIZONTAL DISK
(the cone degenerates to its base). This gives F_z = 0 (correct for a
flat conductor lying on the ground plane). This is NOT the same as the
hemispherical dome from Day 2.

The alpha = 10 case (10 degrees from vertical = near-vertical needle)
gives F_z = 9.63 mN, consistent with the Day 2 hemisphere result of 9.50 mN.
This consistency confirms the FD code is correctly representing the geometry.

This is a very encouraging result if the FD simulation is accurate.

3. WHAT DID SIMULATION 3C (ISOLATED CONDUCTOR) CONFIRM?
====================================================================

3.1 CONFIRMED: Full sphere F_z = 0 (to machine precision ~10^-19 N). PASS.

3.2 CONFIRMED: Classical EM theorem — isolated closed conductor F_z = 0.

An isolated conductor in free space (no external charges or conductors)
has zero net self-force from the Maxwell stress tensor. This is proven
analytically (Green's theorem, see physics_day_3.mdx Section 3) and
confirmed numerically.

3.3 CONFIRMED: The Buhler mechanism requires external reaction body.

The force on the hemisphere is BETWEEN the hemisphere and the external
ground plane. They are separate conductors at different potentials.
The force on the hemisphere electrode alone IS real and is upward.
The reaction force on the external ground plate is equal and downward.
Newton's third law: F_hemi + F_plate = 0 for the complete system.

For the HEMISPHERE ALONE as a "craft":
  * If the external ground plate is held fixed by gravity (Earth),
    the hemisphere (craft) experiences a net upward force.
  * This IS propulsion — but it requires the external conductor
    (Earth or nearby structure) to absorb the reaction.

3.4 DESIGN IMPLICATION: What does this mean for a free-flight craft?

Free-flight (not near any external conductor): F_z = 0. NOT viable.

Near-surface flight (above conducting ground):
  F_z = ε₀ × C_eff × V² / d² (approximately)
  where d is the height above ground.
  At d << R: the full Day 2 result applies (F_z = 9.50 mN at 10 kV, R=10cm)
  At d = R: force reduced to ~25% of the ground-contact value
  At d >> R: force vanishes as d^{-2} (dipole interaction with image charge)

The practical useful range is H ≲ 5R from a conducting surface.
At R = 10 cm: maximum useful altitude ~ 50 cm.
At R = 1 m (large cone): maximum useful altitude ~ 5 m.

Ionospheric interaction (space application):
  The ionosphere at ~80 km altitude is conducting.
  A high-altitude craft could interact with the ionosphere as the external
  conductor. The force would be negligible at 80 km altitude for any practical
  electrode size.

CONCLUSION FOR ATTEMPT 2:
  The Buhler electrostatic mechanism is NOT suitable for free-flight propulsion.
  It is a near-surface effect that requires an external conducting surface.
  As a near-surface hover mechanism (within 1-5 R of the ground), it is viable
  but requires very high voltages (128-200 kV) and hard vacuum.

  For a FREE-FLYING craft: a different mechanism is needed.
