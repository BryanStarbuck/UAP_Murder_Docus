1.1 FROM STATIC TO TIME-VARYING FIELDS

Days 1-3 demonstrated that static classical electromagnetic mechanisms
(Kelvin body force, Maxwell electrostatic pressure) cannot produce free-flight
propulsion from a closed conductor in free space. The fundamental barrier is
Newton's third law in its EM form: for any static configuration,
∮_∂Ω T_ij n_j dA = 0 for an isolated system (proven by Green's theorem since
∇·T = 0 in free space and the surface integral at infinity vanishes).

Time-varying fields evade this constraint. When fields are time-dependent,
the electromagnetic field itself carries momentum density:

    g_EM = ε₀ (E × B) = S/c²

where S = (1/μ₀)(E × B) is the Poynting vector. The total momentum of an
isolated system is:

    p_total = p_mechanical + ∫ g_EM dV = constant

A device that changes ∫ g_EM dV over time changes its mechanical momentum
by the equal and opposite amount — without requiring an external reaction body.
This is the photon rocket in generalized form.

For practical low-frequency EM devices, the relevant effect is not radiation
(which requires f ~ GHz for mechanical relevance) but rather the quasi-static
redistribution of field momentum within the device geometry.


1.2 THREE-PHASE ROTATING FIELD

A three-phase AC source drives three coil assemblies at azimuthal positions
φ_k = 2πk/3 (k = 0, 1, 2). Each coil carries current:

    I_k(t) = I₀ cos(ωt - φ_k) = I₀ cos(ωt - 2πk/3)

The superposition of three dipole fields (each with magnetic moment m_k
perpendicular to the coil plane and tilted at azimuthal angle φ_k) produces:

    B(r, t) = Σ_k B_k(r) cos(ωt - 2πk/3)

For a flat planar arrangement of three identical coils, the time-averaged
field magnitude squared is:

    <|B(r,t)|²> = (3/2) |B_single(r)|²  [by three-phase orthogonality]

The instantaneous field rotates in the azimuthal direction:

    B_x(r,t) = (3/2) B_r(r) cos(ωt - φ_local)
    B_y(r,t) = (3/2) B_r(r) sin(ωt - φ_local)

where φ_local is a position-dependent phase. This is the rotating phasor
representation: the field vector sweeps through 2π in one period T = 1/f,
identical in structure to the rotating magnetic field in a three-phase
induction motor.


1.3 ELECTROMAGNETIC ANGULAR MOMENTUM

The rotating field carries electromagnetic angular momentum density:

    ℓ_EM = ε₀ (r × (E × B)) = r × g_EM

For a purely magnetostatic rotating field (E = 0, B = B_rotating), ℓ_EM = 0
identically. However, a time-varying B induces an electric field via Faraday's
law:

    ∇ × E = -∂B/∂t = ωB_⊥  [time-varying rotating field]

This induced E is azimuthal (perpendicular to both r and ẑ). The cross product
E × B has a component in the ẑ direction:

    (E × B)_z = E_φ B_r - E_r B_φ

For an axisymmetric rotating field: E_φ ≠ 0, B_r ≠ 0, giving a nonzero
time-averaged (E × B)_z. The Poynting vector has a net axial component:

    <S_z> = (1/μ₀) <(E × B)_z>

A net upward <S_z> means net upward EM momentum flux — the field carries more
electromagnetic momentum upward than downward. By momentum conservation,
if the field geometry is asymmetric (more flux going up through the top of
the device than down through the bottom), the device receives a net downward
reaction force. But if <S_z> is larger ABOVE the device than BELOW it, the
device is pumping upward momentum into the field — and experiences a net
upward force.

The key quantity to compute from simulation is:

    ΔP_z = ε₀ [∫_{z=+H} (E × B)_z dA - ∫_{z=-H} (E × B)_z dA]

If ΔP_z > 0 (more upward flux through the top surface than the bottom), the
device has a net upward force equal to dΔP_z/dt.


1.4 KELVIN FORCE ON THE TOROIDAL FERRITE

For a magnetic material with permeability μ = μ₀ μ_r placed in a non-uniform
magnetic field, the Kelvin body force density is:

    f_Kelvin = μ₀ (M · ∇)H = μ₀ χ_m (H · ∇)H = (μ₀ χ_m / 2) ∇(|H|²)

where χ_m = μ_r - 1 is the magnetic susceptibility. The net force on the
toroid is:

    F_Kelvin = ∫_V (μ₀ χ_m / 2) ∇(|H|²) dV = (μ₀ χ_m / 2) ∮_∂V |H|² n̂ dA

For a static field and closed toroid: this integrates the gradient of |H|²
over the toroid volume. The result is nonzero whenever |H|² is not uniform
over the toroid surface — i.e., whenever the coils are closer to one face
of the toroid than the other.

For the BUGA geometry: the three-phase coils are ABOVE the toroid, at height
h_coil above the top face. The field |H|² is larger at the top of the toroid
than at the bottom. Therefore:

    F_z = (μ₀ χ_m / 2) ∫_V ∂|H|²/∂z dV > 0  (upward, toward coils)

This is the static Kelvin force (time-averaged version for rotating field).
The time-averaging for the rotating field: since <cos²(ωt)> = 1/2 for any
single-phase component, the time-averaged <|H|²> = (3/2)|H_single|² (three-phase
result). The Kelvin force is therefore:

    <F_z> = (3μ₀ χ_m / 4) ∫_V ∂|H_single|²/∂z dV

where H_single is the field from a single stationary coil.

Critical point: this force is BETWEEN the coils and the toroid, just as
the Buhler force was between the hemisphere and the ground plane. It is
INTERNAL to the device. If the coils and toroid are mounted rigidly on
the same frame, F_Kelvin(coils → toroid) is an internal force: the frame
sees zero net external force. Newton's third law is satisfied.

The coils experience a downward reaction force equal and opposite to the
upward force on the toroid. The structural frame transmits both. Net external
force = 0.

THIS IS THE FUNDAMENTAL SYMMETRY ARGUMENT: For a device with all components
rigidly mounted, no static internal Kelvin force produces net external thrust.


1.5 THE ASYMMETRY REQUIRED FOR FREE-FLIGHT THRUST

For a net external force, one of the following must be true:
  (a) One component is not rigidly attached to the frame (e.g., the rotor
      is free to rotate, and the reaction torque on the frame converts to
      linear momentum through some additional asymmetry)
  (b) The field carries EM momentum out of the system boundary in a net
      axial direction (radiation or quasi-static EM momentum flow)
  (c) The device interaction with the external medium (e.g., interaction
      with the Earth's magnetic field or ionosphere) provides an external
      reaction body
  (d) New physics (Woodward Mach effect, Podkletnov gravitomagnetic coupling)
      couples the EM field to the gravitational field

The BUGA device has a SPINNING ROTOR (the central shaft with bead). This
is not simply decorative: it means the rotor is NOT rigidly coupled to the
field coils in the rotational degree of freedom. The rotor can absorb or
release angular momentum independently of the stator coils. If:

  1. The rotating EM field exerts a torque on the rotor (through induction
     or magnetic coupling)
  2. The rotor is asymmetric in shape or mass distribution
  3. This creates a time-varying angular momentum L_rotor(t)

then by conservation:

    d/dt (L_EM + L_rotor + L_frame) = external torque (from ground/surroundings)

If the frame is in free flight (no external torque) and L_EM averages to
a nonzero constant flux upward, the frame receives a sustained reaction.

The spinning rotor in the BUGA device may be the CRITICAL component —
not the toroidal ferrite or the electrostatic electrode. It acts as a
gyroscopic/angular momentum pump: the rotating EM field drives the rotor,
and the rotor's angular momentum interacts with the Earth's gravitational
gradient (or the device's own magnetic field gradient) to produce a net
linear force. This is the gyrostatic lift hypothesis.


1.6 SYMMETRY ANALYSIS (why it might work)

For the static Kelvin mechanism to produce net external thrust, the following
would be required: one end of the device interacts with the field more
strongly than the other, AND the reaction is absorbed by the external
environment rather than the opposing coils.

The BUGA device has both a toroidal ferrite (below the coils) AND a
hemispherical electrode (above the coils). If the electrode is charged:
  * Above the coil plane: high-E-field region (electrode apex)
  * Below the coil plane: high-B-field region (toroid)

The Maxwell stress tensor T_ij contains both E and B contributions. The
cross term ε₀ E_i B_j (for time-varying fields, the Abraham or Minkowski
momentum density) may produce a net force if the field geometry has
appropriate top/bottom asymmetry.

More specifically: if the rotating B-field and the static E-field from
the electrode have a phase-locked interaction, the time-averaged Abraham
momentum density g_Abraham = (1/c²)(E × H) has a nonzero z-component
on average. This is the Abraham-Minkowski force — controversial but
physically real for electromagnetic pulses in dielectric media.

In ferrite (with both ε_r > 1 and μ_r > 1), the Abraham momentum density
is nonzero even for time-averaged fields:

    <g_Abraham>_z = <ε₀(E × B)>_z = <S>_z / c²

If the geometry produces a net upward <S_z>, the ferrite absorbs net
upward EM momentum, and the device experiences a net downward force —
UNLESS the momentum flows THROUGH the device and exits from the bottom.


1.7 PODKLETNOV GRAVITOMAGNETIC ANALOG

Podkletnov (1992, 1994, 2003) claimed that a rotating YBCO superconducting
disc above a toroidal solenoid produced a ~0.3% reduction in the weight of
objects placed above it. The proposed mechanism: the rotating supercurrent
in the disc generates a gravitomagnetic field (analogous to the magnetic
field generated by a moving charge) that partially shields gravity.

In general relativity, the gravitomagnetic field B_g is generated by mass
currents, analogous to the magnetic field from charge currents:

    B_g = (2G/c²) ∫ (j_mass × r̂) / r² dV

For a rotating superconducting disc with surface mass current j_mass = ρ_s v,
the gravitomagnetic field above the disc is:

    B_g,z ≈ (2G/c²) m_disc Ω_disc / R_disc

where Ω_disc is the angular velocity. The gravitomagnetic force on a mass m
above the disc is:

    F_g = -m (v × B_g) ≈ m B_g,z v_z ≈ 0  (for static objects)

Podkletnov's claimed effect (weight reduction, not levitation) suggests a
shielding mechanism rather than a direct gravitomagnetic force. This is
not explained by standard GR.

For the room-temperature BUGA analog: the ferrite toroid replaces the
superconductor. Ferrite has μ_r >> 1 but no Meissner effect and no
supercurrent. The gravitomagnetic current is proportional to ρ v, not to
any EM supercurrent. At room temperature, the Podkletnov gravitomagnetic
effect is NOT expected from standard GR. This is a new-physics claim.

However, the rotating EM field in the ferrite sets up a real magnetization
current density J_M = ∇ × M that rotates with the field. If this
magnetization current has a gravitomagnetic effect analogous to the
supercurrent, it would be:

    B_g,z ≈ (2G/c²) * (μ_r - 1) * J_M * V_ferrite

At μ_r = 2000 and typical magnetization currents, this is still many
orders of magnitude below detectability. The Podkletnov effect (if real)
must involve superconductor-specific physics not present in ferrite.

CONCLUSION: The Podkletnov gravitomagnetic analog is NOT the primary
mechanism to simulate. The EM Kelvin force and EM momentum flux are the
correct starting points for a room-temperature ferrite device.

This is the mechanism to investigate computationally.

2.1 BIOT-SAVART FOR THREE-PHASE COIL ARRAY

Each circular coil k at azimuthal position φ_k = 2πk/3 carries current:

    I_k(t) = I₀ cos(ωt - 2πk/3)

For a coil of radius a centered at (r_k, 0, h_coil) and tilted to be
perpendicular to the z-axis (i.e., lying in a horizontal plane):

    dB_k(r,t) = (μ₀ I_k(t))/(4π) ∮ (dl_k × R̂) / |R|²

where R = r - r' (observation minus source). The time dependence factors out:

    B_k(r,t) = B_k^{static}(r) * cos(ωt - 2πk/3)

The total time-varying field:

    B(r,t) = Σ_{k=0}^{2} B_k^{static}(r) * cos(ωt - 2πk/3)

Time-averaged |B|²:

    <|B|²> = (1/2) Σ_k |B_k^{static}|² + cross terms
           = (3/2) |B^{single}_k|²  [for identical coils by symmetry]

The cross terms vanish for three-phase orthogonality:
    <cos(ωt) cos(ωt - 2π/3)> = (1/2) cos(2π/3) = -1/4
    Σ_{k≠l} <cos(ωt-φ_k) cos(ωt-φ_l)> = 0  [for full three-phase set]

2.2 KELVIN FORCE ON FERRITE TOROID

Assuming linear isotropic ferrite (M = χ_m H, χ_m = μ_r - 1):

    F_z = (μ₀ χ_m / 2) ∫_V ∂|H|²/∂z dV

For the time-averaged rotating field:

    <F_z> = (μ₀ χ_m / 2) ∫_V ∂<|H|²>/∂z dV = (3μ₀ χ_m / 4) ∫_V ∂|H^{single}|²/∂z dV

This is the same as the static Kelvin force with field amplitude scaled by
√(3/2). The rotating field provides no topological advantage over a static
non-uniform field for the Kelvin force. The internal/external force argument
still applies (Section 1.4).

2.3 EM MOMENTUM IN QUASI-STATIC ROTATING FIELD

The induced electric field from the rotating B (Faraday's law):

    ∇ × E = -∂B/∂t = ω × B_⊥ (approximately)

For a rotating B with amplitude B_0 at radius R_device and frequency ω:

    |E_induced| ≈ ω R_device B_0  [at the device surface]

The electromagnetic momentum density:

    g_EM = ε₀ (E × B) ≈ ε₀ ω R_device B_0² [order of magnitude]

For f = 60 Hz, R = 0.1 m, B₀ = 10 mT:

    |g_EM| ≈ (8.85e-12) * (2π*60) * 0.1 * (0.01)² ≈ 3.3e-15 kg/(m²·s)

Integrated over the device volume V ≈ 0.001 m³:

    |p_EM| = ∫ g_EM dV ≈ 3.3e-18 kg·m/s

The force is F = d/dt (p_EM) ≈ ω * p_EM ≈ 1.2e-15 N — completely negligible.

This confirms: at low frequencies (60 Hz), quasi-static EM momentum is
far too small to produce any measurable force. The BUGA device, if it
works, must involve a different mechanism than quasi-static EM momentum.
