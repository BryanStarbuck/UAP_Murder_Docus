In saturation: M → M_sat, dM/dH → 0 → effective χ_m ≈ 0 → Kelvin force ≈ 0.

RECOMMENDED REPLACEMENT: Soft iron (electrical steel laminations)
  * μ_r ≈ 200 to 5000 (depending on grade and frequency)

1.3 SPINNING ROTOR

The central vertical shaft has a rotor element (orange/amber bead observed in images).
The rotor is the critical component for potential external thrust (see Theory.mdx Section 1.5).

Rotor specifications (estimated from image):
  Material: likely a permanent magnet or paramagnetic material (orange color
    suggests ferrite oxide or amber resin encapsulating a magnetic element)
  Diameter: ~15-20 mm (estimated from shaft proportions)
  Position: top of central vertical shaft, above the three coil assemblies
  Function: absorbs angular momentum from the rotating EM field (motor effect)
            and potentially releases it via an asymmetric coupling

The rotor as a small permanent magnet would experience a TORQUE from the
rotating magnetic field — analogous to a synchronous motor. It would spin
at the field rotation frequency (or a harmonic) once energized.

Rotor torque estimate:
    τ_rotor = m_rotor × B_rotating × sin(angle between m and B)

For a small permanent magnet (m ≈ 0.01 A·m², B ≈ 1 mT):
    τ ≈ 0.01 × 1e-3 = 1e-5 N·m (synchronizing torque)

At ω = 2π × 60 = 377 rad/s, steady-state rotation:
    P_rotor = τ × ω ≈ 1e-5 × 377 ≈ 3.77 mW (small but non-negligible)

The rotor would reach synchronous speed quickly unless it has significant
inertia or load. Once spinning at ω_rotor = ω_field = 377 rad/s:
  Angular momentum: L = I_rotor × ω_rotor
  For a sphere r = 8 mm, ρ = 3000 kg/m³:
    I_rotor = (2/5) m r² = (2/5) × 0.0025 × 6.4e-5 ≈ 6.4e-8 kg·m²
    L_rotor = 6.4e-8 × 377 ≈ 2.4e-5 N·m·s

  Linear momentum equivalent (if angular momentum converts to linear via gravity):
    F_linear ≈ L_rotor × g / (v_tip × R_rotor) — not a standard formula
  This mechanism requires more detailed analysis (Day 5+).

1.4 CENTRAL HEMISPHERICAL ELECTRODE

At top of shaft, above the spinning rotor.
Diameter: ~40 mm (estimated).
Function: if charged to high voltage, creates electrostatic field concentrated
at apex. Combined with the rotating magnetic field, could create E×B drift
currents in the near field.

E×B drift velocity:
    v_drift = E × B / |B|²

For E = 100 V / 0.02 m = 5000 V/m, B = 1 mT:
    v_drift = 5000 / (1e-3)² × (1e-3) = 5e6 m/s — exceeds c, unphysical

This estimate shows the geometry doesn't support E×B drift at these parameters.
The electrode is likely for cosmetic or functional reasons not yet identified.
