1. BIOT-SAVART FIELD FROM THREE-PHASE COIL ARRAY
====================================================================

1.1 SETUP AND NOTATION

Three identical circular coils. Coil k (k = 0,1,2):
  Center:     r_k = R_c (cos φ_k, sin φ_k, 0),  φ_k = 2πk/3
  Radius:     a = 0.05 m
  Array radius: R_c = 0.05 m (coil centers at R_c from z-axis)
  Height:     z_c = 0.03 m (coils above toroid top face)
  Turns:      N = 100
  Current:    I_k(t) = I₀ cos(ωt - 2πk/3),  I₀ = 1 A,  ω = 2π × 60 rad/s

Peak single-coil field at center of array (on z-axis at z = z_c):
    B₀ ≡ μ₀ N I₀ a² / (2(a² + R_c²)^{3/2})

Numerically:
    a² = 2.5e-3,  R_c² = 2.5e-3,  a² + R_c² = 5.0e-3
    (5.0e-3)^{3/2} = 3.536e-4
    B₀ = (4π×10⁻⁷ × 100 × 1 × 2.5e-3) / (2 × 3.536e-4)
       = (3.14e-10) / (7.07e-4) = 4.44e-4 T = 0.44 mT

For off-axis coils displaced by R_c, the on-axis field is reduced by the
geometric factor (a/(a²+R_c²)^{1/2})² relative to the on-axis case.
Since a = R_c, the peak field is already reduced by (1/2)^{3/2} ≈ 35%.


1.2 BIOT-SAVART NUMERICAL FORM

Parameterize coil k by angle θ ∈ [0, 2π):
    r'_k(θ) = r_k + a(-sin φ_k sin θ + cos φ_k cos θ, cos φ_k sin θ + sin φ_k cos θ, 0)

Simplified (for coil k at position (x_k, y_k, z_c) with radius a):
    r'_k(θ) = (x_k + a cos θ', y_k + a sin θ', z_c)
    where θ' is the angle around the coil's own center

    dl_k = a(-sin θ', cos θ', 0) dθ'

The Biot-Savart contribution:
    dB_k(r) = (μ₀ N I_k)/(4π) × (dl_k × (r - r'_k)) / |r - r'_k|³

Integrating numerically over θ' from 0 to 2π with M_coil = 1000 segments.
The full field at any point r and time t:

    B(r,t) = Σ_{k=0}^{2} cos(ωt - 2πk/3) × B_k^{static}(r)

where B_k^{static}(r) is the field from coil k at I = I₀ (computed once, stored).


1.3 TIME AVERAGES

    <B_i(r)²> = (1/2) Σ_k [B_{k,i}^{static}(r)]²

    <|B(r)|²> = (3/2) |B_0^{static}(r)|²  [by three-phase symmetry, identical coils]

Gradient of time-averaged field magnitude squared:

    ∂<|B|²>/∂z = (3/2) ∂|B_0^{static}|²/∂z

This gradient is evaluated numerically along the z-axis and in the toroid volume.


1.4 ON-AXIS FIELD MAGNITUDE VS. HEIGHT

On z-axis (ρ = 0), each of the three coils (displaced from z-axis by R_c) 
contributes a field with equal z-component B_{k,z} (by three-fold symmetry,
the x and y components cancel). The on-axis total z-field:

    B_z(0, z, t) = cos(ωt) × 3 B_{z,0}^{single}(0, z)

where B_{z,0}^{single}(0, z) = μ₀ N I₀ a² R_c / (2 (R_c² + a² + (z-z_c)²)^{3/2})
                                 × (correction for off-axis)

Numerically at z = 0 (center of toroid top face), z_c = 0.03 m:
    distance² = R_c² + a² + z_c² = 0.0025 + 0.0025 + 0.0009 = 0.0059
    B_{z,0}^{single}(0, 0) ≈ (4π×10⁻⁷ × 100 × 1 × 0.0025 × 0.05) / (2 × 0.0059^{3/2})
                            ≈ (1.57e-11) / (9.07e-4) ≈ 1.73e-8 T

Very small because the coil is displaced from the z-axis. The three coils
contribute B_z,total ≈ 3 × 1.73e-8 = 5.2e-8 T at the center of the toroid top.

The field is much larger directly below each coil than on-axis. The relevant
quantity for the Kelvin force is the field in the toroid volume, not just on-axis.

2. KELVIN FORCE — COMPLETE DERIVATION
====================================================================

2.1 FORCE DENSITY IN LINEAR FERRITE

For linear isotropic material: M = χ_m H, where χ_m = μ_r - 1.
Kelvin force density:

    f_K(r) = μ₀ (M·∇)H = μ₀ χ_m (H·∇)H = (μ₀ χ_m / 2) ∇(H·H)

z-component:
    f_{K,z} = (μ₀ χ_m / 2) ∂(|H|²)/∂z

NOTE: H = B/μ₀μ_r inside the ferrite (linear material). In the coil-generated
external field, H_ext = B_coils/μ₀. Inside ferrite:
    H_int ≈ H_ext / (1 + χ_m N_d)
where N_d is the demagnetization factor of the toroid geometry.

For a thin toroid (h ≪ outer radius): N_d ≈ 0.01 (very small). Thus:
    H_int ≈ H_ext / 1.02 ≈ H_ext  (for χ_m = 1999, this approximation breaks down)

For high-permeability materials: H_int ≈ H_ext / (χ_m N_d) at saturation.
This means the internal H is REDUCED inside the ferrite for large χ_m. The
Kelvin force is properly evaluated using the external field gradient at the
ferrite boundary.

Correct formulation using boundary conditions:
    f_{K,z} = (μ₀/2) (μ_r - 1)/μ_r × ∂(B_tot²)/∂z   [for the toroid in field B_ext]

2.2 FORCE AS SURFACE INTEGRAL

By the divergence theorem applied to the gradient of H²:
    F_z = (μ₀ χ_m/2) ∫_V ∂|H|²/∂z dV = (μ₀ χ_m/2) ∮_∂V |H|² n_z dA

This simplifies to:
    F_z = (μ₀ χ_m/2) [∫_{top} |H(r)|² dA - ∫_{bottom} |H(r)|² dA]

Since the coils are above the toroid:
    |H|²_{top face (z=h_t)} > |H|²_{bottom face (z=0)}

Therefore F_z > 0 (upward, toward coils). The toroid is attracted to the coils.

2.3 NUMERICAL ESTIMATE

At the top face of the toroid (z = h_t = 0.02 m), directly below the coil center:
    H_at_top ≈ B_z_single(coil above at 1cm separation) / μ₀

For a coil of radius a = 5 cm, N = 100 turns, I = 1 A, at distance d = 1 cm below:
    B_z ≈ μ₀ N I a² / (2(a² + d²)^{3/2}) = (4π×10⁻⁷ × 100 × 1 × 0.0025) / (2 × 0.00252^{3/2})
        ≈ (3.14e-10) / (2 × 1.26e-4) ≈ 1.24e-3 T
    H ≈ B/μ₀ ≈ 1.24e-3 / (4π×10⁻⁷) ≈ 988 A/m

At the bottom face of the toroid (z = 0), h_c = 3 cm above:
    B_z ≈ (4π×10⁻⁷ × 100 × 1 × 0.0025) / (2 × 0.003^{3/2})
        ≈ (3.14e-10) / (2 × 1.64e-4) ≈ 9.57e-4 T
    H_bottom ≈ 761 A/m

Time-averaged force estimate (using three-phase factor 3/2):
    F_z ≈ (μ₀ χ_m/2) × (3/2) × (H_top² - H_bot²) × A_toroid
        ≈ (4π×10⁻⁷ × 1999/2) × 1.5 × (988² - 761²) × π(0.08² - 0.03²)
        ≈ (1.257e-3) × 1.5 × (976144 - 579121) × 0.01728
        ≈ (1.257e-3) × 1.5 × 397023 × 0.01728
        ≈ (1.257e-3) × 10298
        ≈ 12.9 mN

This is a surprisingly large force — comparable to the Buhler result from Day 2.
However, this assumes linear behavior (χ_m = 1999). At these field strengths
(H ≈ 1000 A/m, B_int = μ_r μ₀ H ≈ 2.5 T >> B_sat ≈ 0.3 T), the ferrite is
SATURATED. The actual χ_m ≈ 0 in saturation, and the force is near zero.

For the ferrite operating below saturation (H < H_sat ≈ B_sat/(μ₀ μ_r) ≈ 0.12 A/m at μ_r = 2000):
  The coil current must be reduced to I* ≈ I₀ × (H_sat / H_at_coil) ≈ 1 × (0.12/988) ≈ 1.2e-4 A

At I = 1.2e-4 A (near saturation threshold):
    F_z ≈ 12.9 mN × (1.2e-4)² / 1² ≈ 1.85e-7 N

This is orders of magnitude below the Buhler result. The ferrite helps when
used as a static concentrator; it hurts when operated in the rotating-field
configuration at high current because it saturates immediately.

KEY INSIGHT: Ferrite is not optimal for the rotating field mechanism at
practical coil currents. Soft iron (μ_r ≈ 200, higher saturation B_sat ≈ 1.5 T)
would be significantly better:
  H_sat,iron = B_sat / (μ₀ μ_r) ≈ 1.5 / (1.26e-6 × 200) ≈ 5960 A/m > H_at_coil(1A)
  χ_m,iron ≈ 199  (vs ferrite χ_m = 1999 but saturated)

With soft iron, χ_m = 199 at 1 A:
    F_z ≈ (4π×10⁻⁷ × 199/2) × 1.5 × (988² - 761²) × 0.01728
        ≈ (1.25e-4) × 1.5 × 397023 × 0.01728 ≈ 1.29 mN

Still internal force, still zero net external thrust, but Kelvin force magnitude is larger.

3. ELECTROMAGNETIC MOMENTUM FLUX
====================================================================

3.1 ESTIMATE FROM FARADAY'S LAW

Faraday-induced E from rotating B:
    E_φ(ρ, z) ≈ ω ρ B_z(0, z) / 2  [azimuthal field from ∂B_z/∂t]

At ρ = 0.05 m, z = 0.03 m:
    E_φ ≈ (2π × 60) × 0.05 × (B_z / 2)

With B_z ≈ 1.24e-3 T from above:
    E_φ ≈ 376.7 × 0.05 × 6.2e-4 ≈ 1.17e-2 mV/m = 1.17e-5 V/m

Time-averaged Poynting flux z-component at z = z_c:
    <S_z> = <(E × H)_z> ≈ E_φ × H_r  [azimuthal E × radial H]

H_r at the coil plane: H_r ≈ B_r / μ₀ ≈ (10⁻³ / μ₀) ≈ 796 A/m

    <S_z> ≈ (1/μ₀) <E_φ B_r> ≈ E_φ × H_r / 2 ≈ (1.17e-5 × 796) / 2 ≈ 4.66e-3 W/m²

EM force on device = <S_z> × A / c ≈ 4.66e-3 × 0.02 / 3e8 ≈ 3.1e-13 N

This confirms: EM momentum force at 60 Hz is ~10⁻¹³ N — utterly negligible.

4. FREQUENCY SCALING AND CRITICAL FREQUENCY
====================================================================

The EM momentum force scales as:
    F_EM ∝ ω² R² B₀² A / c

For fixed B₀ (fixed coil current), the force increases as f². The critical
frequency f* for F_EM = 1 mN (Buhler scale):

    1e-3 = F_EM(60 Hz) × (f*/60)²
         = 3.1e-13 × (f*/60)²
    (f*/60)² = 3.23e9
    f* = 60 × √(3.23e9) ≈ 60 × 56842 ≈ 3.4 GHz

To match the Buhler millinewton scale with the EM momentum mechanism requires
GHz operation. This is in the microwave range. At this frequency, the coil
geometry must be redesigned as a cavity or patch antenna. The quasi-static
Biot-Savart approximation breaks down entirely above f ~ c/(2πR) ≈ 1 GHz.

This is an important finding: the BUGA device at 60 Hz is in completely the
wrong frequency regime for EM momentum propulsion. If the BUGA device works,
it must be via a different mechanism.
