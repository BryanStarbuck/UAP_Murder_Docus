2. ROTATING THREE-PHASE FIELD — DETAILED DERIVATION
====================================================================

2.1 SETUP

Three circular coil loops in the z = h_c plane (h_c = 3 cm above toroid).
Each coil has radius a = 5 cm, N = 100 turns, current I₀ = 1 A.
Azimuthal positions: φ_k = 2πk/3 for k = 0, 1, 2.
Coil centers at: r_k = (R_array cos φ_k, R_array sin φ_k, h_c)
where R_array = 5 cm (distance from z-axis to coil center).

Current in coil k:
    I_k(t) = I₀ cos(ωt - 2πk/3)

The coils are oriented so their magnetic axes are perpendicular to their
planes. For horizontal coils (lying in the xy-plane), the magnetic moment
of coil k is:
    m_k(t) = N I_k(t) π a² ẑ

This gives a field pointing vertically from each coil.

2.2 BIOT-SAVART INTEGRAL

For circular coil k at position r_k = (x_k, y_k, h_c) with axis ẑ, the
magnetic field at point r = (x, y, z) is:

    B_k(r) = (μ₀ N I₀)/(4π) ∮ dl' × (r - r') / |r - r'|³

where the integral runs over the coil loop. In cylindrical symmetry about
the coil axis:

    B_{k,r}(ρ_k, z_k) = (μ₀ N I₀)/(2π a) * (z_k / √(ρ_k² + z_k²)) * f_r(ρ_k/a, z_k/a)
    B_{k,z}(ρ_k, z_k) = (μ₀ N I₀)/(2π a) * f_z(ρ_k/a, z_k/a)

where ρ_k = |r - r_k|_⊥ (transverse distance from coil k's axis) and
z_k = z - h_c (height above coil k), and f_r, f_z are dimensionless
field shape functions expressible in terms of complete elliptic integrals K(k)
and E(k):

    f_z = (1/π) √(a/(ρ² + (z-h_c)²)) [(K(k_mod) - E(k_mod)(a² - ρ² - z_k²)/Δ²)]

with k_mod = 2√(a ρ) / √((a+ρ)² + z_k²) and Δ² = (a+ρ)² + z_k².

For numerical computation, the field is computed via direct Biot-Savart
discretization (dl parameterized as dl = a dφ' (-sin φ', cos φ', 0)).

2.3 TIME STRUCTURE OF THE TOTAL FIELD

B(r, t) = Σ_{k=0}^{2} B_k^{static}(r) cos(ωt - 2πk/3)

Components (Cartesian):
    B_x(r,t) = Σ_k B_{k,x}^{static}(r) cos(ωt - 2πk/3)
    B_y(r,t) = Σ_k B_{k,y}^{static}(r) cos(ωt - 2πk/3)
    B_z(r,t) = Σ_k B_{k,z}^{static}(r) cos(ωt - 2πk/3)

Time average of products: <cos(ωt - φ_j) cos(ωt - φ_k)> = (1/2) δ_{jk}
(orthogonality for different phases).

Therefore:
    <B_x²> = (1/2) Σ_k (B_{k,x}^{static})²
    <B_y²> = (1/2) Σ_k (B_{k,y}^{static})²
    <B_z²> = (1/2) Σ_k (B_{k,z}^{static})²
    <|B|²> = (1/2) Σ_k |B_k^{static}|²

For identical coils at 120° intervals, the azimuthal average of Σ_k |B_k^{static}(r)|²
is 3 * |B^{single}_k(r)|² (same magnitude by symmetry). Therefore:

    <|B(r)|²> = (3/2) |B^{single}(r)|²

The time-averaged field strength is √(3/2) times the peak single-coil field.

ROTATING CHARACTER: At any fixed point r in the equatorial plane of the coil
array, the field vector B(r,t) traces an ellipse in time (in general). On the
axis of symmetry (r = 0), by symmetry B = B_z ẑ and the field is purely axial
and oscillating (not rotating). The rotating character is most pronounced in
the azimuthal direction at intermediate radii.

2.4 TIME-AVERAGED |B|² ALONG THE z-AXIS

On the symmetry axis (ρ = 0), each coil contributes only a z-component:

    B_{k,z}^{static}(z) = (μ₀ N I₀ a²) / (2 ((z-h_c)² + a² + (x_k²+y_k²))^{3/2}) * (correction for off-axis coil)

For coils displaced from the axis by R_array = a = 5 cm, the on-axis field
is reduced compared to an on-axis coil. The three coils add coherently
(same z-direction). Therefore:

    <B_z²(0,z)> = (3/2) |B_z^{single}(0,z)|²

This is larger above the coil plane (where all three coils converge their
fields toward the axis) than below. The asymmetry creates a Kelvin force
gradient.

2.5 FARADAY-INDUCED ELECTRIC FIELD

∇ × E = -∂B/∂t = ω Σ_k B_k^{static}(r) sin(ωt - 2πk/3)

For the rotating component in the azimuthal direction:
    E_φ ≈ -ω ∫_0^ρ B_z^{avg}(ρ', z) ρ' dρ'  [from ∂B_z/∂t Faraday]

At 60 Hz and B₀ = 1 mT (for I₀ = 1 A, 100 turns, a = 5 cm):
    |E_induced| ≈ ω ρ B_0 = 2π * 60 * 0.1 * 1e-3 ≈ 3.8e-2 mV/m

This induced E is extremely small. The electromagnetic momentum density:
    |g_EM| = ε₀ |E × B| ≈ ε₀ * 3.8e-5 * 1e-3 ≈ 3.4e-19 kg/(m²·s)

Force = d/dt ∫ g_EM dV ≈ ω * 3.4e-19 * 1e-3 ≈ 1.3e-22 N

This is negligibly small (22 orders of magnitude below 1 N). The quasi-static
EM momentum mechanism is inoperative at 60 Hz.

3. KELVIN FORCE ON TOROIDAL FERRITE — DETAILED ANALYSIS
====================================================================

3.1 GEOMETRY

Toroidal ferrite: inner radius a_i = 3 cm, outer radius a_o = 8 cm,
height h_t = 2 cm. Material: soft ferrite, μ_r = 2000, χ_m = 1999.
Position: centered at z = 0 (coils at z = h_c = 3 cm above top face of toroid).

3.2 FORCE DENSITY

For linear ferrite in quasi-static limit (magnetostatics, μ_r = const):

    f(r) = μ₀ (M · ∇)H = (μ₀ χ_m / 2) ∇(|H|²)

    f_z(r) = (μ₀ χ_m / 2) ∂|H|²/∂z

3.3 NET FORCE INTEGRAL

    F_z = ∫_V f_z(r) dV = (μ₀ χ_m / 2) ∫_V ∂|H|²/∂z dV

Using the divergence theorem on the scalar function |H|²:
    ∫_V ∂|H|²/∂z dV = ∮_∂V |H|² n_z dA = ∫_{top} |H|² dA - ∫_{bottom} |H|² dA

Since the coils are above the toroid:
    |H|²_{top face} > |H|²_{bottom face}

Therefore F_z > 0 (upward force on toroid). This is the ATTRACTIVE force
pulling the toroid toward the field source (the coils above it).

3.4 REACTION FORCE ON COILS

By Newton's third law, the force on the coils from the toroid is:
    F_{coils,z} = -F_{toroid,z}  (downward)

Both forces are internal to the device frame. The net external force on the
device = F_{toroid} + F_{coils} = 0.

This is the same conclusion as for the hemisphere-on-ground-plane (Attempt 2):
internal forces between components of a rigidly mounted device cancel exactly.

3.5 THE ROLE OF SATURATION

At high fields (μ₀ H > B_sat ≈ 0.3 T for typical ferrite), the toroid saturates.
Above saturation: M → M_sat = const, χ_m → 0. The Kelvin force drops to zero
when the toroid is fully saturated. This means there is an OPTIMAL current
I* that maximizes F_Kelvin: too little current gives small H and small force;
too much saturates the ferrite and force drops again.

For a_o = 8 cm, N = 100 turns, I* ≈ B_sat / (μ₀ μ_r N/h_coil) ≈ 0.3 / (1e-6 * 2000 * 1e4) ≈ 15 mA

At I₀ = 1 A (our design), H_at_toroid ≈ N I₀ a² / (2(h_c² + a²)^{3/2}) * correction ≈ 100 A/m
    B_in_ferrite = μ₀ μ_r H ≈ 1.26e-6 * 2000 * 100 ≈ 0.25 T  → near saturation

This means the 1 A coil current at 100 turns is in the saturation regime for μ_r = 2000.
The actual force is less than the linear χ_m estimate. Full calculation requires
the nonlinear B-H curve for the specific ferrite grade.

4. ELECTROMAGNETIC MOMENTUM FLUX ANALYSIS
====================================================================

4.1 MOMENTUM DENSITY FROM FARADAY'S LAW

The time-averaged Poynting vector:
    <S_z> = <(E × H)_z> = <E_x H_y - E_y H_x>

For the rotating field, the induced E is azimuthal (E_φ). In Cartesian:
    E_x = -E_φ sin φ,  E_y = E_φ cos φ

H_x = H_x^{rotating}(r,t),  H_y = H_y^{rotating}(r,t)

The time average involves products of trigonometric functions. For the
three-phase rotating field:

    <E_x H_y - E_y H_x> = <E_φ (cos φ H_x + sin φ H_y)>

where E_φ is the Faraday-induced azimuthal field. The cross product involves
the azimuthal component of H times E_φ. For an axially symmetric coil array,
<H_φ> = 0 (the azimuthal field cancels by symmetry). The dominant H is in
the ρ-z plane (H_ρ, H_z components). Since E_φ ∝ ∂B_z/∂t and H_ρ ∝ ∂A_φ/∂z,
the cross product <E_φ H_ρ> ≠ 0 in principle.

For the specific time structure of the rotating field:
    E_φ(r,t) = E_φ^{amp}(r) sin(ωt - φ_E(r))
    H_ρ(r,t) = H_ρ^{amp}(r) cos(ωt - φ_H(r))

    <E_φ H_ρ> = (E_φ^{amp} H_ρ^{amp}/2) sin(φ_E - φ_H)

This is the reactive (quadrature) Poynting component — it oscillates at 2ω
but has nonzero time average only if the E and H phases differ.

For the rotating coil field: E_φ lags H_z by π/2 (Faraday induction), so
φ_E - φ_H = π/2 for the azimuthal components. The time-averaged axial
Poynting flux is:

    <S_z>|_{z=+H} = ∫_ρ ρ dρ dφ * (1/μ₀) <(E × B)_z>

This integral is nonzero. However, as shown in Section 2.5, the magnitude is
<S_z> ≈ E_induced * H ≈ 3.8e-2 mV/m * 100 A/m * 4π×10^{-7} ≈ 4.8e-9 W/m²

Force = <S_z>/c ≈ 1.6e-17 N — completely negligible.

4.2 CONCLUSION ON EM MOMENTUM

At 60 Hz, the EM momentum mechanism produces forces 17+ orders of magnitude
below 1 N. This mechanism is irrelevant for practical propulsion at power-line
frequencies. It would become relevant at:
  * f ~ 1 GHz: force scales as f², improvement of 10^{14} from 60 Hz → ≈ 10^{-3} N
  * f ~ 10 GHz (microwave): ≈ 0.1 N potentially achievable at high power

The BUGA device operates at power-line frequency (observed from the AC connection
in the images). This rules out the EM momentum mechanism as the source of the
anomalous scale readings.

5. INTERPRETATION OF THE BUGA SCALE READINGS
====================================================================

5.1 WHAT THE SCALE READINGS TELL US

Scale image 1 (13_1912715675791159296): 0.12 kg — advanced construction stage
  with large toroidal element and three disc assemblies fully assembled.
Scale image 2 (13_1964248354038435843): 0.058 kg — different build stage
  with dark disc assemblies in hexagonal arrangement, fewer components visible.

Two interpretations:

INTERPRETATION A — Different construction stages (different mass):
  The 0.12 kg reading includes the toroidal ferrite element (added late in
  construction). The 0.058 kg reading is from an earlier stage without
  the toroid. The 62 g difference = mass of toroidal ferrite element.
  Ferrite density ρ ≈ 4.8 g/cm³. For a toroid of dimensions seen in images:
  outer R ≈ 10 cm, inner R ≈ 5 cm, height ≈ 2 cm:
    V_toroid = π (R_o² - R_i²) * h = π (0.01 - 0.0025) * 0.02 ≈ 4.7e-4 m³
    m_toroid = 4800 * 4.7e-4 ≈ 2.3 kg — far too large.
  More realistic (from actual device visible size, R_o ≈ 8 cm, R_i ≈ 3 cm, h ≈ 1 cm):
    V_toroid = π (0.0064 - 0.0009) * 0.01 ≈ 1.73e-4 m³
    m_toroid = 4800 * 1.73e-4 ≈ 0.83 kg — still larger than the 62 g difference.
  Interpretation A is plausible only if the toroid is small plastic-encased
  ferrite (not a large solid block), mass ~50-100 g.

INTERPRETATION B — Same component measured before and during/after operation:
  This would imply 52% mass reduction under EM operation — physically extraordinary.
  Conventional EM forces cannot produce this. Kelvin force is internal (net 0).
  EM momentum at 60 Hz ≈ 10^{-22} N. Thermal effects could explain partial
  readings if the coils are hot and the scale drifts, but not 52%.

INTERPRETATION C — Scale uncertainty or different scale modes:
  If scale 1 is in kg and scale 2 is in different units or showing a partial
  reading (e.g., "058" could be 0.58 kg, not 58 g), the two readings could
  reflect a much larger or smaller difference.
  0.12 kg vs. 0.580 kg: then scale 2 reads MORE (added toroid), consistent
  with Interpretation A.

5.2 MOST LIKELY INTERPRETATION

The two scale readings represent different build stages at different total
masses. The 62 g difference is explained by a small ferrite core or structural
element added between the two measurement sessions. There is NO evidence of
active electromagnetic operation at the time of the scale measurements —
both images appear to show the device at rest (no blurred rotor, no glowing
discharge). The anomalous mass reduction hypothesis requires the measurements
to be taken during active operation, which is not confirmed by the images.

CONCLUSION: The BUGA scale readings do NOT constitute confirmed evidence of
anomalous mass reduction. They are consistent with ordinary mass differences
between construction stages. This is an important de-escalation of the
empirical priority of Section 4.1.

The BUGA device remains worth simulating because:
  * The construction is detailed and shows genuine electromagnetic engineering
  * The material degradation confirms real high-power operation
  * The rotating rotor + toroidal ferrite + three-phase coil architecture is
    a specific design with a testable physics mechanism
  * Even if the scale readings are mundane, the device may still produce
    measurable (if small) forces via the mechanisms analyzed above
