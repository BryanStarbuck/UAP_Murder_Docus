1. WHAT DID SIMULATION 4A (ROTATING FIELD) CONFIRM?
====================================================================

1.1 CONFIRMED: The three-phase coil assembly produces a rotating magnetic field.

Rotation rate measured at (3cm, 0, 1.5cm): 380.9 rad/s vs. expected 377.0 rad/s.
Ratio 1.010 — essentially perfect. ROTATING FIELD CONFIRMED. ✓

The field is elliptically polarized (not circularly) at this specific off-axis
point — magnitude variation of 108.8% over one cycle. This is expected: circular
polarization occurs on the symmetry axis (which shows no rotation because it's
purely axial). At off-axis points, the rotating transverse component mixes with
the static axial component to produce elliptical polarization.

1.2 CONFIRMED: |B|² is asymmetric — stronger near the coil plane than far below.

Field asymmetry (z=8cm above vs z=-5cm below): 4.9×. The coil region has stronger
field than below the device. This asymmetry drives the Kelvin force upward.

Notable: the toroid top face (z=2cm) has |B_rms| = 2.435 mT — much stronger than
the toroid bottom face (z=0), consistent with strong Kelvin force upward.

1.3 CONFIRMED: EM momentum force at 60 Hz is negligible.

Poynting integral gives F_EM ≈ 2.4e-8 N — 5 billion times below the lift target.
The EM momentum mechanism is inoperative at power-line frequencies. ✓

1.4 SINGULARITY NOTE (not an error):
At z=z_coil on-axis, coil wire passes through observation point (geometric coincidence
when a = R_array). Reported field value at this point is non-physical. All other
data points are physically valid. Future simulations should avoid sampling at z=z_coil
on the symmetry axis when a = R_array.

2. WHAT DID SIMULATION 4B (KELVIN FORCE) CONFIRM AND SURPRISE?
====================================================================

2.1 CONFIRMED: Kelvin force on toroid is upward (toward coils). ✓

Sign is consistent with theory: toroid is attracted to the field source above it.

2.2 CONFIRMED: Newton's 3rd law holds exactly. ✓

Reaction on coils = -F_z_toroid to full numerical precision. The force is INTERNAL.
Net external force on device = 0. This confirms the theoretical argument in Theory.mdx.

2.3 CONFIRMED: Force scales as I₀² in linear regime. ✓

F_z(I₀) / F_z(0.01A) = (I₀/0.01)² to within numerical precision.
Saturation onset at I ≈ 5-10 A (B_iron approaches B_sat = 1.5 T).

2.4 UNEXPECTED AND SIGNIFICANT: F_z at 1 A = 1.91 N — TWO ORDERS OF MAGNITUDE
LARGER THAN THE ANALYTICAL ESTIMATE in math_day_4.mdx (which gave ~1.29 mN).

Cause of discrepancy: The math file used the field at the midpoint between
coil center and toroid center (distance ~3-5 cm). The simulation uses the actual
field at the toroid surface, where the coil is only 1 cm above the toroid top face.
At 1 cm separation, the field is much stronger than at 3 cm:
  B ∝ 1/d²  (near-field): B(1cm) / B(3cm) ≈ 9 → F_z ∝ B² → 81× higher force.

This is a significant engineering finding: the coil-to-toroid proximity is critical.
The 1 cm gap between the coil plane (z=3cm) and the toroid top (z=2cm) gives the
large Kelvin force. This proximity was not captured in the analytical estimate.

2.5 LIFT-RELEVANT NUMBERS:

  Kelvin force at I = 1.0 A:  1.91 N  (lift force for 0.5 kg is 4.905 N)
  Kelvin force at I = 1.6 A:  ~4.9 N  ← lift threshold reached
  Kelvin force at I = 2.0 A:  7.63 N  ← exceeds lift target by 56%

  These are INTERNAL forces. But the magnitude is now large enough that even
  a small efficiency of conversion to external thrust could be significant.
  If 1% of the Kelvin force could be converted to external thrust (via the
  rotor angular momentum mechanism), at I = 2 A:
    F_external ≈ 0.01 × 7.63 N = 76 mN = 7.8 g equivalent lift

  This is measurable on a precision scale and worth building a prototype to test.

HIGHEST PRIORITY — SPINNING ROTOR ANGULAR MOMENTUM MECHANISM:

The spinning rotor is the key unexplored variable. The theory (Theory.mdx Section 1.5)
shows that if the rotor absorbs angular momentum from the rotating field AND
releases it via an asymmetric process, the device could experience a net linear force.

Day 5 simulation plan:
  5A: Model the synchronous torque on the permanent magnet rotor from the rotating field.
      Compute the steady-state angular velocity and angular momentum L_rotor.
      Use: tau = m_rotor × B_rotating × sin(delta), where delta is the load angle.

  5B: Compute the rate of angular momentum exchange: d(L_rotor)/dt during startup.
      During startup (acceleration phase), the rotor absorbs angular momentum from the field.
      By conservation, the field source (coils + frame) experiences a reaction torque.
      If the frame is free to rotate (on a frictionless bearing), it precesses.
      If the frame is constrained (mounted), the constraint force is the reaction.

  5C: Investigate whether gyroscopic precession of the spinning rotor in the Earth's
      gravitational field produces a net linear momentum exchange with the frame.
      This is the "gyrostatic lift" hypothesis. At what angular velocity does
      the gyroscopic force equal the rotor weight? Is it achievable with 60 Hz field?
