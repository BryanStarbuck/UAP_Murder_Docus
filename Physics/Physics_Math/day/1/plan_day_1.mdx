KEY FINDING FROM CORPUS:

Dr. Horace Drew's experimental device achieves confirmed upward force
on the inner spinning magnet (500g-equivalent at 17 Hz, 80V, 3A).
However, the total system weight on the scale beneath the device
is unchanged: F_up(magnet) = F_down(coils) exactly. Newton's Third
Law is operating within the system boundary; no net external force.

Drew's own statement: "there's also a force down on the three phase
coils in both orientations, and the force up is only maybe a tiny
bit bigger than the force down. So that's the basic trouble with
the UFO levitation."

This finding directly constrains Attempt 1: a symmetric rotating
magnet array with an internal ferromagnetic target cannot produce
net lift on the combined system. The "reaction surface" in Attempt 1
(ferromagnetic flux concentrator ring) must be EXTERNAL to the craft
(fixed ground element) or the design must exploit a different
mechanism.

UPDATED UNDERSTANDING OF ATTEMPT 1:

Two valid sub-approaches for Attempt 1:

Sub-approach A (Ground-Coupled): The ferromagnetic reaction surface
is fixed to the ground. The Halbach array (craft) pushes upward
against the field gradient created by the ground surface. The craft
rises but cannot fly freely — only lifts off a prepared surface.
Analogous to maglev but using gradient (Kelvin) force rather than
inductive repulsion. Valid for demonstrating the lift mechanism even
if not free-flight capable.

Sub-approach B (Time-Asymmetric): If the rotating array creates a
time-varying field with an asymmetric impulse — stronger upward push
during one phase than downward push during another — the time-averaged
force on the craft could be non-zero upward. This requires the field
topology to break time-reversal symmetry, which in turn requires a
mechanism like: (1) non-linear ferromagnetic material response to a
rotating field, (2) eddy current phase lag creating a retarded force,
(3) radiation pressure analog at the drive frequency.

Day 1 focus: Sub-approach A. Model the static (non-rotating) case
first to establish force scale. The rotating case analysis begins Day 2.
