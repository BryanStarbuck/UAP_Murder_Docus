Q1: WHAT DID SIMULATION OR ANALYSIS SHOW TODAY THAT WAS NOT EXPECTED?
====================================================================

Three unexpected results from the Day 2 simulation:

UNEXPECTED RESULT 1: Geometry factor C = 10.7 (not ~1 as estimated)

The dimensional analysis in math_day_2.mdx correctly showed F_z ~ eps_0 * V_0^2
but estimated the geometry constant C ~ O(1). The simulation gave C = 10.73.
This factor-of-10 enhancement comes from two contributions:
  (a) The image charge geometry concentrates field at the apex (factor ~2-3 in E)
  (b) Higher-order Legendre terms (l=3,5,...) add 51.8% beyond the l=1 dipole
The factor of 10.7 means the lift voltage is 227 kV, not 740 kV.

UNEXPECTED RESULT 2: The lift voltage (227 kV) is feasible in vacuum

The apex E-field of 6.82 MV/m needed for 0.5 kg lift is:
  * Above air breakdown (3 MV/m) — vacuum required
  * Below vacuum field emission onset (100-500 MV/m) — feasible

This is a more optimistic result than Day 2 started with. A single hemisphere
device in hard vacuum at 227 kV could theoretically lift 0.5 kg. Whether
a real device can maintain this voltage without breakdown or emission
currents is an engineering question, not a physics barrier.

UNEXPECTED RESULT 3: Legendre series shows sign reversal at theta = 10 degrees

The E-field at theta = 10 degrees is computed as -15,796 V/m (inward, negative),
between two positive values at theta = 0 and theta = 20 degrees. This is
almost certainly a Gibbs-phenomenon artifact from the truncated Legendre series
at L_MAX = 21. The boundary condition at the hemisphere edge (theta = pi/2)
is a step discontinuity, and the Legendre series exhibits Gibbs ringing near it.

This means the computed geometry factor C = 10.7 may be slightly underestimated.
The true converged value may be C ~ 12-15, reducing V_lift further.
