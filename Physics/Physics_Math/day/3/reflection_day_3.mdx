Q1: WHAT DID SIMULATION OR ANALYSIS SHOW TODAY THAT WAS NOT EXPECTED?
====================================================================

THREE UNEXPECTED FINDINGS:

UNEXPECTED 1: The Legendre series for the hemisphere force DIVERGES logarithmically.

The Day 2 result (F_z = 9.50 mN at L_MAX=21) was believed to be converged with
minor Gibbs corrections of 10-20%. The Day 3 convergence test showed:
  L_MAX=21:  9.50 mN
  L_MAX=51:  10.89 mN (+14.6%)
  L_MAX=101: 12.00 mN (+26.4%)

The series is growing as C ~ 5.3 + 1.8*ln(L), suggesting the partial sums are
converging to a value that depends on the truncation level — i.e., the series
represents an integrand with a non-integrable singularity in the Legendre
representation. The physical cause: square-root singularity of the surface charge
density sigma ~ (pi/2 - theta)^{-1/2} at the rim, where the hemisphere joins the
ground plane. The E_r^2 integrand has ~ (pi/2 - theta)^{-1} singularity, and the
cos(theta) weight does NOT fully cancel this — the integral log-diverges.

This means the DAY 2 RESULT IS NOT THE CORRECT VALUE. It is a lower bound. The true
electrostatic thrust force is larger than 9.50 mN at 10 kV, but we cannot quantify
how much larger without a BEM or FEM solver that handles the rim singularity correctly.

UNEXPECTED 2: Cone angle 30 degrees gives 3.1x the geometry factor of the hemisphere.

V_lift drops from 227 kV (hemisphere) to 128 kV (30-degree cone) at R=10cm.
This is a larger improvement than the physics estimate of ~1.5x. The sharp cone
concentrates field at the apex far more effectively than the hemisphere, and the
FD simulation (while coarse) shows a clear, robust optimum at 25-35 degrees.

If the absolute value is within a factor of 2 of the correct value (plausible for
coarse FD), the 30-degree cone lifts 0.5 kg at 90-180 kV rather than 227 kV.

UNEXPECTED 3: The Buhler mechanism is FUNDAMENTALLY a near-field effect.

The isolated conductor analysis confirmed with Green's theorem proof and numerical
verification that an isolated conductor cannot produce net thrust. The force is
always between the electrode and an external conductor. This means Buhler's result
is a NEAR-FIELD interaction with the vacuum chamber walls, NOT a free-space
propulsion mechanism.

This resolves the project's central question for Attempt 2: the electrostatic
mechanism cannot be the primary propulsion system for a free-flying craft. It is
useful for near-surface hover (altitude < 5 R from a conducting surface).

Q4: ARE THERE PATTERNS ACROSS THE LAST 10 DAYS SUGGESTING STRATEGY CHANGE?
====================================================================

Pattern after 3 days:

  Day 1: Classical magnetostatics (Kelvin force) → falsified in one day. Eliminated.
  Day 2: Classical electrostatics (Maxwell stress) → CONFIRMED in one day. Viable.
  Day 3: Classical electrostatics (continued) → LIMITATION discovered. Near-surface only.

The pattern: both approaches work well within their domains but are limited:
  * Kelvin magnetostatic force: direction always wrong for free flight
  * Maxwell electrostatic force: requires external ground for free flight

Both are classical effects from Maxwell's equations without any new physics.
Both fail for free-flight propulsion because:
  * Newton's 3rd law always applies
  * Any force on the craft comes from interaction with an external body

EMERGING INSIGHT: For a truly self-contained propulsive mechanism (free flight
without reaction mass or external reaction body), the physics MUST either:
  (a) Involve time-varying fields where momentum can be carried by the field
      (radiation pressure, electromagnetic momentum) — photon rocket scaled up
  (b) Invoke physics beyond Maxwell's equations (GCD theory, ZPE fluctuations)
  (c) Use quantum effects (Casimir force variants, sonoluminescence analog)
  (d) Exploit the structure of spacetime itself (gravitomagnetic, Alcubierre)

This reframes the project: purely classical electromagnetic mechanisms
(static or quasi-static) cannot produce free-flight propulsion without
reaction mass. The corpus approaches that DO claim free flight must invoke
either (a) high-frequency EM (microwave/laser photon pressure), or (b) new physics.

STRATEGIC IMPLICATION: Day 4 should start Attempt 3 (BUGA device, Approach 3.3).
The BUGA device uses a ROTATING electromagnetic field — a time-varying configuration.
Rotating fields can carry electromagnetic angular and linear momentum. Whether
the BUGA configuration produces a net time-averaged force on a stationary body
above it is the physics question to answer.
