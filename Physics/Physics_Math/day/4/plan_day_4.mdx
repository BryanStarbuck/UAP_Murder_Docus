PHYSICS MECHANISM TO ANALYZE:
  A three-phase rotating magnetic field (produced by three coil assemblies
  at 120-degree intervals driven by a three-phase AC source) produces an
  EM field that rotates in space with angular velocity Omega = 2 * pi * f.
  The rotating field carries electromagnetic angular momentum L_EM given by:
    L_EM = eps_0 * integral (r x (E x B)) dV
  Any interaction that changes the direction or magnitude of L_EM over time
  produces a time-averaged torque on the field source. If the device
  geometry is asymmetric (toroidal ferrite below, hemispherical electrode
  above), asymmetric coupling may produce a net linear force on the assembly.
  The key question: does time-averaged F_z = <integral T_iz n_i dA> differ
  from zero for the three-phase rotating field above a toroidal ferrite?

SIMULATION PLAN (Day 4):

  Simulation 4A — Three-phase coil Biot-Savart field map:
    Compute rotating B-field from three coil assemblies at 120-degree
    intervals. Verify that the field rotates (phi(t) = 2*pi*f*t).
    Compute instantaneous and time-averaged |B|^2 above and below the
    coil plane. Check for axial asymmetry in the time-averaged field.
    Software: scipy/numpy + Biot-Savart (no new install needed).

  Simulation 4B — Kelvin force on toroidal ferrite in rotating field:
    Compute the Kelvin body force on a toroidal ferrite (mu_r = 2000)
    placed in the rotating field from Simulation 4A.
    F_Kelvin = mu_0 * chi_m * integral (M · grad)H dV
             = (chi_m / (1 + chi_m)) * integral grad(|H|^2/2) * V dV
    Is there a net vertical component? Does it depend on the toroid
    position relative to the coil plane?
    Software: scipy/numpy (analytical Kelvin force integral).

  Simulation 4C — Angular momentum flux analysis:
    Compute <S_z> = (1/T) integral_0^T (E x B)_z dt at the plane z = H
    above the device. If <S_z> ≠ 0 (net axial Poynting flux), the device
    is radiating EM angular momentum in the z-direction. By conservation,
    the device experiences an equal and opposite angular impulse — and
    potentially a linear force if the geometry breaks rotational symmetry.
    This is the key free-flight mechanism test.
