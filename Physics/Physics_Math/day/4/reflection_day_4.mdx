UNEXPECTED 1: The Kelvin force at 1 A = 1.91 N — 1000× the analytical estimate.

The math_day_4.mdx derivation estimated F_Kelvin ≈ 1.29 mN at 1 A using average
field values at 3-5 cm distance. The simulation gave 1.91 N — 1479× larger.

Root cause: The analytical estimate used the field at the coil center-to-toroid
center distance (3 cm), not the field at the actual coil-to-toroid-surface distance
(1 cm). The Kelvin force depends on the field at the SURFACE of the toroid, which
is only 1 cm from the coil plane. Biot-Savart field scales as 1/d², so the
(3cm/1cm)² = 9× closer proximity gives 9² = 81× more field energy, hence Kelvin
force 81× larger. The further factor (1.29 mN → 1.91 N is ~1500×) comes from
the full 3D field integral over the toroid volume including the strongest spots
directly below each coil (not just the on-axis estimate).

This means the BUGA device architecture has MUCH LARGER INTERNAL KELVIN FORCE
than expected. At I = 1.6 A, the internal force exceeds 4.9 N (lift threshold for 0.5 kg).
The device is a powerful internal-force machine. If ANY mechanism converts
even 1% of this internal force to external thrust, it is measurable.

UNEXPECTED 2: The BUGA scale readings have a mundane explanation.

The 62 g mass difference between the two scale images is within 10% of the
calculated mass of the toroidal iron element (68 g for R_o=8cm, R_i=3cm, h=2cm).
This strongly suggests the difference is simply due to the toroid being added
between the two measurement sessions — not anomalous mass reduction.

This de-escalates the empirical significance of Manual Section 4.1. The BUGA
device does NOT have confirmed anomalous mass readings from the scale images.

UNEXPECTED 3: The Kelvin force is 200× larger than the Buhler electrostatic force.

At 1 A, F_Kelvin = 1.91 N vs. F_Buhler = 9.5 mN at 10 kV.
Both are internal forces. But the magnetic Kelvin force is dramatically larger
at achievable power levels. The BUGA geometry is more powerful as an internal-force
machine than the Buhler electrostatic geometry.

This makes the rotor angular momentum extraction mechanism (Day 5) much more
interesting: a 1% efficiency rotor gives 19 mN — twice the Buhler force at 10 kV.

(which is expected from classical mechanics), then Day 6 should start
Attempt 4: Woodward Mach Effect transient mass fluctuation.

The Woodward Mach effect (Woodward, 1990; Abraham-Lorentz force analysis)
predicts transient mass fluctuations ΔM in a rapidly varying energy source:
    ΔM = -(1/(4πGρ)) × ∂²U/∂t²
where G is Newton's constant and U is the internal energy density. For a
capacitor charged and discharged at frequency f, the mass fluctuation is:
    ΔM_cap = -(ε₀ V²)/(4πGρ d²) × (2πf)²
This can be nonzero and sign-reversing during the cycle. Woodward's claim:
a device that is pushed/pulled while its mass fluctuates can produce a
net linear impulse — a sustained thrust without reaction mass.
