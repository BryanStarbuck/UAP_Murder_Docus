1. Faraday disc principle: When a conducting disc (copper) rotates in an external magnetic field B_z (provided by a permanent magnet placed nearby), an EMF is generated across the disc from center to rim:
   EMF = ω × B_z × r² / 2
   where ω is the angular frequency and r is the disc radius. This is the homopolar generator principle — the only electromagnetic device where the moving conductor and the field source can be identical (no commutation required).

2. Force measurement setup: The precision balance arm below the rotating disc measures any anomalous vertical force produced during disc rotation. In mainstream physics, a spinning conducting disc in a magnetic field should produce: (a) no net vertical force (the Lorentz forces are radial, not axial); (b) mild gyroscopic resistance to tilting. If the balance arm detects any weight change (upward or downward force) when the disc spins, this would be anomalous.
