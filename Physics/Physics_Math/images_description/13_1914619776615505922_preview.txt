Classical gyroscope geometry: The spherical body design is significant because a sphere has a unique moment of inertia tensor I_sphere = (2/5)MR² (uniform solid sphere) or I_sphere = (2/3)MR² (spherical shell). The diagonal tensor means I_xx = I_yy = I_zz for a perfect sphere — all principal moments of inertia are equal. This makes the sphere isotropic with respect to rotation — it can spin about any axis with the same angular momentum per unit angular velocity. This is in contrast to a disc (I_disc = MR²/2 for spin axis, MR²/4 for transverse axes), which has different moments for different rotation axes.

2. Precession dynamics: A spherical gyroscope precessing under gravity torque τ = Mg × r (where r is the center-of-mass offset from the support point) precesses at:
   Ω_prec = τ/L = Mg × r / (Iω)
   where ω is the spin angular velocity and I is the spin axis moment of inertia. The red-and-black coloring makes the precession visually apparent — the red hemisphere's position relative to the vertical indicates the precession phase.
