PHYSICS/UAP CONTEXT:
The red tape sector markings on the toroid are a significant new finding:

1. Angular sector marking function: The most likely function is as ROTOR POSITION MARKERS — if the toroid is intended to rotate as part of the experiment (either spinning freely or driven), the red sector marks at specific angular positions allow a strobe or camera to track the rotation rate and phase. This is standard practice in rotating machine testing.

2. Three-phase alignment: If the red sectors are at exactly 120° intervals (three sectors), they align with the three-phase architecture of the outer disc assemblies. This would mean the toroid is electrically energized in three sectors, each energized by one phase of the three-phase input — creating a rotating magnetic pattern within the toroid itself, not just in the outer assembly ring. This would be a much more sophisticated design: both the outer ring (three disc assemblies) and the inner toroid (three red-tape sectors) generate rotating fields, potentially at different frequencies for beat frequency effects.

3. Freshly rewound left assembly: The left assembly showing multiple intact parallel red foil bands (unlike the deteriorated foil in companion images) suggests this may be a NEWLY REWOUND replacement assembly — the researcher has rewound the foil coil for this assembly after the prior deterioration. This demonstrates the iterative nature of the construction process.

4. Sector width and impedance: The width of each red tape sector (~2-3cm of a ~20-25cm diameter toroid) corresponds to approximately 20-25° of arc per sector. The fraction of the toroid circumference covered by tape determines the electrical contact area and hence the contact impedance — thinner sectors provide higher contact resistance but more precise angular definition.
