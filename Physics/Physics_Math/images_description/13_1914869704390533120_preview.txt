PHYSICS CONCEPTS:
* Faraday's paradox (also called the homopolar paradox or Faraday disk paradox) concerns the behavior of a rotating conducting disk in a magnetic field. The classical puzzle: when a magnet and a conducting disk rotate together versus separately relative to the observer, the induced EMF does not obey simple relative-motion symmetry as naive application of Faraday's law might suggest.
* The homopolar motor/generator (Faraday disk) was the first electric motor, generating a DC EMF proportional to the angular velocity and the applied magnetic flux. EMF = (1/2) * omega * B * r^2 for a disk of radius r rotating at angular velocity omega in field B.
* The "paradox" concerns whether the magnetic field co-rotates with the magnet or remains fixed in the lab frame — a deep question about the nature of magnetic flux lines and their physical reality, touching on issues in the foundations of classical electrodynamics.
