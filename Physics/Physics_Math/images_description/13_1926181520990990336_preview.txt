* This is a top-down view of a homopolar motor experiment using a glass bowl as the conducting vessel.
* The glass bowl may contain mercury (an excellent liquid-metal electrical conductor) as the conducting medium for the Faraday disc effect — mercury allows radial current flow while the disc rotates.
* The marking "N" inside the bowl indicates the North pole orientation of the underlying magnet.
* The yellow tape tabs secure wire connections to the bowl rim (the outer electrical contact) while the copper axle provides the inner contact.
* In a homopolar motor using mercury-filled bowl: when current flows from the axle radially outward through the mercury to the bowl rim, while an axial magnetic field B is applied (from a magnet below), the Lorentz force F = q*v × B on the mercury (and any conducting disc) drives circular rotation.
