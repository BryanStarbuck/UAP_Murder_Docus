This gyroscope is a classic educational wire-gimbal gyroscope — the same type shown in companion images 13_1940911383069020160 (hand-held), 13_1941725328814329856 (precessing), and others in the gyroscope series. In this image, the gyroscope is in its primary stable-spin state: angular momentum vector L pointing vertically (along the spin axle), gyroscope balanced upright on the bottom axle pin. The yellow-green anodized inner ring identifies this as the same instrument seen in the precessing states. The wire gimbal structure allows the spinning disc to maintain its orientation in space (demonstrate gyroscopic rigidity) regardless of how the outer frame is rotated — a physical demonstration of angular momentum conservation (L = Iω = constant when no external torque acts). The gyroscope's stable upright precession, balanced on its bottom pin, is a classic demonstration of the interplay between gravitational torque (τ = r × F_gravity) and gyroscopic precession (τ = dL/dt = Ω_precession × L). This behavior is referenced in UAP physics research because gyroscopic stabilization and inertial frame resistance are foundational to theories of UAP inertial mass reduction and non-Newtonian flight dynamics.
