PHYSICS/UAP CONTEXT:
The counterweight balls on the spoke tips are a significant detail. Attaching masses to the ends of the spinning disc's spoke arms modifies the moment of inertia I of the spinning disc: I = Σmᵢrᵢ² — where rᵢ is the radius of each mass element from the spin axis. Adding masses at the outer spoke tips increases I significantly (since the radius r appears squared), which increases the total angular momentum L = Iω for a given spin speed ω. This has several experimental uses:
1. Increasing gyroscopic rigidity (resistance to precession)
2. Studying how moment of inertia affects precession rate (Ω_precession = τ/L = mgd/Iω)
3. Dynamic balancing — ensuring the disc is rotationally balanced even with added masses
4. Studying nutation frequency changes with modified moments of inertia

The unequal number of balls (two visible out of four possible spoke tips) suggests either an asymmetric loading experiment (deliberately unbalanced to study nutation/wobble) or that two balls are present and two are not (which might cause interesting gyroscopic behavior due to dynamic imbalance). This is a sophisticated experimental modification consistent with someone exploring advanced gyroscopic dynamics beyond simple demonstration purposes.
