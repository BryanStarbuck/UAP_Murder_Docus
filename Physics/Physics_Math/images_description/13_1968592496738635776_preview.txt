PHYSICS/UAP CONTEXT:
This image provides a comprehensive workshop overview showing multiple electrokinetic and electrochemical experiments being operated simultaneously:

1. Parallel experimental methodology: The researcher is running several different electrode configurations simultaneously — copper hemispherical bowl electrode, flat disc electrode, minimal Petri dish cell — a systematic approach to testing different geometries and materials for anomalous electrokinetic effects.

2. The copper hemispherical bowl: Copper's high electrical conductivity (σ = 5.96×10⁷ S/m) combined with a hemispherical geometry creates non-uniform current density distributions. In an electrolyte medium, this creates azimuthally symmetric current flow patterns that could produce Lorentz body forces (J × B) in the presence of any ambient or applied magnetic field — directly relevant to MHD rotation experiments.

3. The Petri dish minimal cell: The simplest two-electrode configuration tests fundamental electrokinetic effects — electrophoresis, electroosmosis, and potentially anomalous mass/force effects — with minimal confounding geometry. This follows the scientific methodology of comparing simple and complex configurations.

4. The flat metallic disc electrode: Disc electrode geometry is theoretically significant because it provides a uniform current density distribution across the disc face (unlike cylindrical or hemispherical electrodes). The flat disc is the electrode geometry most directly analogous to the UAP disc morphology.

5. Simultaneous operation: Running multiple experiments simultaneously allows direct comparative observation — if one configuration shows anomalous behavior that others do not, the geometry or material can be identified as the critical variable.
