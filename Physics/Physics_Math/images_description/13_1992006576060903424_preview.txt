PHYSICS CONCEPTS:
* This slide introduces a critical experimental claim about Corbino disc (homopolar motor) spin direction control via high-voltage capacitive power:
  1. **Corbino disc**: Also called a Faraday disc, the Corbino disc is a flat conducting disc with current flowing radially from center to rim (or rim to center) while embedded in an axial magnetic field. The resulting Lorentz force F = IL × B produces tangential torque, causing rotation. In the standard Corbino geometry, current flows radially while the magnetic field is axial, making the entire disc the "motor armature."
  2. **Spin direction reversal via capacitor**: The claim that "a big capacitor can change the spin-direction" refers to using capacitive energy storage to reverse the polarity or phase of the drive current — when a charged capacitor discharges through the disc in the opposite radial direction, the Lorentz force reverses and the disc spins in the opposite direction.
  3. **11 kV AC power**: Operating the Corbino disc with 11 kilovolt AC (alternating current) power introduces an alternating current through the disc. With AC at 11 kV, the current direction reverses with each half-cycle, producing alternating spin direction attempts. However, with a large capacitor in the circuit, the phase relationship between current and voltage can be tuned to favor one spin direction over the other.
  4. **UAP propulsion relevance**: In the UAP ball magnet thesis, spin direction control is critical — the ability to instantly reverse or modulate the spin direction of the conducting disc determines the direction of the Lorentz propulsive force. A high-voltage AC system with capacitive phase control provides the mechanism for rapid directional reversal of UAP propulsion.
  5. **Faraday's paradox connection**: Whether the magnetic field co-rotates with the disc or remains stationary determines the EMF = ½ωBr² computation. With AC power at 11 kV, the electromagnetic induction relationships become time-varying and the standard DC Faraday disc analysis must be extended to the phasor domain.
