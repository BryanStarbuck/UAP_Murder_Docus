PHYSICS CONCEPTS:
* This is a capacitor-discharge homopolar motor experiment — connecting directly to the slide in 13_1992006576060903424 ("A big capacitor can change the spin-direction of a Corbino disc with 11 kV AC power"):
  1. **Capacitor as power source**: Instead of a continuous DC power supply, this experiment uses a large capacitor that stores charge and releases it as a short, high-current pulse through the Corbino disc. This produces an impulsive Lorentz force that briefly accelerates the conducting disc (or mercury pool) to high angular velocity.
  2. **Capacitor energy**: The energy stored in a capacitor is E = ½CV². For a large capacitor (e.g., 1-10 F at 5V in a supercapacitor) charged to modest voltage, this produces E = 12.5 J – 125 J. Discharged through the low-resistance mercury disc, this creates a very high current pulse (I = V/R_disc) over a short time constant τ = RC.
  3. **Impulsive torque**: The impulsive current creates an impulsive Lorentz torque τ = I·(L×B)·r in the disc. By reversing the polarity of the capacitor discharge (or by using an AC capacitor that naturally alternates), the spin direction of the disc can be reversed — exactly as described in the 13_1992006576060903424 slide.
  4. **Mercury spill**: The mercury has again escaped the bowl containment, possibly from centrifugal ejection during high-rpm operation or from physical upset of the apparatus.
  5. **UAP connection**: Capacitor-discharge high-current pulsing is a more efficient method of achieving high angular momentum in the conducting disc than continuous DC — pulsed power delivery can produce higher peak currents and thus higher peak Lorentz force than a continuous DC supply at the same energy budget.
