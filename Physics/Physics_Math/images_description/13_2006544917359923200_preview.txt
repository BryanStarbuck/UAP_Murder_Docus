PHYSICS CONCEPTS:
* Three-disc homopolar motor apparatus — same physics as documented throughout this series. The main apparatus shows the three-position radial symmetry of the Corbino disc array.
* Key new element: the crop circle reference diagram on the floor below the apparatus includes additional labeled components:
  1. **"galinstan"**: Galinstan is a room-temperature liquid metal alloy (gallium-indium-tin eutectic, melting point -19°C), used as a mercury substitute in liquid-metal electrical contacts. Its appearance on this diagram suggests the researcher is either using or planning to use galinstan as an alternative to mercury in the Corbino disc contact system — consistent with the electrolytic (water) Corbino disc also documented in this series as a safer mercury alternative.
  2. **"3-phase magnetism"**: Three-phase rotating magnetic fields — produced by three sets of electromagnets (or permanent magnets) arranged 120° apart and driven by three-phase AC power — create a continuously rotating magnetic field. Applied to a Corbino disc geometry, a three-phase rotating field would produce a continuously varying J × B force that could drive sustained rotation more efficiently than a static axial field. This represents a more sophisticated electromagnetic drive scheme than the simple DC Corbino disc.
  3. **"spinning magnet 2"**: Indicates there is more than one "spinning magnet" in the design — a multi-magnet rotating assembly, consistent with the three-disc apparatus having multiple independent magnet assemblies.
  4. **Three labeled disc components on floor**: The numbered yellow-labeled discs (5, 2, 6) are likely individual Corbino disc components labeled for identification in a multi-disc system.
* The three-phase magnetism / galinstan reference links the physical apparatus to a more sophisticated electromagnetic design that approaches (at laboratory scale) the kind of rotating magnetic field drive that would be needed in a full-scale UAP propulsion analog.
