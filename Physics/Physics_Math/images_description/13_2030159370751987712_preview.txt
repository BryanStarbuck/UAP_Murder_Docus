PHYSICS CONCEPTS:
* Upper panel — Rosensweig instability (normal-field instability) in a ferrofluid: When a ferrofluid (colloidal suspension of magnetic nanoparticles) is subjected to a sufficiently strong vertical magnetic field, the flat free surface becomes unstable and spontaneously forms a regular array of sharp peaks — the Rosensweig or normal-field instability. This is a magnetohydrostatic phenomenon where the magnetic pressure (proportional to B²/2μ₀ at the surface) overcomes the restoring forces of gravity and surface tension. The threshold field strength is B_c = √(2μ₀√(ρgσ)) where ρ is density, g gravitational acceleration, and σ surface tension. The resulting peak spacing is set by the capillary length λ_c = √(σ/ρg). The sawtooth/zigzag pattern visible along the lower rail suggests the fluid is responding to a non-uniform or modulated field along the rail length.
