PHYSICS CONCEPTS:
* This frame reveals the most complex assembly configuration in the series — adding small brass/chrome disc elements around the stator flange, which may represent:
  (1) Contact brushes or sliding contacts for electrical pickup from the rotating flange
  (2) Secondary stator magnet elements in addition to the main roller ring
  (3) Tuning/adjusting elements that change the magnetic gap or field geometry
* The needle electrode resting on the orange bearing ball at the center is significant: this is likely a center-tap pickup electrode — a common configuration in homopolar generators where the center contact point measures the potential at the axis of rotation. In a homopolar generator, the EMF is generated radially between center and rim; the needle touching the center ball allows voltage measurement at the exact center without mechanical contact friction of a conventional brush.
* The number "89" on the cylinder may be a test number, serial number, or measurement value from a previous test.
* The circular single-turn wire loop around the assembly may be a pickup coil for measuring time-varying magnetic flux (dΦ/dt) during operation — by Faraday's law, any rotating magnetic field from the SEG assembly would induce a measurable EMF in this loop.
