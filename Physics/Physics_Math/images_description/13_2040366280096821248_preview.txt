PHYSICS CONCEPTS:
* This axial overhead view provides the clearest documentation of the brass disc contact ring between the chrome stator cylinder outer wall and the roller magnets.
* The 14-16 brass disc elements in a regular ring around the cylinder exterior are consistent with:
  (1) Capacitive coupling elements: each brass disc forms a capacitor with the spinning chrome cylinder outer wall, coupling charge inductively as the cylinder rotates — this is a non-contact electrical pickup analogous to a slip-ring-free commutator
  (2) Eddy current braking/driving elements: the brass discs positioned adjacent to the spinning cylinder would have eddy currents induced in them by the rotating magnetic field, providing either braking torque or, if externally driven, motoring torque
  (3) Hall-effect sensor mounting pads: if semiconductor sensors are mounted under each brass disc, they would measure the local magnetic field strength at each azimuthal position, providing direct field mapping
* The hollow chrome cylinder interior (large bore visible from above) suggests the cylinder is a thick-walled tube, not a solid cylinder — the hollow geometry reduces mass and provides access to the central spindle.
* The central spindle at the cylinder interior center is the homopolar generator center-tap pickup point — the needle electrode (seen in side views) accesses this spindle tip from above.
* The 9-roller configuration (if confirmed by count) at 40° spacing per roller = 3 rollers per phase × 3 phases, consistent with all prior phase analysis.
