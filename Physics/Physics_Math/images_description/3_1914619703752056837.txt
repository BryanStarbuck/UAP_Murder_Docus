KEY PHYSICS CLAIM:
When a ferromagnetic object (iron ball, 1 kg) is lifted by an external DIPOLE MAGNETIC field from above:
- The iron ball appears to have NO WEIGHT (its gravitational force is cancelled by the upward magnetic attraction)
- BUT the reaction force transfers entirely to the man: the man now weighs 101 kg (his own 100 kg + the 1 kg transferred force from the magnetic interaction)

This is a correct application of Newton's third law and force balance in an open system:
- The magnetic field source is external (above the man)
- The magnetic field exerts upward force F on the iron ball
- The iron ball exerts downward force F on the field source (reaction)
- But the field source is external — so the man's scale reading increases by the ball's weight
- The iron ball's scale reading (if it were on a separate scale): zero

PHYSICS EXPLANATION:
In dipole magnetism, the force on a magnetic dipole in a non-uniform field is:
F = ∇(m·B)
where m is the magnetic moment and B is the external field.
This force is transmitted from the field SOURCE (the external magnet) through the field to the BALL.
The field source bears the reaction — not the man directly.
However, if the man is holding the field source (the magnet), he bears the load.
The subtle point: who bears the reaction force depends on where the force originates.

CONTRAST WITH VORTEX MAGNETISM:
The next slide (3_1914620064239902722.jpg) contrasts this with "vortex magnetism," which allegedly produces a different result — the ball retains its weight and the man also retains his original weight (no transfer).
