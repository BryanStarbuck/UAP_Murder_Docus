KEY PHYSICS CLAIM BEING RAISED:
This slide introduces the concept of "vortex magnetism" as a mode of lifting a ferromagnetic object that is fundamentally different from dipole magnetism.

The metaphor "pulling on a string" suggests a direct mechanical-like connection between the lifting mechanism and the object — analogous to how pulling a string on a spinning top causes it to rise (gyroscopic precession combined with upward torque).

VORTEX MAGNETISM — CLAIMED PROPERTIES:
In the non-mainstream physics literature relevant to UAP propulsion:
- "Vortex magnetism" refers to a rotating or toroidal magnetic field configuration
- The rotating field allegedly creates a different interaction with the object's inertial/gravitational mass
- Unlike dipole magnetism (where force is transmitted through field gradients), vortex magnetism allegedly couples to the object's spin states or internal rotational degrees of freedom
- The "spinning top" analogy suggests the field imparts angular momentum to the iron ball, causing it to precess upward
- This is related to concepts like the Barnett effect (spin → magnetization), Einstein-de Haas effect (change in magnetization → mechanical rotation), and hypothetical torsion-gravity coupling

SPINNING TOP ANALOGY:
A spinning top "rises" (precesses rather than falls) because:
- Gravity applies a torque τ = r × Mg
- This torque causes angular momentum precession dL/dt = τ
- The top precesses horizontally rather than tipping over
- If the rotation rate is high enough, the top appears to "defy gravity"
The implication is that "vortex magnetism" applies a similar rotational interaction to the iron ball.
