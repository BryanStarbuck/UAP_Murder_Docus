MATHEMATICAL FRAMEWORK:
The Kruskal-Szekeres coordinates (T, X) are defined by the transformation from Schwarzschild coordinates (t, r):
- For Region I (r > 2M): X = √(r/2M - 1) · e^(r/4M) · cosh(t/4M), T = √(r/2M - 1) · e^(r/4M) · sinh(t/4M)
- The metric becomes: ds² = (32M³/r) · e^(-r/2M) · (-dT² + dX²) + r²dΩ²
- The event horizons at r = 2M correspond to T = ±X (the 45° lines)
- The singularity at r = 0 satisfies T² - X² = 1 (the upper/lower hyperbolas)
- Constant-r surfaces satisfy T² - X² = constant (hyperbolas in T-X plane)
- Constant-t surfaces satisfy T/X = constant (straight lines through origin)

The Einstein-Rosen bridge (wormhole) in this diagram is the spatial connection between Regions I and III at T = 0 (the horizontal axis through the origin). This bridge is not traversable — any signal or particle attempting to pass from Region I to Region III must travel faster than light, since the bridge pinches off before a subluminal traveler can cross.
