MATHEMATICAL CONTENT:
A tesseract (8-cell or 4-cube) is the 4-dimensional analog of the cube. It has:
- 16 vertices, 32 edges, 24 faces, 8 cells (cubic faces)
- When projected to 3D (and then to 2D), it appears as a cube within a cube with connecting edges
- The projection to 2D creates a pattern with hexagonal symmetry (since the tesseract's cross-sections can be hexagonal)
- The argument that 60° rotational superposition of a 3-fold symmetric pattern reveals tesseract topology is geometrically non-trivial: the 3-fold blade pattern has D3 symmetry, and superimposing three copies at 60° intervals effectively creates D6 symmetry, whose projection can resemble tesseract cross-sections
