GEOMETRIC ANALYSIS:
A regular octahedron has:
- 8 equilateral triangular faces
- 6 vertices
- 12 edges
- Symmetry group: Oh (octahedral symmetry, order 48)
- Dual polyhedron: cube/hexahedron

The claim that the West Overton 1999 formation encodes the net of an octahedron is based on:
1. The 6 outer elements correspond to the 6 vertices of the octahedron
2. The hexagonal star arrangement mirrors the standard octahedron net topology
3. The Star of David / hexagram base geometry is the projection of an octahedron viewed along a C3 axis
4. When an octahedron is projected along its 3-fold symmetry axis, the 2D silhouette is a regular hexagon with internal triangular divisions — matching the observed formation

PHYSICS/UAP RELEVANCE:
The octahedron is one of the five Platonic solids and appears throughout physics in multiple contexts: it is the fundamental unit of SiO4 tetrahedral/octahedral coordination in crystal chemistry, the basis of the magnetic octahedral crystal field theory (d-orbital splitting in octahedral ligand fields), and appears in nuclear physics as a possible geometry for quark configurations. In sacred geometry traditions, the octahedron represents the "air element" in the classical five-element system. In UAP physics research, the repeated appearance of Platonic solid geometries in crop formations is interpreted as deliberate encoding of fundamental geometric principles — possibly communicating crystallographic, nuclear, or higher-dimensional geometric information. The net encoding is particularly sophisticated: representing a 3D Platonic solid as its 2D unfolded net requires understanding of polyhedral topology and face-adjacency graphs.
