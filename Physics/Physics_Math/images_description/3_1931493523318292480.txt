PHYSICS/UAP RELEVANCE:
Same as 3_1925816180968751104.txt. The mushroom-shaped internal inclusion within the metal sphere is the key anomalous feature. In conventional metallurgy, internal inclusions occur during casting (gas bubbles, slag, dendrite segregation) but these produce: random shapes (not organized mushroom shapes), uniform composition gradients (not a sharply bounded inclusion with different CT density), and no preferred axial orientation. The organized mushroom shape oriented along the sphere's symmetry axis suggests:
1. Either it was deliberately placed/formed during manufacture by a non-conventional process
2. Or it represents a material phase transition or reaction product that occurred after manufacture
The CT-visible density difference between the mushroom inclusion (reddish-brown = lower HU/density) and the surrounding matrix (white = higher HU/density) indicates the inclusion is made of a LESS DENSE material than the surrounding metallic matrix — this is anomalous for typical inclusion types (most inclusions, such as carbides or oxides, are denser than the metal matrix, not less dense).
