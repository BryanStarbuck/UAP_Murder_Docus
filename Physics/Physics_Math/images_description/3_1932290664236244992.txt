- In the model: the helix appears wider and shorter (more compact vertically) than B-DNA

PANEL 2 — B-DNA (center):
- Ball-and-stick model of B-form DNA (the canonical Watson-Crick helix, 1953)
- B-DNA is the predominant form in physiological conditions (aqueous environment, high humidity)
- Key structural parameters: rise per base pair ~3.4 Å, ~10.5 base pairs per turn, helix diameter ~20 Å, major groove wide and deep, minor groove narrow and deep
- The model shows the most slender and elongated of the three helices
- Base pairs are approximately perpendicular to the helix axis (nearly 0° tilt)
- The double helix path is clearly visible with the two backbone strands winding around each other
- This is the "standard" DNA structure known from most textbooks and the X-ray diffraction images of Franklin, Watson, and Crick

PANEL 3 — Z-DNA (rightmost):
- Ball-and-stick model of Z-form DNA (discovered by Alexander Rich et al., 1979)
- Z-DNA is a LEFT-HANDED double helix — the only common left-handed DNA form, and structurally the most distinct from A and B forms
- Key structural parameters: rise per base pair ~3.7 Å, ~12 base pairs per turn, helix diameter ~18 Å, narrow and deep minor groove, almost no major groove
- Z-DNA has a zigzag phosphate backbone path (hence "Z" — the sugar-phosphate backbone traces a zigzag rather than a smooth helical path)
- The model appears narrower and more elongated than B-DNA; the backbone path shows the characteristic zig-zag
- Z-DNA occurs at GC-rich sequences under high salt or negative superhelical stress conditions
