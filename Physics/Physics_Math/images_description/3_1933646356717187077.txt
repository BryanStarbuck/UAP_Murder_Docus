HISTORICAL CONTEXT:
- Otto Hahn (1879-1968) and Lise Meitner worked together at the Kaiser Wilhelm Institute in Berlin for over 30 years
- In 1938, following Meitner's forced flight from Germany to Sweden (due to Nazi antisemitic laws), Hahn continued the experimental work and discovered that uranium bombarded with neutrons produced barium — a result inexplicable by classical physics
- Meitner and her nephew Otto Frisch (working from Sweden) provided the theoretical explanation: the uranium nucleus had split into two nearly-equal parts (fission), with the mass deficit converted to kinetic energy via E=mc²
- In 1944, the Nobel Prize in Chemistry was awarded to Hahn alone — Meitner was omitted, in what is widely considered one of the most significant Nobel Prize injustices in history
