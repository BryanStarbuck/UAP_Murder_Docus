CONTEXT:
This type of ground-level researcher photograph is typical of crop circle investigation documentation from the 1990s. Organizations like the Centre for Crop Circle Studies (CCCS), Circles Phenomenon Research (CPR), and independent researchers would enter formations with measuring equipment to document the geometry and examine the physical characteristics of the flattened crop. The measurements taken included: stalk bending angle, swirl direction, radial deviation from center, stalk node elongation (heating-induced), and magnetic anomalies (using Gauss meters).

PHYSICS/UAP RELEVANCE:
Ground-level examination of crop formations has yielded physical anomalies cited as evidence of non-human formation mechanisms:
1. STALK NODE ELONGATION: Bent stalks in "genuine" formations show elongation of the nodes (the jointed internodes where the plant bends), consistent with rapid steam generation heating the interior of the node. W.C. Levengood (biophysicist) documented this in multiple papers (1994, 1999).
2. CRYSTALLINE CHANGES: Soil samples from formation interiors show changes in crystal structure of clay minerals (Levengood & Talbott, 1999).
3. RADIAL SYMMETRY: The swirl direction and radial geometry are precisely centered, typically within <0.5% of geometric center across 50+ meter formations.
4. MAGNETIC ANOMALIES: Some formations show statistically significant anomalies in Earth's magnetic field measured by portable magnetometers.
