PHYSICS / PHENOMENON:
* Edward Leedskalnin is one of the most significant figures in UAP physics research for several reasons:
  (1) He built Coral Castle using ~1,100 tons of coral rock — moving and placing massive stones (some weighing 30 tons) entirely alone, reportedly at night, without modern machinery, cranes, or observed help. He claimed to know "the secret of the pyramids" — i.e., how ancient peoples moved megaliths without modern machinery.
  (2) The "spinning magnetic flywheel": Leedskalnin's flywheel device is a ring of magnets arranged radially, capable of being spun freely. He wrote a book "Magnetic Current" (1945) and "Cosmic Force" arguing that standard electromagnetic theory is fundamentally wrong — that magnetism (not electricity) is the primary fundamental force, and that "individual north and south pole magnets" (what today would be described as magnetic monopoles or individual flux quanta) are the basis of all physical phenomena.
  (3) The "Permanent Motion Holder": a pair of magnetic cores that Leedskalnin demonstrated could store a circulating current indefinitely when a brief pulse was applied — this is now recognized as a toroidal magnetic core demonstrating magnetic flux retention (flux pinning in soft iron cores). He called it "perpetual motion" — it is not perpetual motion in the thermodynamic sense, but the magnetic state is extremely long-lived.
  (4) This image argues that non-human intelligence (ET crop artists) drew crop formations in fields specifically referencing Leedskalnin's inventions — suggesting that the ETs are endorsing or validating Leedskalnin's physics as the correct path for understanding UAP propulsion.
* The connection to UAP physics: Leedskalnin's claims about magnetic monopole-like "individual pole magnets" and the primacy of magnetic forces resonate with:
  (a) Paul Dirac's theoretical prediction of magnetic monopoles (1931).
  (b) Modern research on magnetic vortices (skyrmions) in thin magnetic films, where localized quantized magnetic pole-like structures do exist.
  (c) The theoretical model of UAP propulsion using rotating monopolar or dipolar magnetic fields to generate gravitomagnetic thrust.
