PHYSICS / PHENOMENON:
* This visualization depicts the magnetic field and coil geometry of a conical solenoid / rotating magnetic core system. The physical interpretation:
  (1) Rotating magnetic core (red cylinder): a ferromagnetic or superconducting cylinder rotating at angular velocity ω about its symmetry axis. By the Barnett effect (a rotating ferromagnet acquires magnetization parallel to its rotation axis, Mz ∝ ω), or by the London moment in a rotating superconductor (B_London = 2mω/e), the rotating cylinder spontaneously develops an axial magnetic field B_axial. This is a purely mechanical origin of a magnetic field — no current needed.
  (2) Conical helical coil (green): a coil wound in a conical/funnel shape, possibly a gradient coil for spatial field shaping. The conical geometry creates a non-uniform magnetic field (∂B_z/∂z ≠ 0), which generates a gradient force on magnetic dipoles along the axis (F_z = μ_z ∂B_z/∂z). This is the principle of magnetic gradient propulsion — objects with magnetic moment experience a net force along the gradient direction.
  (3) Toroidal-to-poloidal field coupling: the coil's conical-toroidal geometry couples poloidal current flow (up and around the cone surface) to a toroidal magnetic field component at large radius (the coil at the bottom creates a nearly toroidal field geometry). Combined with the axial field of the central cylinder, the total field has both toroidal (azimuthal) and poloidal (meridional plane) components — a tokamak-like field geometry.
  (4) Lense-Thirring / gravitomagnetic interpretation: in the context of UAP physics, the rotating red cylinder represents a rapidly rotating mass (the UAP disc). By the Lense-Thirring effect, a rotating mass generates a gravitomagnetic field B_g = (2G/c²) × (J/r³) (at r >> disc radius), where J is the angular momentum. The yellow arrows represent this gravitomagnetic field pointing along the rotation axis — exactly analogous to the magnetic field of a magnetic dipole. The conical coil then represents the trajectory of test particles (or the spatial distribution of frame-dragging effects) around the rotating mass.
  (5) UAP propulsion mechanism: the combination of a rotating magnetic core (red cylinder) + conical gradient coil (green) creates an axially directed magnetic gradient force. If the UAP disc is the "rotating magnetic core" (Norman Paulsen: counter-rotating magnetic discs) and the surrounding space is the "coil" (field lines), then propulsion occurs via F_z = μ_z ∂B_z/∂z — the disc is effectively a magnetic dipole in its own field gradient. The blue rotation arrow indicates the spin direction that generates the upward force (counterclockwise from above → field points up → gradient force up if the bottom field is weaker → net upward thrust).
  (6) Faraday homopolar generator geometry: the conical coil is topologically equivalent to a stack of circular current loops decreasing in radius toward the top — identical to the geometry of an axial Faraday disc homopolar generator, where current flows radially outward (or inward) in rotating conducting disc. The axial magnetic field threading the disc is generated by the same field topology shown here.
