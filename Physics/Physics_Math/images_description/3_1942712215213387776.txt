PHYSICS CONCEPTS:
* This is an aerial photograph of the famous Barbury Castle crop circle formation of July 1991, Wiltshire, England.
* The formation consists of:
  - A large central nested-ring complex (equilateral triangle with circles at each vertex).
  - Three satellite circles connected by straight lines radiating at 120-degree intervals (triskelion/triquetra geometry).
  - The right satellite has a pinwheel/rotor pattern; the lower-left has a maze-like nested ring with offset inner elements; the upper-left has overlapping circles.
* The Barbury Castle 1991 formation has been mathematically analyzed as encoding:
  - Ratchet spiral geometry (related to the Fibonacci/golden ratio spiral).
  - Tetrahedral geometry projected onto the ground plane.
  - A schematic of a three-phase electromagnetic system.
  - Various interpretations involving the value of pi, harmonic resonance, and geometric symmetry.
* The three satellite circles have been interpreted as representing different rotational states or phases of a single field system.

HIGH-LEVEL SUMMARY:
This is the well-known Barbury Castle crop circle formation, Wiltshire, England, July 17, 1991 — one of the most studied and mathematically complex crop formations of the modern crop circle era. At the PhD physics level, this formation is analyzed for its geometric encoding of tetrahedral symmetry, ratchet spiral geometry, and potential three-phase field diagram properties. The three satellite circles with distinct internal patterns (double-circle/vesica, pinwheel/rotor, and nested-ring-with-offset-body) have been interpreted as representing three distinct physical states, phases, or modes of a rotating electromagnetic system. The formation is frequently cited in UAP physics literature as a potential externally-communicated schematic of a propulsion or energy generation system operating on rotational field principles.
