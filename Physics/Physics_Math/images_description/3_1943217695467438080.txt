* CIRCUIT BOARD GEOMETRY:
  * The nested rectangle frame = IC chip package border (quad flat package, QFP, or DIP analogy)
  * The interior dot grid = silicon die contact grid (ball grid array, BGA, or wire bond pad array)
  * The peripheral pin elements = IC lead frame pins / I/O connections
  * If interpreted as a DRAM memory array: the dot grid encodes an address/data matrix; if as a microprocessor: the die shows functional block regions
* BINARY ENCODING OF THE DOT GRID:

    * Dark standing-crop circles = 1; flattened crop (no circle) = 0
    * Rows × columns of the grid define a binary matrix
    * If the California formation has the same grid dimensions as the UK formation: ~80-140 bits of encoded data
  * The two formations together could encode a longer binary message (concatenated) or the same message (redundant verification)
* AERIAL PHOTOGRAPHY LOGISTICS:

  * US crop formations are less common than UK formations but do occur (Chualar, CA 2013 braille formation is the most famous US example)
* CHUALAR CA 2013 CONNECTION:
  * The most famous US chip-like crop formation appeared near Chualar, California in December 2013

  * The Chualar formation was later claimed by a marketing company, but the binary encoding was real regardless of origin

HIGH-LEVEL SUMMARY:
Oblique aerial photograph (©111th Aerial Photography & Video) of a rectangular circuit board / computer chip crop formation in a vivid green irrigated California agricultural field, with dry brown mountains visible in the background. The formation consists of: outer rectangular flattened border, inner nested border frame, interior regular dot/circle grid of standing crop elements, and peripheral pin-like projections — identical morphology to the UK chip formation (3_1941827587057111040) but at a distinct California location, likely the Salinas Valley or Central Valley. The California dry-mountain landscape and professional aerial credit distinguish this from all UK formations. Two independent geographically-separated formations with the same circuit board morphology suggests intentional standardized design. The dot grid is interpretable as binary data (standing crop = 1, flattened = 0) encoding up to ~80-140 bits; the most famous US analogue is the Chualar CA 2013 braille formation encoding "192." See also 3_1943217732779905024 for close-up interior detail of this formation.
