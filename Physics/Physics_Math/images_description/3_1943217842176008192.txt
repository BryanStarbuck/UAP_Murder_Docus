PHYSICS / CONCEPTS:
* Braille as tactile binary encoding — direct analog to crop formation dot-matrix encoding:
  1. Braille alphabet is a 6-bit binary encoding system: each character defined by a 2x3 grid of 6 positions, each either raised (1) or flat (0). This gives 2^6 = 64 possible combinations, sufficient to encode all letters, numbers, and punctuation.
  2. Direct parallel to crop formation binary encoding: the Crabwood 2002 formation used a dot-matrix binary grid where standing crop tufts = 1 and flat crop = 0 — exactly as Braille uses raised dots = 1 and flat = 0. The Braille photograph validates the binary encoding interpretation.
  3. Information density: Braille ~75,000 bits/m² (6000 bits per standard 11"x11.5" page); Crabwood formation ~0.4 bits/m² (1500 bits over ~3600 m²). Both are spatial binary codes scanned row by row.
