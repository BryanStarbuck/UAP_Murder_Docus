PHYSICS / INFORMATION CONCEPTS:
* COMET SHOEMAKER-LEVY 9 (SL9) IMPACT — HISTORICAL BACKGROUND:
  * Comet D/1993 F2 (Shoemaker-Levy 9) was discovered on March 24, 1993 by Carolyn and Gene Shoemaker and David Levy
  * The comet had been tidally disrupted by Jupiter during a close pass on July 7, 1992, fragmenting into ~21 pieces (labeled A through W)
  * The fragments impacted Jupiter between July 16-22, 1994 — the first directly observed cometary impact on a solar system planet
  * Fragment G (one of the largest): impacted July 18, 1994 at approximately 60 km/s; impact energy estimated at ~6 × 10²⁶ J (approximately 6 million megatons TNT equivalent = 600× the world's nuclear arsenal)
  * The "G" scar: a dark spot approximately the size of Earth (diameter ~13,000 km) visible in the Jovian atmosphere; the scar persisted for weeks
* IMPACT SCAR PHYSICS:
  * The dark oval scar: vaporized bolide material + excavated/upwelled Jovian atmospheric material deposited at high altitude
  * The scar color: dark because of carbon-rich material from the comet nucleus + photochemical reactions in the Jovian stratosphere
  * The scar morphology: elongated (not circular) because the impact angle was oblique (~45° from horizontal) rather than vertical
  * Impact wave: acoustic-gravity waves propagated around Jupiter's atmosphere after each impact, detectable as oscillations in atmospheric bands
* CROP FORMATION RESPONSE:
  * The claim: the Avebury area crop formation (July 26, 1994 — 8 days after the G fragment impact) precisely replicates the morphology of the impact scar as seen in astronomical photographs
  * The G impact scar: dark oval + brighter ring at one edge + possible ejecta streak = matches the crop circle's spiral ring + attached curved arm
  * If genuine ETI response: an intelligence monitoring both Jupiter and Earth's crop fields responded within 8 days by encoding the impact scar geometry in an English grain field
  * This is one of the most striking temporal coincidences in crop circle research: an astronomical event → a physically distant crop formation with matching geometry, within 8 days
