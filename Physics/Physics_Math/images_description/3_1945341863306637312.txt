* BIOLOGICAL THREE-BODY PROBLEM:
  * Three fish maintaining a rotating circular formation requires:
    * Hydrodynamic wake interaction: each fish is swimming partially in the wake of the preceding fish — reducing drag
    * Wake-mediated school behavior: schooling fish use lateral line receptors to detect pressure waves from neighbors; three fish in a rotating ring would mutually synchronize through hydrodynamic coupling
    * This is analogous to a three-body gravitational orbit but with dissipative coupling (hydrodynamic drag) instead of conservative (gravitational) coupling
* HYDRODYNAMICS OF FISH SCHOOLING:
  * A fish swimming at velocity v experiences: drag D = ½ρC_D A v², thrust T from tail oscillation (Strouhal number St = fA/v ≈ 0.25-0.35 for efficient fish swimming)

  * Three fish in a rotating circular formation: the wake of each fish partially benefits the following fish, creating a self-sustaining rotating stable configuration
