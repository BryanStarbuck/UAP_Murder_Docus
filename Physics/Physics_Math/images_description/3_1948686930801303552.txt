The first four are Maxwell's equations in differential form (Gauss's law for electricity, Gauss's law for magnetism, Faraday's law, Ampère-Maxwell law). The fifth is the time-dependent Schrödinger equation.

EQUATIONS INTERPRETATION:
- Maxwell's equations describe classical electromagnetism: ∇·D = ρ (free charge density sources electric displacement), ∇·B = 0 (no magnetic monopoles), ∇×E = -∂B/∂t (changing B induces E — Faraday induction), ∇×H = J + ∂D/∂t (currents and changing E sources magnetic field — Ampère with displacement current)
- Schrödinger equation: H(t)|ψ(t)⟩ = iℏ d/dt |ψ(t)⟩ — the fundamental equation of quantum mechanics describing time evolution of quantum state |ψ⟩ under Hamiltonian H
