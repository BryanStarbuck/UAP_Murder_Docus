BIOLOGICAL SIGNIFICANCE:
- Thymine is one of the four nucleobases in DNA (along with adenine, guanine, cytosine)
- In RNA, thymine is replaced by uracil (thymine without the methyl group)
- In DNA, thymine always base-pairs with adenine (A–T Watson-Crick base pair) via two hydrogen bonds:
  - N3–H of thymine (donor) → N1 of adenine (acceptor)
  - C4=O of thymine (acceptor) ← N6–H of adenine (donor)
- The methyl group at C5 is added post-synthesis by DNA methyltransferases from uracil in some organisms, or is incorporated directly during DNA synthesis
- Thymine was the base used in the Arecibo Message (1974) to represent DNA in the encoded description of humanity — making it relevant to the Arecibo/Chilbolton crop circle research context
