PHYSICS CONCEPTS:
* APPARATUS IDENTIFICATION — PLANAR PLASMA DISCHARGE DEVICE:
  * The flat disc geometry suggests this is a planar (2D) plasma discharge rather than a cylindrical volume discharge
  * In a planar discharge: two electrodes on the same horizontal plane (split by the black insulating gap); voltage applied between them creates a discharge that propagates along the surface between the electrodes
  * This type of discharge is used in: surface dielectric barrier discharge (DBD) actuators (aerodynamic flow control), planar plasma reactors for semiconductor processing, and UAP-physics research for "disc-shaped plasma vortex" demonstration
* SURFACE DISCHARGE PHYSICS:
  * A surface dielectric barrier discharge (SDBD) operates at atmospheric pressure (no vacuum required)
  * The discharge is sustained by a dielectric layer between the two electrodes (prevents spark-to-arc transition, maintains stable plasma)
  * The induced plasma creates an ionic wind: charged ions in the plasma experience the electric field force F = qE and transfer momentum to neutral air via collisions, creating a net airflow (the "ionic wind" or electrohydrodynamic — EHD — actuator)
  * EHD thrust: F_EHD = ρ_c × E = ne(E) × E, where ρ_c is the space charge density and E the electric field; typical EHD thrust: 1-10 N/m² of electrode area
  * This is directly relevant to UAP disc propulsion: a large-area EHD plasma actuator on the underside of a disc-shaped craft could create a downward ionic wind, generating thrust without moving parts
* SPIRAL MODE IN PLANAR DISCHARGE:
  * The appearance of the spiral plasma mode (as seen in the companion top-down view) in a planar device is notable: it requires a spontaneous symmetry breaking from C∞ (circular) to C2 (two-arm spiral)
  * This symmetry breaking could be driven by: an external magnetic field (B_z ≠ 0), a small asymmetry in the electrode gap width, or a nonlinear plasma instability (the Simon-Hoh instability or the diocotron instability in planar geometry)
  * The diocotron instability: occurs in a thin sheet of plasma with velocity shear; growth rate γ = kΔv/2 where k is the wave number of the perturbation and Δv the velocity difference across the shear layer
* HIGH-VOLTAGE PLASMA GENERATION:
  * The orange cable and alligator clip connection suggest a DC high-voltage supply (1-30 kV range typical for atmospheric plasma)
  * For the spiral plasma to be stable and visible: the discharge must be in the glow discharge regime (not spark/arc), requiring: current density J ≈ 0.1-10 mA/cm², voltage V ≈ 1-10 kV, at pressures p = 0.01-1 atm
  * The power input: P = V × I; for a glow discharge at 5 kV, 100 mA: P = 500 W — consistent with the bright plasma visible in the image
