PHYSICS CONCEPTS:
* COMETARY FADING AFTER OUTBURST:
  * After the peak of the Holmes outburst (October 24, 2007, magnitude ~2.8), the comet faded as the dust and gas expanded and diluted
  * The surface brightness I(ρ, t) of the expanding coma evolves as: I(ρ, t) ∝ Q(t) / (v_expansion × ρ) where Q(t) = production rate (decreasing after outburst) and ρ = projected angular radius
  * The coma radius expanded at ~0.5 km/s → at 1 week after outburst: radius ≈ 0.5 × 7 × 86400 km ≈ 302,000 km ≈ 2 × 10^5 km
  * At Holmes distance 1.6 AU: 302,000 km / (1.6 × 1.5 × 10^8 km/AU) × 206265 arcsec/rad ≈ 259 arcsec ≈ 4.3 arcmin — a very extended coma
* BACKGROUND INFRARED SOURCES:
  * The orange-yellow points scattered in the background are real astrophysical sources (background stars, galaxies) detected at mid-infrared wavelengths
  * At 3.6 μm (Spitzer IRAC Channel 1): background stellar contamination from evolved red giant stars (RGB, AGB) which are bright in the near-infrared
  * Background galaxy contamination at mid-IR: starforming galaxies with warm dust emission at λ_rest ≈ 3-10 μm redshifted to the observed wavelength
* COSMIC RAY REJECTION:
  * The dark spots in the background: in stacked astronomical images, cosmic rays appearing as hot pixels in individual frames are usually removed by median-stacking multiple frames; residual cosmic ray hits may appear as slightly dark or anomalous pixels in the stacked image
  * Cosmic ray flux at low Earth orbit (Spitzer LEO to L2 orbit): ~10 hits/cm²/hour for protons above 10 MeV; at L2 (Herschel), the rate is higher due to reduced Earth magnetic shielding
