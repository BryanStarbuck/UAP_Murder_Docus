PHYSICS / INFORMATION CONCEPTS:
* EXTREME INNER COMA ZOOM WITH CENTROID CROSSHAIR:
  * This is the same type of image as 3_1960140859267223552 — a heavily zoomed inner coma region with the entire frame filled by the central coma emission
  * The bright green crosshair marks the astrometric centroid of the nucleus — the point used for position measurement and orbit determination
  * Centroid calculation: x_c = Σ(I_i x_i) / ΣI_i (intensity-weighted mean position in x and y separately)
  * The accuracy of the centroid determines the precision of the astrometric position: for a Gaussian PSF, centroid error ≈ FWHM / (2.35 × S/N)
  * For an interstellar object (3I/ATLAS): precise astrometry is critical to determine the hyperbolic orbit and confirm interstellar origin (eccentricity e >> 1)
* HYPERBOLIC ORBIT CONFIRMATION:
  * Cometary orbit determination requires ≥3 positions at different times
  * Hyperbolic vs. parabolic vs. elliptic: e = √(1 + 2EL²/(μ²m³)) where E = total energy, L = angular momentum
  * For 1I/'Oumuamua: e = 1.20 ± 0.02 (confirmed hyperbolic)
  * For 2I/Borisov: e ≈ 3.36 (strongly hyperbolic)
  * For 3I/ATLAS (if confirmed): e >> 1 required
* NOISE ANALYSIS:
  * The extreme noise (dark and bright pixels) in the orange background is:
    * Shot noise: σ_shot = √N (Poisson statistics, N = detected photons)
    * Read noise: σ_read (detector amplifier noise, typically 2-10 electrons)
    * Dark current: σ_dark (thermal electron generation, reduced by cooling)
    * Total noise: σ_total = √(σ_shot² + σ_read² + σ_dark²)
  * At extreme zoom (many display pixels per detector pixel), the individual photon-counting statistics are visible
* POSSIBLE SECONDARY SOURCE:
  * The small bright spot slightly above-left of the nucleus could be:
    * Cosmic ray event (single pixel, very bright — most likely)
    * Background star (possible, would appear as a point source)
    * Secondary cometary nucleus (relevant for split comets, less likely)
  * Cosmic ray identification: a single pixel that is many sigma above the local background, with no neighboring pixel enhancement — the standard definition
