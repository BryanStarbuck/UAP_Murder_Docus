PHYSICS CONCEPTS:
* MULTIPLE BEAM DIFFRACTION / DIFFRACTION GRATING OUTPUT:
  * The fan of multiple beams at different angles is characteristic of a DIFFRACTION GRATING output:
    * A reflection or transmission grating produces multiple orders of diffraction: m = 0, ±1, ±2, ...
    * The grating equation: d(sin θ_in + sin θ_m) = mλ where d = grating period, θ_in = angle of incidence, θ_m = angle of m-th order diffraction, λ = wavelength
    * Each visible beam corresponds to one diffraction order: the central beam = zeroth order (specular), upper/lower = ±1st, ±2nd, etc.
  * Alternative: a Fabry-Perot etalon producing transmission fringes at specific angles
* ALTERNATIVELY — SPHERICAL ABERRATION / ZONE PLATE FOCUSING:
  * If the element is a ZONE PLATE rather than a simple lens: zone plates produce multiple focal lengths at f/n² (n=1,2,3...), which can produce multiple bright spots along the optical axis
  * But the fan geometry (multiple angular beams, not axial spots) is more consistent with grating diffraction
* ALTERNATIVELY — TOTAL INTERNAL REFLECTION + LEAKAGE:
  * A thick glass element with internal reflections could produce multiple exit beams through frustrated total internal reflection at different angles
* SINGLE-SLIT / MULTI-SLIT DIFFRACTION:
  * If the element is a multi-slit aperture (not a lens): the Fraunhofer diffraction pattern from N slits gives principal maxima at: sin θ_m = mλ/d
  * Intensity envelope: I(θ) = I₀ × [sin(πa sinθ/λ)/(πa sinθ/λ)]² × [sin(Nπd sinθ/λ)/sin(πd sinθ/λ)]²
  * Where a = single slit width, d = slit separation, N = number of slits
