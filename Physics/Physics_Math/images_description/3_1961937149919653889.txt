PHYSICS CONCEPTS:
* MARTIAN MOON COMPOSITION AND ORIGIN:
  * Both moons have albedo and spectral type consistent with D-type or C-type carbonaceous asteroids (or extinct cometary nuclei)
  * Spectral slope in VNIR: S' ≈ 5-15%/1000Å for Phobos (D-type features)
  * Origin hypotheses:
    1. Captured asteroid hypothesis: Phobos and Deimos are Outer Main Belt or Trojan asteroids captured into Martian orbit via three-body gravitational interaction
    2. Co-accretion hypothesis: formed in place from the same nebular disk as Mars (problematic: current orbits are nearly circular and equatorial, unlike captured objects)
    3. Giant impact hypothesis: debris from a large impact on Mars assembled into the moons (similar to Earth's Moon formation)
  * Mars Moons eXploration (MMX) mission (JAXA, 2024 launch): will return samples of Phobos to determine origin
* TIDAL FORCES ON PHOBOS — ROCHE LIMIT:
  * Phobos orbits Mars at only 9,376 km (semi-major axis), well inside the synchronous orbit (20,427 km) and approaching the Roche limit:
  * Roche limit: d_Roche = R_Mars × (2 × ρ_Mars/ρ_Phobos)^(1/3)
  * ρ_Mars = 3.93 g/cm³; ρ_Phobos ≈ 1.87 g/cm³ (very porous interior: ~25-45% macroporosity)
  * d_Roche ≈ 3389km × (2 × 3.93/1.87)^(1/3) ≈ 3389 × 2.00^(1/3) ≈ 3389 × 1.26 ≈ 4,270 km (for fluid body)
  * For a rigid body: d_Roche_rigid = R_Mars × (ρ_Mars/ρ_Phobos)^(1/3) × 1.26 ≈ 7,200 km
  * Phobos at 9,376 km is OUTSIDE the rigid-body Roche limit but approaching it
  * Consequence: Phobos is spiraling inward due to tidal dissipation, with infall rate ~1.8 cm/year; will cross the Roche limit and disintegrate in approximately 30-50 million years
* STICKNEY GROOVES — TIDAL FRACTURE:
  * The parallel grooves on Phobos surface: generated by tidal stresses from Mars as Phobos orbits
  * Tidal stress: σ_tidal ≈ 3 G M_Mars ρ_Phobos R_Phobos / d³ where d = orbital distance
  * At d = 9,376 km: σ_tidal ≈ 3 × 4.3×10⁻³ N/m² (small but sufficient to crack a porous body)
  * Alternative: crater chains from ejecta launched from Phobos at low escape velocity (v_escape ≈ 11 m/s for Phobos)
* DEIMOS SMOOTHNESS — REGOLITH REDISTRIBUTION:
  * Deimos is significantly smoother than Phobos: the regolith (fine ejecta) settles back due to Deimos's gravity
  * Deimos escape velocity: v_escape ≈ 5.6 m/s
  * Crater retention: small craters are buried by regolith infill; only larger craters remain visible
  * This contrasts with Phobos: its grooves suggest the regolith is mobilized by tidal stresses, preventing smooth crater infill
* UAP PHYSICS CONNECTION:
  * Phobos anomaly: Soviet Phobos 2 spacecraft (1989) observed anomalous shadows and a large "object" near Phobos before contact was lost — this became a point of speculation about artificial structures
  * Monolith on Phobos: a 90m tall erratic boulder on Phobos has been discussed as anomalous
  * Deimos and Phobos as potential UAP staging bases: their low gravity and position near Mars make them logistically ideal for non-human operations in the inner solar system
