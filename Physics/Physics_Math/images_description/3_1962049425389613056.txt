* NICKEL TETRACARBONYL Ni(CO)₄ — STRUCTURE AND PROPERTIES:
  * Chemical formula: Ni(CO)₄ (nickel tetracarbonyl)

  * Point group symmetry: T_d (full tetrahedral symmetry)
  * Ni oxidation state: Ni(0) — nickel in zero oxidation state
  * Ni electron configuration: [Ar]3d¹⁰ in Ni(0) → 18-electron complex (filled d shell)
* BONDING — METAL CARBONYL CHEMISTRY:
  * CO ligand bonding to Ni: SYNERGIC BONDING (Dewar-Chatt-Duncanson model):
    * σ-DONATION: CO donates electron density from its HOMO (5σ orbital, carbon lone pair) into empty Ni d orbitals
    * π-BACKBONDING: Ni donates electron density from filled d_π orbitals into CO LUMO (2π* antibonding orbital)
    * The π-backbonding WEAKENS the C≡O triple bond: ν_CO decreases from 2143 cm⁻¹ (free CO) to ~2000-2060 cm⁻¹ in Ni(CO)₄
  * IR spectrum: Ni(CO)₄ shows one strong T₂ symmetry CO stretching mode at 2057 cm⁻¹ (T_d has only one IR-active CO stretch of T₂ symmetry, not the A₁ mode at 2060 cm⁻¹ which is Raman-active only)
* MOND PROCESS — INDUSTRIAL CHEMISTRY:
  * The Mond Process (Ludwig Mond, 1890): Ni + 4CO → Ni(CO)₄ (at 50-60°C); then Ni(CO)₄ → Ni + 4CO (at 220-230°C)
  * Used industrially to purify nickel to >99.99% purity from impure alloys
  * This is also the mechanism of NICKEL CARBONYL TOXICITY: Ni(CO)₄ is an extremely toxic volatile liquid (bp 43°C); inhalation → CO releases in lungs → tissue damage; nickel deposits in lungs and brain
