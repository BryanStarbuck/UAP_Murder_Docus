* ISOPHOTE ANALYSIS AND COMA MORPHOLOGY:
  * Inner concentric isophotes (tight, elliptical): represent the steady surface-brightness gradient around the nucleus
    * Surface brightness profile: I(ρ) = I₀ × (ρ/ρ₀)^(-k) where k ≈ 1.0 for steady-state, k > 1.0 for outburst (rapidly fading outer coma)
    * The close spacing of inner isophotes = rapid brightening toward nucleus → steep k value
  * Outer teardrop isophote: the single outer contour at the detection threshold (~3σ above sky) shows the full coma extent
    * The teardrop shape: LEFT extension >> RIGHT extension from nucleus
    * This asymmetry is the DUST TAIL: dust grains pushed anti-solar by radiation pressure
    * Syndyne/synchrone analysis: grains ejected at different times (synchrones) and with different β = F_rad/F_grav (syndynes) form distinct features in the outer coma
    * The teardrop outer isophote = the envelope of all synchrones for 0 < t_ejection < t_observation (all grains ejected since outburst)
* TAIL DIRECTION AND SOLAR WIND:
  * The elongation of the outer teardrop to the LEFT: anti-solar direction projected on sky
  * Comet Holmes in October-November 2007: at heliocentric longitude λ ≈ 50° from Earth's perspective
  * Anti-solar direction in the image coordinate: LEFT (west on the sky) → Holmes's velocity vector is approximately perpendicular to the anti-solar direction (since Holmes was near its maximum distance from the Sun along the orbit axis)
  * Ion tail direction (not shown): would be slightly different from dust tail due to solar wind aberration (tan(α) = V_orbit_comet / V_solar_wind ≈ 20 km/s / 400 km/s ≈ 0.05 rad ≈ 3°)
