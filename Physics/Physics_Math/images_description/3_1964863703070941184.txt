PHYSICS/SCIENCE RELEVANCE:
1. The rhetorical question "Not for a homopolar motor?" challenges the reader to consider whether Newton's Third Law applies to a homopolar motor — or whether it somehow does not.
2. Standard physics position: All motors obey Newton's Third Law. When a homopolar motor exerts a Lorentz force on the current-carrying conductor (action), there is an equal and opposite force on the magnetic field source — so if the magnets are fixed to the craft, the craft experiences the reaction force. No violation of Newton's Third Law is proposed here.
3. UAP propulsion claim: Some researchers claim that a homopolar motor driving current through a superconducting or plasma medium can create a situation where the "reaction force" is transferred to the electromagnetic field (which propagates away at the speed of light rather than pushing back on the device). This would make it appear that the device violates Newton's Third Law while actually conserving momentum in the full electromagnetic field.
4. This is directly related to the Abraham-Minkowski controversy in electrodynamics: the question of how electromagnetic momentum is partitioned between field and matter. If the field carries away momentum, a device could appear to generate thrust without equal reaction — analogous to a photon rocket or an EmDrive-type device.
5. Alternatively, the question may be making the opposite point: that a homopolar motor powered by an internal energy source DOES obey Newton's Third Law (the rotating disc pushes on the magnetic field, the magnetic field pushes back on the disc/motor housing), and this IS the mechanism for thrust — no violation needed.
6. The boat analogy is apt: a boat propeller pushes water backward (action), the reaction pushes the boat forward. A homopolar motor in plasma might push the plasma medium backward (action), propelling the craft forward (reaction) — this is the MHD (magnetohydrodynamic) drive principle.
