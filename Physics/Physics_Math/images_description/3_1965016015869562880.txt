PHYSICS/SCIENCE RELEVANCE:
1. The distinctly different core color (cyan rather than white) combined with the dark central void suggests this frame captures the TOROIDAL phase of the plasma: a toroidal plasma has a ring of luminous gas surrounding a dark (low-density) central axis — exactly what the cyan ring + dark void structure shows
2. A toroidal plasma (donut-shaped) is the most stable confined plasma geometry — it is the basis of the tokamak fusion reactor and is considered one of the most likely geometric configurations for a plasmoid UAP
3. The cyan color emission (~480-490nm wavelength) is characteristic of: ionized oxygen (OII lines at 500nm), ionized carbon (CIII at 464nm), or helium (HeII at 468nm) — all possible components of a high-energy atmospheric or exotic plasma
4. The color evolution between frames (warm white in earlier frames → cyan in this frame) could represent temporal evolution of the plasma: initial ignition (hot white) cooling to a stable toroidal configuration (cyan ring + dark central void)
5. The persistence of the satellite light clusters (same positions and colors as in companion images) confirms these are real light sources that remain stationary relative to the main glow — they are not noise
