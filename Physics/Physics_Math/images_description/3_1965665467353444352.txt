PHYSICS/SCIENCE RELEVANCE:
1. This formation represents the most complete "circuit diagram" in the homopolar motor crop circle series: it shows not just the motor disc but the complete assembly: top power terminal (element 1), three-phase input (element 2 = trident), motor with ~16 brush contacts (element 3), return path disc (element 4), flexible brush connection (element 5), and output terminal (element 6)
2. The curl elements on each satellite stem are the key new feature: in electrical engineering, a curl or coiled symbol represents a spring (the spring-loaded brush that presses against the rotating commutator). This indicates each satellite brush contact has a spring-loaded mechanism — exactly how real electrical brushes work in motors and generators
3. The sixteen satellite brushes (twice the ten-brush formation in 3_1965665449406009345.jpg) continue the series of escalating brush counts: 1 → 2 → 10 → 16. A 16-brush motor is symmetrically compatible with a 4-phase or 8-phase system (16/4 = 4 brushes per phase, or 16/8 = 2 brushes per phase)
4. The trident at the top is a three-pronged input — consistent with a three-phase AC input to the motor. The three-phase current is rectified or commutated into DC to drive the homopolar rotor.
5. The complete vertical assembly (top terminal → input coupler → motor → return coupler → output terminal) describes a fully closed circuit homopolar motor — the current path being: top terminal → through trident → radially through rotating disc → through 16 brushes → down through bottom disc → curl → output terminal
6. This is the most functionally detailed formation in the series and suggests the most advanced technical knowledge: not just the concept of a homopolar motor, but the specific engineering implementation details (spring-loaded brushes, three-phase input, proper terminal connections)
