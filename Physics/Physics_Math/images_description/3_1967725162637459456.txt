PHYSICS/SCIENCE RELEVANCE:
1. BINARY ASCII STEGANOGRAPHY: The Poirino 2011 formation (like the formations documented in 3_1965924435929739268.jpg) encodes a message in binary ASCII — the satellite circles represent binary digits, and when decoded give the name "Ea Enki" in English ASCII characters. This is a second confirmed use of binary ASCII encoding in crop circles (the first was the 2002 Crabwood formation encoding "Beware the bearers of FALSE gifts & their BROKEN PROMISES")
2. "EA ENKI SPACE": The decoded text explicitly names the Sumerian deity Ea Enki — this is presented as the entity signing its work. The "space" at the end may be a formatting character, continuation indicator, or separator between messages
3. POIRINO 2010-2011 SEQUENCE: The title states this is "a continuation of the message from Poirino 2010" — implying a deliberate multi-year message sequence at the same location. The 2010 Poirino formation also encoded binary data (reportedly encoding the mathematical constants π, φ, and e, or Nikola Tesla's name, depending on the researcher's analysis)
4. 7-POINTED STARBURST: The 7 blades of the starburst formation have been interpreted as: 7 classical planets of antiquity (Sun, Moon, Mars, Mercury, Jupiter, Venus, Saturn), 7 Anunnaki senior deities, or 7 musical notes — consistent with the Sumerian cosmology of Ea Enki's domain. Alternatively, 7 could encode 7-bit ASCII (the original 128-character ASCII standard is 7-bit)
5. CONNECTION TO EA ENKI CROP CIRCLE SERIES: This image is part of a series identifying Ea Enki as the creator of multiple crop circles: East Field 2007 (lunar crescent signature), Crooked Soley 2002 (DNA encoding), Poirino 2011 (binary name signature). The consistent attribution to a single entity across multiple formations and multiple years is the core of the ancient astronaut crop circle thesis in this collection
6. ITALIAN LOCATION: The Poirino formations near Turin, Italy are notable because they appeared outside the traditional Wiltshire, UK concentration of complex formations — suggesting the source is not confined to one geographic area (not human creators from Wiltshire)
