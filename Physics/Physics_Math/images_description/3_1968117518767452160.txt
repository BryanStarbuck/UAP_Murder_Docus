PHYSICS/SCIENCE RELEVANCE:
1. DNA DENATURATION PHYSICS: The melting temperature T_m of double-stranded DNA depends on: GC content (G-C base pairs have 3 hydrogen bonds, A-T have 2, so GC-rich DNA has higher T_m), salt concentration, pH, and DNA length. The van't Hoff equation and the nearest-neighbor thermodynamic model describe the cooperative denaturation transition (sigmoidal melting curve: fraction denatured vs. temperature). For a typical DNA: T_m ≈ 69.3 + 0.41(%GC) °C (in 1M NaCl)
2. DNA DENATURATION IN UAP CONTEXT: The thermal denaturation of DNA relates to the collection's theme of UAP radiation effects on biological matter: UAP encounters documented in this collection (Cash-Landrum 1980, Falcon Lake 1967) involve intense electromagnetic/thermal radiation exposure. Radiation-induced DNA damage includes: direct strand breaks, thermal denaturation at local hotspots, and indirect effects via reactive oxygen species (ROS). The image may be included to show what happens to human DNA under intense EM/thermal UAP radiation exposure
3. SINGLE-STRANDED DNA (ssDNA): After denaturation, the two single strands retain their complementary sequences. If cooled slowly (annealing), they re-hybridize (re-nature). This is the basis of PCR (polymerase chain reaction) — heat to denature, cool to anneal primers. The Eya-Enki/genetic engineering thesis connects here: PCR-like processes at the molecular level would be required for genetic engineering of human DNA
