1. SIX-PHASE HOMOPOLAR MOTOR: The six-fold symmetry (C6) encodes a six-phase symmetric electrical system. In a six-phase homopolar motor: the current in each arm is offset by 60° in phase from adjacent arms, and the resultant rotating magnetic field rotates at the input frequency. Six-phase systems are used in high-power electric machines because they have lower torque ripple than three-phase systems (the torque ripple frequency is 6× the rotation frequency rather than 3×)

If three arms have red (one direction) and three alternate arms have blue/opposite, this encodes a balanced three-phase (2-arm pairs) or six-phase system where adjacent arm currents flow in opposite directions — consistent with the alternating-phase brush contact geometry required for maximum motor efficiency

4. SNOWFLAKE/HEXAGONAL SYMMETRY: C6 symmetry is the symmetry of the honeycomb lattice (graphene, hexagonal crystal packing) and of the six-arm snowflake. In electromagnetic devices: six-fold symmetry produces a perfectly balanced rotating field with zero net radial force on the rotor (all radial forces cancel by symmetry)
5. OUTER CONNECTING ARCS: The small circles between arm tips form a ring at the outer boundary — this is the commutator ring or slip ring of the motor, connecting all six arm terminations in a ring circuit
