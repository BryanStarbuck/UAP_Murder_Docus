PHYSICS/SCIENCE RELEVANCE:
1. 8-BRUSH HOMOPOLAR MOTOR: 8 satellite discs at 45° intervals encode 8 brush contacts around the rim of the Faraday disc. The EMF at each brush = ½ ω B R² (same at all brushes since all are at radius R). With 8 brushes in parallel: total current capacity = 8× single-brush current, and the output voltage ripple frequency = 8× rotation frequency
2. TWO-RING BULLSEYE: The two-ring structure (outer ring + inner ring + central disc) encodes three concentric zones of the motor disc: outer rim (where brushes contact), intermediate disc body (current-carrying region), and axle core (inner electrical connection). Each zone operates at a different voltage in the Faraday disc:
   - Rim voltage V_R = ½ ω B R²
   - Intermediate zone voltage V_r = ½ ω B r² (r < R)
   - Axle voltage = 0 (reference)
3. 45° ANGULAR SPACING: 8-fold rotational symmetry (C8) produces a particularly smooth rotating field: the torque ripple has 8 ripples per revolution (ripple frequency = 8 × f_rotation). This is superior to 3-phase motors (3 ripples/rev) and approaching DC-motor smoothness (∞ ripples/rev in the ideal limit)
4. SMALL FLANKING DOTS: The 1-2 small dots flanking each satellite may represent: (a) the spring housing and adjustment mechanism for the brush, (b) a secondary/auxiliary probe contact for monitoring brush voltage without affecting the main current, or (c) the two contact surfaces of a split-ring commutator segment
5. BOTTOM EXTENSION: The descending chain of smaller discs (70-80px + 40-50px + tiny dot) at the bottom terminal is the external circuit connection path — encoding how the motor disc's axle contact is connected through the external circuit back to the rim brush contacts. The diminishing sizes represent the decreasing cross-section of the connection wires as they transition from the disc to the external terminals
