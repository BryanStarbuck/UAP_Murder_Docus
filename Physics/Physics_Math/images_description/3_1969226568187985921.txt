PHYSICS/SCIENCE RELEVANCE:
1. 10-BRUSH HOMOPOLAR MOTOR: The 10 J-hooks at 36° intervals define a 10-brush homopolar motor geometry (C10 symmetry). The 10-brush configuration provides: 10-fold symmetric current injection into the rotating disc, reduced current density at each brush contact point, and a torque ripple frequency of 10× the rotation rate. This is a practical engineering choice for large-diameter homopolar motors where current density at each brush is a limiting factor
2. J-HOOK AS BRUSH HOLDER: The curved J-hook shape is a realistic representation of a brush holder in a large homopolar motor: the "shank" of the J holds the brush element, the curved tip ensures the brush maintains radial contact with the spinning disc ring as the disc wears down, and the spring force is provided by the elasticity of the hook. This is similar to the carbon brush holders in conventional DC motors but adapted for the high-current, low-voltage regime of homopolar generators
3. CONCENTRIC RING MOTOR CROSS-SECTION: The three-ring bullseye (outer thick ring + gap + second ring + gap + solid inner disc/dashed ring) represents the cross-section of a multi-channel homopolar motor where: the outer ring = the primary current delivery ring, the second ring = an intermediate voltage-tapping ring, the inner disc = the central electrode or armature. This multi-ring design allows different voltage taps at different radii, enabling variable-output operation
4. DASHED RING AS BOLT CIRCLE: The dashed ring of 16-18 white marks inside the solid inner disc is the bolt circle — the ring of bolts attaching the rotating disc to the axle hub. The ~17 dashes match the 16-18 brush count seen in other motor disc diagrams in this collection. This confirms the inner dashed ring = the bolt attachment points for the rotor disc to the axle, and the ~17-bolt count = the ~16-18 brush contact count on the satellite disc ring of the complete assembly
5. CLOCKWISE HOOK DIRECTION AS ROTATION ENCODING: The clockwise curl of all 10 J-hooks encodes the rotation direction of the motor. In the homopolar motor context: clockwise rotation (viewed from above) corresponds to the current flowing outward (from center to rim) in the disc, and the Lorentz force F = IL × B (with B pointing upward) producing an upward thrust. The hook curl direction is a direct encoding of the Lorentz force cross product
