FORMATION GEOMETRY — TWO-SIDED "DRAGONFLY" / ANTENNA FORM:
* OVERALL SHAPE: The formation has bilateral (left-right) symmetry about a central vertical axis, with an upper arm system and a lower arm system, creating a figure resembling a dragonfly, winged insect, or dipole antenna

* UPPER PORTION:
  * Two long sweeping arms curve outward from a central intersection point, arcing upward and outward to the left and right
  * A flattened eye/lens shape (vesica piscis or lens element) at the center of the crossing point — approximately where the two upper arms intersect
  * The arms are narrow flattened paths (approximately 3-5m wide in the field)
  * Two small satellite circles at the upper tips of the arms (one at upper-left tip, one at upper-right tip)
  * A small satellite circle above and between the two arm tips (approximately center-top)

* LOWER PORTION:
  * Two long arms curve downward from the same central crossing point, arcing outward and down to the left and right (mirroring the upper arms)
  * Another lens/eye shape at the center (lower crossing)
  * Approximately 5-6 small satellite circles arrayed in a horizontal line at the widest point of the lower arm crossing (center of the formation) — appearing as a row of circles between the upper and lower arm systems
  * Five satellite circles along the left arm and another set along the right arm at the lower corners
  * Small isolated satellite circles at the lower-left and lower-right tips of the lower arms

* CENTRAL ROW OF CIRCLES:
  * A horizontal chain of approximately 5-6 circles of similar size, positioned in the center of the formation at the crossing/waist
  * This is the most prominent internal feature

PHYSICS / INFORMATION CONCEPTS:
* CROSSED-DIPOLE / BUTTERFLY ANTENNA TOPOLOGY:
  * Two pairs of curved arms (upper and lower), each pair forming one arm of a dipole, with both pairs sharing a central feedpoint = crossed dipole or "butterfly" antenna configuration
  * The upper arm pair: one arm of dipole 1 (oriented upper-left to upper-right)
  * The lower arm pair: one arm of dipole 2 (oriented lower-left to lower-right)
  * Together: a crossed dipole or biconical antenna — two orthogonal linear dipoles sharing the same feedpoint, producing a more omnidirectional radiation pattern than a single dipole
  * The crossed dipole fed with 90° phase shift produces circular polarization (RHCP or LHCP), which is the standard format for satellite communications and UAP radar tracking systems

* LENS/EYE ELEMENTS — BALUN AND IMPEDANCE MATCHING:
  * The lens-shaped elements (vesica piscis) at the crossing points = balun elements (balanced-to-unbalanced impedance transformer)
  * A balun in a dipole antenna: matches the balanced antenna impedance (~73Ω for a half-wave dipole) to the unbalanced feedline impedance (50Ω for coaxial cable)
  * The lens shape specifically = a double-stub matching network or butterfly stub, providing wideband impedance matching

* CENTRAL ROW OF CIRCLES — PHASE ARRAY ELEMENT SPACING:
  * The row of 5-6 circles at the center = the driven elements of a phased array, with inter-element spacing encoded by the circle positions
  * For a 5-element array with half-wave spacing: element positions at x_n = n × λ/2 where n = 0, 1, 2, 3, 4
  * Array factor: AF(θ) = Σ_n a_n e^{jkx_n sin θ} where k = 2π/λ and a_n are element excitation amplitudes
  * The circle sizes may encode the relative excitation amplitudes a_n (Taylor, Dolph-Chebyshev, or binomial taper)

* SATELLITE TIPS — DRIVEN + PARASITIC ELEMENTS:
  * Small circles at arm tips = parasitic (director or reflector) elements of a Yagi-Uda antenna
  * The tips of the formation arms may encode the locations of parasitic elements that narrow the beam and increase directivity

* BIOLOGICAL MORPHOLOGY — INSECT WING ANALOGY:
  * The dragonfly/insect morphology is a recurrent motif in this formation series
  * Insects are piezoelectric resonators (wing cuticle is a piezoelectric material): insect wingbeats at f = 20-500 Hz produce mechanical resonances at RF frequencies via piezoelectric coupling
  * In the UAP context: a biological transducer analogy for converting mechanical rotation into electromagnetic radiation

HIGH-LEVEL SUMMARY:
Aerial photograph (©Steve Alexander) of a bilateral "dragonfly" or butterfly antenna crop formation in a ripe tan-golden grain field. The formation features two pairs of long curved sweeping arms radiating from a central waist — an upper pair arcing upward-outward to left and right, and a lower pair arcing downward-outward — creating bilateral symmetry. Two lens/vesica-piscis elements mark the central crossing points (upper and lower); a horizontal row of 5-6 circles spans the waist between the arm pairs; small satellite circles appear at arm tips and along the lower arms. The physics encoding: a crossed-dipole or biconical antenna with two orthogonal dipoles sharing a feedpoint, producing circular polarization (RHCP/LHCP) when driven with 90° phase offset; the central lens elements encode balun impedance matching networks; the row of circles encodes a 5-element phased array with Taylor or Chebyshev amplitude taper; the arm-tip circles encode Yagi-Uda parasitic elements (directors/reflectors) for beam narrowing and directivity enhancement. The bilateral symmetry is exact, encoding the balance condition of a differential (balanced) antenna feed.
