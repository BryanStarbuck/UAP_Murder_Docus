PHYSICS CONCEPT:
This extreme close-up documents a key physical characteristic used in crop circle research to distinguish genuine formations from hoaxes: the bending of crop stalks at the node (stem joint) rather than breaking. When a stalk is mechanically stomped by a board, it breaks or kinks. When exposed to a brief pulse of heat or microwave energy, the cellular fluid in the node expands rapidly, softening the node material, and the weight of the seed head causes the stalk to bend and remain bent after cooling. Elongated nodes (visibly swollen) are another characteristic cited in genuine formations. This type of close-up photographic documentation is used by researchers like W.C. Levengood and the BLT Research Team to establish physical evidence of energetic (non-mechanical) formation mechanisms.
