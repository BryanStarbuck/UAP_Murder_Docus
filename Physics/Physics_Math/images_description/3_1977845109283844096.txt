PHYSICS CONCEPT:
This multi-exposure composite shows 3I/Atlas (or 3I/Atlas at an early stage) moving through the star field over time, with its green cometary coma and tail visible at four trajectory positions. The vivid green color is caused by two spectroscopic processes: (1) C2 (Swan bands) emission at ~511nm from dicarbon photodissociation; (2) CN violet system emission at ~388nm from cyanide photodissociation — both driven by UV solar radiation. The parallel tail directions confirm the antisolar orientation (consistent with radiation pressure + solar wind sweeping the tail away from the Sun). The four positions trace the comet's apparent motion across the sky (a combination of its actual trajectory and Earth's orbital motion). The bright green active coma distinguishes 3I/Atlas from 1I/Oumuamua (which showed no cometary outgassing), making 3I/Atlas more consistent with a natural comet composition.
