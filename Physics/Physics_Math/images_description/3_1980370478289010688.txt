UFO incident of July 19-27, 1952 — the most extensively radar-documented mass UAP sighting in American history prior to the 21st century. The specific physics data embedded in these headlines is significant: "Radar Spots Blips Like Aircraft for Nearly Six Hours; Got 7200 Feet Up" and "Saucer Outran Jet" together establish specific performance parameters of the observed objects. The headline "'Saucer' Outran Jet" refers to the F-94 Starfire jet interceptors scrambled from Andrews AFB that were unable to close with the objects. The F-94's maximum speed was approximately 855 km/h (Mach ~0.7 at altitude). "Outran jet" means the tracked objects accelerated beyond Mach 0.7 from a hovering state, a maneuver requiring extreme thrust-to-weight ratios unachievable by any known 1952 propulsion technology. The "nearly six hours" of continuous radar contact rules out transient sensor artifacts, instrument malfunctions, or atmospheric ducting events (which are brief and non-persistent). Multiple independent radar systems at Washington National Airport (now Reagan National), Andrews AFB radar, and the Civil Aeronautics Authority radar center all simultaneously tracked the same objects — providing triple-redundant, multi-sensor correlation that eliminates single-sensor artifact explanations. The physics import of simultaneous multi-radar tracking is that each radar system operates on different frequencies and different geometric angles, making correlated false returns essentially statistically impossible.
