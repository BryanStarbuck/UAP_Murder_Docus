PHYSICS CONCEPT:
This is a false-color spectral emission map of a COMETARY COMA — most likely from the interstellar object 3I/Atlas (discovered July 2025) or a solar system comet observed with an integral field unit (IFU) spectrograph. The detection of both CN (cyanogen radical, C₂N) and Ni (neutral atomic nickel) in the coma is scientifically significant:

CN is a canonical coma tracer produced by photodissociation of parent molecules HCN and CH₃CN (acetonitrile) in the coma: HCN + hν → CN + H. CN emission at 388nm (the B²Σ⁺ → X²Σ⁺ violet system) is a standard measure of cometary activity (Q_CN production rate). The ~6,000 km physical scale bar indicates the observation probes the inner coma, where the parent-to-daughter photodissociation cascade is ongoing, and the collision-dominated zone (within the contact surface) may still partially suppress extended emission.

Ni is an anomalous coma species first definitively detected by Bromley et al. (2021, Nature) in solar system comets and subsequently identified in many objects. The presence of Ni in cometary comae is not explained by classical dust grain sublimation — neutral Ni is produced only by photodissociation of an unknown Ni-bearing parent molecule at temperatures far too low for thermal sublimation (comae operate at 170-200K in the outer solar system). This suggests either: (1) A photochemical production mechanism from an unidentified organometallic Ni compound (e.g., nickelocene Ni(C₅H₅)₂); or (2) A non-thermal desorption mechanism (UV photodesorption, solar wind sputtering) of Ni from dust grain surfaces.

For an interstellar object (ISM origin), the detection of both CN and Ni with morphologies consistent with solar radiation-driven outflow indicates the object is sublimating material that is compositionally similar to solar system comets — supporting either: (a) A common chemical origin for cometary volatiles across star-forming regions; or (b) Active outgassing from a body that formed in its home planetary system and was ejected with a composition similar to Oort Cloud or Kuiper Belt objects. The anti-sunward extension (Finson-Probstein fan directed away from Sun) is driven by radiation pressure on dust grains and solar wind on ions, with β = Q_rad/Q_grav controlling the dust trajectory distribution. The compact Ni emission relative to CN indicates Ni production occurs closer to the nucleus — consistent with a refractory parent that survives longer than volatile HCN before dissociating.

In RC's UAP physics documentation context, the detection of metallic species (Ni) in the coma of an interstellar object is significant because: metallic outgassing is not expected from a natural comet, and alternative interpretations (metallic components of an artificial object, structural metal being ablated) are not excluded by the spectral data alone. This connects to the broader "3I/Atlas as artificial object" thesis documented elsewhere in this collection.
