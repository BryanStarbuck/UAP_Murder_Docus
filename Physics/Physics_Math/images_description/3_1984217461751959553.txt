Primary Phenomenon — Exhaust Plume:
* The dominant visual feature is a large luminous blue-white exhaust/propellant plume filling the upper-left and center of the frame.
* The plume originates from a bright point source at the left edge (approximately 30% from the top, at the left margin) — this is the rocket engine exhaust nozzle or the rocket itself.
* From this bright point, the plume expands dramatically to the right in an asymmetric cone shape, creating the following visual structure:
  - Inner bright core: Two-three narrow, intensely bright blue-white rays stream directly to the right from the point source, creating the impression of a focused exhaust stream.
  - Outer expanded envelope: A large, diffuse, softly-lit blue-white cloud or "skirt" expands from the inner core, spreading upward and downward in a dramatic sweeping arc. The outer envelope has a smooth, billowing, almost nebular appearance.
  - The overall shape resembles a comet, jellyfish (medusa), or Magellan Cloud nebula.
* The plume coloration: bright white at the inner core, transitioning to electric blue in the mid-region, and soft pale blue-gray at the diffuse outer edges.
* The plume appears to have a slight curl or sweep upward at the right side of the expanded envelope.

Physics of the Phenomenon:
* This is a well-documented phenomenon caused by a rocket engine burning at high altitude where atmospheric pressure is extremely low. The exhaust gases (H₂O, CO₂, CO from liquid or solid propellant combustion) expand supersonically into the near-vacuum, creating a large expanding disk or cone that scatters sunlight (even when the ground observer is in darkness — the rocket is at sufficient altitude to be in sunlight). The result is a dramatic spiraling or expanding cloud pattern visible for hundreds of kilometers.
* This phenomenon has been frequently misidentified as UAP sightings.
