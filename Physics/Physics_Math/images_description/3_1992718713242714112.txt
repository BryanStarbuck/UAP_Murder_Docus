Star Trails (Inverted):

Physical Interpretation:
* The color inversion + false-color heat map reveals the radial profile of the coma brightness: the color gradient is a visual representation of the surface brightness profile I(r) of the coma as a function of radial distance r from the nucleus.
* The nuclear inversion to black means the nucleus condensation was saturated or near-saturated in the original exposure — the inverted black dot is a processing saturation artifact.
* The blue-violet fringe between the red core and pink outer zone may represent a real color gradient in the coma: the innermost coma (closest to the nucleus) may have a different molecular excitation temperature than the outer coma, with different molecular emission bands dominating at different radii.
* In a standard comet color image: the inner coma (near nucleus) is dominated by direct solar reflection from dust grains (white/yellow) while the outer coma is dominated by molecular emission (green for C₂, blue for CO⁺). In color inversion: yellow/white → purple/black, green → magenta/pink, blue → orange/red. The observed gradient (black → red → pink → white) is consistent with this interpretation.

RC's Contextual Use:

* The prominent dark black nucleus in the inverted image is key: it shows the nucleus was bright enough to saturate the coma in the original — indicating an unusually active or unusually luminous nucleus for an object at 1.604 AU from the Sun.
* The slight ellipticity of the coma (wider than tall) hints at a nascent tail structure even at this post-perihelion outbound distance.
