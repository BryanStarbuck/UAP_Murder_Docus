Coma Structure in Green False Color:
* Nuclear condensation: a brilliant WHITE-GREEN or WHITE circular spot at the center of the coma (approximately 2-3% of frame width). The nucleus is the brightest region, rendered in near-pure white at the center.
  - There is a faint CYAN/TURQUOISE tinge immediately adjacent to the white nuclear spot — possibly the contribution of the blue CO⁺ ion emission mixing with the green C₂ emission at the innermost coma boundary.
* Inner coma: a bright YELLOW-GREEN (lime green) zone surrounding the nucleus, approximately 5-8% of frame width radius. This is the brightest green zone, corresponding to peak C₂ Swan Band emission.
* Middle coma: medium GREEN (pure green) → DARK GREEN, extending outward from the yellow-green inner zone. The color gradients smoothly radially.
* Outer coma: DARK GREEN → very dark green → black-green background. The outer coma boundary is not sharp but grades smoothly.
* Overall coma shape: slightly ELLIPTICAL with the long axis oriented roughly horizontally (east-west or anti-solar direction), consistent with a faint tail extension or coma asymmetry toward the right.
* CONCENTRIC RING STRUCTURE: a notable feature is visible banding in the coma — approximately 3-4 faint concentric ring structures (alternating slightly brighter and slightly darker zones) visible in the middle-to-outer coma region. These represent:
  - Either coma shells (successive sublimation events creating periodic density shells, as seen in comets like C/1995 O1 Hale-Bopp).
  - Or processing artifacts from unsharp masking / high-pass filtering applied to reveal structure.
  - In either case, the presence of ring structure in 3I/ATLAS's coma is a key observation RC documents.

RC's Contextual Use:
* This green heat-map version directly confirms the C₂ Swan Band emission interpretation: the green is not a white-light reflection but an emission feature, and the green false-color enhancement shows the spatial distribution of C₂ emission in the coma.
* The concentric ring/shell structure in the coma is significant: it suggests either periodic outgassing events from the nucleus or a complex coma envelope structure. For an interstellar object with an anomalously green coma, concentric shells would indicate active sublimation of C₂-producing species (acetylene, ethylene, ethane) at different depths in the nucleus.
* The cyan fringe around the white nucleus suggests a real color gradient in the innermost coma: white (nucleus photometric light) → cyan (mixed green C₂ + blue CO⁺) → pure green (outer C₂ dominated coma).
* The slight ellipticity hints at the ion tail beginning to be resolved even in this broadband image.
