Physical Interpretation:
* The posterization serves a specific diagnostic purpose: by reducing the image to discrete brightness levels, the processing converts the smooth brightness profile into clear boundary contours — essentially creating an isophote map.
* The white nucleus zone = all pixels above the posterization threshold (the coma core brighter than ~70% of full scale).
* The grey coma zone = all pixels between the two posterization thresholds (~20-70% of full scale).
* The black background = below the noise threshold (<20% of full scale).
* The green pixel patches at the nucleus-coma boundary are not noise — they represent the highest-brightness pixels that still have strong green color (not saturated to white). This is the "sweet spot" where C₂ emission is strong enough to be visible in color but not so bright it saturates to white in the Lum filter channel.
* This processing confirms that the green C₂ emission is concentrated at a specific radial distance from the nucleus — consistent with the photodissociation shell model: C₂ is produced at a specific radius from the nucleus where solar UV photodissociation of precursor molecules (C₂H₂, C₂H, CN₂, etc.) is most efficient.

RC's Contextual Use:
* The posterization reveals the C₂ emission ring morphology: the green patches are concentrated at the inner coma boundary (the photodissociation zone), not at the nucleus itself or at the outer coma. This is evidence for a distinct emission shell rather than a uniformly emitting coma.
* The white nucleus blob shape in the posterized image shows the nucleus condensation is somewhat asymmetric — possibly elongated toward the right-of-center, which could indicate a tail beginning to develop in the anti-solar direction.
* The speckled noise texture in the background makes the image look like a traditional halftone print or a scan of a printed document — this processing connects the image to the newspaper/document imagery elsewhere in the series (like the Roswell Daily Record).
