Physical Interpretation:
* The bifurcated tail (ion tail + dust tail) is the key diagnostic feature: it confirms that 3I/ATLAS has both volatile outgassing (producing CO⁺ in the ion tail) AND solid dust/particulates (producing the dust tail). This is consistent with cometary outgassing of a complex mixture.
* The unusual bright blue point at the ion tail terminus may represent a plasma condensation (plasmoid) — a blob of compressed CO⁺ plasma that has been released from the main tail by magnetic reconnection in the interplanetary magnetic field. These plasmoid events are observed in comets like Halley and C/1996 B2 (Hyakutake).
* The pink nucleus color (vs. the teal outer coma) reveals the inner coma's dust-to-gas ratio: the pink color = dust continuum (yellow/white) + CN violet = a dusty inner coma consistent with high-dust-to-gas outgassing.
* The overall eye-shaped coma (elongated horizontally, pointed at both the tail and anti-tail directions) is characteristic of a moderately high activity comet at ~1.5-2 AU.
