LEFT PANEL — Cley Hill Crop Formation (July 9, 2010):
* Aerial photograph of a large crop formation in a golden-brown grain field (summer-ripe wheat).
* Caption: "July 9, 2010 near Cley Hill" (black text below the photo).
* The formation is large — filling most of the aerial frame.
* Outer boundary: a large HEXAGON outline — a regular six-sided polygon with straight sides connecting 6 sharp corner points. The hexagon is formed by flattened crop along its perimeter.
* Inside the hexagonal boundary: a complex 3D-perspective isometric cube/cross pattern:
  - The interior contains an isometric drawing of the "three-dimensional cross" — the same shape as in 3_1993102940765310976.jpg.
  - The 3D cross is drawn in the crop using alternating flattened and standing crop to create the visual illusion of 3D depth and interlocking bars.
  - Specifically: three "bars" of crop laid flat at different orientations (aligned with the three isometric axes at 120° to each other in the crop plane) create the visual illusion of three mutually perpendicular bars passing through each other.
  - The isometric shading of the 3D cube is achieved by: some sections flattened to create the "dark" faces of the isometric bars, others left standing to create the "light" or "middle" faces.
  - The overall visual effect is a large-scale isometric 3D cross drawn in the field — precisely the same geometry as the blue 3D cross in the companion image.
* The hexagonal outer ring contains the isometric 3D cross centered within it.
* At the center of the formation: a small central element (either a small circle or the central intersection of the three bars).
* The formation is very precise in geometry — the hexagon is regular, the isometric perspective is consistent, and the internal structure is systematically laid.
* The crop color varies: lighter-colored areas (standing crop) vs. darker/browner areas (flattened crop), creating the contrast necessary for the 3D illusion.

RIGHT PANEL — 3D Cross Reference Diagram:
* A 2D isometric vector graphic of the "three-dimensional cross" shape.
* Caption: "RC thanks to Lucy Pringle" (black text below the graphic).
* Three colors are used to create the 3D illusion:
  - LIGHT GREY: the top faces of each bar (the illuminated top surfaces) — one bar pointing upper-left, one upper-right.
  - GREEN: the front-left or front-right facing surfaces of the bars — creating depth.
  - BLACK: the bottom-right shaded faces of the bars — the "shadow" faces.
* The three-color isometric rendering (grey/white = top, green = side, black = bottom) creates the clear visual illusion of three mutually interlocking rectangular bars.
* This serves as the reference geometry key — showing exactly which parts of the formation correspond to which faces of the 3D cross.
