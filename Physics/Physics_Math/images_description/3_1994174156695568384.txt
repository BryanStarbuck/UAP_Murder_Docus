Physical Interpretation:
* If the yellow dust zone is at a different angular position (upper-right vs. lower-left) relative to the nucleus than in the companion image, this is significant: it may indicate either (a) the images are from different epochs with different Sun-comet-telescope geometries, or (b) the images have different rotational orientations (the telescope field rotated between sessions).
* The Sun direction can be determined from the yellow dust fan position: yellow at upper-right → Sun approximately at upper-right direction from the comet in this image's orientation.
* The higher color saturation reveals additional structure: the distinction between the yellow (dust) and cyan (ion) inner zones is more clear, showing the directional separation of dust vs. gas emission at the nucleus scale.

RC's Contextual Use:

* The different orientation of the yellow dust zone (if from the same epoch) would indicate a genuine physical asymmetry in the dust emission rather than an image orientation artifact — possibly a directional jet.
