PHYSICS CONCEPTS:
* This is almost certainly a Rosetta spacecraft image of Comet 67P/Churyumov-Gerasimenko at perihelion (closest approach to Sun), approximately August 2015
* BILOBATE STRUCTURE: 67P/C-G's distinctive two-lobe shape is believed to result from:
  * A low-velocity collision of two separate cometary nuclei (contact binary formation)
  * Or extreme outgassing-driven mass loss reshaping a single nucleus
  * The bilobate shape has aerodynamic significance — the neck region experiences concentrated solar heating and is the most active outgassing site
* OUTGASSING JETS:
  * The dominant jet from the neck region is caused by concentrated solar heating at the neck — the geometry focuses solar illumination
  * Jet composition: primarily H₂O vapor at perihelion, with CO₂, CO at larger heliocentric distances
  * Jet physics: sublimating gas escapes at the local thermal velocity (~300-600 m/s for water at cometary surface temperatures)
  * The jets exert non-gravitational forces on the nucleus: observed as non-gravitational parameters in orbital fitting (Marsden parameters A₁, A₂, A₃)
* The ROSETTA MISSION (ESA, 2004-2016) was the first spacecraft to orbit a comet and deploy a lander (Philae) — its images of 67P/C-G represent the highest-resolution cometary nucleus observations ever made

HIGH-LEVEL SUMMARY:
A grayscale close-up spacecraft image (consistent with ESA Rosetta/OSIRIS camera) of the bilobate nucleus of Comet 67P/Churyumov-Gerasimenko, showing two connected rocky/icy lobes joined at a narrow neck, with multiple prominent outgassing jets — the dominant jet erupting from the neck region and extending upward into the black starfield. The rough, boulder-strewn surface texture of both lobes is clearly visible. The image documents active sublimation physics: concentrated solar heating at the neck geometry drives the dominant jet, while secondary jets emerge from active regions on both lobes. The Rosetta mission (2004-2016) produced these images, representing the highest-resolution cometary nucleus observations available.
