PHYSICS CONCEPTS:
* COMETARY COMA CHEMISTRY:
  * Green inner coma: C2 (diatomic carbon) Swan band fluorescence at 516nm; C2 is produced by photodissociation of larger carbon-containing parent molecules (e.g., C2H2, C2H6, PAHs) by solar UV
  * CO, OH, CN, NH, NH2 also contribute to the coma emission spectrum
* ION TAIL PHYSICS:
  * The blue plasma tail is collimated by the interplanetary magnetic field (IMF) embedded in the solar wind
  * CO⁺ at 420nm (blue) and H₂O⁺ at 619nm (red) are primary ion tail contributors; the dominant blue color indicates CO⁺ and CO₂⁺ fluorescence
  * Ion tail disconnection events (caused by IMF sector boundary crossings or solar wind dynamic pressure increases) are not visible here
  * Tail-to-antisolar direction: the ion tail points nearly directly away from the Sun (within ~1-3°), while the dust tail follows the orbit's curvature
* DUST TAIL PHYSICS:
  * Yellowish-white color from broadband solar light scattering by dust grains (β-meteoroids)
  * The dust tail curves along the orbital plane due to the ratio of solar radiation pressure to solar gravity (β parameter) for different grain sizes
  * The Finson-Probstein model predicts the dust tail morphology based on grain size distribution and ejection epoch
* OBSERVATIONAL PARAMETERS:
  * Wide-field imaging (estimated field of view: 1-3°)
  * Long exposure to capture tail structure — integration time of several minutes to hours
  * Color composite: likely three-filter (B/G/R or clear + Hα + OIII) or standard LRGB imaging
  * The sharp contrast between the vivid green coma and blue ion tail and the black sky is consistent with a moderately bright comet (estimated visual magnitude 5-8 at time of imaging)

A wide-field astrophotograph of a bright comet showing vivid green C2 Swan band inner coma (516nm diatomic carbon fluorescence), a broad blue ion tail (CO⁺ and CO₂⁺ plasma emission, upper-right), and a shorter yellow-white dust tail (lower-left, broadband solar scatter).
