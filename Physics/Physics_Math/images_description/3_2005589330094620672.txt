PHYSICS CONCEPTS:
* COMETARY COMA IMAGING — ENHANCED PROCESSING:
  * Professional cometary science uses multiple techniques to enhance faint structure:
    1. ADAPTIVE LAPLACIAN / ROTATIONAL GRADIENT (Larson-Sekanina technique): highlights jet and asymmetric structures in the inner coma
    2. DIVISION BY AZIMUTHAL AVERAGE: removes the radially symmetric background to reveal deviations (jets, fans)
    3. FALSE-COLOR MAPPING: assigns colors to surface brightness levels; the rainbow mapping in the LEFT panel makes gradient transitions visible to the human eye
  * The cometary jets in the LEFT panel: narrow enhanced brightness regions extending from the nucleus — produced by active sublimation areas on the rotating nucleus surface
* JET PHYSICS:
  * Cometary jets occur when volatile ices (H₂O, CO₂, CO) sublimate at specific "active areas" on the nucleus surface when illuminated by the sun
  * The jet consists of gas + entrained dust ejected at ~0.5-1.5 km/s from the nucleus
  * The jet expands and curves slightly with solar radiation pressure and the Coriolis effect from the rotating nucleus
  * The dark "shadow" region behind the nucleus (upper-right in left panel) is the low-density region downstream from the jet — material is swept away by the jet into the fan-shaped distribution
* DUST COMA vs GAS COMA:
  * Dust coma (right panel): broadband reflectance — grains scatter sunlight across all wavelengths; size distribution ~10⁻⁶ to 10⁻² m; Mie scattering
  * Gas/CN/C2 coma (left panel): specific emission at narrow wavelengths; C2 (516nm), CN (388nm), C3 (405nm); characteristic green and violet fluorescence
  * The left panel shows a SMALLER, MORE CONCENTRATED coma than the right — confirming the gas species have shorter scale lengths than the dust (gas scale length ~10⁴-10⁵ km vs. dust tail ~10⁶-10⁷ km)
