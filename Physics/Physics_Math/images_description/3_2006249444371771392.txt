PHYSICS CONCEPTS:
* OBJECT IDENTIFICATION: "3I ATLAS" — the designation "3I" means THIRD INTERSTELLAR OBJECT:
  * 1I/ʻOumuamua (2017): first confirmed interstellar object, anomalous acceleration (non-gravitational)
  * 2I/Borisov (2019): second interstellar object, identified as a cometary body
  * 3I/ATLAS: the THIRD interstellar object, discovered in 2025, designated 3I
  * "ATLAS" = Asteroid Terrestrial-impact Last Alert System (ATLAS survey telescope network) — the discovery instrument
  * MPC designation sequence: 3I indicates it was confirmed as a third interstellar interloper
* SIGNIFICANCE OF 3I/ATLAS:
  * This would be a major astronomical discovery — the third confirmed interstellar object passing through our solar system
  * If 3I/ATLAS is a comet (which this image suggests based on the coma and possible tail), it is more similar to 2I/Borisov than to ʻOumuamua
  * The observation date (December 27, 2025) places this as recent news — 3I/ATLAS would have been an active research target at this time
  * The false-color imaging technique is used to map the surface brightness gradient of the inner coma and detect asymmetries that indicate jet activity
* FALSE-COLOR SURFACE BRIGHTNESS MAPPING:
  * Same technique as 3_2005589330094620672: logarithmic color mapping of the coma surface brightness
  * Magenta/pink = highest surface brightness (nucleus + inner coma)
  * The concentric contours trace isophotes (lines of equal surface brightness) of the coma
  * Asymmetry in the contours indicates non-uniform outgassing (active areas on nucleus, jet structures)
* INTERSTELLAR COMETS — COMETARY PHYSICS:
  * Cometary activity of interstellar objects: the presence of a coma means the object is actively outgassing as it approaches the Sun
  * For 2I/Borisov: CO-dominated outgassing, unusually high CO/H₂O ratio compared to solar comets
  * For 3I/ATLAS: the coma morphology visible here would allow measurement of the production rates of gas species (C₂, CN, C₃, OH) and dust
  * The asymmetric coma (as suggested by the elongated inner contours) could indicate a jet — common in active comets
* STAR TRAILING:
  * The parallel elongated streaks from star trailing are a standard artifact in comet imaging
  * To image the comet at best resolution, the telescope tracks the comet's own non-sidereal motion
  * Stars, which move siderally, trail across the field during the exposure
  * Trail length ∝ (exposure time × comet apparent motion relative to stars)
  * For 16-minute exposure: if 3I/ATLAS moved ≈ 1.5-3 arcmin relative to background stars, trails would be 1.5-3 arcmin long

HIGH-LEVEL SUMMARY:
A professional false-color astronomical image of 3I/ATLAS, the third confirmed interstellar object, observed on December 27, 2025 from Balen, Belgium by Alfons Diepvens (MPC station C23) using a TEC 20cm f/9 apochromatic refractor with a CCD camera cooled to -30°C in a 16-minute luminance-filter exposure (2×2 binned). The false-color mapping reveals the comet's coma surface brightness gradient: magenta-pink nucleus region (highest brightness) surrounded by teal → orange-red → yellow contours indicating an asymmetric, jet-influenced inner coma. Background star trails run parallel across the image (a tracking artifact from following the comet's non-sidereal motion). 3I/ATLAS is the third interstellar object confirmed to pass through our solar system (following 1I/ʻOumuamua in 2017 and 2I/Borisov in 2019), discovered by the ATLAS survey network. The presence of an active coma confirms cometary volatile activity similar to 2I/Borisov, enabling measurement of interstellar cometary chemistry (CO, C₂, CN, H₂O production rates) that constrains the composition of material in other stellar systems.
