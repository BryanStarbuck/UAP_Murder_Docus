PHYSICS / SCIENTIFIC CONTEXT:
This image illustrates two compact nuclear fusion confinement concepts:

1. INERTIAL ELECTROSTATIC CONFINEMENT (IEC) — shown in the top row:
   * Box 1: The electrostatic confinement geometry — a spherical cathode (blue circle) surrounded by an anode (red circle), with arrows indicating the inward electric field that accelerates ions (red + charges) toward the center to achieve fusion conditions.
   * Box 2: Scattered particles before confinement.
   * Box 3: A particle scattering event off a surface/boundary.
   * Box 4: Fusion reaction and energy release (explosion/starburst).

2. INERTIAL ELECTROMAGNETIC CONFINEMENT — shown in the bottom row via stylized geometric diagrams:
   * The four circular designs (turbine, spiral, rosette, starburst) represent different electromagnetic confinement topologies or field configurations — possibly toroidal, poloidal, and helical field geometries for plasma confinement.

The top sequence describes the Farnsworth–Hirsch fusor operating principle: ions are electrostatically accelerated into a central virtual cathode where they achieve sufficient kinetic energy to overcome the Coulomb barrier and fuse. The bottom row generalizes to electromagnetic confinement variants (analogous to compact tokamak, field-reversed configuration, or rotating magnetic field approaches).
