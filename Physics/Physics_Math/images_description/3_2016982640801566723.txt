PHYSICS / CONCEPTS:
* This variant introduces two completely new engineering labels not seen in other versions:
  1. "stainless-steel tube" (magenta): labels the outer boundary of the beaded ring — the tube serves as:
     - The structural housing/containment vessel for the Galinstan or coil assembly
     - A non-magnetic conductive tube (stainless steel is paramagnetic, very weakly magnetic) — non-magnetic property means it won't distort the rotating magnetic field
     - In a liquid metal (Galinstan) design: the stainless steel tube contains the liquid metal, provides the current conduction path via the tube wall, and serves as the structural toroidal chamber
     - Stainless steel has good corrosion resistance with liquid metals (Galinstan is compatible with stainless steel at low temperatures)
  2. "ball bearings" (magenta): labels the beaded ring elements themselves — this is a crucial alternative interpretation:
     - Instead of "wire coils," the beaded elements may be BALL BEARINGS arranged in a race
     - Ball bearings in a ring = a bearing race — used to support the rotating element (the central disc or the ring itself)
     - In the Muller dynamo and similar devices, the rotating disc is supported by ball bearings at the central shaft
     - Alternatively: "ball bearings" may refer to conductive ball bearings used as current paths in an electrostatic/electromagnetic propulsion system (conductive spheres in a ring creating capacitive coupling or inductive elements)
  3. The "Λ" (lambda) symbols above and below: Λ may indicate:
     - The Greek letter λ (lambda) = wavelength — the wavelength of the resonant electromagnetic mode
     - A caret (^) indicating "upward force" or "Lorentz force direction"
     - Lambda in physics also: cosmological constant (in GR), or Λ-hyperon particle
  4. "3-phase magnetism" appears TWICE (upper-left and left-center): emphasizing two separate three-phase field components at different positions in the device
* The stainless-steel tube + ball bearings + 15 wire coils labeling suggests a hybrid design where both mechanical bearings and electrical coils are present in the ring assembly
