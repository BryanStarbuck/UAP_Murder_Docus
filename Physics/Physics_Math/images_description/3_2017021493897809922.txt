PHYSICS / CONCEPTS:
* The Drew-Dickerson dodecamer (1980): the first single-crystal X-ray structure of B-form DNA, solved by Richard Dickerson and Horace Drew at CalTech. The specific sequence is d(CGCGAATTCGCG) — a 12-base pair palindromic duplex
* Why B-DNA is relevant to this UAP physics collection:
  1. Axial symmetry: the end-on view of the DNA helix shows the same circular/ring topology as the beaded ring motor stator — 10-fold symmetry (one full helical turn) with base pairs as the "beads"
  2. The double helix = counter-rotating fields: the two anti-parallel strands of DNA form a structural analogy to counter-rotating magnetic fields (as in the Paulsen disc testimonial). The two strands wind in opposite directions relative to each other (anti-parallel, though same chirality) — direct analog of the "two magnetic discs rotating in opposite directions"
  3. DNA as an antenna: some alternative physics researchers propose that DNA is a fractal antenna capable of receiving and transmitting scalar/longitudinal electromagnetic waves — connecting biological information storage to the electromagnetic propulsion framework
  4. The helix pitch and charge distribution: the negatively charged phosphate backbone of DNA creates a helical charge distribution that generates a helical electric field — analogous to a helical coil antenna
  5. Quantum coherence in DNA: some researchers (Penrose-Hameroff, et al.) propose quantum coherence in DNA base pairs as fundamental to biological information processing — connecting quantum biology to exotic physics
