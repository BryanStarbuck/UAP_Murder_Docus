PHYSICS / CONCEPTS:
* 7-circle hexagonal formation (6-around-1) with concentric ring interiors:
  1. Hexagonal (flower of life) arrangement: 6 circles surrounding a central circle, each touching its neighbors — this is the hexagonal close-packing of circles, which is the densest 2D packing. In motor physics, this encodes a 6-pole topology (6 outer circles = 6 stator poles, central circle = rotor disc)
  2. Concentric rings within each circle: the alternating ring structure within each of the 7 circles encodes the multi-turn winding cross-section of each pole's coil — multiple concentric turns of wire around each pole core. The number of visible rings (approximately 5-6 in the center, 3-4 in the outer circles) gives the relative turn count per pole
  3. Overlapping vesica-piscis zones: in the actual formation, the overlap regions between circles encode the mutual inductance coupling zones between adjacent pole coils. High overlap = high mutual inductance = strong coupling between phases
  4. C-shaped crescent arcs: the crescent-shaped flattened crop regions at the inter-circle boundaries represent the flux fringe fields at the edges of pole pieces — the "leakage flux" in motor design that occurs at pole boundaries
  5. Six-phase interpretation: each of the 6 outer circles could represent one of 6 phases (a 6-phase motor uses two three-phase sets displaced by 30°), giving lower torque ripple than a standard 3-phase motor. Six-phase motors are used in high-power, high-reliability applications (ship propulsion, rail traction)
  6. The scale of this formation (estimated ~100m across) makes it one of the largest encodings in the Wiltshire corpus — suggesting the designers wanted the 6-circle topology to be impossible to miss
