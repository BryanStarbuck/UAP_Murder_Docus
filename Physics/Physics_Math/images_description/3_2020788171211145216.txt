PHYSICS / CONCEPTS:
* 5-arm or 6-arm pinwheel — 5-phase or 6-pole rotating magnetic field topology:

  1. Pinwheel / triskelion / rotating-star: the most fundamental encoding of a rotating electromagnetic field in crop formation geometry. A pinwheel with N arms encodes an N-phase (or N/2-pole) rotating magnetic field. The CW sweep direction of the blades encodes the direction of rotation of the field vector B(t). The curvature of each blade encodes the temporal phase evolution of the field as it rotates — the curved shape of each blade traces the integral curve of the rotating field vector.

  2. 5-arm version (pentagonal symmetry): if 5 blades, the formation encodes a 5-phase rotating field (phase spacing 72° = 2π/5). A 5-phase rotating field is energetically more efficient than 3-phase for certain high-power applications because the field is smoother (lower harmonic content). A 5-phase field vector B(t) = B₀ Σ_{k=0}^{4} cos(ωt - 2πk/5) [cos(2πk/5) x̂ + sin(2πk/5) ŷ] rotates continuously with very low ripple. The formation may be encoding a specific phase count for the disc propulsion system.

  3. 6-arm version (hexagonal symmetry): if 6 blades, the formation encodes a 6-pole 3-phase rotating field (the standard industrial motor topology), where 3 phases × 2 poles/phase = 6 visible arms. This is consistent with the "6 wire coils" annotation visible in related diagram images (3_2019232966652862464). A 6-pole rotating field has the smallest synchronous speed for a given frequency: n_s = 60f/p = 60×50/3 = 1000 RPM (for 50 Hz, 3-pole-pairs). Lower synchronous speed = higher torque density for the same shaft power — consistent with the heavy-lift requirements of a disc propulsion system.

  4. Concentric ring system — stator boundary and field confinement: the two or three concentric rings at the formation boundary encode the multi-layer stator structure. In the motor design described in the Muller dynamo and related specifications, the stator consists of: (a) the inner air gap (innermost flattened region), (b) the stator ring itself (the standing-crop annular band between the two flattened regions), and (c) the outer housing/yoke (outermost flattened ring). This three-zone structure is standard in all motor designs: air gap → stator iron → outer yoke.

  5. Central standing hub — zero-velocity rotation axis: the small circular standing-crop hub at the center of the pinwheel encodes the physical rotation axis of the motor — the point of zero velocity in the rotating frame. In a rotating disc propulsion system, the center hub is the mechanical pivot point (the ball bearing assembly in the Muller dynamo design) and the electrical output terminal for hub current extraction in the Faraday homopolar generator configuration.

  6. Blade curvature — Archimedean spiral trajectory of the rotating field: the curved shape of each pinwheel blade (not radially straight but gently swept/curved) encodes the Archimedean spiral trajectory r = a + bθ. In a rotating field at angular velocity ω, a test particle launched radially outward at velocity v_r traces an Archimedean spiral in the lab frame: r(t) = v_r t, θ(t) = ωt. The ratio of spiral pitch b = v_r/ω encodes the ratio of radial (centrifugal) force to tangential (magnetic) force acting on the crop stalks during the formation event — a direct measure of the field rotation rate ω relative to the induced stalk-bending velocity v_r.
