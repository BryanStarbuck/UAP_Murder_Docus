SECTION 1 — CORE GOAL AND PHILOSOPHY
====================================================================

1.1 THE GOAL

The goal is to design, simulate, and eventually build a craft that
achieves lift using magnet-based or electromagnetic methods. The craft
must physically lift off the ground. It must not rely on conventional
aerodynamic lift. It must not rely on combustion or rocket thrust.

The working hypothesis is that magnetic, gravitomagnetic, electrostatic,
or electromagnetic mechanisms can produce net upward force without
conventional propellant or aerodynamic surfaces.

The goal is not to prove any one theory correct. The goal is to find
what works by experimenting across multiple approaches simultaneously.


1.2 PHILOSOPHY OF MULTIPLE ATTEMPTS

Do not commit to one physics framework. Run multiple attempts in
parallel or sequentially. Each attempt is a different hypothesis about
which mechanism produces lift. Some attempts will fail. Failure is
information. Treat every result as data.

When starting a new day, look across all existing attempts and decide
which one to advance or whether to start a fresh attempt with a new
approach. Do not keep running an approach that shows no empirical
progress after many days of work.

A failed attempt is not abandoned forever. It may be revisited after
new knowledge changes the evaluation.


1.3 LEARNING FROM EMPIRICAL TRUTH

Simulation results and physical measurements override theory. If a
simulation shows no net force for a given design, update the theory.
Do not defend a model against simulation evidence.

Document every surprising result. Surprising results are where progress
hides.

Anomalous measurements in the research data are high priority. See
Section 4 for documented anomalies that need explanation.


1.4 STARTING IDEAS

The research corpus collected in this project contains documented
observations from real experimenters in multiple countries. These
include:

* Rotating magnetic disc assemblies observed operating with claimed
  anomalous weight changes
* Soviet-era technical documents describing specific parameters for
  electromagnetic lift systems recovered from or inspired by UAP craft
* Independent electrostatic propulsion validation from NASA Kennedy
  Center research
* Counter-rotating permanent magnet disc designs with over-unity energy
  claims
* Crop circle geometric patterns that map onto electromagnetic apparatus
  geometries with suspicious precision

None of these constitute proof. All constitute directions worth
investigating seriously.

The above, our starting idea, is not final. We're not locked into only the scope of that. We can go beyond the scope of that.

3.6 APPROACH: PLASMA MHD PROPULSION

Supporting physics from multiple sources in the corpus. Cernohajev
specifies MHD generators without rotating parts. BUGA device shows
plasma discharge elements.

MHD thrust: conducting plasma in crossed E and B fields experiences
Lorentz force j cross B where j is current density. Net force on plasma
transmitted to vehicle via magnetic coupling with surrounding field coils.

For lift application: orient thrust vector vertically. Plasma loop
above craft. B field horizontal. j vertical through plasma. Force
horizontal (reaction on coils) does not produce lift. Need j horizontal
and B vertical or a more complex geometry. Rotating plasma torus in
vertical B field produces complex force geometry worth mapping.

Simulation approach: OpenFOAM MHD solver. Python with FEniCS for
custom EM formulations. Start with simple Hartmann flow validation
then scale to complex geometry.

3.7 APPROACH: GRAVITOMAGNETIC FIELD GENERATION (PODKLETNOV ANALOG)

Source: E. Podkletnov and R. Nieminen, 1992 paper in Physica C.
Rotating YBCO superconductor above toroidal solenoid showed apparent

replication; results disputed.

Room-temperature analog question: The BUGA device appears to implement

Ferrite replaces superconductor. Rotating EM field replaces mechanically
rotating disc.

Key difference: Superconductor carries persistent surface currents with
zero resistance. Ferrite does not. Whether the rotating EM field in
ferrite can approximate the surface current distribution of a rotating
superconductor is the central physics question.

Simulation target: Compute J_surface distribution on rotating
superconducting disc (from London equations) versus rotating EM
field in ferrite (from Maxwell plus constitutive relations). Compare
field geometries above each. Determine whether similar gravitomagnetic
fields (if any) could result.

Note: Gravitomagnetic effects are real within general relativity
(Lense-Thirring effect). However, the Podkletnov claimed effect is
orders of magnitude larger than GR predicts for any achievable rotating
mass. If real, it requires new physics.

SECTION 5 — SIMULATION FRAMEWORK
====================================================================

5.1 SIMULATION TOOLS AVAILABLE

In order of increasing complexity and computational cost:

* magpylib (Python): Fast analytical computation of permanent magnet
  fields and forces. Best for: Halbach arrays, rotating magnet configs,
  rapid parameter sweeps. Cannot handle induced currents or plasma.

* FEMM (Finite Element Method Magnetics, free): 2D axisymmetric EM
  field solver. Good for: solenoid design, magnet geometries, force
  calculation. Limitation: 2D only, no time-varying fields natively.

* Elmer FEM (open source, 3D): Full 3D EM + heat + structural. Good
  for complex 3D geometries. Steeper learning curve.

* OpenFOAM with MHD solver: For plasma and conducting fluid problems.
  Good for MHD propulsion simulation.

* COMSOL Multiphysics (commercial, if available): Best overall for
  coupled EM + thermal + mechanical problems. Use if access available.

* Python scipy / numpy: Custom analytical models for rapid hypothesis
  testing before committing to FEM.


5.2 SIMULATION DIRECTORY STRUCTURE

{SIMULATION_DIR}/{WHICH_SIMULATION_SOFTWARE}/software/
  * All code for that software goes here
  * Python scripts, FEM model files, input decks

Naming convention for simulation runs:
  * run_{attempt_number}_{day_number}_{description}.py or .fmm or .sif


5.3 SIMULATION VALIDATION REQUIREMENT

Before using any simulation framework for new physics investigation,
validate it against a known result. Options:

* magpylib: Reproduce analytical field of a dipole at measured distance.
  Compare to B = (mu_0 over 4 pi) times (m over r^3) formula.

* FEMM: Reproduce Kelvin gradient force on a paramagnetic sphere in a
  solenoid field. Compare to analytical result.

* OpenFOAM MHD: Reproduce Hartmann flow profile between parallel plates.

Do not skip validation. A simulator that cannot reproduce known results
cannot be trusted for unknown results.


5.4 WHAT TO RECORD FROM EVERY SIMULATION RUN

* What the simulation was testing

Return to a prior attempt when:
* New knowledge from a later attempt casts the prior one in a new light
* A simulation improvement makes it possible to test something the
  prior attempt could not test
* Reflection analysis identifies a design flaw that was fixable but
  was not fixed before moving on

Attempts are numbered sequentially from 1 upward.
Attempt.yaml in each attempt directory has a name field. Choose a
name that describes the core mechanism, not the outcome. Names should
be readable in isolation. Example: Rotating_Permanent_Magnet_Array,
not Attempt_1_v2_final.


6.4 HOW MANY ATTEMPTS TO RUN SIMULTANEOUSLY

Run no more than two attempts in parallel within a single work day.
Focus is more productive than breadth within a single day. Over the
course of multiple days, breadth emerges naturally from sequential
attempt focus.

SECTION 9 — PHYSICS REFERENCE NOTES
====================================================================

9.1 KELVIN BODY FORCE

f = mu_0 times (M dot nabla) H

Force per unit volume on a magnetic material in a non-uniform field.
M is magnetization, H is applied field. Net lift force requires:
* Non-zero vertical gradient of H at the location of the material
* Magnetization M aligned with the gradient direction

For a permanent magnet array below a ferromagnetic target, the gradient
is upward near the poles and downward away from them. Net force depends
on integral of (M dot nabla)H over the target volume.


9.2 THREE-PHASE ROTATING MAGNETIC FIELD

Three coils at 120-degree intervals carrying AC currents 120 degrees
out of phase generate a rotating magnetic field vector B(t) that
rotates at angular frequency omega without any mechanical movement.

If a conductive or magnetic rotor is placed in this rotating field,
induced eddy currents create a torque via Lenz's law (induction motor
principle). The three-phase architecture is why three-disc assemblies
at 120-degree intervals appear in multiple research devices.


9.3 TOROIDAL MAGNETIC CIRCUIT

Toroidal core of high-permeability material (ferrite, mu_r 1000-5000)
confines magnetic flux within the torus with minimal external leakage.
Interaction of rotating external field with this confined flux creates
specific force geometry that differs from interaction with open-core
geometries.


9.4 LENSE-THIRRING GRAVITOMAGNETIC EFFECT

General relativity predicts that rotating mass drags spacetime.
A rotating gyroscope near a massive rotating body precesses at rate
Omega_LT = (G/c^2 r^3) times (2J - 3(J dot r_hat) r_hat) where J is
angular momentum of the central body.

For any achievable mechanical rotation, this effect is immeasurably
small. The Podkletnov claimed effect is 10^10 times larger than GR
predicts. If real, it requires beyond-GR physics.


9.5 HALBACH ARRAY

Arrangement of permanent magnets with spatially rotating magnetization
direction that concentrates flux on one side of the array and cancels
it on the other. Widely used in linear motors and maglev systems.

Standard Halbach: strong flux below array, near-zero above.
Modified Halbach with vertical bias: can create asymmetric vertical
gradient for Kelvin force application.


9.6 SPHERICAL ELECTROSTATIC FIELD GEOMETRY

Hemispherical conductor at potential V in free space creates field
lines that concentrate at the apex. Near the apex, E approximately
equals V over r^2 where r is distance from center. This creates an
electrostatic pressure gradient in the vertical direction.

For Buhler-style asymmetric thrust: asymmetric charge distribution
on a body surface creates net electrostatic pressure force. In vacuum
(no ion wind), this force must be transmitted to the body through
internal charge distribution on the conductor surface.

11.1 Magpylib
Language: Python
Type: Analytical 3D static magnetic field from magnets and coils
Install: pip install magpylib
URL: https://magpylib.readthedocs.io/
Strengths:
  * Fast vectorized computation of B and H fields in 3D
  * Handles permanent magnets (cube, cylinder, sphere, custom)
  * Handles current loops, line currents, arbitrary coils
  * Computes forces and torques between objects
  * Visualization via Plotly / Matplotlib / PyVista
  * GPU-capable via Numba or PyTorch
Best for: Halbach arrays, rotating magnet configs, quick parameter sweeps
Limitations: Static fields only; no induced currents, no plasma, no FEM

Language: Python
Type: 3D magnetostatics — permanent magnets, coils, saturated iron
Install: pip install radia (or via radiasoft)
URL: https://github.com/ochubar/Radia
Strengths:
  * Specifically designed for complex magnet arrays (undulators, etc.)
  * Handles arbitrary permanent magnet shapes and magnetization
  * Handles currents in conductors
  * Computes fields, forces, torques, field integrals
  * Strong support for levitation and magnet array designs
  * Python-native
Best for: Complex 3D magnet array designs, force/torque computation
Limitations: Static magnetostatics; no time-varying, no plasma
Validation target: Reproduce Halbach array flux cancellation on one
  side. Verify flux doubling on opposite side vs analytical expectation.
Status: Not yet installed or validated.

Language: Python
Type: 3D quasi-magnetostatic FFT/PEEC solver (voxel-based)
Install: pip install pypeec
URL: https://pypeec.otvam.ch
Strengths:
  * GPU/FFT-accelerated for large systems
  * Handles coils, inductors, transformers, magnetic materials
  * Computes fields, forces, inductance
  * Good for current-loop and coil array problems
Best for: Coil arrays, inductive coupling, magnet-current interaction
Limitations: Voxel geometry (staircasing); no plasma
Status: Not yet installed or validated.


11.4 DoSA-3D (Open Actuator)

Strengths:

Status: Not yet installed or validated.

FIRST VALIDATION PRIORITY
====================================================================

Before using any tool for new physics, validate it per Section 5.3.
Match the simulation tool to the physics of the current attempt.

IMPORTANT: Magpylib is a MAGNETOSTATICS tool. It cannot simulate
electrostatic phenomena. The Buhler result (Section 3.4, Section 4.3)
is electrostatic. Do not attempt to validate Magpylib against the
Buhler result — they are different physics domains.

Validation by tool and attempt:

* Tool: Magpylib (for Attempts using permanent magnets or static coils)
  Test: Reproduce the analytical on-axis dipole field of a single
    10×10×5 mm N52 magnet at 10 cm distance.
    Compare to: B_z = (mu_0 / 4*pi) * 2*m / r^3
    where m = (Br/mu_0) * V_magnet, Br = 1.48 T.
  Why: Validates that Magpylib computes static magnetic fields correctly
    before trusting it for Kelvin force and Halbach array analysis.
  Applies to: Attempt 1 (Rotating Asymmetric Magnet Array), Attempt 2
    (Counter-Rotating Disc), and any future permanent-magnet attempt.

* Tool: scipy/numpy electrostatic solver (for Approach 3.4 Buhler)
  Test: Reproduce Buhler's millinewton-range asymmetric electrostatic
    force from known electrode geometry and charge distribution.
    Buhler result: net force in mN range in hard vacuum, ion-wind-free.
  Why: Validates the electrostatic solver before using it to scale
    toward lift-force magnitudes.
  Applies to: Any attempt based on Approach 3.4 (Buhler electrostatic)
    or Approach 3.5 (Cernohajev GCD charge density parameters).
  Note: Create this attempt and its validation when Approach 3.4 becomes
    the active focus. Do not attempt this validation with Magpylib.
