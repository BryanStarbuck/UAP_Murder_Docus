      * Note any departures from standard Newtonian or GR framework
      * For electrostatic thrust problems: state explicitly whether the conductor
        geometry has an EDGE SINGULARITY (corner or rim where two surfaces meet).
        If yes, note that Legendre series will diverge and recommend BEM.
      * For magnetic Kelvin force problems: estimate the Kelvin force using the
        field at the SURFACE of the magnetic material, not at the centroid or at
        the source-to-target center-to-center distance. The surface field can be
        10x-1000x larger than the centroid field for close coil-ferrite geometries.
        The analytical estimate from centroid distance will underestimate F_Kelvin
        significantly: underestimate factor ~ (d_centroid/d_surface)^4 or more.

SIMULATION LEARNING
--------------------------------------------------------------------

[ ] SIMULATION learned for this day
      * Analyze all simulation output for this day
      * Document what the simulation confirmed vs. what surprised
      * Note parameter changes worth testing in future days
      * After computing F_z via Legendre series, check convergence: run at
        L_MAX=21 AND L_MAX=101. If series grows > 10% from 21 to 101, the
        rim singularity is significant. The L_MAX=21 result is a lower bound.
        Flag for BEM validation on a future day and do NOT report as final value.
      * For Biot-Savart simulations: before sampling the field, check whether any
        observation point coincides with the coil wire. If coil radius a equals
        the array radius R_array, the coil wire passes through (0, 0, z_coil) on
        the symmetry axis (each coil's wire at azimuth pi passes through the axis).
        Avoid sampling at exactly (0, 0, z_coil) in this geometry — offset by at
        least 1 mm to avoid the 1/R^3 singularity. This is a geometric coincidence
        in the specific case a = R_array, not a general Biot-Savart problem.
      * Update attempt Attempt.yaml with simulation findings
