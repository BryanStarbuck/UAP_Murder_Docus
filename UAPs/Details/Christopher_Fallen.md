## Circumstances of Death

Christopher Fallen was last heard from on February 2, 2024, when he sent a text to his father. On February 5, he missed scheduled business calls with coworkers at Fourth State Communications. On February 6, his father and coworkers went to his home, forced entry, and discovered his body.

He was found with his head wrapped in tape, a plastic bag secured with a belt around his neck, and his hands and shins bound with duct tape. The house appeared looted. His vehicle (a red Volkswagen GTI), electronic devices, and personal items were missing.

A photograph dated approximately February 3 — recovered from digital communications between the two suspects — shows Fallen sitting on a bed with hands bound, shirt over his eyes, face and clothing covered in blood. This indicates he was held captive for approximately three days before dying.

Police located Fallen's stolen vehicle at a Starbucks parking lot on February 6. Suspect **Daniel Lee Hadders**, 38, was apprehended nearby carrying Fallen's identification, 13 credit cards, house key, multiple cell phones, and personal documents.

The second suspect, **Andres Lorenzo Chavez**, had met Fallen through a dating app. They began a relationship and Fallen allowed Chavez to live in his home. Chavez introduced Fallen to Hadders, who was homeless but occasionally stayed at the residence. Both suspects had criminal histories (Chavez: hit-and-run, auto theft since 2020; Hadders: drug offenses, credit card fraud since 2021).

Chavez fled to El Paso, Texas, where he was found dead of a drug overdose in approximately April–May 2024 — less than three months after the murder, before he could stand trial.

On **October 15, 2025**, a jury convicted Hadders of first-degree murder, kidnapping, aggravated burglary, conspiracy, tampering with evidence, and credit card fraud. On **February 18, 2026**, Hadders was sentenced to **life in prison**.

## Background

Christopher Fallen was a space plasma physicist with deep ties to U.S. defense research infrastructure.

**Education:**
- B.S. in Mathematics and Physics (summa cum laude) — Fort Lewis College, Durango, CO (2000)
- M.A. in Mathematics — University of Kansas (2004)
- Ph.D. in Space Physics — University of Alaska Fairbanks (2010)

**Career:**
- **Research Associate Professor of Space Engineering Physics** — UAF Geophysical Institute (2010–2019)
- **Chief Scientist, HAARP** — High-frequency Active Auroral Research Program, Gakona, Alaska (March 2018–March 2019). HAARP is the world's most powerful ionospheric research facility, operating a 180-antenna phased array capable of 3.6 megawatt effective radiated power
- **Senior Research Physicist** — Air Force Research Laboratory (AFRL), Kirtland AFB, Albuquerque, NM. AFRL's Directed Energy Directorate at Kirtland researches high-energy lasers, high-power microwaves, laser plasma experiments, and optics
- **Chief Scientist** — Fourth State Communications, LLC, Cheyenne, WY (at time of death). Developing non-satellite beyond-line-of-sight (BLOS) communications using artificial ionospheric plasma structures — technology with clear military/defense applications
- **Remote Instructor** — Embry-Riddle Aeronautical University (at time of death)
- **Senator and President, UAF Faculty Senate** / **Chair, University of Alaska Faculty Alliance**
- Amateur radio callsign: KL3WX

**Research specializations:**
- Ionospheric physics and space plasma physics
- High-power HF radio modification of the ionosphere
- Artificial aurora/airglow experiments using HAARP
- Artificial ionization — demonstrated how high-power ionospheric modification creates ionization exiting Earth's atmosphere
- VLF radio wave generation via ionospheric heating
- Luxembourg Effect cross-modulation experiments
- Non-satellite beyond-line-of-sight communications via artificial plasma structures

His Google Scholar profile shows approximately 160 citations across publications in the Journal of Geophysical Research: Space Physics and other peer-reviewed journals.

## The Counterargument

The criminal case is strong and straightforward:
- Two identified suspects with criminal histories whom Fallen had personally invited into his home
- Digital communications between the suspects documenting the crime
- Photographs of the crime in progress recovered from suspects' devices
- Hadders caught with Fallen's property within hours of the body being discovered
- Jury conviction and life sentence for Hadders
- DA Sam Bregman called it "a calculated and horrific act of violence" — framing it as robbery-murder, not assassination
- No investigator, journalist, or law enforcement official has suggested any connection to Fallen's defense work
- The motive (robbery) is supported by the looting of the home and theft of vehicle, electronics, and credit cards
