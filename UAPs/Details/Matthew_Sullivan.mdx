## Assessment: HIGHLY SUSPICIOUS

According to Rep. Eric Burlison (R-MO), a sitting member of the House Oversight Committee, Sullivan was a UAP whistleblower his office had reached out to and scheduled for a private interview, and he was found dead roughly two weeks before that meeting. The medical examiner ruled the death an accidental overdose, but Burlison has repeatedly called the circumstances suspicious and referred the matter to the FBI. The pattern — a whistleblower death shortly before scheduled Congressional testimony — matches one of the most frequently documented patterns across this investigation.

**Reporting update (2026):** Sullivan's identity is now corroborated by mainstream outlets. He is named as Matthew James Sullivan, 39, a former Air Force intelligence officer who worked for the Air Force Intelligence Agency, the National Air and Space Intelligence Center (NASIC), and the National Security Agency, and who is described as one of roughly 40 firsthand witnesses referenced in David Grusch's 2023 inspector-general complaint. His death has been folded into a broader FBI inquiry into a cluster of aerospace and defense-connected scientists who have died or gone missing under unusual circumstances. The official cause remains an accidental overdose; the suspicion rating here reflects the timing and the sitting Congressman's on-record concerns, not a proven homicide.

## Circumstances of Death

Per Rep. Burlison's April 17, 2026 interview:

> "My office found out about it last year. We sent a letter to the FBI about a gentleman named Matthew Sullivan, who actually we had reached out to. He was scheduled to come in for an interview. Within two weeks, he had suspiciously committed suicide. That report was referred to the Office of Inspector General. They deemed it credible and urgent. They then referred it to the FBI."

Key elements from Burlison's account:
- Burlison's office had personally reached out to Sullivan
- An interview was scheduled
- Death occurred within approximately two weeks of that scheduling
- Burlison's office then sent a formal letter to the FBI
- The Intelligence Community Inspector General (ICIG) or a similar IG reviewed the report and found it "credible and urgent"
- The matter was referred back to the FBI for investigation

Additional forensic and biographical details (exact date, location, age, surviving family) were not disclosed in the public interview and have not been independently verified in reputable news sources as of April 2026.

- Reported to have been a former **U.S. Air Force intelligence officer** who, per news reporting, worked for the **Air Force Intelligence Agency**, the **National Air and Space Intelligence Center (NASIC)**, and the **National Security Agency (NSA)**
- Described by Rep. Burlison and by reporting as a UFO/UAP **whistleblower** and **firsthand witness**
- Described as a **close friend and colleague of David Grusch and Jake Barber** — two of the most significant named UAP whistleblowers to come forward to Congress — and named as one of roughly **40 firsthand witnesses** referenced in Grusch's 2023 inspector-general complaint
- Allegedly involved in the technology side of an alleged **"legacy" UAP program**, reportedly with **firsthand experience of recovered craft** believed to be of non-human origin; per reporting, this work was carried out for a private corporation working with a U.S. intelligence agency
- Was close enough to the topic that Burlison's office reached out proactively to schedule a private interview

Grusch testified under oath in July 2023 before the House Oversight Committee regarding a multi-decade UAP crash retrieval and reverse-engineering program. Jake Barber has publicly described himself as involved in UAP retrieval operations and has spoken openly about the dangers of coming forward. A close colleague of both, allegedly with direct exposure to recovered craft, would plausibly have had significant knowledge of classified UAP-adjacent programs.

Burlison says a letter intended to alert the FBI "had never been received" during the Biden administration and had to be resubmitted, and that the government's refusal to discuss any active investigation is "kind of the tell that they are actively investigating."

## Why This Death Raises Questions

- **Timing** — Death roughly two weeks before a scheduled private Congressional interview matches the investigation's most frequently documented pattern
- **Firsthand craft claim** — Per Burlison, Sullivan was in a "legacy program" with "firsthand experience with craft," making him a uniquely high-value witness
- **Formal IG review** — The report was not dismissed; the Office of Inspector General deemed the referral "warranted and credible" and directed that it be investigated
- **Lost FBI letter** — Burlison says the letter meant to alert the FBI "had never been received" during the Biden administration and had to be resubmitted — a striking failure for a case flagged as credible
- **FBI involvement** — The matter is now an active FBI referral, folded into a broader inquiry into a cluster of aerospace/defense figures who have died or gone missing
- **Whistleblower context** — Sullivan was a close friend of two of the highest-profile named UAP whistleblowers (Grusch and Barber) and one of ~40 firsthand witnesses in Grusch's complaint
- **Sitting Congressman on record** — The account comes from Rep. Burlison speaking publicly as a House Oversight member, not from anonymous social media

## The Counterargument

- The Northern District of Virginia medical examiner ruled the death an **accidental overdose** — a lethal combination of alcohol and prescription medications (alprazolam, cyclobenzaprine, imipramine) — not a homicide
- Accidental overdoses involving alcohol and multiple central-nervous-system depressants are tragically common and do not, by themselves, indicate foul play
- Death "within two weeks of a scheduled interview" is a suggestive timeline but not, by itself, proof of foul play
- The FBI referral is ongoing; no conclusions have been publicly released, and no evidence in the public record establishes that Sullivan was killed
- Skeptics (e.g., commentary at Osborne Ink) have argued that Grusch and allies may be framing a private tragedy as a national-security mystery
